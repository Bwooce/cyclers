## 1. Conventions used by Tables 14 to 19 (READ from sections II, III and V; Eq. numbers are the report's)

- Frame: barycentric rotating-pulsating (Nechville) coordinates (x, y), independent variable the true anomaly v of the primaries'
  orbit (eccentricity e, semilatus rectum p = 1 - e^2, r = p / (1 + e cos v), unit semi-major axis, mean motion 1, so the primaries'
  period is 2 pi). The primaries sit at x1 = -mu (mass m1 = 1 - mu) and x2 = 1 - mu (mass m2 = mu), mu being the "mass ratio" column.
  Dots are d/dv. Equations of motion, Eq. (33), p.6 (z = 0):
  xdd - 2 ydot = (r/p) [ x - m1 (x - x1)/r1^3 - m2 (x - x2)/r2^3 ],  ydd + 2 xdot = (r/p) [ y - m1 y/r1^3 - m2 y/r2^3 ],  r/p = 1/(1 + e cos v).
  (COMPUTED: `core.er3bp.er3bp_eom` at z = 0 is this, term by term, and uses the same mu convention; its prime is d/df with f = v.)
- Table headings read "ELL.RESTR.PROBL. MU=0.5 (E VARIABLE)) PERIAPSIS FAMILY 8" etc. Columns for the periodic families: NR, then
  XO and YDOT0 (initial x and ydot, with y0 = 0 and xdot0 = 0), then X1 and YDOT1 (final x and ydot, with y1 = 0 and xdot1 = 0),
  MASS RATIO, ECC. Table 18 (10P) has YO and XDOT0 instead (x0 = 0, ydot0 = 0) because those orbits cross the y axis at the start.
- Where the orbit starts: "PERIAPSIS" families (8P, 11P, 10P) start at v = 0 (primaries at minimum separation) and "APOAPSIS"
  families (8A, 11A) at v = pi (maximum separation); the final state is half a revolution later (v + pi). READ for 10P (p.66: "only
  one-quarter of the orbit (v = 0 to pi) must be integrated"); for the others COMPUTED: starting at v = 0 (P) or v = pi (A) and
  integrating for pi reproduces X1, YDOT1 and gives y1 = xdot1 = 0 (section 5), while the wrong start gives residuals of order 1.
- Periods (READ, p.51 to 66): 8P, 8A, 11P, 11A have period 2 pi (symmetric about the x axis, perpendicular crossings of the syzygy axis
  at both apsides); 10P has period 4 pi, symmetric about both axes ("x and y symmetric", p.66), integrated over a quarter.
- The collision table (Table 19) lists final conditions only: the ejection from m2 happens at v = 0 (Section 3).


## 2. Families 8P, 8A, 11P, 11A and 10P (section IV.E to IV.I, pp.51 and 66; Tables 14 to 18, pp.61 to 81)

All five families have mu = 0.5 (equal masses) and a variable eccentricity; Table 20 (p.95) records their sizes: 8P 118, 8A 92,
10P 76, 11P 52, 11A 93 orbits. In the tables below the "MASS RATIO" column (0.5000000 on every printed row, except the printer fault of
Table 18 row 56) is omitted. The last column, "max residual", is MINE (COMPUTED): the largest of |x1 computed - x1 printed|,
|ydot1 computed - ydot1 printed|, |y1| and |xdot1| after integrating the printed start for half a revolution with
`core.er3bp.propagate_er3bp` (DOP853, rtol = atol = 1e-13), from v = 0 for P families and v = pi for A families; it is not in the
paper. Rows 55 and 56 of Table 18 show 7e-01 because of the printer fault described in section 6.

### 2.1 Facts printed for each family (READ, pp.51 and 66)

- Family 8 (Fig. 15 is its (a1, a2) stability curve; Figs. 16 and 17 are the orbit plots of 8P and 8A). 8P begins at e = 0 with
  an orbit taken from Stromgren's circular equal-mass problem, in fact from Bartlett's class g (Ref. 18): x0 = -0.4017933,
  ydot0 = +3.1437189, mu = 0.5, e = 0 (Eq. 179; matches Table 14 row 1 to the printed digits), period 2 pi (p.51).
  Text: "About 120 orbits have been obtained in family 8P with eccentricities ranging from 0.0 to 0.075 (Table 14)" - the table runs to
  e = 0.975 with 118 rows, so "0.075" is a slip for 0.975 (INFERRED; the table is unambiguous). "It is not yet known whether this
  family can be prolongated all the way to e = 1.0"; there seems to be "an additional difficulty because the satellite has a close
  approach with one of the primaries when e approaches 0.97". Stability computed only up to e = 0.55: all unstable, region 6 (even
  semi-instability) of the stability diagram (Fig. 2) (p.51).
- Family 8A starts at e = 0 from the same orbit as 8P (the first rows of Tables 14 and 15 are identical). About 100 orbits to e = 0.85
  (the table has 92 rows; "no orbits with eccentricities higher than 0.85" because the differential correction was converging more
  and more slowly, and a "moderately close approach with one of the primaries" for the last orbits). Stability determined for all of
  them up to e = 0.85: 8A "starts in region 4, and passes (in region 2) all the way up to e = 0.85, except that the last computed
  orbit (e = 0.85) itself has just crossed the parabola a2 = a1^2/4 + 2. Therefore this orbit belongs to region 1 and is stable,
  unlike all of the other orbits of this family, which are unstable" (p.66). The 8A statement reads "region 4 ... in region 2"; I
  transcribe it as printed (the middle clause is unclear in the scan).
- Family 11 (Fig. 18 is its stability curve, Fig. 19 the 11P orbit plot, Fig. 20 the 11A plot). 11P begins at e = 0 with an orbit of
  period 2 pi taken from Stromgren's problem (also Bartlett's class g): x0 = -0.07084826, ydot0 = +0.82832745, mu = 0.5, e = 0
  (Eq. 180; Table 16 row 1 gives -0.0708483 and 0.8283275). "51 orbits have been computed with eccentricities from 0 to 0.453
  (Table 16)" (the table has 52 rows: the e = 0 orbit and 51 more). Not known whether it goes beyond e = 0.453: the last orbits show
  increasing convergence difficulty (the last, e = 0.453, took 10 iterations with very slow convergence; e = 0.454 diverged), not
  apparently due to a close approach with a primary (p.66).
  Stability of 11P: begins with the even instability of the circular problem at e = 0; the orbits then move in region 4
  (even-even instability, four real positive roots). "a2 reaches a maximum of about 13400 in the neighborhood of e = 0.18, and then
  decreases all the way to 1000 at e = 0.45. a1 is permanently increasing from -5000 to -200 when the eccentricity goes from 0.0 to
  0.45." All unstable (p.66).
- Family 11A starts from the same orbit as 11P (Tables 16 and 17 row 1 identical). About 100 orbits with e = 0.0 to 0.895
  (Table 17 has 93 rows); at e = 0.895 "there is a close approach with one of the primaries". Stability determined only up to e = 0.80
  (p.66, top of the right column): "a1 and a2 both go through a minimum: a1 = -5500 at e = 0.18, and a2 = -5200 at e = 0.67. From
  e = 0.0 to 0.30, all of the orbits are in region 6; from e = 0.30 to 0.80, they are all in region 3. All of the orbits of this family
  are thus unstable." (The paragraph sits under the family 11A heading in the second column.)
- Family 10P: "periodic orbits that are symmetric with respect to both the x-axis and the y-axis in the rotating-pulsating coordinate system"
  (Fig. 21). All have mu = 0.5. "In the circular restricted three-body problem, it is well known that only in the case of equal
  masses are there such periodic orbits that are symmetric with respect to both rotating coordinate axes" (Stromgren computed several);
  10P shows the elliptic problem has them too. First orbit (e = 0) from Bartlett's class nu: x0 = 0, y0 = -0.29524274,
  xdot0 = -0.31965294, ydot0 = 0 (Eq. 181; Table 18 row 1 gives -0.2952427 and -0.3196529). Period 4 pi, only a quarter integrated
  (a right-angle start on the y axis to a right-angle end on the x axis); at the start the primaries are at minimum elongation, so these
  are "p-orbits". 76 orbits with e = 0 to 0.70 "in a single computer run with an automatic program"; stopped at e = 0.70 because of slow
  convergence. "No attempt has yet been made to determine the reason for this slow convergence or to prolongate the family ... The
  stability and the characteristic exponents of these orbits have not been determined" (p.81).

## 3. The family of periodic collision orbits (section IV.J, pp.81 to 83; Table 19, pp.88 and 89)

### 3.1 What was computed (READ, pp.81 to 83 and p.18, p.25 to 26)

- "Using the regularized computer program, it was possible to find a family of 69 periodic collision orbits." Integrations used
  an eighth-order predictor-corrector (Adams-Moulton) in Birkhoff regularised coordinates within 0.07 canonical units of each
  primary (starter: Runge-Kutta-Merson, fourth order, automatic error control and step adjustment); outside those two discs the
  recurrent power series (terms up to twelfth order, integration step 0.03, against 0.005 for the Adams-Moulton) (p.81).
  Orbits that collide with m2 (= mu) only; the collision angle theta is kept at zero throughout (p.81).
- Why a one-parameter family in mu: ejection orbits depend only on the energy E and the angle theta (Eqs. 125 to 130; section 4.2).
  For symmetry (perpendicular crossing at v = pi) theta must stay fixed and only E can vary, so "for e and mu constants there will
  generally be no periodic symmetric collision orbits. If E and e are varied simultaneously by differential corrections, periodic
  orbits may then be found; these orbits form a one-parameter family in mu" (p.25). The corrector adjusts (E, e) at fixed mu to make
  y_F = 0 and xdot_F = 0 at the end of a half revolution (v = pi) (pp.25 to 26, p.81). No variational equations in Birkhoff
  coordinates existed: "the partial derivatives in this case have been obtained by numerical differencing of perturbed solutions"
  (p.26).
- Starting the search (p.81 to 82; Fig. 22, p.81): at mu = 0.5, theta = 0, eccentricity 0.1 to 0.5, energy between 0 and -1.5.
  Orbits with E above about -0.7 all escape; below -1.2 they are close satellite orbits and were dropped. The lines y_F = 0 and
  xdot_F = 0 were drawn in the (E, e) plane from the final values of y and xdot after a half revolution; an intersection was found
  "close to E = -0.79, e = +0.42", and from there the differential corrections gave the exact orbit. (Table 19 row 37, mu = 0.5, has
  E = -0.7897312 and e = 0.4169879, consistent.) Earlier attempts at mu = 0.5 directly failed ("unsuccessful"); a series of
  collision orbits in the equal-mass problem was then run (p.81).
- Family following (p.82): once the mu = 0.5 orbit existed, "it was not difficult to follow the corresponding family along
  increasing and decreasing mass-ratios" (Figs. 23 and 24 give e against mu and e against E). At e = 0 the family joins "a classical
  collision orbit of the circular three-body problem, with period 2 pi" (Table 19 row 1: mu = 0.6641096). At the other end (e = 1)
  "there seems to be a triple collision of both primaries and the satellite. The last orbit computed corresponds to e = 0.995,
  mu = 0.40. ... to be able to study the end of this family numerically, a program is necessary that can handle (and regularize
  eventually) the triple-collision phenomenon" (p.82). Table 19's last row is e = 0.9863937, mu = 0.3915046; the e = 0.995 orbit is
  not in the table (INFERRED: it was computed but not printed).
- Figs. 25 and 26 (p.83) show an e = 0 and an e = 0.98 orbit in Birkhoff coordinates; Fig. 27 (p.84) shows orbits at mu = 0.664,
  0.55, 0.50, 0.369 in inertial and rotating coordinates (the inertial captions read 0.664, 0.5 and 0.369). Fig. 23 (e against mu)
  has mu from 0.35 to 0.75 with energy labels from E = -1.15 at (mu about 0.66, e = 0) to E = -0.48 at e near 1. In Fig. 24 (e
  against E) the top label reads "mu = 0.30" at e of about 0.995, against the text's mu = 0.40 and Table 19's 0.39; I read the label
  at 300 dpi and it is 0.30. INFERRED: a slip for 0.40 (Fig. 23's axis starts at 0.35).

### 4.2 Subroutine DERIV with regularised Birkhoff coordinates (listing p.119 to 120; text p.90; equations Eqs. 88 to 130, pp.14 to 18)

This is the routine used by the regularised program. State X(1..7) = (xi, eta, xi', eta', E_m, v, t), where the prime here is d/ds, the
regularised independent variable, which does not appear in the right-hand sides. Constants in COMMON: AM2 = mu = m2, AM1 = 1 - mu = m1,
XI1 = -mu, XI2 = 1 - mu, E = e, P = 1 - e^2, SP = sqrt(P), XO = x0 = 1/2 - mu (Eq. 89).

Coordinates (READ, Eqs. 88 to 99, p.14). Median coordinates X = x - x0, Y = y put the primaries at X1 = -1/2 (m1) and X2 = +1/2 (m2). The
Birkhoff plane zeta = xi + i eta is defined by Z = X + iY = (zeta + 1/zeta)/4, a two-to-one conformal map branched at the primaries:
m1 is zeta = -1 and m2 is zeta = +1. With S = xi^2 + eta^2 and D = xi^2 - eta^2:

    X = (xi/4)(1 + 1/S),   Y = (eta/4)(1 - 1/S),   X^2 + Y^2 = (S + 1/S + 2D/S)/16,
    r1 = (S + 1 + 2 xi)/(4 sqrt(S)),   r2 = (S + 1 - 2 xi)/(4 sqrt(S)),   r1 r2 = ((S + 1)^2 - 4 xi^2)/(16 S),
    J = |dZ/dzeta|^2 = r1 r2 / S,   dv/ds = J                                           (Eqs. 93, 98, 99, 100)

Equations of motion (Eqs. 110, 116, 117), with the median energy E_m, U = (X^2 + Y^2)/2 + x0 X + m1/r1 + m2/r2 and
(r/p) = 1/(1 + e cos v):

    xi''  - 2 J eta' = d[(r/p) J U]/d xi  + E_m dJ/d xi
    eta'' + 2 J xi'  = d[(r/p) J U]/d eta + E_m dJ/d eta
    E_m'  = - e sin v (r1 r2 U) / (S (1 + e cos v)^2)             (Eq. 116, with r^2/p^2 = 1/(1 + e cos v)^2)
    v'    = J                                                     (Eq. 117)
    t'    = r1 r2 r^2 / (sqrt(p) S) = J r^2 / sqrt(p)             (Eq. 117; r = p/(1 + e cos v))

a seventh-order system. The derivatives are coded, in the listing's order (all double precision, no intermediate variables dropped):

    XIS = xi^2, ETS = eta^2, S = XIS + ETS, D = XIS - ETS
    EX = (xi/4)(1 + 1/S)                          [= X]
    XSYS = (S + 1/S + 2 D/S)/16                   [= X^2 + Y^2]
    R1 = (S + 1 + 2 xi)/(4 sqrt S),  R2 = (S + 1 - 2 xi)/(4 sqrt S),  RR = R1 R2,  AJ = RR/S   [= J]
    SS = S^2;  C = 4 S SS
    DJDXI = xi (2D - S - 1)/C,   DJDET = eta (2D + S - 1)/C                  (Eq. 113a: dJ/dxi, dJ/deta)
    DXDXI = (1 - D/SS)/4,   DXDET = -xi eta/(2 SS)                           (dX/dxi, dX/deta, Eq. 102)
    C = 8 SS
    DX2DX = xi (SS + 4 ETS - 1)/C,   DX2DE = eta (SS - 4 XIS - 1)/C          (d(X^2+Y^2)/dxi, /deta; = 2 A_eta and -2 A_xi, Eq. 108)
    DR12X = xi (SS - 4 ETS - 1)/C,   DR12E = eta (SS + 4 XIS - 1)/C          (d(r1 r2)/dxi, /deta, Eq. 113d)
    C = 4 sqrt(SS S) = 4 S^(3/2);  S1 = S - 1
    DR1DX = (S1 xi + 2 ETS)/C,  DR1DE = (S1 - 2 xi) eta/C                    (Eq. 113b)
    DR2DX = (S1 xi - 2 ETS)/C,  DR2DE = (S1 + 2 xi) eta/C                    (Eq. 113c)
    ECV = e cos v;  C = 1 + ECV;  EP1 = 1/C;  R = P/C                        [EP1 = r/p]
    C2 = XSYS/2 + XO EX
    RRV = AM1 R2 + AM2 R1 + RR C2                                            [= r1 r2 U]
    A = -2 EP1/SS;  B = EP1/S
    DJVDX = A xi RRV  + B (AM1 DR2DX + AM2 DR1DX + C2 DR12X + RR (DX2DX/2 + XO DXDXI))
    DJVDE = A eta RRV + B (AM1 DR2DE + AM2 DR1DE + C2 DR12E + RR (DX2DE/2 + XO DXDET))
    F(1) = X(3);  F(2) = X(4)
    F(3) = 2 AJ X(4)  + DJVDX + X(5) DJDXI
    F(4) = -2 AJ X(3) + DJVDE + X(5) DJDET
    F(5) = -RRV e sin(X(6)) / (S C^2)
    F(6) = AJ
    F(7) = RR R R/(S SP)

DJVDX and DJVDE are d[(r/p) J U]/d xi and /d eta, with J U = RRV/S (the product r1 r2 U avoids the singular terms m/r in U). READ
and checked against Eqs. (110), (113) and (116); the identification of DX2DX with 2 A_eta is mine (INFERRED from Eq. 108b).
The energy variable X(5) is the "median" energy E_m, not the barycentric E: E_m = E + (r/p) x0^2/2 (the listing's XS2 = x0^2/2,
and the output step subtracts XS2*EP1). The paper's text then says that Eq. (86)'s E must not be used inside the integration because
r1 and r2 appear in its denominators (p.16), and the regularised E_m' above is integrated instead.

Conversion subroutines (listing pp.120 to 121; COMPUTED: CARBR followed by BIRCR returns the input state to the printed digits, checked on one state):

- CARBR(XC, XB): Cartesian median (X, Y, Xdot, Ydot) to Birkhoff. A = X^2 - Y^2 - 1/4, B = 2 X Y; (U, V) = COMSQR(A, B) is the complex
  square root with V of the sign of B; xi = 2 (X + U), eta = 2 (Y + V) (the other determination, the second point of the 2-to-1 map, is
  obtained by changing the signs of A and B in COMSQR, p.92); with DXDXI = (1 - D/SS)/4, DYDET = DXDXI, DXDET = -xi eta/(2 SS),
  DYDXI = -DXDET: xi' = DYDET Xdot - DXDET Ydot, eta' = -DYDXI Xdot + DXDXI Ydot. (X5, X6 are copied.)
- BIRCR(XB, XC): the inverse. X = (xi/4)(1 + 1/S), Y = (eta/4)(1 - 1/S), Xdot = (DXDXI xi' + DXDET eta')/AJ, Ydot = (DYDXI xi' + DYDET eta')/AJ.
  Fails when r1 = 0 or r2 = 0 only in the sense that velocities in these formulas carry r1 and r2 in the denominators (p.92).
- COMSQR: A = sqrt((AA + T)/2), B = +/- sqrt((-AA + T)/2) with T = max(sqrt(AA^2 + BB^2), |AA|).

Ejection initial conditions (READ, Eqs. 124 to 130, p.18, and the listing's labels 3 and 4, p.117). With the energy equation written so it
stays valid at r1 = 0 or r2 = 0, r1 r2 E = (S/2)(xi'^2 + eta'^2) - (r/p){ r1 r2 [(X^2 + Y^2)/2 + x0 X + x0^2/2] + (m1 r2 + m2 r1) },
a collision with m2 is xi = +1, eta = 0 (S = D = 1, r1 = 1, r2 = 0), and then (xi'^2 + eta'^2)/2 = m2 r/p, so
xi' = sqrt(2 m2 r/p) cos theta, eta' = sqrt(2 m2 r/p) sin theta (Eq. 130); for m1, xi = -1 and the same with m1 (Eq. 127). "The
magnitude of the regularised velocity does not depend upon the energy E"; E and theta are the two parameters of the ejection family.
The listing sets XB(3) = sqrt(2 AM1 EP1) cos(theta) (m1) or sqrt(2 U EP1) cos(theta) (m2, U = mu) with EP1 = 1/(1 + e cos(v0)),
XB(5) = E_input + XS2 EP1, and (R1, R2) = (0, 1) or (1, 0). The physical direction is twice the angle in the Birkhoff plane near
the branch point (Z - 1/2 is about (zeta - 1)^2/4), so theta = 0 is ejection along +x, away from m1 (INFERRED; consistent with Fig. 27).

Main-program structure (ORBIT, listing pp.116 to 118; READ, p.95 and listing). The orbit is integrated in Birkhoff variables with the
fixed-step routine RUK (a fourth-order Runge-Kutta in the listing; the text says the production runs used an eighth-order
predictor-corrector) while r1 < XI(21) or r2 < XI(22) (default 0.07), and by recurrent power series (ERPSV with variable step DVC = r1 r2 h)
elsewhere, converting between the two at each region change (CARBR/BIRCR with the energy shift above, and r = p/(1 + e cos v),
rdot = e sin v r^2/p reset for the power-series state). The end of the orbit is placed at v = SF = pi (XI(14) + XI(16)) by secant
steps in s: DSS = (SPREC - S)(v - SF)/(v - vprev), repeated until |v - SF| < 1e-13. Axis crossings are counted through
YACT = eta (xi^2 + eta^2 - 1), whose sign is the sign of y. Alarms (IALARM, set from the listing's labels 12 to 16 and 30): p <= 0 (e >= 1), xi^2 + eta^2 below 0.001 or above 1000 (the orbit
has run to infinity in the Birkhoff plane), E_m above 50, more than 15 refinement steps at the end of the orbit, and the
crossing-count and step-count limits.

### 4.3 Subroutine DERIV with rotating-pulsating coordinates, with variational equations (listing pp.106 to 107; text p.90)

State X(1..22): x, y, xdot, ydot (1 to 4), the 16 variational variables (5 to 20), time (21), true anomaly (22); independent variable v.
With C1 = x - x1, C2 = x - x2, S12 = C1^2 + y^2 (= r1^2), S22 = C2^2 + y^2 (= r2^2), C4 = 1 + e cos v, R = P/C4, C7 = 1/C4,
V3 = m1/r1^3 + m2/r2^3:

    F(1) = X(3), F(2) = X(4)
    F(3) = 2 X(4) + C7 (x - m1 C1/r1^3 - m2 C2/r2^3)
    F(4) = -2 X(3) + C7 y (1 - m1/r1^3 - m2/r2^3)
    DFXX = C7 (1 - V3 + 3 (m1 C1^2/r1^5 + m2 C2^2/r2^5))
    DFXY = 3 C7 y (m1 C1/r1^5 + m2 C2/r2^5)
    DFYY = C7 (1 - V3 + 3 y^2 (m1/r1^5 + m2/r2^5))
    for I = 5, 9, 13, 17 (four columns of the matrix):
        F(I) = X(I+2);  F(I+1) = X(I+3)
        F(I+2) = DFXX X(I) + DFXY X(I+1) + 2 X(I+3)
        F(I+3) = DFXY X(I) + DFYY X(I+1) - 2 X(I+2)
    F(21) = R^2/Q (Q = sqrt(p), so dt/dv = r^2/sqrt(p), Eq. 34), F(22) = 1

The four columns' initial values must form the unit matrix; the result is the principal fundamental matrix R (p.90). The author
warns that precision is lost on close approaches or at high e, and that a simple Runge-Kutta will not give high-precision
variations. The plain pulsating DERIV (listing p.107, 5 variables plus v) is the same without the variational columns. READ and checked
against Eq. (33) and its differentiation; the second derivatives written out above are my regrouping of the coded C9, C10 terms.

### 4.4 Subroutine DERIV with inertial coordinates (listing p.108; text p.89)

Barycentric inertial (xi, eta, xidot, etadot, t), independent variable the time t = mean anomaly (valid for e up to and including 1).
AM = X(5) + CHI (CHI a mean-anomaly offset); KEPLER solves E - e sin E = AM by Newton iteration to 1e-13 with a bisection fallback when
the slope 1 - e cos E would be below 1e-3 (the case e = 1); then COSV = r cos v = cos E - e, SINV = r sin v = sqrt(1 - e^2) sin E;
primary positions CX1 = -mu COSV, CY1 = -mu SINV (m1) and CX2 = (1 - mu) COSV, CY2 = (1 - mu) SINV (m2); accelerations
-m1 (xi - CX1)/s1^3 - m2 (xi - CX2)/s2^3 and likewise for eta (the listing's UMM = 1 - mu = m1, RU = mu = m2). This is a
plain Cartesian N-body-style integration with analytic primary motion: no pulsation and no rotation, so it is independent of the form of
Eq. (33). I used it as a third formulation (section 5).

### 4.5 ERPSV: recurrent power series with variational equations (listing pp.102 to 106; text pp.90 to 91; Tables 1 and 2 of part A)

READ from the text and the first listing pages (p.102 to 103); the later pages (104 to 106, the long list of convolution sums) were
skimmed for structure only and are not transcribed. Independent variable v, state (13 components) X(1..7) = x, y, xdot, ydot, r (the
primaries' distance), rdot, t; X(8), X(9) are z, zdot (unused in two dimensions); X(10..13) = r1, r2, r1^-3, r2^-3. A 4 x 4
matrix A holds the 16 variational partial derivatives. Taylor coefficients are in P-arrays of at most 20 terms (NT terms), the
variables being P1, P2 (x, y), P4, P5 (xdot, ydot), P7, P8, P9 (r, rdot, t), P10, P11 (r1, r2), P12, P13 (r1^-3, r2^-3), P14 = 1 - m1 P12 - m2 P13,
P15 = P1 P14 + m1 m2 (P13 - P12) (x-force), P16 = P2 P14 (y-force), P18 = P7^2, P19 to P34 the 16 matrix elements, P35 to P38
(the direction cosines (x - x1)/r1, (x - x2)/r2, y/r1, y/r2), P39 to P42 (3 cos^2 - 1 combinations), P43, P44 (3 cos cos), P45 to P47 = the
second derivatives of the potential (P45 = 1 + m1 P12 P39 + m2 P13 P40, P46 = m1 P12 P43 + m2 P13 P44, P47 = 1 + m1 P12 P41 + m2 P13 P42,
that is Omega_xx, Omega_xy, Omega_yy) and P48 to P50 = P7 times those, divided by p (the (r/p) Omega second derivatives). The first lines of the recursion
for the (N+1)-th coefficient from order N are, as printed (p.103): P1(N+1) = P4(N)/N; P2(N+1) = P5(N)/N; P4(N+1) = +2 P5(N);
P5(N+1) = -2 P4(N); P7(N+1) = P8(N)/N; P8(N+1) = P18(N); P9(N+1) = P18(N)/(N Q); P10(N+1) = mu P4(N); P11(N+1) = -(1 - mu) P4(N);
P12(N+1) = P13(N+1) = 0; the convolution sums over the products are then added (for example P4(N+1) gets sum of P7 P15/p, and
P4, P5 are then divided by N), and the same is done for the 16 variational series (Eqs. 139 and 141 and Tables 1 and 2 of part A).
INIT = 1 first step, 2 following steps (r1, r2,
r1^-3, r2^-3 carried from the previous step), 3 to evaluate the series only (odd-v interpolation points). DT is the step in v.
The program evaluates the series by Horner's scheme, DO 120 K = 2, NT. The author notes that the code is written with long lists of
statements instead of nested loops for speed on most compilers (p.91).

### 4.6 Differential-correction subroutine SEARCH, SOLVE2 and INPOL (listing pp.121 to 123; text pp.92 to 93)

SEARCH(X, G, YO, Y, YF, DEL, EPS, N, M, NP, NAL, MC, EP, L) is a general-purpose finite-difference corrector (Eqs. 186 to 190):
nominal solution with initial parameters x_i, final y_i; deviations Delta y_i = Y_i - y_i; the partials d y_i/d x_j = (y_ij - y_i)/delta_j
from solutions with the j-th initial parameter perturbed by delta_j; solve Delta y = (dy/dx) Delta x for the correction; repeated until
|Delta y| or |Delta x| fall below given epsilons; EP is a determinant limit below which the matrix is declared singular. It calls
a general matrix inversion (not printed), and SOLVE2 is a short two-variable solver because the symmetric-orbit problem needs only two
variables (x0 and ydot0, or E and e). This is the corrector that produced the collision family. It is "adapted to symmetric periodic
orbits only" (p.95). INPOL is a Lagrange-Aitken interpolator on the saved table of past orbits (the last ten or so) for the next guess.

## 7. How the collision family relates to Henon 1968 and to Gomez and Olle 1986 (#899)

What each object is (READ in the three sources; digests `docs/notes/2026-10-04-digest-henon-1968-consecutive-collision-orbits.md`
and `docs/notes/2026-10-04-digest-gomez-olle-1986-elliptic-restricted-mu-0.md`):

| | Broucke 1969 collision family | Henon 1968 consecutive-collision arcs | Gomez and Olle 1986 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mass ratio | finite, 0.3635 to 0.6641 | mu = 0 (the secondary only marks a point; arcs are Kepler ellipses round the primary) | mu = 0 (rotating pulsating two-body problem) |
| Primaries' orbit | elliptic, e = 0 to 0.9864 (printed) | circular | elliptic, e_p from 0 to 1 |
| Object | one periodic orbit of the full restricted problem that starts at a collision with m2 and, by symmetry, ends at one, once per period 2 pi | a Kepler arc that meets the secondary's position twice (times -tau and +tau): the building block of second-species orbits | the same arcs for the elliptic primaries (OCC), plus double-collision orbits (rectilinear sidereal orbits colliding with both primaries) |
| Collision handled | exactly, by Birkhoff regularisation (the orbit passes through m2) | by construction: the arc is defined as the Kepler ellipse through the secondary's position | by the same algebra |
| Free parameters | E and theta at ejection; theta fixed at 0, so E varies with (e, mu) | (a, e) of the ellipse from tau and eta | tau, eta, signs |

Relations (INFERRED unless marked):

1. Same singular mechanism, different regime. Poincare's second-species solutions are periodic orbits of the restricted problem whose
   near-collision arcs tend, as the secondary's mass goes to zero, to the collision arcs Henon tabulates (Henon 1968 section IV, READ in
   his digest). Broucke's orbits are the exact collision limit at FINITE mass: the satellite really hits m2 and the regularised flow
   continues through the pass (in Birkhoff variables the collision is the branch point zeta = +1 of the 2-to-1 map, and the regularised orbit is
   smooth through it, as the 100-step integration of section 5.3 shows). They are not second-species orbits and their mass ratios (0.36 to 0.66) are far from the
   limit; they cannot be used as seeds for a mu -> 0 continuation without first following mu down, which Broucke's own family does not do
   (its mu minimum is 0.3635).
2. A common test of a regularised or collision-aware integrator. Broucke's orbit has one collision per period 2 pi. The e = 0 member (mu = 0.6641096, E = -1.1470386, x1 = 0.4317709,
   ydot1 = 3.5154248, period 2 pi) is "a classical collision orbit of the circular problem", the finite-mass analogue of the circular
   Henon arc. So the first row of Table 19 is the one published circular-problem collision orbit available to cross-check a
   regularised propagator against Henon-style ellipse bookkeeping at mu > 0 (but at mu = 0.664, which has m2 the LARGER primary; the project's
   convention puts the Moon at mu of about 0.0122).
3. The e -> 1 end of Broucke's family is a triple collision (the primaries collide with each other at v = 0 as well as with the
   satellite, p.82). The Gomez and Olle double-collision orbits are a different thing: a rectilinear sidereal orbit that hits P1 at one epoch
   and P2 at another, at mu = 0, for e_p in (0, 1) (READ in their digest). Broucke's rectilinear problem (e = 1, part A section IV.B) is
   the neighbour of Gomez and Olle's e_p = 1 case; the two have not been compared here.
4. Periodic symmetric structure. Henon and Gomez and Olle impose the symmetry (x, y, t) to (x, -y, -t) at a collision epoch; Broucke
   imposes the same symmetry with the collision at v = 0 and a perpendicular crossing at v = pi (xdot = y = 0). In Broucke the second
   condition fixes (E, e) at given mu; in the second-species theory the same symmetry fixes (tau, eta).


## 8. Techniques applicable to the project's problems

### #899 (second-species and collision-orbit enumeration, continuation through near-collision seeds)

- Table 19 is 69 published collision-orbit states (mu from 0.3635 to 0.6641, e from 0 to 0.986), each independently verified here
  with a regularised propagator (section 5.3): a sourced, non-circular test set for any collision-aware or regularised integrator
  the project builds for #899. The test: start at xi = 1, eta = 0 with xi' = sqrt(2 mu/(1 + e)), eta' = 0 and E_m = ENERGY + (1/2)(1/2 - mu)^2/(1 + e); integrate to
  v = pi; compare X1, YDOT1 and require y1 = xdot1 = 0 (tolerance about 1e-4 at high e, 1e-6 at low e).
- Continuation method with a two-variable corrector (E, e) at fixed mu, using theta fixed at 0 and the perpendicular-crossing
  conditions; the family is then followed in mu. It is the same structure as the project's second-species seeding: a symmetric orbit from
  a collision state, corrected on (E, e) rather than on the initial state of a regular orbit. Broucke obtained the partials by numerical
  differencing of perturbed solutions (no regularised variational equations existed in 1969); the project's version should do the
  same first (cheap, ~100 steps per orbit) and keep the analytic regularised transition matrix (#928) as the improvement.
- Fig. 22's search recipe (draw the lines y_F = 0 and xdot_F = 0 in the (E, e) plane from a grid of final values, intersect, then
  correct) is a cheap way to seed a collision-orbit continuation that fails from a guess; Broucke reports that guessing mu = 0.5 directly
  "was unsuccessful".
- What this does not give: no second-species orbit and no small-mu member. The family's mu never goes below 0.3635 (section 7).

### #912 (elliptic periodic-orbit code lacks the published method)

- Tables 14 to 17 give, at the SAME e, a periapsis-start and an apoapsis-start orbit of the same family with a common e = 0 member
  (8P and 8A; 11P and 11A): exactly the pairing Peng and Xu 2015 use (two groups from one circular orbit). The paired orbits have
  different stability (8P all region 6; 8A region 4 then 2, and 1 at the end), which is the published example that the two
  counterparts need not share stability. So they are ready controls for a corrector that must start at either apse.
  (Broucke's families are equal-mass, mu = 0.5, not Earth-Moon.)
- Broucke's corrector: symmetric orbits are corrected in two variables only ((x0, ydot0), or (y0, xdot0) for 10P), imposing the strong
  periodicity criterion y1 = xdot1 = 0 at half a revolution (v0 + pi), with a least-squares step when the 2 x 2 (4 x 4 for the general
  case) matrix is nearly singular, and an extrapolation table of the last ~10 family members for the next guess (pp.25 to 26, 94).
  That is a design for the project's missing apoapsis start and for the half-revolution perpendicular-crossing residual; #930's fix
  (event-located crossings instead of a fixed-time residual) is the same idea.
- Conversions the corrector needs when started at apoapsis: an A orbit has v0 = pi, and the half-revolution end is v0 + pi = 2 pi
  (equivalently 0); `core.er3bp` takes f_span = (pi, 2 pi) directly (used in section 5.1).

### #925 (elliptic-problem controls that do not reproduce: model or paper)

- Evidence for "model sound": 429 of 431 printed orbits in Tables 14 to 18 (e = 0 to 0.975, mu = 0.5, periapsis and apoapsis starts)
  close in `core.er3bp` at the seventh digit, and the same states close in two independent formulations (the regularised Birkhoff equations from
  the listing, and a plain barycentric inertial integration with the primaries on Kepler ellipses, sections 5.2 and 4.4). The inertial
  formulation does not use Eq. (33), the pulsating frame, or the true-anomaly independent variable, so it is the "independent
  elliptic formulation written not from `core.er3bp`" that #925 asks for, at one mass ratio. This supports the model's orbit dynamics (as the Peng and Xu
  resolution did for item (2)); it does not by itself settle item (1), the Mako and Salamon stability bands, because it tests
  closed orbits, not stability, and it does not test mu near 0.01 or z-motion.
- Because Broucke's (a1, a2) coefficients (section 4 of part A; Eq. 166 and the seven regions, pp.26 to 29) are the same quantities as
  Hadjidemetriou's (alpha, beta) (alpha = a1, beta = a2, the quartic lambda^4 + a1 lambda^3 + a2 lambda^2 + a1 lambda + 1 = 0, regions
  bounded by the parabola a2 = a1^2/4 + 2 and the lines a2 = -2 a1 - 2 and a2 = 2 a1 - 2), the stability bands the project computes for these
  families can be compared with Broucke's printed regions (8P region 6 to e = 0.55; 8A region 4 then 2 to e = 0.85, region 1 at 0.85 itself; 11P region 4 to
  e = 0.45; 11A region 6 to e = 0.30 then region 3 to 0.80). Those are text statements read from the scan, and I have not
  reproduced them numerically here (follow-up F1).

### #928 (a regularised propagator and transition matrix for close passes)

- Section 4.2 is a complete, tested, planar, e >= 0 regularised formulation: Birkhoff coordinates (the 2-to-1 conformal map
  Z = (zeta + 1/zeta)/4 that sends both primaries to branch points), independent variable s with dv/ds = J = r1 r2/S, median energy E_m as a
  seventh state variable, no explicit s in the right-hand side, regular at both primaries. In the project's units (mu is the
  secondary's mass fraction, m1 at -mu) it applies unchanged to the elliptic problem and, with e = 0 and E_m constant, to the circular one.
  It already integrates ejection from a primary (Table 19 reproduced). Differences from the Levi-Civita form planned in #928: Birkhoff regularises
  both primaries at once in one chart (so a single integration through either collision); the Levi-Civita form of `scripts/`
  (#670) regularises one primary.
- Controls from this paper, in order of cost: (i) the per-orbit statistics of section 5.3 (69 collision orbits, about 100 DOP853 steps each,
  residuals 8e-6 median); (ii) a regular orbit round trip (CARBR then BIRCR returns the input; integrating a regular orbit in Birkhoff
  variables reproduces `core.er3bp` to 5e-12 at v = 0.5, 1, 2, 3, section 5.2a); (iii) the e = 0 Table 19 row against the circular Henon arc
  bookkeeping of section 7.
- For the transition matrix: Broucke did NOT have one (partial derivatives by numerical differencing of perturbed solutions, p.26). The
  derivative expressions already written out in section 4.2 (DJDXI, DJDET, DXDXI, DR1DX, ...) are the first derivatives of J, r1, r2 and
  X, which are what a variational equation in Birkhoff variables needs (second derivatives still have to be derived, with the A_xi, A_eta
  relations of Eqs. 111 and 112 as checks). The pulsating-coordinate variational equations of section 4.3 are an independent
  published form against which an eventual regularised transition matrix can be compared away from the primaries (the two should give
  the same transition matrix after the coordinate Jacobian of CARBR is applied; the symplectic check relative to the matrix S (Eqs. 156 and 159, p.26 to 27)
  holds in the pulsating form).
- Warning for a re-implementer: in Birkhoff variables dv/ds = J is small far from both primaries (J = r1 r2/S falls as S goes to 0 or infinity),
  so a fixed cap on the s interval ends the integration early (it did in my first run); use the event v = target with a large s limit.
  Also the Birkhoff map is two-to-one: CARBR returns one of the two preimages (the second is obtained by the sign choice in COMSQR); a
  continuation must keep to a sheet or track it.

### #930 (the elliptic corrector can pass a bad orbit silently)

- Broucke's closure test is mechanical and cheap: the symmetric criterion y1 = xdot1 = 0 after half a revolution (and the target
  (x1, ydot1) reproducible from the same start), with an internal consistency check on the monodromy: the coefficients of lambda^4 and
  lambda^0 must be equal and those of lambda^3 and lambda^1 equal (p.27, Eq. 162: "used for checking the precision of the numerical
  computations. In most cases, a precision of at least 10^-10 has been obtained for these two relations"). That is a
  hard check that does not depend on the corrector's tolerance. A bad orbit that the corrector accepts will fail one of the two.
- Test data: all 429 rows of Tables 14 to 18 are known-good periodic orbits at e up to 0.975; the corrector's accept/reject decision can
  be checked against them (accept all, with the half-revolution residual below 1e-5 for e below 0.8). The deliberately wrong cases of
  the Gomez and Olle 1986 control already noted in #930 are the complementary negatives.
- Printed defect to watch: Table 18 rows 55 and 56 (section 6 item 1) would be silently mis-parsed by any table reader that trusts the
  column positions; a closure check catches them (0.68 residual).

### #931 (stability classification and general-three-body controls)

- Broucke's stability coefficients are Hadjidemetriou's (alpha, beta) (above), and Broucke's seven regions are the regime catalogue
  #931(b) asks for: parabola Delta = 0 (a2 = a1^2/4 + 2) and its two tangent lines a2 = +-2 a1 - 2 touching it at (a1, a2) = (+-4, 6), giving
  six unstable regions (names proposed for the six kinds of instability, Fig. 3 root configurations, Table 3 of part A) and one stable region; the circular
  problem lies on the line a2 = -2 a1 - 2 (p.28). The classifier tests of #931(b) can use these regions as the synthetic spectra.
- The collision orbits and the 10P family are periodic orbits of the elliptic restricted problem, not of the general three-body problem;
  Hadjidemetriou's Table I rows are unrelated to them. Nothing here supplies a general-three-body control.
