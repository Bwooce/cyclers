## 0. What the paper is, and what "numerical regularization" means

A short methods paper (about seven pages of content) that writes out, as a computer algorithm, the Kustaanheimo-Stiefel (KS) regularisation of ONE binary in an N-body system. Abstract (p.167): "The regularization of a single binary collision in the gravitational problem of n-bodies is developed for three dimensional motion using Kustaanheimo and Stiefel's generalization of Levi-Civita's method. Detailed formulas are given showing how the conventional set of equations of motion for n bodies may be modified to allow for the treatment of close approaches and collisions of two of the particles. Numerical results are presented showing numerical integrations through arbitrary close approaches with reasonable time steps and uniform accuracy. Even with actual collisions (numerically a machine zero) the time step is finite and, of course, depends upon the desired accuracy."

"Numerical" here does NOT mean step-size control. It means a regularisation presented as a ready-to-code algorithm for a general N-body code. Concretely it is all three of these, in one scheme: (a) a coordinate change (KS, four-dimensional, canonical, Hamiltonian derivation, p.169), (b) the time transformation dt = r dtau with r the separation of the regularised pair (p.170), and (c) numerical measures: choose the pair with the smallest mutual distance at each moment as the regularised one (p.171), avoid cancellation of large terms in the centre-of-mass momentum (p.171), and use the regularised Hamiltonian, which is identically zero, as the energy check (p.171). The integrator is deliberately plain (fifth-order Runge-Kutta, variable step, "admittedly rather inefficient", p.173), because the only objective is to compare rectangular against regularised coordinates.

Relation to the other papers: this is the unperturbed-pair-plus-perturbation KS Hamiltonian that Aarseth and Zare (1974, section 8, p.202) say is recovered from their eight-dimensional form by dropping the third body ("a Hamiltonian which is identical to the unperturbed KS formulation of Peters (1968)"), and that Heggie (1974) extends to a global form. It does not treat a massless particle explicitly (see section 3).

## 1. The method written out (READ pp.168-172)

### 1.1 Modified Jacobian coordinates (section 2, p.168)

Inertial frame at the centre of mass, rectangular coordinates r_i = (x_i, y_i, z_i), momenta P_i. Hamiltonian
H = (1/2) sum_{i=1..N} P_i^2/m_i - sum_{i<j} m_i m_j / r_ij, with r_ij = |r_i - r_j|,
G = 1. Let (k, l) be the pair with the smallest mutual distance, r_kl = min(r_ij). Define, for that pair only:
r = r_l - r_k, r_g = M_1 r_k + M_2 r_l, with M_1 = m_k/(m_k + m_l), M_2 = m_l/(m_k + m_l)
(r_g is the centre of mass G of the pair; Fig. 1). The canonical transformation is generated by S = (-M_2 r + r_g).P_k + (M_1 r + r_g).P_l + sum'_j r_j.P_j (the sum excludes j = k, l). With P_r conjugate to r and P_g = P_k + P_l the pair's momentum, the new Hamiltonian is
H' = P_r^2/(2 mu) - m_k m_l/r + R,
mu^-1 = m_k^-1 + m_l^-1, r = |r|, P_r = |P_r|, and (p.169)
R = (1/2) ( P_g^2/(m_k + m_l) + sum'_{i} P_i^2/m_i ) - sum**_{i<j} m_i m_j/r_ij,
the sums excluding k and l and the pair's mutual term: R is the "perturbation" Hamiltonian, containing the pair's centre-of-mass motion, every other body, and the other bodies' attraction on both members of the pair, and is "functionally dependent upon the q_i" only (it contains no regularised variable and is not needed in closed form).

### 1.2 The KS transformation (section 3, pp.169-170)

Levi-Civita regularisation does not extend to three dimensions in complex variables; KS extends it to four dimensions with spinors, but "in the present application it seems preferable ... to use Hamiltonian formalism". Add a cyclic fourth coordinate q4 (the physical coordinates x, y, z are q1, q2, q3), H' = (1/(2 mu)) sum_{i=1..3} P_i^2 - m_k m_l/r + R, and introduce (eq. 1)
q1 = u1^2 - u2^2 - u3^2 + u4^2, q2 = 2 (u1 u2 - u3 u4), q3 = 2 (u1 u3 + u2 u4),
with q4 left unspecified except through (eq. 2) dq4/dt = -2 u4 du1/dt + 2 u3 du2/dt - 2 u2 du3/dt + 2 u1 du4/dt (this is the bilinear condition that fixes the extra degree of freedom). The Jacobian matrix of (q to u) is, written inversely as dq/dt = A du/dt (read on the image),
A = 2 [ [u1, -u2, -u3, u4], [u2, u1, -u4, -u3], [u3, u4, u1, u2], [-u4, u3, -u2, u1] ],
and A A* = 4 r I_4 (A* the transpose; eq. before 3), so A is orthogonal up to the factor 4r, A^-1 = A*/(4r) = J (eq. 3). Momenta transform as p = J* P_u, i.e. p = (1/(4r)) A P_u, and
sum p_i^2 = p*p = (1/(4r)) sum P_ui^2, "the orthogonality of A which preserves the form of the momenta as the sum of squares in the Hamiltonian". The Hamiltonian becomes
H'' = (1/(8 mu r)) sum_{i=1..4} P_ui^2 - m_k m_l/r + R.
Regularising time transformation tau = integral dt/r, so dt = r dtau; new Hamiltonian K = (H'' - h) r with h the constant value of H; K is identically zero on the solution:
K = (1/(8 mu)) sum P_ui^2 - m_k m_l + (R - h) r, with r = u1^2 + u2^2 + u3^2 + u4^2.
"Since the vector u and the corresponding momenta have four components this means the addition of two first order differential equations to the equations of motion. Also there is the equation for dt/dtau. Thus for a system of N particles we must integrate a system of 6N + 3 first order differential equations instead of the usual 6N" (p.170).

### 1.3 The algorithm (section 4, pp.171-172)

The pair with the smallest mutual distance is the regularised one; K is the "energy check". Primes denote d/dtau. Equations of motion (p.171):
- r = u1^2 + u2^2 + u3^2 + u4^2;
- r_g' = (r/(m_k + m_l)) P_g;
- r_i' = (r/m_i) P_i, for i = 1..N excluding i = k, l;
- u' = P_u/(4 mu) (a four-vector; the paper writes "U' = (1/4mu) P_4", P_4 read as the four-component momentum P_u);
- P_i' = r m_i sum_{j != i} m_j r_ji/r_ji^3 (the force on body i multiplied by r, with the printed sign convention for r_ji; for i = k omit the term j = l and for i = l omit j = k);
- P_g' = P_k' + P_l'.
The omitted terms of P_k' and P_l' "cancel analytically when P_g' is formed, but since they are the largest terms, numerically this would lead to small differences of large numbers (subtraction error). Their use here is justified since they are only used for the computation of P_g'" (p.171): the pair's mutual force never enters. Each pair r_ij is computed once and added to the two sums (compute r_ij = r_i - r_j and a = r_ij/r_ij^3; replace P_i by P_i - a m_j and P_j by P_j + a m_i), and R is accumulated in the same loop. With the 4 x 3 matrix (p.171, 172, read on the images)
A* = [ [u1, u2, u3], [-u2, u1, u4], [-u3, -u4, u1], [u4, -u3, u2] ]
define the perturbation gradient (4 x 1)
dR/du = -2 mu A* (P_k'/(r m_k) - P_l'/(r m_l)),
where "the terms in parentheses are available from the computation of P_i', and they do not involve a division by r" (the division is a factor already carried). Then (p.172)
P_u' = 2 (h - R) u - r dR/du.
COMPUTED check: differentiating K above, P_u' = -dK/du = -2 (R - h) u - r dR/du, which is the printed form. Control (p.172): -u4 P_u1 + u3 P_u2 - u2 P_u3 + u1 P_u4 = 0 (the bilinear relation between u and P_u that holds for the physical solutions).

Transformations (p.172-173). Original to regularised: r = r_l - r_k, r_g = M_1 r_k + M_2 r_l, P_g = P_k + P_l, P = -M_2 P_k + M_1 P_l, and for x > 0 (x the first component of r): u4 = 0, u1 = sqrt((r + x)/2), u2 = y/(2 u1), u3 = z/(2 u1); for x < 0: u3 = 0, u2 = sqrt((r - x)/2), u1 = y/(2 u2), u4 = z/(2 u2). Momentum P_u = 2 A* P. Inverse: r = (u1^2 - u2^2 - u3^2 + u4^2, 2 (u1 u2 - u3 u4), 2 (u1 u3 + u2 u4)), r = |u|^2, r_k = -M_2 r + r_g, r_l = M_1 r + r_g, P = (1/(2r)) A P_u (A the transpose of A*), P_k = -P + M_1 P_g, P_l = P + M_2 P_g.
COMPUTED checks: (i) the x < 0 branch satisfies the KS map: with u3 = 0, x = u1^2 - u2^2 + u4^2 and r = u1^2 + u2^2 + u4^2 give r - x = 2 u2^2, y = 2 u1 u2 and z = 2 u2 u4, matching the printed u's; (ii) the 4 x 3 matrix A* is, term for term, one half of the 4 x 3 matrix A_1 of Aarseth and Zare's eq. 51 (their A_1 = 2 [[Q1, Q2, Q3], [-Q2, Q1, Q4], [-Q3, -Q4, Q1], [Q4, -Q3, Q2]]), and P_u = 2 A* P is their P_k = A_k p_k, and the two branch rules (x positive / negative) are their eqs. 48 and 49. So the two papers carry the same transformation; and the u' = P_u/(4 mu) and r' relations are the two-body limit of A and Z's eq. 70.

## 2. Numerical results (section 5, pp.173-174; tables read on the image)

Integrator: fifth-order Runge-Kutta, variable step (a scheme of Zonneveld, Mathematical Centre Amsterdam, Math. Tracts 8, which provides an error estimate from material already available in the normal use of the method, p.175). "No attempt is made to improve the integration scheme which admittedly is rather inefficient for the case considered": the test compares rectangular against regularised equations only.
Two problems, G = 1. (1) Three bodies, masses 3, 4 and 5 (the Pythagorean problem of Szebehely and Peters 1967; the discussion, p.175, calls it "Burrau's problem (A.N. 195, 113; 1913)"); initial conditions not printed here, they are in Szebehely and Peters 1967 (not held) and in Aarseth and Zare 1974 Table I (positions (1, 3), (-2, -1), (1, -1), at rest; INFERRED to be the same problem since the masses and the problem name agree). Integrated to t = 25, during which "fourteen close approaches occur". (2) Twenty-five bodies, each of mass 1/25, with initial conditions of "Dr M. Lecar" (printed elsewhere in the volume, not held here).
Systems compared (p.173): R, usual rectangular coordinates; L, the regularised (Levi-Civita) system of this paper (the paper calls it Levi-Civita for the three-body case, which is planar, and the same equations are used); V, a system in which only the independent variable is changed, dtau = V dt, "where V is the potential energy" (a Sundman-type time transformation without coordinate change, discussed by Szebehely in the same volume).

Table 1 (p.174), three bodies, computations to t = 25 (IBM 7094, double precision, 16 decimal digits, at the Yale Computer Center):
| System | Number of steps | Time of computation (s) | "Constant of energy" |
|---|---|---|---|
| R | 17631 | 395 | 7e-7 |
| V | 4300 | 280 | 8e-7 |
| L | 3450 | 155 | 2e-11 |
| L | 600 | 26 | 7e-8 |
Table 2 (p.174), twenty-five bodies, computations to t = 2.55 (IBM Stretch, 14 decimal digits, at the Naval Weapons Laboratory, "roughly 3.4 times faster than the 7094"):
| System | Number of steps | Time of computation (s) | "Constant of energy" |
|---|---|---|---|
| R | 195 | 175 | 0.5e-3 |
| L | 48 | 53 | 0.6e-8 |
Printed statements: with R "the integral of energy deviates from its initial value more and more with each close approach"; this is also true "to a lesser degree with system V"; "with system L the constant of energy is uniformly preserved throughout the integration"; "the overall speed of computation of system L is about 15 times higher than that of system R for comparable accuracy" (p.173-174); in the 25-body case the same behaviour is observed, and "the degeneration of the constant of energy in system R and a uniform preservation of its value in system L was found. The integration in system L was continued to t = 12.12, requiring 1702 steps" (p.174). COMPUTED checks of the printed ratios: 395/26 = 15.2 (the "about 15" compares the R row with the 600-step L row, whose energy value 7e-8 is ten times smaller than R's 7e-7, so "comparable accuracy" is conservative); 17631/3450 = 5.1 and 17631/600 = 29 in steps; 195/48 = 4.1 in steps and 175/53 = 3.3 in time for the 25-body case. The column header is "Constant of Energy"; the text says it measures the deviation of the energy integral from its initial value. It is not stated whether the entries are relative or absolute errors, nor, for L, whether they are measured with the regularised Hamiltonian K (which section 4 calls the energy check and which is identically zero) or with the physical energy; I cannot tell from the page, so compare only the orders of magnitude (INFERRED ambiguity).

Discussion (p.175, read): Nahon asks how the initial conditions of the three-body film example were chosen (Peters: Burrau's problem). Broucke asks, for three dimensions, what initial values are taken for u1..u4 and their derivatives; Peters: "One has a choice which is usually made by setting one of the variables to zero. This is analogous to the choice of the Riemann sheet in the two dimensional case with complex variables (see Section 4b of the paper)" (this is the fixed gauge u4 = 0 for x > 0, u3 = 0 for x < 0). Leimanis asks how the error was estimated with Runge-Kutta: Zonneveld's fifth-order scheme gives an expression for the fifth-order contribution; "the method works very well for the three body case, [but] the numerous evaluations of the forces required by Runge-Kutta make the method rather inefficient for the 25 body case. I intend to revise the integration scheme."

## 3. Comparison with KS (Stiefel-Scheifele), Sundman and Aarseth-Zare

| Item | Peters 1968 | Stiefel and Scheifele 1971 (digest) | Aarseth and Zare 1974 (digest) | Sundman option in `core/cr3bp_regularized.py` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Coordinates | KS 4-vector u for one pair, Hamiltonian form, canonical | KS u-method, Newtonian and canonical forms | two KS maps, one reference body | none (Cartesian) |
| Time | dt = r dtau | dt = r ds, and Sundman family dt = r^n ds | dt = R1 R2 dtau | dt/ds = r1 r2 (or r1, r2) |
| Energy | h is the constant value of the Hamiltonian (actual energy; bound h negative); K identically zero | h is NEGATIVE energy (h positive elliptic) | P9 = -H in extended phase space | not used |
| Perturbation | all other bodies in R; P_u' = 2 (h - R) u - r dR/du | total-energy form with V and P | F_i in Cartesian, eqs. 60 to 71 | none (force is the full CR3BP) |
| Consequence | force law regular at r tending to 0 | same | regular for R1 or R2 tending to 0 | force law still singular |
Peters's form and the book's total-energy form (the one #928 now recommends) are the same structure written by two authors (a harmonic-oscillator part u'' + (h/2) u with the perturbation entering through dR/du, in Peters's sign convention for h). Peters has no hyperbolic discussion; h has either sign in his equations (P_u' = 2 (h - R) u - r dR/du), and the equations are regular for any sign, which is the same statement as the book's section 11 (INFERRED). Peters has no time-dependent perturbation: R depends on the moving other bodies, but because his system is closed and autonomous h is constant; in a driven model (elliptic restricted problem, a prescribed ephemeris) h is not constant (the book's h' = ... term, see the S and S digest section 12 caveat 3). On Sundman: V (dtau = V dt with V the potential energy) is a Sundman-type time transformation without a coordinate change. In Table 1 it needs 4300 steps against 17631 for R at about the same energy error (8e-7 against 7e-7), but only 280 s against 395 s because each step costs more; the KS system L reaches 2e-11 in 3450 steps, a factor of about 4e4 smaller energy error than V at 20 percent fewer steps (COMPUTED from the table entries; INFERRED comparison, since the runs have different step counts and the energy column is ambiguous). This supports the project's own finding that the Sundman propagator in `core/cr3bp_regularized.py` (a time transformation only) is not a coordinate regularisation. Relative to Aarseth and Zare: Peters's formulation is their two-body limit; their addition is the second KS map, and their statement that the massless case fails applies to the eight-dimensional form, not to this one.

Massless particle (INFERRED, not in the paper): Peters writes the pair with masses m_k and m_l and the reduced mass mu. With m_k tending to 0 the factor 1/(8 mu) in K diverges; the working variable is the regularised velocity V = P_u/mu (A and Z, p.202: V1 = P1/mu13 = 4 dQ1/dtau, binding energy per unit mass h = -P9/mu13), in which the mass cancels from both sides of the equations. M_1 tends to 0 and M_2 to 1, so the pair's centre of mass is the heavy body, and the equations reduce to massless KS about the moon with the Earth in R. Peters does not do this and prints no massless example.

## 6. Techniques applicable to the project's problems

All uses INFERRED; nothing built or run.

**#928 (a float regularised propagator and transition matrix for close passes; current recommendation: one moon-centred KS code, K^2 = mu_moon, planar data u3 = u4 = 0, total-energy form of the Stiefel and Scheifele book, V(0) = 0, Coriolis as a force).** Does this paper change that? No. It confirms it, and adds three small, concrete things. (1) A third printed copy of the 3D map: the KS forward map for both branches, the inverse, the 4 x 3 matrix A*, the momentum map P_u = 2 A* P and P = (1/(2r)) A P_u, and the bilinear control -u4 P_u1 + u3 P_u2 - u2 P_u3 + u1 P_u4 = 0 (p.172). It agrees with Aarseth and Zare's (48) to (53) (checked above); the book's map is the third. A map written once and tested against all three removes sign-convention risk, a test the task entry does not yet list: assert the code's matrix equals A and Z eq. 51 and Peters's A* times two on random u, and that P_u = 2 A* P satisfies the control to rounding. (2) The bilinear control as a running monitor: integrate with the constraint violation recorded (the book's "Bil" column is the same quantity; the S and S digest section 16.4 already proposes it); Peters's own energy check is K = 0 (the regularised Hamiltonian is identically zero), which in the total-energy form corresponds to the algebraic h against the integrated h. (3) The cancellation point (p.171): never form a force as a difference of two large terms when a sum of the other terms is available; in the project's setting, when the Earth's attraction on the moon-centred frame is computed as a direct minus indirect term (a difference of nearly equal large numbers when the particle is near the moon), use the relative form from the start. This applies to the perturbation function in the CR3BP in moon-centred coordinates (the centrifugal plus the other primary's direct attraction minus the value at the moon, V(0) = 0, in the S and S setup), which already has this structure. A pair-selection rule ("regularise the pair with the smallest mutual distance") is the many-body version of the centre switch the S and S digest already says is needed for passes near both primaries (and for #890 and #895 with two moons). Not provided: a transition matrix, hyperbolic orbits, time-dependent perturbations, a massless limit; the S and S recipe (lift, 10 x 10 flow, project, fixed-time correction) stays as recorded. The paper's value for the #928 comparison with the Sundman propagator is Table 1: a time-only transformation (V) is far less accurate than the coordinate-regularised system (L) at a comparable step count, so a "Sundman versus KS" comparison on orbit 12i is expected to show a large difference in the closest passes (a prediction, not yet tested).

**#929 (integrator controls through one moon flyby).** The paper's whole experimental design is the control #929 asks for: the same problem integrated in several formulations (R, V, L), reporting steps, time and energy conservation through repeated close approaches (14 in Table 1). The project's version is plain DOP853, the Sundman propagator and the KS propagator through one flyby, reporting the integrator's step count, the Jacobi constant and the bilinear constraint at fixed tolerance. Two practical points from the paper: (i) report accuracy against step count, as Peters does, not tolerance alone (the book also does; S and S section 16.4); (ii) the integrator was deliberately kept simple (RK5) so that the formulation was the only variable: use the same integrator and tolerance in every arm. The reversibility test (forward then backward) is not in this paper (it is in Aarseth and Zare and Heggie).

**#899, #924, #890 and #895.** No new technique beyond the S and S, Aarseth and Zare and Heggie notes. #924: Table 1 shows regularised variables keep the integral of energy uniform through 14 close approaches, which is what a variational-vector computation needs for a clean baseline. #890/#895: the nearest-pair rule above.
