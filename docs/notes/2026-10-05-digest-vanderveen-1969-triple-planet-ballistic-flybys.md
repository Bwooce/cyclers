## 0. Verdict

These are one-shot ballistic Earth-Venus-Mars-Venus-Earth round trips on real ephemeris. They are NOT
periodic or cyclers: each trip ends at Earth after 664-795 days, and the paper never chains trips.
- The opportunities "recur" only qualitatively on the 6.4-year Mars-Venus alignment cycle.
- They repeat nearly identically only after 32 years, when the configuration repeats in absolute space
  (Fig. 3: 1981 and 2013).

So VanderVeen is NOT a literal collision for `#942` R1(c), a Venus-Mars cycler. It is still relevant prior
art:
- It publishes ballistic V-M-V passages in which Mars supplies the turn.
- It notes that the 1987 #7/#3 case "requir[ed] a powered turn at Mars (because of insufficient turning)",
  a real-ephemeris statement of the weak Mars turn.
- It notes that success depends on Mars' heliocentric distance at the Mars-Venus alignment.

## 1. Content (READ)

- **Mars-Venus alignments (Table 1, p.383):**
  - 37 alignments from 14.6 Oct 1975 (JD 2442700.1) to 1.9 Jul 1999, with heliocentric longitudes.
  - The Gillespie-Ross swingby type numbers run #1-#7.
  - The pair combinations give opportunities at intervals of 1.83, 1.83 and 2.74 yr, within the 6.4-yr
    cycle (abstract; p.384).
- **The three classes:**
  - #5/#5: 1977 and 1983. Symmetric profile. The excursion stays between the orbits of Venus and Mars.
    Mars passage at 18,200 fps.
  - #3/#7: 1978 and 1985.
  - #7/#3: 1981 and 2013. Also 1987, which was powered at Mars.
- **Table 2 (p.384):** eight 1977 families (A-1 to D-2), with dates of Earth departure, first Venus
  passage, Mars passage, second Venus passage and Earth return, plus v_inf (EMOS), declination and passage
  radii. Example values from the text layer:
  - Earth departure 3074-3228 (JD - 2440000).
  - Mars passage about 3507-3520.
  - Return 3802-3896.
- **Table 3 (p.386):** typical members.
  - 1977 716-day #5/#5: E 23 Jan 77 (3166) v_inf 0.2145; V 22 Jun 77 (3316) 0.2250; M 3 Jan 78 (3511)
    0.1167; V 20 Aug 78 (3740) 0.2395; E 9 Jan 79 (3882) 0.1532.
  - 1978 795-day #3/#7: E 28 Nov 78 (3840) 0.1722; V 11 May 79 (4004) 0.3444; M 25 Nov 79 (4202) 0.1636;
    V 29 Jan 80 (4419) 0.2186; E 31 Jan 81 (4635) 0.2384.
  - 1981 790-day #7/#3: E 26 May 81 (4750) 0.2262; V 28 Dec 81 (4966) 0.2029; M 5 Oct 82 (5278) 0.2534;
    V 1 Mar 83 (5394) 0.3673; E 25 Jul 83 (5540) 0.1957.
  - Passage radii and speeds are in the scrambled columns, so I do not quote them.
- **Table 4 (p.387):** the 1977 low-energy 720-day triple.
  - E 17 Jan 77 (3160) 0.1625; V 1 Jun 77 (3295) 0.2466, 1.051 radii; M 28 Dec 77 (3505) 0.1455, 3.072
    radii; V 19 Aug 78 (3739) 0.2411, 1.051 radii; E 7 Jan 79 (3880) 0.1519.
  - A 664-day variant, and the Venus-flyby abort.
  - Values are as I matched them from the text layer. Re-read them on the page image before using them as
    a control.
- **Repeatability (pp.385-386, Conclusions):**
  - "triple-planet ballistic flyby missions are considerably more restrictive ... their repeatability is
    not easily verified".
  - None was found in 1994; 1975 was difficult; 1987 needed a powered Mars turn.
  - The 2013 #7/#3 profile is "nearly identical" to 1981, one full 32-yr cycle later.
  - "Successful ballistic triple-planet flybys appear to be possible only when the Venus-Mars-Venus
    aphelion lies between 1.5 and 1.64 a.u." (observation 7, p.386).

## 2. Gate relevance

- **`#942` R1(c), Venus-Mars:** there is no literal collision.
  - Nothing here is periodic, and no Venus- or Mars-hosted repeating return appears.
  - A Venus-Mars cycler result should cite VanderVeen for the ballistic V-M-V geometry and the Mars-turn
    limit on real ephemeris.
  - The 32-yr absolute repeat is a natural near-period for any real-ephemeris V-M-V cycler check (INFERRED).
- **R1(a):** this is the first published ballistic multi-planet trajectory in which Mars bends the path at
  a passage (radius 3.07 planet radii, Table 4). It is not a cycler.
