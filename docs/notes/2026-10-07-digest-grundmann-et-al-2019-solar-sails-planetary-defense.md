## 0. Verdict

**This is a solar-sail programme overview (DLR GOSSAMER heritage) aimed at small-body and
planetary-defence missions. It has nothing on cyclers, periodic orbits, resonant tours or the
restricted three-body problem. No catalogue implication. Background only; filing is optional.**

## 1. Content (READ, text layer)

- Status 20 years after DLR's 1999 (20 m)^2 ground deployment test, and in view of NASA's NEAScout.
  It reviews the GOSSAMER-1 deployment qualification and the DLR-ESTEC Gossamer Roadmap lessons.
- Mission types: single and Multiple NEA Rendezvous ((M)NR); sail-propelled head-on retrograde kinetic
  impactors (RKI); Displaced L1 (DL1) space-weather warning; Solar Polar Orbiter (SPO). The DL1 is a
  sail-displaced equilibrium point, which is the only three-body item, and it is mentioned only as a
  mission type with no dynamics.
- The reference MNR sequence (Table 1, from Peloni et al., their ref. [10]) visits 5 NEAs (2000 SG344,
  2015 JD3, 2012 KB4, 2008 EV5, 2014 MP) between 2025 and 2034. Each stay lasts over 100 days, and the
  characteristic acceleration is 0.2 mm/s^2. Targets can be re-sequenced in flight.
- Landers: MASCOT-type nanolanders carried and released by the sail. Larger landers shuttle between
  sail and asteroid (OKEANOS-like). A 2017 Planetary Defense Conference exercise diverts an MNR mission
  to a fictitious impactor, "2017 PDC", and re-targets to 2011 AG5 (PDC'13).
- High-energy launch: small sailcraft on GTO rideshares or a stripped Ariane 5 ECA escape.
- Keyword search: no hit for cycler, periodic orbit, halo, resonance or three-body. "Gravity assist"
  appears only in a general remark about combining gravity-assist sequencing with low thrust (section 6).
