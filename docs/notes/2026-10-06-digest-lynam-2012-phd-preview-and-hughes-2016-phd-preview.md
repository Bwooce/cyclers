# Digest: two partial dissertation previews, Lynam 2012 and Hughes 2016 (Purdue) (#960, #942, #943)

Both uploads are the Purdue e-Pubs 24-page previews: the ETD acceptance form, front matter, table of
contents, lists of tables and figures, abstract, and the opening of chapter 1 or 2 (watermarked
"PREVIEW"). They are filed as **partial previews**. The full dissertations stay in the wanted list,
marked "preview held".

## A. Lynam, A. E. (2012), "Mission Design and Navigation of Multiple-Satellite-Aided Capture Trajectories and Cyclers for Missions to Jupiter", PhD dissertation, Purdue (AAI3545317)

- Filed as `cyclers_pdf/papers/lynam-2012-multiple-satellite-aided-capture-cyclers-jupiter-purdue-phd-PREVIEW-front-matter-24pp.pdf`.
  24 pages, text layer, md5 c891929c52d11dcf52927d95dc399a1b.
- **Chapter map (TOC and sec. 1.2, p.2-3):**
  - Ch. 2: multiple-satellite-aided capture (double, triple and quadruple sequences, Laplace-resonance
    phase angles). Corresponds to a conference paper [10] and a journal article [11].
  - Ch. 3: connection to interplanetary trajectories (Lynam & Longuski [12, 13]; the Callisto-Ganymede-Io
    and Callisto-Ganymede-JOI flyby windows).
  - Ch. 4: navigation covariance and Monte Carlo statistical Delta-V [14].
  - **Ch. 5: Laplace-resonant triple cyclers** [15, 16]: half-period, single-period and multiple-period
    Io-Europa-Ganymede cyclers, apojove angles, STK integration, the radiation field and rings. The list
    of tables includes "Orbital Parameters for a Ganymede-Io-Perijove-Europa Triple Cycler".
  - Ch. 6: conclusions and future work.
- **Verdict:**
  - The cycler content is the Io-Europa-Ganymede Laplace triple cyclers. Their journal form (Lynam &
    Longuski 2011, Acta Astronautica 69) is held, and the conference form is AIAA 2010-8256.
  - The Callisto content is capture sequences (CGI, CGJ, JOI-I-G-C staircase), not cyclers.
  - **No `#943` gc/ge collision.**
  - Relevant to `#945` R2 and `#953` R7 as the IEG triple-cycler source; the held 2011 paper covers it.

## B. Hughes, K. M. (2016), "Gravity-Assist Trajectories to Venus, Mars, and the Ice Giants: Mission Design with Human and Robotic Applications", PhD dissertation, Purdue (AAI10248372)

- Filed as `cyclers_pdf/papers/hughes-2016-gravity-assist-trajectories-venus-mars-ice-giants-purdue-phd-PREVIEW-front-matter-24pp.pdf`.
  24 pages, text layer, md5 561168b13da02b3ce172dba61b136a54.
- **Chapter map (TOC):**
  - Ch. 1-2: Lambert, Tisserand graph and pathfinding; planetocentric frames and approach trajectories.
  - Ch. 3: Uranus trajectory catalogue 2024-2038 (with a Saturn probe option and aerocapture).
  - Ch. 4: Neptune.
  - Ch. 5: fast Mars free returns, including 5.3.5 "The Case for Venus". This is the held Hughes et al.
    2015 JSR paper.
  - **Ch. 6: "Verification of Trajectories to Establish Earth-Mars Cyclers"**: v_inf leveraging and
    low-thrust establishment of Earth-Mars cyclers (Aldrin, S1L1, Case 3; table "Orbital Elements and
    Number of Vehicles for Selected Cycler Trajectories"). This corresponds to the held Rogers, Hughes,
    Longuski & Aldrin 2015.
- **Verdict:**
  - All 16 "cycler" occurrences are Earth-Mars (the establishment of the Aldrin, S1L1 and Case 3
    cyclers).
  - The Venus content is one-shot EVE free returns (Ch. 5.3.5).
  - **No Earth-Venus cycler; no collision with `#942` ev-A or ev-C.**
