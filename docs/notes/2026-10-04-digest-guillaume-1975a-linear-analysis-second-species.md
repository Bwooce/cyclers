## 0. Headline findings

1. The coefficients of the 1973 local forms are NOT in this paper. Pages 253 and 254 (the pages Perko cites for "second species bifurcations") contain only (i) the end of Section 5 (general remarks on the three examples), (ii) the Conclusions, and (iii) the reference list. The Conclusions (p.253-254) say that the zero-order deflection angle can vanish for a new kind of second species solution (passing in an O(mu^lambda) neighbourhood of the basic orbit, 0 < lambda < 1), that "Breakwell-Perko's matching theory is no longer valid", that a new matching theory has to be built, and that "this new tool will be used later on to analyze the transitions mentioned above (second species bifurcations)". The f1 f2 = mu hyperbola and the cubic are not derived, not printed, and not mentioned by equation anywhere in the paper. What Perko's citation points to is the programme statement, not a formula. The coefficient question that the 1973 digest left open is therefore NOT settled; it moves again: the derivation is in neither 1975 paper (paper II, 449-467, gives the extended matching but not the bifurcation algebra). The only explicit published coefficients of the same hyperbolic structure that the project holds are Perko 1981 eq. (9) (see the Perko digest).
2. The paper does give, in full, the input-output (I/O) equations and the linear description of second species solutions with deflection angle not zero (Sections 1 to 3, Table I), the classification of the basic conics (Section 4), and three worked applications to periodic symmetric solutions (Section 5), all as equations without any numerical example. There is no table of numbers, no orbit, and no stated value of mu anywhere (the only numerical statements are exact identities such as C(0+) < 3 and the multiplier 2 sqrt(3 - C)). The paper says why: "we do not have a good computer at our disposal" (p.253).
3. The Appendix is referenced repeatedly ("see Appendix for the computations of dX2', dX1, dY2' and dY1", pp.242, 246, 252; "see note 5 above", pp.231, 244) but is NOT printed within pages 213-254 as held (page 254 ends with the references). The "note 5" is the footnote on p.219 (definition of the partial derivatives dF/dx0 etc. at mu = 0). So the explicit coefficient expressions of Table I and eqs. (4.9), (4.20) (where an element is given: the coefficient 2 cos alpha x0 ydot0/(e cos g0) of dp_x0 in (4.20), p.246) are given only as the symbol coefficients plus that one explicit formula. The matrices are partial derivatives of the two-body flow at the collision, which can be computed numerically.
4. The turn-gate relation is not changed (Section 6 below). The paper's own relation is the same hyperbola: e* = |cosec beta1(0)| (2.14) and rho_p = (mu/V^2)(|cosec beta1(0)| - 1) (p.237); the zeroth-order turn 2 beta1(0) is the oriented angle between V1 and V1' (2.10), which is exactly the demanded turn of the project's gate. The I/O equations show the gate is the zero-order condition: the real turn is 2 beta1 = 2 beta1(0) + O(mu) (2.9).
5. Two necessary conditions for a node to be a second species passage, which the project's gate does not currently test: equal speeds |V1| = |V1'| (2.1) and beta1(0) not zero (2.11).

## 1. Setting (READ pp.213-216)

- Planar circular restricted problem Earth E (mass 1 - mu), Moon M (mass mu), distance 1 and Earth-Moon angular rate 1. Sidereal Hamiltonian H* = H0 + mu R, H0 = (px*^2 + py*^2)/2 - 1/r, R = 1/r - 1/rho + (x* cos t + y* sin t) - 1/2, r = ES, rho = MS (0.1). Synodic frame (rotation through t) centred at E: x = x* cos t + y* sin t, y = -x* sin t + y* cos t, px = xdot* cos t + ydot* sin t, py = -xdot* sin t + ydot* cos t (0.2); H = (px^2 + py^2)/2 - (x py - y px) - (1 - mu)/r - mu/rho + mu x - mu/2 (0.4); H = -C/2 (0.5). (The paper's printed H has "+ mu x - mu/2"; the Hamiltonians differ by constants and by the origin at the Earth, mu x. COMPUTED consistency below.)
- Definition of a second species solution (SSS), p.214-215: a solution (x, y, px, py)(t, mu) defined for 0 < mu < mu0 on an interval I_mu, with at least two times t0 and t0' with rho(t0, 0+) not zero and rho(t0', 0+) not zero and a time tp between them with rho(tp, 0+) = 0 (Poincare's concept, made precise "perhaps not satisfactorily", p.215).
- Constraint on C (p.215, eq. 0.6): for mu small enough, an SSS must have C(mu) < C2(mu), the Jacobi constant of the Earth-Moon equilibrium L2 (otherwise the zero-velocity oval around the Moon is closed and collapses to M), and so C(0+) < C2(0+) = 3. READ.
- Why regularisation does not help (p.215-216, relevant to `#928`, section 9): after translating to the Moon (x = xi + 1, y = eta, px = p_xi, py = p_eta + 1), writing H in homogeneous form (0.7), and applying the Levi-Civita transformation xi + i eta = (u + i v)^2, p_xi + i p_eta = (p_u + i p_v)(u + i v)/(2(u^2 + v^2)) with nu = 4(u^2 + v^2), the Hamiltonian becomes K = (p_u^2 + p_v^2)/2 - 2(3 - 2 p_t)(u^2 + v^2) + O[(u^2 + v^2)^(3/2)] + O(mu) and the equations are u'' - 4(3 - 2 p_t) u = O[u^2 + v^2] + O(mu), same for v (0.8). At mu = 0 the point (u, v) = (0, 0), where the Moon is, is an equilibrium with both characteristic multipliers equal to 2 sqrt(3 - 2 p_t) = 2 sqrt(3 - C(0)), unstable because 3 - C(0) is positive by (0.6). So the regularised problem is a perturbation of an unstable equilibrium and "we should thus have to analyze global properties of solutions which come close to an equilibrium without staying there" (p.216). The author therefore abandons rigorous accuracy (no existence proofs; Perko had just informed him of such proofs as a private communication, footnote p.216) and asks the modest question of whether analytic expressions can be given for "regular" portions of the periodic symmetric (PS) families of Broucke (1962, 1968) and Deprit (1967, 1968).

## 3. Section 2: the input-output equations (READ pp.225-233, Table I)

Definition (p.225-226): a Keplerian segment K(t1, t2) of type (a, b; n), a, b in {0, 1}, n a positive integer, is a union of (n + 1) conic arcs each colliding with M exactly at its beginning and its end ("conics with consecutive collisions", Henon 1968), except that the first begins at M only if a = 1 and the last ends at M only if b = 1. K of type (0, 0; 0): an arc not through M; the BPM arcs r(0) and r'(0) are K(t0, t1) of type (0, 1; 0) and K(t1, t0') of type (1, 0; 0).

 C(0) = 3 - V1^2 (2.1), C'(0) = 3 - V1'^2 (2.1'), hence V1 = V1' = V = sqrt(3 - C(0)).

## 4. Section 3: symmetric conjunction near M (READ pp.234-238)

## 5. Section 4: basic orbits (READ pp.238-247)

Conics with collision (4.1): x(0)(t1 - t0, x0, y0, px0, py0) - 1 = 0, y(0)(...) = 0 define a three-dimensional manifold V3 in the space E5 of (t1 - t0, x0, y0, px0, py0), differential dX1 + V d(t1 - t0) = 0, dY1 = 0 (4.1); the rank is 1 + rank of the second equation and the Jacobian determinant det [ d(x, y, px, py)(0)/d(x0, y0, px0, py0) ] = 1 (Hamiltonian flow, p.239). For mu = 0, I/O 1 and I/O 3 reduce to (4.1) written in C. Parametric form (4.2): x0 = x(tau, 1, 0, px1, py1), etc., (px1, py1) = (xdot1, ydot1 + 1) the synodic components of rdot(0); "a time delay Delta t1 corresponds to a rotation of the conic of collision in the sidereal frame but has no effect on the path in the synodical system".
Conics with consecutive collisions: collisions at t1 and t2, G = x1 py1 - y1 px1 = x2 py2 - y2 px2 gives py1 = py2 (4.6) and equal energy with r1 = r2 gives px1 = +- px2 (4.7). Two types:
- Conics T: px1 = px2, py1 = py2, x1 = x2 = 1, y1 = y2 = 0 (4.8): same phase-space point at t1 and t2, periodic. Of two kinds: (a) the sidereal retrograde circle of radius 1, synodic x(0)(t) = cos 2(t - t0), y(0)(t) = -sin 2(t - t0); (b) sidereal ellipses of rational mean motion with t2 - t1 = 2 i pi, E2 - E1 = 2 j pi, n = j/i (families T_ij of Henon). The path parameters lie on the circle px1^2 + py1^2 = 2 - (j/i)^(2/3) (p.242), from n^2 a^3 = 1 and h = -1/(2a) (COMPUTED check: h = (px^2 + py^2)/2 - 1 with r = 1, so p^2 = 2 - 1/a = 2 - n^(2/3); for n = 2: 2 - 1.587401 = 0.412599, for n = 1/2: 2 - 0.629961 = 1.370039; these use the printed relation; the numerals are mine). Variations along T_ij (4.9): with (a + da, e + de, g + dg, M0 + dM0) the elements, dX2 - dX1 + V d(t2 - t1) = -3 i pi (V - sin alpha1) da/a + V d(t2 - t1) = 0 and dY2 - dY1 = -3 i pi cos alpha1 da/a = 0, giving (cos alpha1 not zero) d(t2 - t1) = 0 and da = 0: along T_ij the half-period t2 - t1 and a (or n) are constant (the derivation uses the Appendix, which is not printed).
- Conics S: px1 = -px2, py1 = py2, x1 = x2 = 1, y1 = y2 = 0. These are symmetric about EM (the symmetry of (0.3): x(t* + t) = x(t* - t), y(t* + t) = -y(t* - t), px(t* + t) = -px(t* - t), py(t* + t) = py(t* - t), with t1 + t2 = 2 t*, t2 - t1 = 2 tau), cross EM orthogonally, are mostly ellipses but also hyperbolas and one parabola (Henon's conics S). Taking t0 = t*, (4.10): x(0)(tk - t0, x0, 0, 0, py0) = 1, y(0)(...) = 0, k = 1, 2, define continuous one-parameter families, each a curve in (tau, x0, py0) or (tau, x0, C); detailed description is left to Henon 1968 (and Bruno 1972, 1973).
 Expressed in (x0, C) (4.11): X_x0k dx0 + X_Ck dC + V d(tk - t0) = 0, Y_x0k dx0 + Y_Ck dC = 0. Important remark (4.12): X_x01 = -X_x02, X_C1 = -X_C2, Y_x01 = Y_x02, Y_C1 = Y_C2, so d(t2 - t0) = -d(t1 - t0) and the second equation holds for k = 1 and 2 simultaneously; proved from the time-reversal symmetry (4.13) x(Dt) = x(-Dt), y(Dt) = -y(-Dt), derivatives (4.14), (4.15) xdot(Dt) = -xdot(-Dt), ydot(Dt) = ydot(-Dt), and (4.16) dx/dmu, dy/dmu with the same parities; at t1 these give dX1/dx0 = -dX2/dx0, dY1/dx0 = dY2/dx0 etc. (4.17), (4.18). Also X_b1 = -X_b2, Y_b1 = Y_b2 (from X_b(t2 - dtau) = -X_b(t1 + dtau)), hence X_B2 = -X_B1, Y_B2 = Y_B1 (4.19). These are exact symmetry identities usable as unit tests (section 7).
- Complete arc of S passing through M at t1 and t2 and crossing EM orthogonally at (t1 + t2)/2 = t0: I/O 1 and I/O 2 become Y_x01 dx0 + Y_px01 dp_x0 + Y_C1 dC + mu Y_B1 + (mu/V^2) cotg beta1(0) + O(mu^2) = 0 and the same with subscript 2. Subtracting, using (4.12), (4.19):
 (Y_px02 - Y_px01) dp_x0 + (mu/V^2)(cotg beta2(0) - cotg beta1(0)) + O(mu^2) = 0, which after computing the coefficient (Appendix) is
 2 cos alpha (x0 ydot0)/(e cos g0) dp_x0 + (mu/V^2)(cotg beta2(0) - cotg beta1(0)) + O(mu^2) = 0   (4.20), alpha = alpha2 = pi - alpha1 (p.246).
 Consequences: (1) if dp_x0 = 0 then cotg beta2(0) = cotg beta1(0), as follows directly from the symmetry of the basic orbit; (2) conversely if the basic orbit has equal deflection angles at t1 and t2 (Fig. 11), then dp_x0 = 0 (if ydot0 cos alpha not zero): the SSS crosses EM orthogonally near (t1 + t2)/2 to O(mu^2). So a periodic SSS with a complete arc of S as basic orbit is periodic symmetric, to O(mu^2); a once-per-period near-M passage has as basic orbit a complete arc of S or of T, and for T beta1(0) = 0 so BP matching does not apply: "the only periodic SSS which pass near M once in a period and to which Breakwell-Perko's matching theory is applicable are not only periodic but also symmetric with respect to EM, at least to O(mu^2)" (p.246-247, previously Guillaume 1973; here shown as a particular consequence of (4.20)). (3) At the intersection of families S and T, px1 = px2 = 0, the conics are periodic symmetric basic orbits of the first or second kind (Guillaume 1969, 1974), the only first-kind one being the circle of radius 1, retrograde (conic T) or direct (the equilibrium of the rotating two-body problem).

## 6. Section 5: applications to periodic symmetric SSS (READ pp.248-253)

Framework (p.248): every PS solution is two points of equal C in the (x0, C) plane; for an SSS the basic orbit is closed and made of arcs of conics with consecutive collisions; as mu tends to 0 either x0(mu) tends to 1 (symmetric conjunction near M, Section 3) or (x0, C) tends to a point of a conic S.
