## 1. What the paper does (READ pp.437-438)

Abstract: "Elliptic orbits around the large primary are matched to hyperbolas, osculating at closest approach, around the small primary of the circular restricted three-body problem. The distance of closest approach to the small primary is assumed to be of the same order as the mass-ratio mu of small to large primary. The dependence of the hyperbola on initial conditions for the elliptic orbit is carried to second order jointly in mu and in the variations of the initial conditions, which are three-dimensional rather than two-dimensional."
It is the second-order extension of Breakwell & Perko 1965 (Proc. 16th IAF Congress; Progress in Astronautics 17, 1966), whose first-order result was that the planet's effect on the approach asymptote and the time of closest approach is "certain gross biases obtainable by quadrature ... together with a logarithmic 'local' time bias dependent on the hyperbolic eccentricity" (p.437). The new result gives the sensitivities of the hyperbola to the initial conditions "correct through the first power of mu" (p.438). Lancaster (1972) did an equivalent analysis, with the hyperbolic-eccentricity influence on the velocity at infinity left to a numerical procedure; Lancaster & Allemann (1972, AIAA 72-49) made comparisons with numerical integration; this paper prints no numerical comparison.
Scope: circular restricted problem; variations dr0, dv0 of the Earth-side initial conditions of order mu; three-dimensional vectors; the closest approach to the small primary of order mu (a deflection of order one); the matching region is t - t1 = O(mu^(1/2)) (eq. 2.1.12).

## 2. Setting and notation (READ pp.438-439)

Units: the small primary moves on a unit circle at unit angular velocity. Position R(t) of the particle relative to the large primary, equations (2.1.1)-(2.1.2): Rddot = -R/R^3 + mu f(R, t) with f(R, t) = (R1(t) - R)/|R1(t) - R|^3 - R1(t)/|R1(t)|^3 (+ the direct term, as printed), R1(t) the small primary's position.
Outer expansion (2.1.4): R(t) - R1(t) = r0(t) + rho^(1)(t) + rho^(2)(t) + ..., r0 = R^(0) - R1 with R^(0) the two-body orbit through the Moon's position R1(t1) at a time t1 (t1 - t0 of order one; sigma = sgn(t1 - t0)); rho^(i) of order mu^i, and the effect of dr0, dv0 (assumed of order mu) is in rho^(1). tau = |t1 - t| = sigma (t1 - t) (2.1.13). v1 = Rdot^(0)(t1) - Rdot_1(t1) = rdot0(t1), the relative velocity at the encounter (not zero); i = v1/v1 and j perpendicular in the plane containing both primaries at t1; Gamma(t) = (3 Rhat Rhat^T - 1)/R^3 the gravity-gradient matrix of the Earth's two-body field (2.1.8); Phi(t, t0) the two-body transition matrix with blocks Phi_rr, Phi_rv, Phi_vr, Phi_vv.
Inner expansion (2.2.3): r*(t) = r(t) + rho*^(1) + rho*^(2) + ..., r(t) the osculating hyperbola at closest approach about the small primary, rho*^(i) the perturbation by the large primary.

## 3. The outer expansion to second order (READ pp.438-443, eqs. 2.1.4-2.1.32)

- First order (2.1.9): rho^(1)(t) = Phi_rr(t, t0) dr0 + Phi_rv(t, t0) dv0 + integral of Phi_rv(t, t') mu f(R^(0)(t'), t') dt'. Its singular part (2.1.18) is logarithmic: rho^(1)(t) = (mu sigma v1/v1^3) ln(tau0/tau) + rho_b^(1)(t) with tau0 = |t1 - t0|, where rho_b^(1) and its time derivative have finite limits rho_1b and rhodot_1b at t1 (2.1.19, 2.1.20, 2.1.22: (rho_1b, rhodot_1b) = Phi(t1, t0)(dr0, dv0) + integrals - (mu v1/v1^3)(sigma, 1/tau0)).
- The second derivative is not finite: rhoddot_b^(1) has the term (mu sigma/v1^3) Gamma1 v1 ln(tau0/tau) with the finite remainder Gamma1 rho_1b + (mu sigma/(6 v1^3))(Gamma1 - 3 Gamma_xx... printed "(10 Gamma1 - 3 Gamma_xx) v1" in eq. 2.1.21) so that, read on the image (2.1.21): rho_b^(1)(t) = rho_1b - sigma tau rhodot_1b + (tau^2/2){(mu sigma/v1^3) Gamma1 v1 ln(tau0/tau) + Gamma1 rho_1b + (mu sigma/(6 v1^3))(10 Gamma1 - 3 Gamma_xx) v1} + O(mu tau^3).
- Second order (2.1.10 to 2.1.30): rho^(2)(t) = (mu^2 sigma v1/(v1^6 tau0)) (ln(tau0/tau) - 3/2) + (mu/v1^3)(3 i i^T - 1)(rho_1b/(2 tau) - sigma rhodot_1b ln(tau0/tau)) + rho_b^(2)(t) (2.1.24), with the finite limit rho_2b (eq. 2.1.25, read on the image):
  rho_2b = 2 mu^2 sigma v1/(v1 tau0 ...) + (mu/v1^3)(3 i i^T - 1)(sigma rhodot_1b - rho_1b/tau0) + integral over [t0, t1] of { Phi_rv(t1, t)[ mu (df/dR^(0)) rho^(1)(t) - Phi_2(rho^(1)(t), R^(0)(t))/(2 |R^(0)(t)|^7) ] - (mu/(v1^3 tau))(3 i i^T - 1)[ mu sigma v1/(v1^3 tau) ln(tau0/tau) + rho_1b/tau - sigma rhodot_1b ] } dt,
  with the tensor Phi_2(A, B) = 15 (B.A) B - 3 |A|^2 ... (2.1.10: "15 (B.A)^2 B - 3|A|^2|B|^2 B - 6 (A.B)|B|^2 A" as printed; the exact form is on p.439). The integral has an integrable singularity: the subtracted terms remove the 1/tau and ln tau divergences.
- The derivative (2.1.28) has the singular terms (mu^2 v1/(v1^6 tau^2))[ln(tau0/tau) - 1/2], (mu sigma/v1^3)(3 i i^T - 1)(rho_1b/(2 tau^2) - sigma rhodot_1b/tau), (mu^2/(2 v1^6)) Gamma1 v1 ln^2(tau0/tau) and (mu^2/v1^6)((7/3) Gamma1 v1 - 2 Gamma_xx v1) ln(tau0/tau), with the matrix Lambda = Gamma1 - 2 i i^T Gamma1 - 2 Gamma1 i i^T + (rho) Gamma_xx (5 i i^T - 1) (2.1.29a) and a finite part V2 (2.1.29b) given by a quadrature over [t0, t1].
- Complete outer expansion through O(mu^(5/2)) (2.1.32): R(t) - R1(t) = -sigma v1 tau - (1/6) Gamma1 v1 tau^3 + (mu sigma v1/v1^3) ln(tau0/tau) + rho_b^(1)(t) + rho^(2)(t) + terms (tau^4, tau^5, mu tau^3, mu^2 tau, mu^2/tau ...), the O(mu^3) remainder satisfying a nonlinear equation but being "O(mu^3) in the matching region" (Perko 1967 for the estimate).

## 4. The inner expansion (READ pp.443-448, eqs. 2.2.1-2.2.14)

- Equation of motion relative to the small primary, (2.2.1)-(2.2.2): r*ddot = -mu r*/r*^3 - (1 - mu) f*(R1(t), r*), the perturbation by the large primary.
- The osculating hyperbola (2.2.6, 2.2.7): with a the length defined by mu/a = v_inf^2, "so that a = O(mu) on the assumption that v_inf = O(1)", b the perpendicular distance from the small primary to the incoming asymptote (called Delta in Breakwell & Perko 1965 and in Perko's later papers), e_H = [1 + (b^2/a^2)]^(1/2) the hyperbolic eccentricity, r = a(e_H cosh xi - 1), and unit vectors i* along the arrival asymptote and j* perpendicular to it. "It will turn out later that b also is O(mu) provided that dr0 and dv0 of the outer expansion are also O(mu)." Kepler's equation (2.2.8) and the use of the hyperbolic anomaly xi as the independent variable (a suggestion of P. Guillaume, p.448).
- The first inner perturbation rho*^(1) has the dominant part -(sigma/6) F*(t_p) v_inf tau*^3 with F* = 3 R1hat R1hat^T - 1 (2.2.9); its exact evaluation (2.2.11-2.2.13) needs the transition matrix along the hyperbola (Battin's formulas) and exponential integrals in e^xi; the vector coefficient of e^xi, "omitted by Lancaster and Allemann (1972), but instead a numerical procedure was used", is worked out here (p.448).
- The complete inner expansion is (2.2.14): r*(t) = r(t) + rho*^(1)(t) + terms (mu tau*^3, tau*^4, tau*^5) + O(mu^3).

## 5. The matching and its results (READ pp.448-450, eqs. 2.3.1-2.3.6; read on the images of pp.449 and 450)

- The two expansions are written in the common variable xi (Kepler's equation used for t(xi), 2.3.1-2.3.2) and matched; with t_p = t1 + sigma delta, delta = O(mu). It suffices to match the constant terms and the terms linear in e^xi. "Although all terms through O(mu^(5/2)) must match, it suffices ... to match the constant terms and the terms linear in e^xi", the other terms (in xi, xi e^xi, xi^2 e^xi, e^(2 xi)) being verified to match (p.448-449).
- Constant terms (2.3.3): sigma v1 delta + rho_1b - (mu sigma v1/v1^3) ln(a e_H/(2 v_inf tau0)) + sigma delta rhodot_1b + rho_2b + (mu sigma/v1^3)(3 i i^T - 1) rhodot_1b ln(a e_H/(2 v_inf tau0)) = -(sigma a/v_inf) v_inf + b + O(mu^3) (the exact grouping is on the page image).
- Coefficients of e^xi (2.3.4): the velocity at infinity v_inf in terms of v1, rho_1b, rhodot_1b, delta, rho_2b-like terms and the logarithms, with "the error O(mu^(5/2)) replaced by the smaller error O(mu^3), since e^xi, which is O(mu^(-1/2)), will appear only with integral powers of mu". Omitting the second-order terms gives the first-order result (2.3.4a): v1 + rhodot_1b = v_inf + O(mu^2), "as in Breakwell and Perko (1965). This implies that i - i* = O(mu)".
- (2.3.5), the displacement and the time of closest approach (read on the image): sigma v_inf(-delta + a/v_inf) + b = rho_1b + rho_2b - (sigma a v_inf/v_inf) ln(a e_H/(2 v_inf tau0)) + O(mu^3). The paper's own reading: "b is that part of rho_1b + rho_2b which is perpendicular to v1 + rhodot_1b, and sigma(-v_inf delta + a + a ln(a e_H/(2 v_inf tau0))) is the projection of rho_1b + rho_2b along v1 + rhodot_1b. Note that b and delta are O(mu)." So the miss vector b carries no logarithm; the logarithm appears only in the time of closest approach (through delta), as a ln(a e_H/(2 v_inf tau0)) with a = mu/v_inf^2.
- (2.3.6), the velocity at infinity to second order (read on the image of p.450): with x_1b = rho_1b . i and Gamma_xy = i^T Gamma1 j (j perpendicular to i in the plane containing both primaries at t1):
  v_inf = v1 + rhodot_1b + V2' - (a^2 e_H^2/(8 v1^2)) Gamma1 v1 + (1/(2 v1^2)) Gamma1 v1 (a - sigma x_1b)^2 + ((sigma a - x_1b)/v1)[Gamma1 rho_1b + (mu sigma/(6 v1^3))(7 Gamma1 - 3 Gamma_xx) v1] + (sigma a/v1) ln(a e_H/(2 v_inf tau0)) (2 Gamma1 b - (1/2) Gamma_xx b + (5/2) Gamma_xy sigma a j) + (2 sigma/(a v1)){a^2 Gamma1[ ] + sigma a i[ ] + b j*[ ]} + O(mu^3),
  the last braces coming from (2.2.13). "Equations (2.3.5) and (2.3.6) are the second-order matching formulas. The dependence on initial conditions dr0 and dv0 is given explicitly in the formulas for rho_1b, rhodot_1b and rho_b^(1)(t), all of which appear again in the expressions for rho_2b and V2'. The right members of (2.3.5) and (2.3.6) are thus mainly non-homogeneous quadratic functions of dr0 and dv0, also containing, however, multiples of ln e_H, which is, of course, a transcendental function of dr0 and dv0. (2.3.6) contains, in addition, positive and negative powers of e_H."
- Closing remark (p.450): the parts of the definite integrals in rho_2b and V2' that do not involve dr0, dv0 reduce to single and double quadratures over [t0, t1]; "The partial derivatives of delta, b and v_inf with respect to dr0 and dv0 are obtainable, on the other hand, through O(mu) by, at most, single quadratures. These expressions could be useful in a stability analysis, for small mu, of periodic orbits of the restricted three-body problem which pass close to the small primary." (The 1981 Perko papers retract the use for stability at critical generating orbits, Part B abstract; the use for sensitivities stands.)

## 6. What second order changes, and what it does not (the questions asked)

1. The turn-gate relation is untouched. The hyperbola is exactly the osculating Kepler hyperbola (2.2.6, 2.2.7): periapsis r_p = a(e_H - 1) with a = mu/v_inf^2, so r_p = mu (e_H - 1)/v_inf^2, and the asymptote directions give sin(turn/2) = 1/e_H. This holds at both orders; second order changes the values of (b, delta, v_inf) that enter e_H = [1 + (b v_inf^2/mu)^2]^(1/2), not the relation between them. Confirmed again, from READ eqs. 2.2.6-2.2.7 (the paper does not print r_p itself; the periapsis and the turn follow from a hyperbola with those a and e_H; INFERRED as an identity, not a statement of the paper).
2. First order (Perko 1976 Appendix A, Part A) versus second order here, in the same variables. Perko's (b.2) is j* . Delta = mu j . x_1b + mu^2 j . x_2b - (mu^2/V1)[j . xdot_1b][i . x_1b] + O(mu^3), where his x_1b, x_2b are this paper's rho_1b, rho_2b. COMPUTED consistency check against (2.3.5): with i* = (v1 + rhodot_1b)/|v1 + rhodot_1b| and j* perpendicular, j* = j - (j . rhodot_1b/v1) i to first order, so b = j* . (rho_1b + rho_2b) = j . rho_1b + j . rho_2b - (j . rhodot_1b/v1)(i . rho_1b), which is exactly Perko's expression. So the "cross term" in Perko's (b.2) is the rotation of the matching direction by rhodot_1b, and the whole second-order content for the miss distance b is: the quadrature rho_2b (2.1.25), and that rotation.
3. The map from Earth-side initial conditions to the hyperbola parameters:
   - miss distance b: first order j . rho_1b (error O(mu^2) in Perko's counting, with logarithmic factors absorbed); second order adds j* . rho_2b and the rotation term, error O(mu^3). No explicit logarithm appears in b beyond those integrated inside rho_2b (the integral converges; the singular parts are subtracted in 2.1.25).
   - time of closest approach: the first-order logarithmic bias (mu/v^3)[ln(.) - 1] is already in Breakwell & Perko 1965; second order adds the rho_2b projection and the term sigma delta rhodot_1b in (2.3.3); the logarithm is ln(a e_H/(2 v_inf tau0)), which at a = O(mu) is a "mu ln mu" term.
   - velocity at infinity: first order v1 + rhodot_1b + O(mu^2); second order (2.3.6) adds terms of order mu^2 ln^2(mu), mu^2 ln(mu) and mu^2, with coefficients built from the gravity-gradient matrix Gamma1 and its components Gamma_xx, Gamma_xy.
4. The "mu ln mu" terms (COMPUTED scales at mu = 0.0121529529, |ln mu| = 4.410): mu |ln mu| = 0.0536; mu^2 |ln mu|^2 = 2.87e-3; mu^2 |ln mu| = 6.5e-4; mu^2 = 1.48e-4; mu^3 = 1.8e-6. The logarithms are in the perilune time and in v_inf, not in b.

## 7. Printed numbers usable as sourced tests

None: the paper has no table, no numerical example, no value of mu, and no figure. What is testable is structural (all are statements of the paper):
- Formal orders: first-order matching formulas hold to O(mu^2), second-order to O(mu^3) (stated for (2.3.4)-(2.3.6)); the matching region is t - t1 = O(mu^(1/2)).
- a = mu/v_inf^2 = O(mu) and b = O(mu) when dr0, dv0 = O(mu).
- Reductions: omitting all O(mu^2) terms gives v1 + rhodot_1b = v_inf (2.3.4a) and i - i* = O(mu); b is the part of rho_1b + rho_2b perpendicular to v1 + rhodot_1b (2.3.5).
- The identity of section 6, point 2, between this paper's (2.3.5) and Perko's (b.2), which a coded implementation must satisfy.
A numerical test the paper licenses (not run): for a flyby at small mu in the project's CR3BP code, integrate from Earth-side initial conditions through the encounter, fit the osculating hyperbola at closest approach, and compare (b, delta, v_inf) with the first-order and the second-order formulas; the discrepancy should scale like mu^2 (first order) and mu^3 (second order) when mu is varied over a decade.

## 8. Relation to the other papers

- Guillaume 1975 (paper II, digest in `docs/notes`): extends the Breakwell-Perko matching to variations of order mu^nu, 1/3 <= nu <= 1, where b = O(mu^nu) and the deflection tends to zero; its first-order equations coincide with this paper's first-order result for nu = 1 (Perko 1977 p.278: "These Equations (7) agree with the Breakwell-Perko matching Equations (3) in Breakwell and Perko (1965) for nu = 1"). It does not contain this paper's second-order terms. Guillaume 1975a (paper I) uses the first-order BPM equations with error O(mu^2) for the periodic symmetric solutions.
- Perko 1976 (Part A): Appendix B quotes exactly this paper's second-order results as (b.1) to (b.3) ("the Breakwell and Perko (1974) Equations (3.5) and (3.6)"; the printed numbering in this paper is 2.3.5 and 2.3.6) and uses them for the AB(1, 1) and AA(0, 1) near-Moon families, where the order lost per near-Moon passage requires second-order matching over the first arc. Perko 1976b (Part D) cites it as [4] for the same, and notes that the "second order terms given in [4] being too lengthy to include".
- Perko 1977 and 1981 (Parts C and B): their matching uses Guillaume's first-order extension with the O(mu^nu) variations; the second-order content of this paper is not used there.
- Gomez & Olle 1991: the project's first-order periapsis relation is this paper's hyperbola; their asymptotics are first order.

## 9. Techniques applicable to the project's problems

### `#899`: the seed-accuracy scale at the Earth-Moon mass
Question: does second order shrink the "about 10,000 km" first-order error quoted in the digest of Perko 1976b and 1981b, and by how much?
- Correction to that figure first (dated note added in that digest). The 10,000 km scale there (0.026 Earth-Moon distances) came from the uniform error bound delta^3 |ln mu|^2 with delta = max(mu^(1/2), mu^nu) in Perko 1981b Theorem 2. That bound is for the nu-family regime (variations of order mu^nu, near a bifurcation) and is a deliberately crude uniform estimate with an unknown constant; it is not the accuracy of the first-order matching formulas for an O(mu) flyby.
- What this paper changes, for the regime it covers (variations of order mu, a flyby with an O(1) deflection, i.e. the project's Earth-Moon cycler flybys): the formal error of the matched hyperbola parameters drops from O(mu^2) (first order) to O(mu^3) (second order), a factor of about mu = 1/82 in the formal order. COMPUTED scales in kilometres (1 Earth-Moon distance = 384,400 km), each to be multiplied by an unknown O(1) coefficient that the paper does not give: mu^2 = 57 km; mu^2 |ln mu| = 250 km; mu^3 = 0.69 km; mu^3 |ln mu| = 3.0 km; mu^3 |ln mu|^2 = 13 km. So the formal gain is from the order of 60 to 250 km to the order of 1 to 13 km. In the velocity, the second-order terms are of order mu^2 |ln mu|^2 = 2.9e-3 of the lunar orbital speed (1.0228 km/s), about 3 m/s times a coefficient involving Gamma1 v1/v1^6, which is large for slow encounters (v1^2 of order 0.03 to 0.1 in the project's units), so for slow flybys the coefficient can be large (INFERRED from the 1/v1^6 powers in 2.1.28 and 2.3.4).
- The relative importance: b itself is of order mu (about 4,700 km times a coefficient), so the first-order relative error in b is O(mu) (about 1 percent) and the second-order relative error O(mu^2) (about 0.01 percent). The deflection beta = 2 arctan(mu/(b v_inf^2)) then has a relative error of the same size. These are formal orders; no numerical check exists in the paper.
- Caveat: asymptotic series at mu = 0.0122 are not guaranteed to improve; Henon (1997 p.225) saw the generating family diverge from the periodic family at mu_EM for family i. A second-order seed is expected to be better than first order for flybys with v1 of order one and not too close to a bifurcation; for slow or near-resonant encounters (the Hitzl & Henon critical orbits, v1^2 below 0.1) the validity numbers mu |ln mu|/v1^3 are of order one and neither order is usable (digest of Hitzl & Henon, section 5).
- Practical rule: use the second-order formulas (2.3.5) and (2.3.6) with the quadratures rho_1b, rho_2b as the seed for the P2 corrector when v1 is of order one; the residual of the seed against a corrected orbit then measures the neglected O(mu^3) and the unknown coefficients, giving a calibration of the constants on the Casoliva Table 3 rows (a test that can be pre-registered: median |seed - corrected| in b and v_inf over the rows with v1^2 above 0.5 should be at least three times smaller at second order than at first).

### `#906` (turn gate)
- The turn and periapsis relation is unchanged (section 6). Second order changes b at relative O(mu) and v_inf at O(mu^2 ln^2 mu), so a prediction of the demanded turn from Earth-side data changes by about one percent times a coefficient. The gate should still use the integrated rotating-frame turn; the matched prediction is a seed and a consistency check (a gate input that disagrees with the second-order prediction by more than a few percent for a flyby with v1 of order one flags a frame error).
- The 3D remark: the formulas hold for three-dimensional variations (p.437), so the matching also supports out-of-plane encounters; the gate's turn is the angle between the 3D relative velocity vectors.

### `#928` (regularised propagator and transition matrix near a close approach)
- The singular structure of the outer expansion is explicit (section 3): rho^(1) has a ln(tau0/tau) term, rhoddot^(1) a ln term, rho^(2) a 1/tau and ln(tau0/tau) terms, rhodot^(2) 1/tau^2 and ln^2 terms. A state transition matrix propagated by the outer variational equations through a close approach therefore has logarithmic and inverse-time singular parts; these set where the outer STM may be used (about mu^(1/2) in time, 0.48 d at mu_EM, per the digest of Perko 1976b and 1981b) and what a regularised STM must reproduce.
- A ready validation target for a regularised STM through an encounter: the paper's final remark says the partial derivatives of (delta, b, v_inf) with respect to (dr0, dv0) "are obtainable through O(mu) by, at most, single quadratures". Those sensitivities, computed from the formulas, are a mu-small analytic comparison for the encounter block of the project's regularised STM (compute d(b, delta, v_inf)/d(dr0, dv0) numerically with the regularised code at small mu and compare with the quadrature formula); agreement to O(mu) is the pass criterion, a test not yet run.
