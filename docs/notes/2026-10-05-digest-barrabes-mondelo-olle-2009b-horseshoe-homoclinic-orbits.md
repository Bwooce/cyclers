## 0. What the paper is, and what it is not

A purely numerical study of homoclinic connections to the equilibrium point L3 (not L1 or L2, and not a periodic orbit) of the PLANAR circular restricted three-body problem, as the mass parameter mu is varied. It is NOT a paper about the Earth-Moon cyclers, about L1 or L2 Lyapunov orbits, or about resonant orbits: L3 is the collinear point on the far side of the large primary, and the "horseshoe" is the co-orbital horseshoe of Janus and Epimetheus. It holds no continuation of a periodic orbit family, no stability analysis of a periodic orbit and no Jacobi-constant table: every printed number is a mass parameter at which a connection exists (section 3 below). Its transferable content for this project is (a) the two-dimensional "function of mu with a transversal zero" method for locating mu values at which an invariant-manifold connection exists, (b) the numerical pitfalls of that method near small mu and near mu values at which a manifold hits the small primary, and (c) the cascade structure of multi-round connections near a one-round value. It prints no figure of a family characteristic curve and no table.

## 1. Model and conventions (p.199, READ)

Planar CR3BP in the rotating frame, origin at the barycentre, period of the primaries 2 pi. Primary of mass 1 - mu at (mu, 0); primary of mass mu at (mu - 1, 0); mu in (0, 1/2]. Equations (1): x'' - 2y' = Omega_x, y'' + 2x' = Omega_y, with
 Omega(x, y) = (x^2 + y^2)/2 + (1 - mu)/r1 + mu/r2 + mu(1 - mu)/2, r1 = sqrt((x - mu)^2 + y^2), r2 = sqrt((x - mu + 1)^2 + y^2).
Jacobi integral (2): C = 2 Omega(x, y) - x'^2 - y'^2. Symmetry (3): (t, x, y, x', y') -> (-t, x, -y, -x', y'). Equilibria ordered x_L2 <= mu - 1 <= x_L1 <= mu <= x_L3: L1 between the primaries, L2 beyond the SMALL primary on the left, L3 on the right of the LARGE primary (the opposite x-orientation to the Szebehely and `core.cr3bp` convention, see section 7). Position and Jacobi value at L3 (p.200, READ): x_L3 = 1 + (5/12) mu + O(mu^3), C_3 = 3 + 2 mu + O(mu^2). Note the printed C includes the constant mu(1 - mu) term, so it differs from the `core.cr3bp.jacobi_constant` value by exactly mu(1 - mu) (COMPUTED, section 7). Spectrum of DX(L_i): {+-i omega, +-lambda}, a centre x saddle, so the stable and unstable manifolds W^s and W^u of L3 are one-dimensional.

The mu values in play: the paper's search interval is [0.0002, 0.02], stated to contain the Earth-Moon value mu = 0.01215 and the Sun-Jupiter value mu = 0.000953875 (p.199, READ).

## 2. Numerical method (p.200, READ)

- Branches: W^{u/s}_+ is the branch leaving (arriving at) L3 into the upper half plane y > 0, W^{u/s}_- into y < 0; by (3), W^u_- and W^s_+ are mirror images, as are W^u_+ and W^s_-.
- Initial condition on a branch: L3 + s v, v the unit eigenvector of the saddle eigenvalue lambda > 0 (unstable) or lambda < 0 (stable), s = 1e-6, double precision; the manifold is followed by integrating (1) forward (unstable) or backward (stable) with the Jacobi constant checked equal to C = C_3 along the way. "Most figures have been checked reproducing the computations using quadruple precision." Linear (first-order) approximation of the manifold only, no higher-order parameterisation; the paper says later (p.206, p.208) that this and double precision are why it cannot go beyond k = 5 rounds.
- Connection types: fix a Poincare section Sigma and indices j, k; the homoclinic orbit of type (+j, +k) follows W^u_+ to its j-th crossing of Sigma and W^s_+ backward to its k-th crossing, and requires the two crossing points q^u_j(mu) = q^s_k(mu) (4). Types (-j, -k) likewise; (+j, +k) and (-j, -k) connections are non-symmetric; if a non-symmetric (-j, -k) connection exists its mirror is a (+j, +k) connection; (-j, +k) and (+j, -k) connections are symmetric. The definition depends strongly on Sigma.
- Detection for a section Sigma = {x = c}: with x^u = x^s on the section, form d_y(mu) = y^u - y^s, d_x'(mu) = x'^u - x'^s, d_y'(mu) = y'^u - y'^s; because the energy is the same on both branches, a connection exists when two of the three vanish (p.201). These functions can be discontinuous in mu when loops appear in the manifolds.
- Symmetric homoclinic orbits (SHO), the main object from section 4 on: take Sigma = {y = 0}, x'_j(mu) = the x' component of the j-th crossing of W^u_- with y = 0. If x'_j(mu) = 0 (orthogonal crossing, eq. (5)), applying (3) gives the mirror trajectory on W^s that meets the first at the j-th cut with y = x' = 0: an SHO. A j-round SHO is one that surrounds L3, L4 and L5 j times; Lambda_j = {mu > 0 : a j-round SHO exists} (p.203-204). The detection criterion is a transversal zero of x'_j(mu) in the (mu, x'_j) plane.

## 4. The dynamics: one-round, two-round and multi-round connections

Section 4, one round (pp.203-205, READ). The function x'_1(mu) (Fig. 4 left) has a decreasing sequence of transversal zeros mu^1_n -> 0 (n -> infinity), all below 0.01, the first of which are around mu ~ 0.004 in Fig. 4; each corresponds to a horseshoe SHO that surrounds L4 and L5 once and crosses y = 0 orthogonally with x < x_L3 (Fig. 4 right, mu = 0.0037257851523, drawn with sharp corners, the plot has a flat top and bottom and a narrow end near x ~ 1 close to L3). The paper cites Font 1999 for an expression of these values (not held, not identified). x'_1(mu) has JUMP discontinuities: just before a jump, the projection of the W^u_- branch forms a loop that crosses the x axis close to L3 (Fig. 5 left, a similar figure in Llibre, Martinez and Simo 1985 for L2), so the first crossing is no longer the orthogonal one. The loop-bearing SHO is detected from x'_2(mu): in a neighbourhood of every jump of x'_1(mu), a piece of x'_2(mu) cuts the horizontal axis (Fig. 6): there is a "loop value" at which the manifold has an orthogonal crossing "in the middle of the loop" (Fig. 5 right). This SHO still surrounds L4 and L5 once, so it belongs to Lambda_1. The loop values form a second sequence mu^{1-bar}_n -> 0, also in Lambda_1. W^u_+ gives the same qualitative behaviour with different values.
Notation (p.205): a bar above a superindex means the orthogonal intersection exhibits a loop; the sign + or - means the sequence decreases or increases; mu^delta_n is the one-round sequence without (delta = 1) or with (delta = 1-bar) a loop; mu^{delta,k+-}_{n,m}, mu^{delta,k-bar+-}_{n,m} are k-round sequences tending to mu^delta_n.

Section 5, two rounds (pp.205-207). Near a transversal value mu^1_n a zoom of x'_2(mu) (Fig. 8 left) shows two sequences of zeros of x'_2: {mu^{1,2-}_{n,m}}_m increasing and {mu^{1,2+}_{n,m}}_m decreasing, with mu^{1,2-}_{n,m} < mu^1_n < mu^{1,2+}_{n,m} and both limits (m -> infinity) equal to mu^1_n. Each is a two-round SHO with the orthogonal crossing at the SECOND intersection with y = 0 at x > x_L3 (Fig. 7 top left). The jump discontinuities of x'_2 accumulating at mu^1_n give two further sequences {mu^{1,2-bar-}_{n,m}} (increasing) and {mu^{1,2-bar+}_{n,m}} (decreasing): two-round SHO with no loop at the first crossing but a loop at the second, so the orthogonal crossing is the THIRD intersection, x > x_L3 (Fig. 7 top right), given by zeros of x'_3 close to a jump of x'_2, itself close to a zero of x'_1. The four sequences are ordered (p.207):
 mu^{1,2-}_{n,m} < mu^{1,2-bar-}_{n,m} < mu^1_n < mu^{1,2-bar+}_{n,m} < mu^{1,2+}_{n,m}.
This four-fold structure is stated to agree with the analytic results of Koltsova and Lerman (1995) and Koltsova (2003) (not held; the paper cites Grotta Ragazzo 1997 and Mielke, Holmes and O'Reilly 1992 for the cascade). For a loop value (mu^1-bar sequence) one needs x'_4 and x'_5 (loop on the left of L3 at the first three crossings, Fig. 8 right, x'_1 to x'_4 near mu = 0.004192 to 0.004207), and again four sequences {mu^{1-bar,2+-}} and {mu^{1-bar,2-bar+-}}, two-round orbits in Lambda_2 (Fig. 7 bottom, mu^{1-bar,2-}_{n,m} = 0.004192163077). The W^u_+ branch gives the same qualitative results with different values.

Section 6, k rounds, k >= 3 (pp.207-209). The analytic theory (Grotta Ragazzo 1997; Mielke et al. 1992) predicts a cascade: for mu tending to a one-round value there are 2-, 3-, ..., k-round SHO for any k. The numerical illustration is in a narrow neighbourhood I of mu^1_l = 0.0037257851523. Functions x'_k(mu), k = 3, 4, 5 (Figs. 9, 10) oscillate with the maximum (minimum) values decreasing (increasing) to 0 as mu -> mu^1_l, which gives infinitely many zeros on both sides: Lambda_k intersect I is infinite on both sides. For k = 3 there is one increasing sequence in Lambda_3 tending to mu^1_l from the left and one decreasing from the right; they are the three zeros of x'_3 in each interval (mu^{1,2-bar-}_{l,m}, mu^{1,2-bar-}_{l,m+1}) and (mu^{1,2-bar+}_{l,m+1}, mu^{1,2-bar+}_{l,m}) away from the edges (Fig. 9 right), giving {mu^{1,3-}_{l,m}}_m and {mu^{1,3+}_{l,m}}_m; the zeros of x'_3 near the edges are exactly mu^{1,2-bar+}_{l,m}, mu^{1,2-bar+}_{l,m+1}. Near these zeros x'_3 has jump discontinuities that correspond to points of Lambda_3 with a loop in the second passage near L3 and the orthogonal crossing at the fifth intersection, i.e. zeros of x'_5 giving {mu^{1,3-bar-}_{l,m}} and {mu^{1,3-bar+}_{l,m}}. For k = 4 the structure differs: associated to EACH value of Lambda_2 there is an infinity of Lambda_4 values accumulating to it, a DOUBLE infinity accumulating at mu^1_l (Fig. 10 left), because SHO in Lambda_4 are two-round with respect to SHO in Lambda_2, so Grotta Ragazzo and Mielke et al. give an infinity of SHO in Lambda_4 for each SHO in Lambda_2; loops give {mu^{1,2+-,4+-}_{l,m,k}} and the three other bar combinations. The x'_5 behaviour relative to x'_4 mirrors x'_3 relative to x'_2 (sequences {mu^{1,5+-}_{l,m}}, {mu^{1,5-bar+-}_{l,m}}). The thickness of the x'_5 graph (Fig. 10 right) "reveals that we are approaching the limit of numerical accuracy"; the authors cannot compute x'_k for arbitrary k with the linear manifold approximation in double precision.

Conclusions (p.209, READ): there exist TWO sequences of mu values tending to zero for which a one-round connection exists (one intersection with y = 0, or three, because of a loop); for each fixed one-round mu the multi-round connections are a cascade accumulating to that value (illustrated for two, three and four rounds, with the similarities and differences depending on the number of revolutions).

What the paper does NOT contain: any periodic orbit (the "horseshoe periodic orbits" of Barrabes and Mikkola 2005, Barrabes and Olle 2006, Schanzle 1967, Taylor 1981 are cited only as context); any statement of stability or of bifurcation of periodic orbits (the homoclinic cascade is the geometric footprint of the Devaney blue-sky and Henrard accumulation of periodic horseshoe families, which the introduction cites but this paper does not compute); any Floquet or monodromy computation; any L1 or L2 homoclinic orbit; any three-dimensional result.

## 5. Relation to the companion paper (Nonlinearity 22:2901, 2009) and to the Casoliva families

- Companion: the Nonlinearity paper (digest 742) is a continuation ALGORITHM (a predictor-corrector with a multiple-shooting extension) for families of homoclinic connections of periodic orbits to L1/L2 Lyapunov and halo orbits, with the family parameter energy or mu. This paper uses no continuation algorithm: it finds each connection by a 1D root search on x'_j(mu) at fixed energy C = C_3 for a point equilibrium, and explores the fan of connections by plotting the section functions against mu (the "dynamical aspects" of the title are the structure of the zero set, not stability). It is the L3, equilibrium-point sibling of the numerical-existence part of the companion. Both papers are by the same three authors and the reference to "Barrabes, Mondelo and Olle (2008) Numerical continuation ... Preprint" in this paper's reference list is the preprint of the companion.
- Casoliva et al. families: the Casoliva Earth-Moon cyclers (2008 conference paper section V, 2010 JGCD) use homoclinic connections of the L1 or L2 Lyapunov orbit (the 2008 table 3: energy -1.4502, five Earth flybys) and second-species resonant seeds; the held digests record that. This paper has no L1 or L2 content, so none of its sequences is a seed for a Casoliva row. I did not re-check the "Class 2 is built on L1 or L2 homoclinic connections" statement in the task brief against the 2010 digest; the #899 correction in `data/OUTSTANDING.md` names second-species resonant seeds from Barrabes and Gomez at mu = 1e-6 continued in mu as the Class 2 route, not homoclinic connections. Where this paper helps is the mu axis (section 6).
- The L2-analogue behaviour that this paper does report for small mu is a warning for any L1/L2 mu-continuation: above mu = 0.01173615 a manifold branch enters the half plane around the small primary, and "there exist values of mu for which one of the invariant manifolds collides with the small primary" (p.201, citing Barrabes and Olle 2006), and values at which no L3 homoclinic exists but a heteroclinic to a Lyapunov orbit does (Fig. 1 bottom right, mu = 0.014562349014). Both are statements about L3 manifolds, not L1/L2, so they warn but do not quantify what happens to L1/L2-based Class 2 seeds.

## 6. Techniques applicable to the project's problems

#899 (second-species and Class 2 seeds, continuation in mu toward the Earth-Moon value, `data/OUTSTANDING.md` #899 correction):
1. The structural lesson that matters: connections in mu are a COUNTABLE set of isolated values, not a continuum, with multi-round values accumulating on any given one-round value from both sides; hence continuation of a homoclinic-type object in mu at fixed number of rounds will, in general, hit a fold or a jump, not slide continuously to a target mu. A search near a given mu should therefore be a root search of a section function over mu (or over a second parameter), and a continuation should carry a jump-detection rule (here: a discontinuity of x'_j(mu) identifies a loop appearing, and the orbit continues as the next index x'_{j+2}), not treat a discontinuity as failure.
2. Small-mu singularity: below mu = 0.0002, with s = 1e-6, the section functions fall below 1e-6 and the result depends on the starting distance s. For a Barrabes-Gomez seed at mu = 1e-6 (Casoliva 2008) the same caution applies to any manifold-based start; the project's seeds at mu = 1e-6 are second-species (Poincare) solutions, not manifold-based, so this applies only if a homoclinic variant of #899 is attempted.
3. Mu ranges: the Earth-Moon value mu = 0.01215 sits inside the paper's search interval but just above the mu = 0.01173615 threshold at which the small-primary and L1/L2 manifolds start to matter for L3 connections; the Fig. 2 connection at mu = 0.012143988024852 (type (+2, -5)) differs from the standard 0.01215 by 6.0e-6 in mu (0.012143988 versus 0.01215). For L3 this is a numerical fact, not a cycler result.
4. Pairing with the Casoliva three-step strategy (mass, then Jacobi constant at fixed mass, then mass): this paper gives no data on whether a multi-parameter walk avoids the lunar-impact endings; its only analogue is that the section-function zero set is discrete in mu at fixed C = C_3, so a second parameter (C) would be needed to move along a connected branch, which is exactly Casoliva's step two. INFERRED.

#931 (stability classification and general three-body controls): this paper gives no stability numbers, no Floquet multipliers, no bifurcation values for any periodic orbit; there is nothing here to pin a classifier. The only #931-relevant statement is qualitative: the homoclinic cascade (four sequences around each one-round value, a double infinity for k = 4) is the topological footprint of a blue-sky accumulation of horseshoe periodic orbits (cited from Henrard 1973 and Henrard and Navarro 2004, not computed here), so a stability classifier applied to a horseshoe-orbit mu-continuation should expect an infinite sequence of fold or period-multiplying events accumulating on the one-round mu values 0.0037257851523 and 0.00419758. INFERRED, and unchecked; no periodic orbit data is printed to test it.

Other uses: the L3 values (x_L3 = 1 + 5 mu/12, C_3 = 3 + 2 mu with the paper's Omega) are a clean, cheap independent test of any CR3BP routine that supports a mirrored x-orientation and the mu(1 - mu) constant, see section 7.

2. Equilibrium: max |cr3bp_eom| at x_core = -x_L3 is below 1e-12 for every tabulated mu from 0.0002 to 0.02 (largest 9.1e-13 at mu = 0.0037257851523, 1.6e-13 at 0.00196201).
3. Jacobi at L3 with the paper's Omega (including mu(1 - mu)/2): C = x^2 + 2(1 - mu)/r1 + 2 mu/r2 + mu(1 - mu): mu = 0.001 gives 3.0019989790, mu = 0.0037257851523 gives 3.0074373893, mu = 0.01215 gives 3.0241489429.
