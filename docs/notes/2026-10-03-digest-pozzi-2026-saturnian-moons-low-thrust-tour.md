## What the paper is
A mission-design study for a one-way tour of Saturn's five Inner Large Moons (ILMs). Abstract: "a mission touring the Inner Large Moons of Saturn - Rhea, Dione, Tethys, Enceladus, and Mimas ... The tour begins at Rhea and concludes at Mimas, employing a trajectory concept that alternates between extended observation phases around each moon and Saturn-centered low-thrust spiral arcs to transition efficiently to the next target in the sequence."

Dynamical models (Sections 2, 4):
- Science (observation) orbits: "J2-perturbed Circular Restricted Three-Body Problem" per Saturn-moon pair (Sec. 2.2), with halo orbits about L1 and L2 and their hyperbolic invariant manifolds (HIMs). Homoclinic and heteroclinic connections between halo orbits are found at Poincare sections (Sec. 4.1). Halos are generated by differential correction with xz-symmetry (Howell 1984 algorithm, ref [38]).
- Inter-moon transfers: Saturn-centred N-body ephemeris model (SPICE; Saturn J2, ILM and Titan third-body, Sun only for the Rhea-Dione and Dione-Tethys legs; Sec. 2.3 and Sec. 3) with constant-thrust low-thrust arcs steered by a Q-law (Eq. 21-26). The patching is CR3BP-to-2BP/N-body at each moon's sphere of influence (SOI, Laplace radius times f = 4.5, Eq. 20).
- Propulsion (Table 4): Tmax 36 mN, Isp 1600 s, Pin 640 W (RTG-powered Hall thruster). Initial mass 1000 kg at Rhea.

The tour order is fixed and one-way: Rhea (Rh) -> Dione (Di) -> Tethys (Te) -> Enceladus (En) -> Mimas (Mi), outward-in in Saturn-centric radius (Rhea at 527.04e3 km down to Mimas at 185.52e3 km, Table 1). Titan is used only as the earlier capture body (see below), not as a tour stop.

## Trajectory content (direct quotes, section numbers)
- Sec. 1, structure: "The trajectory is structured into sequential blocks, from Rhea to Mimas, each consisting of an observation phase around the target moon, followed by an inter-moon powered segment."
- Sec. 4.1 (repetition within a moon's block, not between moons): "A notable advantage of this method is that the heteroclinic and homoclinic connections can be swept for an arbitrary duration with minimal to negligible propellant consumption. Their looping nature supports comprehensive observational campaigns of the lunar surfaces". The connection types considered: heteroclinic A (Northern L1 to Northern L2), B (Northern L1 to Southern L2), C (Northern L1 to Southern L1), D (Southern L2 to Northern L2); homoclinic E (Northern L1 to Northern L1), F (Southern L2 to Southern L2).
- Sec. 4.2: "Each transfer originates from a halo orbit around the L1 point of the outer moon and terminates at one around L2 of the inner moon." Reason for a powered arc: "the HIMs do not intersect in configuration space due to the near-circular nature of the Saturn-centered orbits", so the moon-to-moon legs are low-thrust, not ballistic.
- Sec. 5.1: halo families per moon, 25 members per family, 4 families (N/S x L1/L2), each halo discretised into 100 points. All connection types found except Types B, E, F in Saturn-Mimas. "Strictly speaking, these transfers cannot be considered fully fuel-free" (position error below 1 km, velocity error below 15 m/s at the section).
- Sec. 5.2: 100 x 25 x 2 = 2.5e7 candidate trajectories per segment; best leg selected by minimum propelled-arc delta-v. "approximately 100, 110, 160, and 290 revolutions around Saturn" on the four low-thrust legs (Saturn-orbit spirals, not repeated moon encounters).
- Sec. 5.3: "if each science orbit were repeated 100 times, the total mission duration would increase to approximately 5 years." The repetition is of the homoclinic/heteroclinic loop at one moon, at marginal propellant cost.
- Interplanetary precursor (Sec. 5.2 and 7, taken from the authors' ref [22]): 12.34-year Earth-Saturn transfer, 21 January 2028 to 24 May 2040, then "a subsequent unpowered capture at Saturn via Titan flybys". The present paper does not design that phase.
- Sec. 6 on architecture: "The tour is divided into five observational blocks, one for each ILM, starting at Rhea and ending at Mimas."

## VERDICT (question 2: is anything periodic, repeating or cycler-like between moons?)
No. The inter-moon structure is a one-way, non-repeating tour Rhea -> Dione -> Tethys -> Enceladus -> Mimas, each leg a continuous low-thrust spiral (about 4.4 km/s in total, so not ballistic or near-ballistic), after which the sequence ends at Mimas; nothing returns to a previously visited moon. The only periodic or repeating objects are the halo orbits and the homoclinic/heteroclinic loops confined inside a single moon's CR3BP sphere of influence, which are traversed repeatedly (the paper says the connections "can be swept for an arbitrary duration"), but those are single-moon dwell orbits, not cyclers between bodies. Most decisive quote: "The tour begins at Rhea and concludes at Mimas" (Abstract), together with "Each transfer originates from a halo orbit around the L1 point of the outer moon and terminates at one around L2 of the inner moon" (Sec. 4.2). The paper's own literature framing places it against "flyby-based missions" and "resonance hopping" tours, not against cyclers; it cites Russell and Strange 2009 only as ref [19] in the list of prior Saturnian studies (Introduction, with [18, 20]) and does not discuss moon cyclers.

## What it does NOT contain
- No repeating-encounter or free-return moon cycler; no multi-revolution itinerary returning to earlier moons; no resonance sequence (the tour is not designed by resonance hopping).
- No ballistic (or near-ballistic) inter-moon transfers: the HIMs do not intersect in configuration space, so every moon-to-moon leg is a powered Q-law arc.
- No Titan stop in the tour itself; no flybys designed (the paper contrasts itself with Cassini and Strange-Campagnola-Russell flyby tours); no Jacobi-constant-matched transfer between two different moons.
- No vinf, flyby altitude, or per-encounter data, because there are no gravity-assist encounters.
- No ephemeris-epoch-specific halo initial conditions: halo initial states (position and velocity vectors), monodromy eigenvalues and stability indices are not tabulated.
- Halo orbit periods and Jacobi ranges are given only as family ranges (Table 5), not as per-member lists. Eccentricity and inclination of the moons are neglected in the CR3BP phases (stated as future work: J2-perturbed ER3BP).
- Interplanetary arrival and Titan capture are cited from ref [22], not computed here.
- Journal-version text not compared against this v1 preprint.

## Suggested topology label(s)
- Primary: "halo". Sec. 4.1 and 5.1: "halo orbits used as staging points for heteroclinic and homoclinic loops" (Abstract), and halo families around L1 and L2 of each of the five Saturn-moon systems are the core objects.
- Secondary: "mga-tour" in the loose sense of a non-repeating multi-moon tour (Rhea, Dione, Tethys, Enceladus, Mimas, one pass each, ending at Mimas), with the strong caveat that there are no gravity-assist flybys; the paper itself describes the tour as "an alternative to traditional flyby-based missions" (Abstract). If the label set is read strictly as flyby-based, then use "halo" alone.
- Not "repeated-moon", "pump-tour", "tulip", "nrho" or "resonant": there are no repeating inter-moon encounters, no V-infinity leveraging, no tulip or NRHO orbits, and no resonance hopping (the resonance and leveraging methods are cited only as the prior work this concept departs from, Sec. 1 and 6).
