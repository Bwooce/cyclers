## 0. What the paper is

Abstract (p.1187): "A new periodic solution with a binary collision and with several close approaches is found in the problem of three bodies by numerical integration using regularized variables. The masses of the participating bodies are 3, 4, and 5 and the distances between the bodies at the beginning of the motion are approximately 3, 4, and 5." Burrau's initial conditions (the Pythagorean problem, masses 3, 4, 5 at rest at the apices of a 3-4-5 triangle) give an asymptotic, not periodic, motion (1967a). The key observation (p.1187): at t about 15.830 bodies 2 and 3 pass within about 4e-4 while the first body's velocity is small; "this combination of circumstances, namely a near collision and an almost zero velocity occurring simultaneously, suggests the possibility of the existence of a periodic orbit." They modify the initial POSITIONS (all six initial velocities remain zero) by a differential correction so that the motion is exactly periodic with a binary collision at half-period. Of the "two methods" (modify the initial conditions, or the conditions at the near collision) the first "was found to be simpler".

## 2. The printed results (READ pp.1187-1190)

- Total energy of the periodic orbit E = -12.7616527695; period T = 31.8229622453 (p.1188). "The coordinates at t = 0 and t = T differ by less than 1e-10. The initial and final velocities differ also by less than 1e-10, or since the initial velocities are zero, the final velocity components are less than 1e-10." The procedure "could have been continued to furnish an error smaller than 1e-10 but this value was deemed satisfactory." Energy constant to |dE| < 1e-10 during the integration.
- At t = T/2 the second and third bodies collide and the first body acquires zero velocity; error allowed 1e-10, "actually the value of 1e-11 was obtained." Fig. 1 (periodic orbit from t = 0 to T/2, bodies numbered at unit time steps; collision between bodies 1 and 2 labelled C(1,2) and the simultaneous zero velocity of body 3 labelled C(3) in the caption as printed, which contradicts the text's "second and third bodies collide and the first body acquires zero velocity"; the caption's body labels look swapped; INFERRED, and my integration confirms the text, section 5).
- Several close approaches (abstract); the closest approach of the Burrau case is at t about 15.830 with r23 about 4e-4 (p.1187).
- Remarks (p.1188-1189): (i) In the 12-dimensional phase space the orbit has all six velocities zero at t = 0; "Moving in the six-dimensional configuration space in the neighborhood of Burrau's initial conditions no other periodic orbit was found". Conjecture: modifying the zero velocities too would give a family of periodic orbits, including ones without collision. (ii) The solution is symmetric in time about T/2: x_i(t) = x_i(T - t), y_i(t) = y_i(T - t); at T/2, x2 = x3, y2 = y3, and xdot1 = ydot1 = 0; after T/2 the motion repeats with reversed velocity vectors. (iii) Periodicity x_i(t) = x_i(t + T); (iv) symmetry about t = 0, x_i(t) = x_i(-t); combinations such as x_i(t) = x_i(t - T) = x_i(T - t) = x_i(-t); velocity components are equal in absolute value and differ in sign where (-t) occurs.
- Numerical method (p.1189): fifth-order Runge-Kutta and Levi-Civita regularisation "described in detail in Paper 2". Differential correction: with the six-vector r of the initial positions x1, y1, x2, y2, x3, y3 and the 12-vector rho of positions and velocities, the conditions xdot_i(0) = ydot_i(0) = xdot_i(T) = ydot_i(T) = 0 leave only the position partials; Delta r_i = (dr_i/dr_j^0) Delta r_j^0 + (dr_i/drdot_j^0) Delta rdot_j^0 with Delta rdot^0 = 0 (eq. 3), the 6x12 matrix A = {a_ij = d rho_i / d r_j^0}, Delta rho = A Delta r^0, giving 12 conditions on 6 unknowns; solved by least squares: minimise e^2 = sum_j [(Delta r_j)^2 + (Delta rdot_j)^2], normal equations B Delta r^0 = C with B = A* A and C = A* Delta rho. "The actual computation requires about 12 iterations. The same A matrix is used for the last four iterations (six or seven significant figures, sufficient). The A matrix is computed by changing the initial coordinates by 1e-5 units" (finite differences). B B^-1 minus the identity has elements less than 1e-12 in absolute value; "the actual test of periodicity, however, is an integration using the initial conditions obtained."
- Collision test at T/2 (p.1189): near the collision the integration is in the regularised system x = xi^2 - eta^2, y = 2 xi eta, dtau = dt/r, r = xi^2 + eta^2 (x, y the coordinates of m2 relative to m3); tau_c is the regularised time at collision (xi = eta = r = 0). Iterating for xi = 0 at t = T/2 gives eta_c = 0.12e-10 and tau_c = (1/2) T = -0.75e-10 (as printed; the second expression is unclear: presumably the difference of the regularised-time at which collision is reached and the half period, INFERRED); "Since r is eta^2, the condition for collision (r = 0) is satisfied to 20 significant figures."
- Physical scales (pp.1189-1190): tau = (L^3/GM)^(1/2) (eq. 5). The 3, 4, 5 pc example: masses 3, 4, 5 solar masses, tau = 1.43e7 yr, T = 4.55e8 yr. Wild's triple system of irregular spirals: M = 0.25e12 solar masses (masses 0.75e12, 1e12, 1.25e12), total 3e12, L = 10 kpc (initial separations 30, 40, 50 kpc), tau = 3.2e7 yr, T about 1e9 yr, half period about 5e8 yr to the collision, V = L/tau about 300 km/s; "Prior to the first close approach, at t about 1.80, corresponding to approximately 6e7 yr, the two largest masses are at a distance of 6 kpc ... The relative velocity at this time is approximately 1400 km/sec." COMPUTED: 31.8229622453 x 1.43e7 = 4.55e8 yr; 31.82 x 3.2e7 = 1.02e9 yr; 10 kpc/3.2e7 yr = 305 km/s; 1.80 x 3.2e7 = 5.8e7 yr. The 1.80 and the 6 kpc (0.6 unit) do not match the first close approach of the Burrau orbit (1.879, 1e-2, m2-m3) or of the periodic orbit (1.897); the 6 kpc is the m1-m3 approach distance 0.6 at t = 3.026 (INFERRED); not reproduced.

## 3. Flagged inconsistency in Table I, and its resolution (COMPUTED)

Computed from the table as printed (section 5): the centre-of-mass condition fails: the mass-weighted sum of the x column is 3 x 0.0694920571 + 4 x (-0.0129612126) + 5 x (-0.0313262594) = +2.39e-8 (the y column sums to exactly 0). A correction that does not move the centre of mass would sum to zero in both columns (a centre-of-mass shift is dynamically neutral, but the periodicity procedure minimises the correction norm, so the sums need not vanish; however the y column does). The energy of the table-as-printed state is -12.76165275485, which differs from the printed E = -12.7616527695 by 1.46e-8, and the integrated state at t = T misses its initial position by 1.5e-7 (printed claim: less than 1e-10). If the printed -0.0129612126 for x2 is replaced by -0.0129612186 (the 9th decimal 2 replaced by 8), then: the x-column sum is -1.0e-10 (zero within rounding), the energy is -12.7616527697 (matching the printed value to 2e-10), and the position and velocity residuals at T fall to 3.6e-10 and 3.7e-10. A one-parameter scan of the x2 value shows a sharp minimum there (x2 printed as -0.0129612185: residual 2.2e-9; -0.0129612186: 3.6e-10; -0.0129612187: 2.9e-9; -0.0129612126, as printed: 1.5e-7). Random perturbations of all six entries by 5e-11 change the residual by under 1 percent, so rounding of the printed digits cannot explain the 1.5e-7. CONCLUSION: the printed x_2 is very probably a typesetting or transcription slip for -0.0129612186; I read "...126" on a 400 dpi image of the scan (it may be an error in the printing rather than in my reading). Use -0.0129612186 for a periodic orbit test and report the slip. The initial positions with this correction: body 2 at (-1.9870387814, -1.0531023120).

## 5. Quick check (COMPUTED, scratch only, 2026-10-05)

Integrator: DOP853 (scipy), rtol 1e-12 and 1e-13, in a Levi-Civita-regularised formulation for the pair (2,3): relative vector w with R = |w|^2, dt = R dtau, second-order perturbed KS equations u'' = (h/2) u + (R/2) L^T (F_k - F_l) with the pair's binding energy integrated by h' = 2 u'.L^T (F_k - F_l) (Aarseth 1971 eqs. 24, 27; the planar block of the 4-vector); the centre of the pair Q and body 1 integrated in Cartesian coordinates with the time factor R. The collision of 2 and 3 is therefore integrated through without a singularity. Positive control: the same code reproduces Burrau's Table I close-approach times of the 1967a paper (all 17 within 2e-3) and the 4.138e-4 minimum at t = 15.8299203 with energy conserved to 7.8e-13 to t = 16.
Results with the Table I positions as printed (x_2 = -0.0129612126 etc.): E = -12.76165275485 (printed -12.7616527695; difference 1.46e-8); T/2 reached with r23 = 2.4e-5 and |v1| = 3.8e-8; at t = T: maximum position error 1.537e-7 and maximum velocity 1.024e-7 (the same at both tolerances 1e-12 and 1e-13); relative energy change 5e-12 over the period. With the corrected x_2 = -0.0129612186: E = -12.7616527697; at t = T the position error is 3.6e-10 and the velocity 3.7e-10; the collision: minimum r23 = 3.8e-22 at t = T/2 - 1.8e-10, v1 about 4e-11 there; energy conservation 1e-10 or better. Close approaches of the periodic orbit (r23 local minima; all other pairs for reference), times and distances: 1.8974 (1.02e-2), 3.8144 (5.99e-2), 8.7626 (1.14e-2), 11.7805 (0.198), collision at 15.9115, then mirror-image times 20.0425 (0.198), 23.0604 (1.14e-2), 28.0086 (5.99e-2), 29.9256 (1.02e-2); pair (1,3) minima 0.572 (t = 3.03), 0.1245 (7.04), 0.461 (9.97), 0.256 (14.7, 17.1 symmetric about T/2), 0.461 (21.9), 0.1245 (24.8), 0.572 (28.8); pair (1,2) never below 1.7985 (t = 2.95 and 28.87), and 2.7119 at T/2. So the periodic orbit's close approaches are nearly those of the Burrau orbit (compare 1.879, 3.801, 8.760, 11.611 in the 1967a digest) shifted by up to 2e-2 in time, consistent with the small (7 percent) modification of the initial positions. The sensitivity: the printed x_2 digit slip alters the closing residual by a factor of 400 (1.5e-7 against 3.6e-10), showing that the closing error is dominated by the collision's amplification of initial-condition errors; the paper's "less than 1e-10" statement requires every digit of the initial conditions.

## 7. Techniques applicable to the project's problems

All INFERRED; nothing built.

**#931(a) (a published periodic general-three-body orbit with a collision).** This is the strongest published control in the corpus for a general three-body integrator through a binary collision: an initial state with six printed 10-digit coordinates (with the x_2 correction of section 3), zero velocities, a printed period T = 31.8229622453 and energy E = -12.7616527695, and an exact structure to test (collision of bodies 2 and 3 at T/2 with body 1 at rest, time-reversal symmetry, closing at T). Tests, in increasing demand: (i) the closing residual after one period, at most 1e-9 with a regularised integrator at tolerance 1e-12 (my run: 3.6e-10); a plain Cartesian integrator cannot pass through the collision and should be expected to fail near T/2, which is a negative control and the reason the problem tests the regularisation; (ii) |E - E_printed| at most 1e-9; (iii) the symmetry x_i(T - t) = x_i(t); (iv) the minimum r23 at T/2 within 1e-6 of zero; (v) the sensitivity control: perturbing the initial coordinates by 1e-8 must change the closing residual by a factor of about 30 to 100 (my test: 6e-9 in x_2 gives 1.5e-7). The x_2 slip is itself a control: an integrator that cannot distinguish -0.0129612126 from -0.0129612186 has no collision accuracy.
**#928 (regularisation).** The required machinery is exactly a pair regularisation that carries the rest of the system in Cartesian coordinates and switches pair when needed (here one pair suffices because only the pair (2,3) collides). The test for a project KS or Levi-Civita propagator: integrate through the T/2 collision and recover the printed T and E; with the planar equations of the Aarseth 1971 digest, a pass through exact collision (r23 of order 1e-22) is the extreme of the project's own 8.7e-6 pass bound (#896) and shows the regularised pair equations are regular through zero separation, which no restricted-problem test of the project demonstrates (the restricted problem has no closer controls than 8.7e-6).
**#929 (integrator comparison).** The periodic orbit gives a quantitative accuracy axis for the comparison of plain, Sundman and regularised integrators: the closing residual at T against steps and tolerance (my regularised run: 2661 steps at 1e-13 for the whole period, residual 3.6e-10).
