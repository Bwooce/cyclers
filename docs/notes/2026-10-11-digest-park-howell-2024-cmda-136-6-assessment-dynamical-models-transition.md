## 0. Verdict

An analysis paper on which perturbation, lunar-distance pulsation or solar gravity, dominates the difference between the Sun-Earth-Moon ephemeris model (HFEM) and the Earth-Moon CR3BP, and what that means for choosing a one-frequency intermediate model
(ER3BP for pulsation, BCR4BP for solar gravity). It rewrites all models in a common 13-coefficient equation of motion in the pulsating-rotating frame, shows analytically that the solar perturbation is, to good approximation, linear in the spacecraft's y-excursion
and rotates at twice the Sun angle, fits the dominant frequencies, maps where pulsation or solar gravity dominates, and tests the result on the Earth-Moon L2 halo orbits in the 9 to 10 day period range ("challenging region"). Result: for that region the ER3BP periodic orbit
is a consistently better initial guess than the direct CR3BP guess (fewer Newton iterations), the BCR4BP is not, and the transition outcome depends mainly on the pulsation angle theta_1, not the Sun angle theta_2. Nothing here is a cycler.
It contains sourced initial conditions for four periodic orbits (Tables 8 and 9) that reproduce independently (sec. 8), and sourced fit coefficients (Tables 3 and 4).

## 1. Introduction (pp.1-4)

- Motivation: ARTEMIS Lissajous orbits (2010), EQUULEUS L2 quasi-halo (Oguri et al. 2020), Gateway 9:2 NRHO. The CR3BP offers structures but flight needs an ephemeris model. Two forces dominate the gap in cislunar space: "pulsation" of the non-uniform Earth-Moon system and solar gravity.
- "Direct" transition (patch points from the CR3BP orbit corrected in the HFEM) works for many regions but needs trial and error; some L2 halo members, for example near the 3:1 synodic resonance, do not keep their geometry (Davis et al. 2017; Boudad et al. 2022).
- Intermediate models: periodically perturbed one-frequency systems (ER3BP, BCR4BP, QBCP, HR4BP) versus multi-frequency models (Gomez et al. 2002, five frequencies). Challenge: choosing which one-frequency model to use.
- Four aims (p.3): (1) analytical impact of the extra forces on an arc; (2) patterns by frequency; (3) evaluation of the ER3BP and BCR4BP; (4) a worked L2 halo transition.

## 2. Dynamical models (pp.4-12)

- 2.1 frames (Eqs. 1 to 6, Fig. 1 CAPTION): inertial frame and pulsating-rotating frame with origin at the Earth-Moon barycentre B, x_hat along the Earth-Moon line, z_hat along the lunar angular momentum; R = B + l C rho (Eq. 2: translation, scaling by the instantaneous distance l, rotation C). Eqs. 3 and 4 differentiate Eq. 2; Eqs. 5 and 6 relate d/dT and d/dt. Table 1: notation for T (dimensional, s) and t (nondimensional).
- Eq. 7: the pulsating-frame acceleration solved from the inertial EOM; Eq. 8: the common form rho'' = b-vector + (b4, b5, b6 block) rho' + (b7 to b12 block) rho + b13 grad Omega (Gomez et al. 2002); Eq. 9: potential Omega = (1-mu)/rho_Ec + mu/rho_Mc + sum of mu_j/rho_jc over additional bodies.
- Table 2 (symbolic): b1 to b13 per model. CR3BP: all zero except b5 = 2 (Coriolis), b7 = b10 = 1 (centrifugal), b13 = 1 (Eq. 10). ER3BP: b4 = -e sin f/(2 sqrt(1+e cos f)), b5 = 2 sqrt(1+e cos f), b12 = -e cos f, b7 = b10 = 1 (Eqs. 11, 12; Omega_E = Omega_C/(1+e cos f) - e cos f z^2/(2(1+e cos f)); f_dot = sqrt(1+e cos f)... printed "1+e cos f" under a radical, text layer garbled).
  BCR4BP: b1, b2 from the Sun's pull on B (b1 = -(mu_S/rho_S,4^2) cos theta_S,4 and b2 likewise with sin; Eqs. 13, 14), the rest as the CR3BP; not coherent (the Moon stays on a circle despite the Sun). HFEM: the b's built from DE440 states of B, l, h and the Moon's acceleration A_EM and jerk J_EM (constructed assuming point masses, not read from SPICE).
- 2.3 isochronous correspondence (Eqs. 15, 16, 17, Fig. 2 CAPTION): uniform time t' = t* = sqrt((mu_E + mu_M)/l*^3) (earlier literature) versus non-uniform time t' = sqrt((mu_E + mu_M)/l^3), which keeps b13 = 1 in every model and scales the initial velocity consistently; Eq. 17 is the inertial-to-pulsating state map including the t' scaling of the velocity. Fig. 2a (CAPTION): a 14.8 d L2 halo propagated half a period in the HFEM from the epochs of maximum and minimum Earth-Moon distance with each time law; the non-uniform law stays closer to the CR3BP orbit. Eq. 18: f_dot = sqrt(1+e cos f) for the ER3BP.

## 3. Analysis of the HFEM (pp.12-22)

- 3.1 (Eqs. 19 to 26): split rho_H'' = rho_C'' + rho_P,H'' + rho_A,H'' (CR3BP, pulsation, additional bodies). Eq. 20: the pulsation acceleration depends only on b4, b5 and b12a = -l''/(t'^2 l). Eqs. 21 to 23: after cancellations the additional-body term reduces to the "pseudo-gravity" expression of Eq. 23 plus small b6, b11, b12 - b12a terms. Assuming the Sun dominates, rho_S >> 1 and |z_S| << rho_S, Eq. 24:
  rho_A,L'' = (3 mu_S/rho_S^5)[2 x_S y_S y x_hat + (y_S^2 - x_S^2) y y_hat], i.e. Eq. 25: (3 mu_S/rho_S^3) y (sin 2 theta_S x_hat - cos 2 theta_S y_hat): linear in the y-excursion, direction angle -sgn(y) 90 deg + 2 theta_S (Eq. 26). Fig. 4 CAPTION: contour plots of rho_A,L, rho_A,H and the difference at two epochs (09/23/2023, theta_S about -105 deg; 10/07/2023, about 85 deg); the discrepancy is about 1e-4 n.d. at most, while the acceleration exceeds 0.01 n.d. in the L4,5 regions.
- 3.2 frequencies (Eqs. 27 to 40, Tables 3 and 4): nu_1* about 0.9915 (anomalistic, pulsation) and nu_2* about 0.9250 (synodic) in uniform time; one-frequency fits l ~ l0 - l1 cos theta_1, b4 ~ b40 - b41 sin theta_1, b5 ~ b50 + b51 cos theta_1, b12a ~ b12a0 - b12a1 cos theta_1 (2^20 samples over 1550 to 2500). Tables 3 and 4 (data file): Q0, Q1 and a maximum relative error E of 27 to 41 percent (uniform) and 26 to 39 percent (non-uniform); the claim is that the fit captures more than half of each time-varying component.
  Eq. 31: T(t) = integral of sqrt(l^3/(mu_E + mu_M)) dt, the map between times. In non-uniform time nu_1 = 0.9896 and nu_2 = 0.9234. The solar position approximates x_S ~ rho_S* cos theta_2, y_S ~ rho_S* sin theta_2 with theta_2 = -nu_2 t + theta_2,R and rho_S* about 389 (E 17 and 15 percent uniform, 10 and 9 percent non-uniform). Fig. 5 CAPTION: the (theta_1, theta_2) track over 25 years from 09/23/2000; apogee epochs lie near theta_1 = 180 deg, perigee near 0 deg (less reliably), epochs with y_S = 0 near theta_2 = 0 and 180 deg.
- 3.3 map of which force dominates (Eqs. 41 to 46, Figs. 6 to 8 CAPTION): pulsation acceleration magnitude C_P times in-plane speed with C_P = sqrt(b4^2 + (b5-2)^2) (Eq. 42); solar acceleration magnitude C_S |y| with C_S = 3 mu_S/rho_S^3 (Eqs. 43, 44); gamma = (|a_A| - |a_P|)/(|a_A| + |a_P|) (Eq. 45, +1 solar, -1 pulsation); Eq. 46 the Jacobi constant. Over 20 years from 09/23/2023: (C_S/C_P) between 0.17 and 0.97, so C_P usually larger. Figs. 7 (JC = 3.10) and 8 (JC = 2.50): the inner region near the primaries and near the x axis is pulsation-dominated, the L4,5 regions and slow motion near the Hill region are solar-dominated; the lunar vicinity is pulsation-dominated at every epoch because the solar term scales with |y|. Conclusion: for L2 halos (y-excursion below 0.15 n.d.) pulsation dominates, so the ER3BP is the better intermediate model.

## 4. Evaluation of the models (pp.22-27)

- 4.1 ER3BP (Eqs. 47 to 50, Figs. 9 to 12, Table 5): nu_E = 2 pi/t_E with t_E = integral over 0..2 pi of df/sqrt(1+e cos f) (Eq. 47), Fig. 9 nu_E against e; nu_E = 0.9994 at e = 0.055 (COMPUTED 0.99943), always below 1, against the HFEM nu_1 = 0.9896 (the Moon's perigee precesses, an effect the ER3BP lacks). Linearised ER3BP coefficients b4 ~ -(e/2) sin theta_E, b5 ~ 2 + e cos theta_E, b12a ~ -e cos theta_E (Eqs. 48 to 50) match the HFEM fits' form; matching Table 4 amplitudes suggests e between 0.05 and 0.055 (Peng and Bai 2018 used 0.0554). Epoch initialisation f_R = theta_1,R (Figs. 11, 12 CAPTION: b4 from the HFEM and ER3BP in step near the reference epoch and gradually dephasing); apsis shortcut: f_R = 180 deg at lunar apogee, less reliable at perigee.
  Table 5 (qualitative): ER3BP gives close estimates of the pulsation acceleration with a gradual degradation due to the frequency difference.
- 4.2 BCR4BP (Eqs. 51, Fig. 13, Table 6): rho_A,4'' = -(mu_S/rho_S,4^3) rho_S,4 - (mu_S/rho_Sc^3) rho_Sc (Sun on B plus Sun on the spacecraft). With rho_S,4 about 389, n_S about 0.0748, nu_4 = 1 - n_S about 0.9252 against the HFEM nu_2 about 0.9234. The BCR4BP acceleration grows radially from B while the HFEM one depends on |y|: the two disagree in the interior at every epoch because the BCR4BP is not coherent (its b7 to b12 are constants that do not cancel b1 to b3). Epoch initialisation theta_S,4,R = theta_2,R. Table 6: coherence issue, a fundamental limitation.

## 5. Application: L2 halo transitions (pp.27-35)

- Setup: Earth-Moon L2 southern halo family (Fig. 14 CAPTION: CR3BP orbits coloured by period; their 1-year HFEM analogs with only the Sun as extra body); the challenging region is periods 9 to 10 d. Fig. 15 CAPTION: two sample CR3BP halos with three-lobe counterparts in the intermediate models: the 3:1 sidereal halo (period 2 pi/3, about 9.1 d, one third of the ER3BP period) and the 3:1 synodic halo (period 2 pi/(3 (1 - n_S)), about 9.8 d, one third of the BCR4BP period). Counterparts by continuation in e and in mu_S (Ferrari and Lavagna 2018; Boudad et al. 2020).
- Corrector (Eq. 52): X_(i+1) = X_i - alpha J^T (J J^T)^-1 F, minimum norm, convergence |F| < 1e-10; 12 revolutions of the CR3BP orbit stacked from apolune with five segments per revolution and the baseline epoch at the apolune of the 7th revolution (4 revolutions for the ER3BP or BCR4BP orbit, since one of their revolutions equals three CR3BP ones); 300 epochs from 01/01/2000 sharing the same theta_1 (180 deg) or theta_2 (0 deg).
- Table 7 (parameters), Table 8 (3:1 sidereal ICs, CR3BP and ER3BP) and Table 9 (3:1 synodic ICs, CR3BP and BCR4BP) are in the data file. Newton-iteration count is the measure of closeness to the converged basin.
- 5.1 ER3BP results (Fig. 16): for every theta_2 the ER3BP guess needs consistently fewer iterations than the direct CR3BP guess (alpha = 1), the error |F| falls monotonically with the ER3BP guess but needs a few iterations to find the basin with the CR3BP guess; the transition depends mainly on theta_1. The ER3BP orbit predicts the three-lobe geometry the HFEM analog takes. The benefit is stated to hold for this region only (small y-excursion).
- 5.2 BCR4BP results (Figs. 17, 18): with theta_2 = 0 fixed and theta_1 varied, the BCR4BP guess is not better than the CR3BP guess (more iterations or failure, alpha = 0.5, cap 100), except near theta_1 about 0. Using the BCR4BP geometry with theta_1 = 0 epochs (inconsistent with the model but consistent with the dominant frequency) converges in fewer steps than the CR3BP guess, regardless of theta_2. The authors read this as the dynamics being governed by theta_1.
- Iteration counts are plotted only, not tabulated.

## 6. Concluding remarks (p.35) and appendices (pp.35-37)

The common-frame analysis separates pulsation from solar gravity, the two have different dominant frequencies, the ER3BP and BCR4BP are assessed against them, and the L2 halo example validates the approach for the chosen parameters; a first step toward assigning intermediate models by dominant frequency.
Appendix A (Eqs. A1 to A6): derivation of Eq. 22 from Eq. 21 by showing the cancellations of b1 to b3, b7 to b10 against the solar gradient term (the x-component of rho_A,H, b1, b7 - 1, b8, b9 expressed through mu_j, rho_jE, rho_jM). Appendix B (Eqs. B1 to B15): linearisation for rho_j >> 1 (1/rho_jc^3 ~ 1/rho_j^3 + 3 (x_j x + y_j y + z_j z)/rho_j^5, with Earth and Moon terms), giving Eqs. 10 to 12 and the final Eq. 24/25; b6, b11 and b12 - b12a linearised with the Moon's A_EM . z_hat and J_EM . z_hat expressions.

## 9. Relevance to this project

- **Model hierarchy as background (spec sec. 14 same-phase judgement and the #1007 ephemeris chains), nothing more.** The paper supplies a way to say when two ephemeris epochs are equivalent from the model's point of view: epochs with the same pulsation angle theta_1 and Sun angle theta_2 (Fig. 5, Eq. 10 of the later UTS paper) give the same ER3BP or BCR4BP phase, and for an L2 halo in the 9 to 10 d range the transition outcome depends mainly on theta_1. It does not address Earth-Venus-Mars,
  Earth-Mars or any heliocentric configuration; its "phase" is the Earth-Moon pulsation and solar angle, not a planetary alignment. The frequencies (nu_1 = 0.9896, nu_2 = 0.9234 in non-uniform time; 0.9915 and 0.9250 uniform) and the linear solar-perturbation law (Eqs. 24 to 26) are the reusable results.
- **Cycler status.** No cycler, resonance chain between bodies or repeated-encounter object. The 3:1 sidereal and 3:1 synodic "resonances" are ratios of a halo's period to the ER3BP sidereal (anomalistic) period or the BCR4BP synodic period of the Earth-Moon system, orbits around the Moon, with a single working moon. Not a repeated-moon paper.
- **Gate anchor.** There is no gate anchor for this paper; the existing Sanaga-Park-Howell anchor {model-transition} is the closest, and this paper's content supports the same label. If an anchor is added: primary Earth, body_set {Moon}, topology label {model-transition}, plus a periodic-orbit label for the 3:1 halos if the vocabulary allows (they are halo orbits, family L2 southern).
- **Sourced controls.** Tables 8 and 9 give four periodic orbits at seven digits that reproduce independently, usable as a CR3BP, ER3BP and BCR4BP periodic-orbit positive control (not needed by the cycler gates).

## 10. Cautions

- The ER3BP-benefit claim is for one region (small y-excursion, 9 to 10 d L2 halos) and one parameter set; the paper says it does not generalise to large y-excursion.
- The one-frequency fits explain only more than half of each time-varying component (E 26 to 41 percent).
- The transition metric is Newton iteration count; no solution-quality metric (distance from the CR3BP orbit, delta-V) is tabulated.
- The ER3BP equation of motion in the text layer is garbled in places (radicals and the f_dot definition); the reading above agrees with the closure check.
- "Tabulated" iteration counts for Figs. 16 to 18 do not exist; do not cite numbers from them.
