## 0. What this paper is, and the two answers it gives

A structural paper at mu = 0 with NO numerical table and no stability or mu > 0 computation. It builds a three-dimensional global section Gamma of the phase space (section 2.E) in which every periodic orbit and every arc is a curve ("characteristic"), classifies all solutions of the planar restricted problem at mu = 0 (fixed points, the circular families I_d and I_r, the rational tori D_N, the asymmetric arcs T_N and Henon's symmetric arcs S), and describes how the characteristics intersect. The only printed numbers are the eccentricity list of section 2.B, the parity table of section 3.D, the range of Henon's diagram, bounds such as N < 2 sqrt 2, and one local expansion (type III). The one place where it gives a formula that Bruno 1981 uses is section 4.C (collision velocity).

**Answer 1, Bruno 1981 eq. (1) against Henon 1968 eq. (27).** Not a slip. READ p.36, eq. (3.9): cos tau = eps a (cos eta - eps'' e), sin tau = eps eps' a sqrt(1 - e^2) sin eta, tau = a^(3/2) (eta - eps'' e sin eta), and the sentence in section 3.D: "Note that eps' and eps'' here are analogous to eps' and eps'' in Henon (1968) and Guillaume (1970), but our eps differs from the eps of Henon (1968) by the factor eps''." So Brjuno's eps (the same symbol as Bruno 1981) is eps_Henon x eps''. Check: Henon 1968 eq. 27, cos tau = eps_H a (eps'' cos eta - e), equals eps_H eps'' a (cos eta - eps'' e) because eps''^2 = 1, which is Brjuno's form. Brjuno's eps is also defined differently: eps = +1 if P2 and P3 are on the same side of P1 at the central instant (t0-tilde = 0), -1 if on opposite sides (t0-tilde = pi) (p.36, p.30-31), and the triplet of signs in his Figure 17 is "the signs of eps eps'', eps', eps''" (p.36), which is Henon's triplet (eps_H, eps', eps''). The elimination (3.10) is a = (1 - eps cos tau cos eta)/sin^2 eta, e = (eps'' cos eta - eps eps'' cos tau)/(1 - eps cos tau cos eta), and the timing equation (3.11) is (1 - eps cos tau cos eta)^(1/2) [eta (1 - eps cos tau cos eta) - sin eta (cos eta - eps cos tau)] - tau |sin eta|^3 = 0. COMPUTED consequence: Henon's eq. (30) with his product eps_H eps'' written as eps is identical to (3.11), and the two papers' tables agree. For A0 (eps_H = -1, eps'' = +1) the two eps coincide, which is why Bruno 1981's Table IV statement "eps = -1, eps' = -1, eps'' = 1" reads the same in both conventions. My earlier remark (Bruno 1981 digest, section 1.2) that eq. (1) was a slip was wrong. See the dated notes added to that digest.

**Answer 2, the W column of Bruno 1981 Table IV.** This paper does not define W; it appears only in Bruno 1981 (eq. 10 to 12). What this paper does give is the collision velocity (section 4.C, READ p.49): v1 = eps'' e sqrt(a) sin eta (the paper prints "e eps''"), v2 = c - 1 = eps' sqrt(a (1 - e^2)) - 1, V^2 = v1^2 + v2^2 = 3 - 2c - 1/a = 3 + 2H = 3 - C, incoming angle alpha with cos alpha = v1/V, sin alpha = v2/V, exit velocity (v1', v2') = (-v1, v2) for an S arc (alpha' = pi - alpha) and equal to the arrival velocity for a T_N arc. Those are exactly Bruno 1981 eqs. (3) and (4). I then solved for what the printed Table IV W column is: COMPUTED, W = (1/V^2)(V/sqrt(2 - 1/a) - 1) with V^2 = 3 - C, reproduces all 17 printed rows to within 5e-6 (the rounding of the 5-decimal print), including the first row (a = infinity, W = 0.09781) and the last (W = 0.25 at e = 0). The denominator sqrt(2 - 1/a) is the SIDEREAL speed at the collision (vis-viva at r = 1, sidereal radial plus tangential components; v1^2 + c^2 = 2 - 1/a), not the synodic radial speed |v1| of Bruno's eq. (12), whose denominator is sqrt(2 - 1/a + a e^2 - a) = e sqrt(a) |sin eta|. The two coincide at e = 1 only, which is why Table I (e = 1) agrees with eq. (12) to 7 digits while the Table IV column (e < 1) does not. So the printed Table IV W is the e = 1 formula of Table I evaluated with the true Jacobi-constant speed V, not eq. (12); which one is the physically correct first-order closest approach is a separate question (section 5.2). It was not defined in this paper.

## 1. Setting and notation (READ pp.9-13)

- Section 0 (pp.9-11): local and global sections. A characteristic of a family is the intersection of the family with a section hypersurface; at an "exceptional" point (the orbit tangent to the section) the characteristic of a nonsymmetric family is a parabola, and for a symmetric orbit with its point in the plane of symmetry pi there are two characteristics, one inside pi and one a parabola (Figure 0). Arcs are solutions that cannot be continued on either side (they end in collisions).
- Section 1 (pp.11-13): masses m1 = 1 - mu, m2 = mu, the unit of distance P1P2 and the angular rate of P2 equal to 1, origin at P1 (not the barycentre), sidereal Hamiltonian H1 = (1/2)(Y1^2 + Y2^2) - m1/r1 + m2 R1 (eq. 1.1), synodic H = H0 + mu R with H0 = (1/2)(y1^2 + y2^2) + x2 y1 - x1 y2 - 1/r1 (eqs. 1.3; READ text, the printed form has m1 = 1 - mu absorbed), momenta y1 = xdot1 - x2, y2 = xdot2 + x1 (1.4). The Jacobi constant is C = -2H (section 4.C, p.49).
- The program (p.12): periodic orbits of the two-body problem (section 2), then mu = 0 with collisions with P2 breaking solutions into arcs (sections 3 and 4); "the generating periodic solutions for mu = 0 (i.e. the limits of periodic solutions for mu > 0) are either periodic solutions or combinations of arcs." The follow-up papers (generating and generated periodic solutions) are announced and are the preprints Bruno 1981 lists.

## 2. Two-body problem and the section Gamma (READ pp.13-28)

- Sidereal: a = a, e = sqrt(1 - c^2/(a m1)), N = a^(-3/2) (eq. 2.4), n = N sgn c the mean angular velocity (direct c > 0, retrograde c < 0). Synodic mean motion n - 1; the synodic orbit lies in the ring a(1 - e) <= r1 <= a(1 + e) (2.6'). For rational N write N = |n| = (p + q)/p (and, for n < 0, N = (p + q')/p with q' = -(2p + q)), p > 0 and q coprime (2.7): the synodic orbit closes after q revolutions about P1 (n > 0) or q' (n < 0) and has synodic period 2 pi p. Figures 1 to 7: N = 3, 2, 5/3, 3/2, 4/3, 1, 1/2, with (p, q) = (1, 2), (1, 1), (3, 2), (2, 1), (3, 1), (1, 0), (2, -1) (READ p.14, eq. 2.8; the text layer is garbled here, the image is clear). In each figure a is fixed and e takes, picture number 1 and 9: 7/9, 2 and 8: 1/3, 3 and 7: 1/9, 4 and 6: 5/9, 5: 1 (n > 0 for pictures 1 to 4, n < 0 for 6 to 9, picture 5 the collision orbit made of p + q arcs).
- Phase-space structure at mu = 0 (section 2.C): D = bound solutions (a > 0); D+, D-, D0 by the sign of c; D_N the 3-dimensional varieties of rational N; the circular families I_d, I_r (e = 0), with period T = 2 pi |n - 1|^(-1) and trace Tr = 2 cos T (2.11); a fixed point at n = 1 (a = 1, e = 0). For 0 < |c| < sqrt(a) the invariant set is a 2-torus filled with periodic solutions of trace 2 when N is rational. "In D- minus I_r there are no trajectories whose synodical orbits close after one revolution" (q' <= -2).
- The plane of symmetry pi: x2 = y1 = 0 (orthogonal crossing of the x1 axis), coordinates x1 and z2 = y2 sgn x1 = c/r1 (2.15), and the global section Gamma: z1 = (x1 y1 + x2 y2)/r1 = 0 (2.18), the points where r1 is extremal, with coordinates (x1, x2, z2) or polar (r1, theta, z2); a and c depend only on r1 and z2 (2.19). Normalised coordinates ã, ẽ (2.20): ã = a-signed (ã = a for theta = 0, ã = -a for theta = pi in pi), ẽ = c/|c| times sqrt(z2^2 r1) (INFERRED from the garbled definition: ẽ^2 = z2^2 r1, so that, from (3.5), ẽ = eps'(1 + eps'' e) for an arc point). The set A = D intersect Gamma is |ẽ| < 2 (2.20, 2.21). The circular families I_d, I_r are the planes ẽ = +1 and -1 (exceptional sets). D_N is the circular cylinder a = N^(-2/3) (2.21), with 2(p + q) points on a rational periodic solution at angles theta_k = theta_0 + k pi q/(p + q) (2.22).
- The generic points (ã, ẽ) of Gamma and their meaning: eps = sgn ã, eps' = sgn ẽ, eps'' = +1 for |ẽ| > 1 (pericentre points) and -1 for |ẽ| < 1 (apocentre points) (READ p.36).

## 3. Arcs at mu = 0 (READ pp.28-45)

### 3.1 The collision equations (section 3.B, eqs. 3.1 to 3.7)

At mu = 0, body P2 does not attract P3 but collisions break two-body solutions into pieces; a piece beginning and ending at a collision is an arc; the set of arcs is C2 (collisions with P2) and C1 (with P1). Sidereal motion X1 = a (cos E - eps'' e), X2 = a sqrt(1 - e^2) eps' sin E, t - t0 = N^(-1)(E - eps'' e sin E) with N^(-1) = a^(3/2) (eps' = sgn n, eps'' = +1 for a pericentre start, -1 for apocentre; eq. 3.1, 3.2) and P2 at (cos t, sin t). A collision at time t1 = tau + t0 with eccentric anomaly eta: cos(tau + t0) = a (cos eta - eps'' e), sin(tau + t0) = a eps' sqrt(1 - e^2) sin eta, tau = N^(-1)(eta - eps'' e sin eta) (3.4). Two arc types:
- Case 1: both collisions at the same point of the sidereal orbit, so the two bodies complete whole revolutions between them and N is rational; the arc lies on a periodic trajectory of D_N and the set is T_N (asymmetric arcs).
- Case 2: the collisions at different points Q1, Q2, symmetric about the X1 axis; at (t1 + t2)/2 the three bodies are on the X1 axis; the synodic orbit is symmetric about x1 and the set is Henon's S. With t0-tilde = (t1 + t2)/2 = gamma_0 + 2 k pi, gamma_0 = 0 (P2, P3 on the same side of P1) or pi (opposite sides). The five quantities tau, eta, t0, a, e satisfy four equations, so one-parameter families.
- The arc's initial (apse) point lies in Gamma at a = a, theta = -t0, ẽ = eps'(1 + eps'' e) (3.5), and a collision needs a(1 - e) <= 1 <= a(1 + e) (3.6), which defines a body of revolution Omega in Gamma; Omega intersect pi has four domains omega_1 to omega_4 (Figure 18 boundaries; omega_1 and omega_2 touch at ã = 1, ẽ = +-1; omega_3 and omega_4 at ã = -1, ẽ = +-1). Eq. (3.7): 1 = a (1 - eps'' e cos eta).

### 3.2 The asymmetric families T_N (section 3.C, Figures 11 to 16)

For rational N the characteristic is drawn on the developed cylinder Delta_N (a = (p/(p + q))^(2/3) fixed). For a not equal to 1, eps'' e = (1 - 1/a)/cos eta (3.8), possible only for |cos eta| >= |1 - 1/a| (intervals J_k = [k pi - arccos|1 - 1/a|, k pi + arccos|1 - 1/a|]); each J_k with eps' = +-1 gives a closed characteristic chi_k T_N on the torus; 2(p + q) characteristics for N not equal to 1, one for N = 1 (with two self-intersections, at theta = 0, ẽ = 1 and theta = pi, ẽ = -1, the second an inflexion with common vertical tangent). N = 3 gives an empty Omega intersect Delta_3. Existence: "for each set D_N with a rational N < 2^(3/2) there is one connected family of arcs T_N" (p.32), the same bound j/i <= 2 sqrt 2 as Henon 1968 eq. 42 (N = (p + q)/p = j/i). The angle theta on a characteristic of T_N is the angle through which the symmetric synodic orbit of Figures 1 to 7 must be rotated to pass through (1, 0).

### 3.3 The symmetric families S (section 3.D, Figures 17 and 18)

Equations (3.9) to (3.11) as in the answer above. Henon solved (3.11) for tau < 5.5 pi and eta < 6.5 pi, Figure 17 (the same diagram as Henon 1968 Figure 4); Brjuno's Figure 18 plots the same families as characteristics in the (ã, ẽ) plane using "tables of the families S more detailed than in Henon (1968)" which Henon sent him (acknowledgement, p.50). Descriptions, READ pp.38-41:
- Every symmetric arc has exactly one point in the plane of symmetry pi. A_l characteristics lie in the half-plane ã < 0 in omega_3 and omega_4; each chi_0 A_l passes once through (ã, ẽ) = (-1, -1) with a vertical tangent and has ã <= -1 elsewhere; each branch oscillates between the limits of its domain going from ẽ = -1 to ẽ = -infinity, except the omega_4 branch of A_0, which tends monotonically to the asymptote ẽ = -2 and continues into the hyperbolic arcs of Henon's Table 1. In omega_3 the chi_0 A_l cross the vertical lines ã = -(2k)^(2/3), k = 1, 2, ..., only at boundary points. None passes through (-1, +1).
- B_l characteristics lie in omega_1 and omega_2 and pass through both (ã, ẽ) = (1, +1) and (1, -1), dividing each into two end parts and a middle part. For odd l the middle part lies in omega_2 with ã < 1 and the end parts in omega_1 with ã > 1 (cross the lines ã = (2k + 1)^(2/3) only at boundary points); for even l the middle part lies in omega_1 with ã < 1 and the end parts in omega_2 (lines ã = (k + 1)^(2/3)); the middle parts do not intersect each other and tend to the vertical ã = 1 as l grows.
- C_{p,p+q} characteristics are closed curves with |ẽ| < 1, each in one domain omega_i, according to (p mod 2, q mod 2) (READ p.40): omega_1: (0, 0); omega_2: (1, 0); omega_3: (1, 1); omega_4: (0, 1). Each touches the boundary of its domain at two points on the line ã = (p/(p + q))^(2/3). In omega_3 and omega_4 they intersect only each other; in omega_1 and omega_2 they also cross the B characteristics. The S characteristics are everywhere dense in the omega_i. (Consistent with Henon's eps eps'' = (-1)^(i+j) = (-1)^q for C_ij with i = p, j = p + q: eps = + for q even, which are omega_1, omega_2, the half-plane ã > 0.)

### 3.4 Intersection types of the plane characteristics (section 3.E, pp.41-45)

The intersection points (ã', ẽ') of characteristics of S in pi are of six types: I (interior point of omega_i with |ã'| = (p/(p + q))^(2/3) for coprime p, q, so the two-body orbit is periodic and its synodic orbit passes twice through (1, 0), splitting into two arcs L' and L''); II (boundary point of omega_i with |ã'| = (p/(p + q))^(2/3) not equal to 1, not on the P2 curve); II* (the same on the curve P2**); III (ã = ẽ = -1); III* (ã = 1, ẽ = -1); IV* (ã = ẽ = 1).
- Type I: tau' + tau'' = p pi and eta' + eta'' = (p + q) pi; with tau_{2l}', eta_{2l}' = l (tau' + tau'') + tau', l (eta' + eta'') + eta' etc., the solutions of (3.9) through that (ã, ẽ) are (tau'_{2l}, eta'_{2l}) and (tau''_{2l+1}, eta''_{2l+1}), l = 0, 1, 2, ..., "and there are no other solutions" (3.12); the arcs of S'_{2l} tend to (L'L'')^l L' (L''L')^l and those of S''_{2l+1} to L''(L'L'')^l L'(L''L')^l L'' (Figure 20 draws S''_1).
- Types II and II*: through each (tau_k, eta_k) = k (pi p, pi (p + q)), k = 1, 2, ..., pass two local families, only one of which (S_k) contains the orbit L described k times (arcs tend to L^k, Figure 22 for S_2 and S_3). This is exactly Henon 1968 special solution (40)/(41): tau/pi = i, eta/pi = j integers with the tangent ellipse a = (i/j)^(2/3), with i = k p, j = k (p + q) (the table's repeated rows C12 at (1, 2) and (2, 4) show k = 1 and k = 2). Characteristics there have a common tangent with the domain boundary and curvature that grows with k.
- Type III (READ p.44, eq. printed): near ã = ẽ = -1 the characteristics chi_0 A_k satisfy -ã = 1 + (1/2)(ẽ + 1)^4 + ((2k + 1)/32) (-1)^(k+1) pi (1 + ẽ)^5 + O((1 + ẽ)^6), k = 0, 1, 2, ...; all have contact of the fourth order with the vertical and differ in the fifth-order term; a >= 1 on them with a = 1 only at e = 0 (the arcs have tau and eta both tending to k pi + pi/2). The C families have a < 1 and approach but do not reach this point. COMPUTED (60-digit solves of (3.10), (3.11) near tau = eta = (k + 1/2) pi, e from 0.005 to 0.02): the fifth-order coefficient is confirmed in size and sign (the slope of (a - 1)/e^4 against e is +0.10 for k = 0, eps'' = +1, against pi/32 = 0.098; negative, about -0.27, for k = 1 against -3 pi/32 = -0.29), but the FOURTH-order coefficient is not 1/2: (a - 1)/e^4 tends to 0.0833 = 1/12 (0.08383, 0.08433, 0.08534 at e = 0.005, 0.01, 0.02 on one side and 0.08285, 0.08236, 0.08141 on the other, for k = 0; the two sides converge to 1/12). The printed 1/2 (read at 300 dpi, plainly "1/2") is therefore an error in the paper, apparently for 1/12. Check against Henon 1968 Table 2: at tau/pi = 0.6, 0.4 (e = 0.31069, 0.31255) a - 1 = 0.00057, 0.00122, in ratio to e^4: 0.061, 0.128, bracketing 1/12 with the 5th-order term +-0.1 e; with 1/2 they would be near 0.5 e^4 = 0.0047. Use 1/12.
- Type III*: near ã = 1, ẽ = -1, Delta ẽ = (-1)^(k+1) [Delta a - Delta a^2 + f(k) Delta a^3 + O(Delta a^4)], Delta ẽ = ẽ + 1, Delta a = ã - 1, f(k) > 0 and f(k) -> infinity as k -> infinity; tau and eta both tend to k pi. COMPUTED consequence: the first two terms are exactly 1 - 1/a expanded (from (3.7), eps'' e cos eta = 1 - 1/a = Delta a - Delta a^2 + Delta a^3 - ... with cos eta = (-1)^k at eta = k pi), so the given terms are the trivial part; f(k) is the first non-trivial number and is not given.
- Type IV*: the characteristics of B_k at ã = ẽ = 1 have different tangents (positive slope for k even, negative for k odd), tending to vertical as k grows; the arcs shrink to the point (1, 0), and tau, eta tend to tau_k, eta_k close to (k + 1/2) pi but not equal to it (see Henon's diagram).

## 4. Spatial characteristics (section 4, READ pp.45-49)

Not needed for the plane problem. After one apse passage the point in Gamma moves to theta = theta_0 + eps' n pi/N, a = a0, ẽ = eps'(1 - eps''_0 e_0) (4.1). The spiral cylinders pi_k: theta = k pi (1 - a^(3/2)) and its pi-shifted partner (4.2) carry the characteristics; an arc with eta between l pi and (l + 1) pi crosses the section at 2l + 1 apse points on pi_{-l}, ..., pi_l (4.4); on pi_k with k odd the picture is the mirror image of pi_0 about the lines ẽ = +-1 (4.3), and A_0, B_1 lose their pieces with eta/pi < 1 on pi_1, and disappear entirely on pi_2. Near the type I to IV* intersection points the spatial characteristics on pi_i(p+q) and Delta_N are classified (pp.47-49): for example S'_k has k + 1 characteristics near (ã', ẽ') and S''_k has k, S_k has exactly k near a type II point, T_1 gives two characteristics at types III and IV*, none at III*.

## 6. Consistency with the other papers

- Henon 1968: same equations (section 3.3), same families and naming; Brjuno uses a different sign switch (eps = eps_H eps'') and says so. Brjuno adds the classification of intersection points, the plane/spatial characteristics and the T_N asymmetric arcs, which Henon 1968 does not treat (his Section II case 1 is P = Q, "trivial", and case 2 is the rotation about PQ; Brjuno's T_N are the case-1 arcs, with one collision deleted from a rational periodic orbit; they form the p:q resonant seeds). The bound N < 2^(3/2) is Henon eq. 42.
- Henon 1997 (digest): the book's S-arc and (alpha, beta) integer-pair arcs are the same objects; T arcs are the book's "ordinary periodic orbits cut at the secondary" (not checked in the book beyond this identification, INFERRED).
- Hitzl & Henon 1977: critical orbits (extremal C) come from the same families; Brjuno 1978 gives no critical-orbit analysis (his later preprint 103/1978 does, listed in Bruno 1981 Appendix, not held).
- Bruno 1981: eq. (3) is 4.C of this paper (Bruno 1981 cites "part 4-B of Bruno (1972)": in this translation the section is 4.C, so the preprint's numbering differs). The two Bruno 1981 questions are settled above.
- Guillaume 1973, 1975, 1975a: this paper cites Guillaume's 1970 thesis and 1969 paper for the heliocentric formulation (section 1), the symmetry plane (section 2), and the bifurcation-orbit classification, which it says corresponds to its type I to IV* classification (bibliographic notes, p.50). Guillaume 1973's families I_d, I_r (circular, basic characteristics of the first kind) are the same sets as Brjuno's I_d, I_r (planes ẽ = +-1 in Gamma). Guillaume's conics S and symmetries (x(t* + t) = x(t* - t)) are the symmetric arcs here.
- Perko 1976: the generating arcs of his theorem are the S families (and composite chains) of this classification; Perko's statement of density of the (a0, e0) region a0(1 - e0) < 1 < a0(1 + e0) matches (3.6) and Brjuno's "everywhere dense" remark.

## 7. Techniques applicable to the project's problems

### 7.1 #899 (second-species continuation to Earth-Moon, Casoliva 2010 reproduction), and does this paper add arc types beyond Henon's enumerator?

Yes, one: the asymmetric arcs T_N (the resonant periodic orbit with one collision deleted). Henon 1968/1997 enumerate only the symmetric S arcs. At mu = 0 the T_N arcs are the cleanest p:q resonant skeletons: a = (p/(p + q))^(2/3) fixed, a one-parameter family in the phase of the orbit (theta along the characteristic), and the resonant (p-q) cyclers of Casoliva et al. at small mu are plausibly of this type (a closed resonant orbit crossing the secondary's orbit, with the secondary's phase as the free parameter; whether each Casoliva seed is a T_N or an S arc was not checked). For T_N the two ends of the arc are at the same sidereal point and the departure velocity equals the arrival velocity (section 4.C, p.49), so the demanded turn at mu = 0 is zero. So for #899: (a) the S enumerator from Henon covers arcs with a turn (alpha' = pi - alpha), (b) a T_N seed generator is a separate, simple piece (choose p, q, eps', eta in J_k with eps'' e = (1 - 1/a)/cos eta, from (3.8)) and gives the resonant Casoliva-type seeds without the timing equation; the S arcs at its Type II points (tau, eta) = k (pi p, pi (p + q)) are the same orbit described k times with an extra reflection and are the bifurcation seeds between the two classes. The mapping p, q (mean motion N = (p + q)/p, a = (p/(p + q))^(2/3)) to Casoliva's p-q labels must be checked in that paper before use (the 1968 digest flagged the same). The seeds the paper does NOT give: no numbers, no mu-correction (that is Perko 1976 and Bruno 1981 section 2).
