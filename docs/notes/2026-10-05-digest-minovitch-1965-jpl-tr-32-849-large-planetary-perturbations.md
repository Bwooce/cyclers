## 0. Verdict for `#942` R1

- This report contains NO periodic, repeating or cycler Earth-Venus or Earth-Venus-Mars trajectories.
- It is about single-encounter "postencounter" trajectories that use one planetary flyby, mostly Jupiter,
  to reach deep-space, solar-probe or out-of-ecliptic orbits.
- The Earth-Venus content is Table 18, "Earth-Venus-Deep Space trajectories for varying values of d" (Sect.
  III.B). Minovitch notes that their postencounter trajectories "all pass beyond the orbit of Mars. This
  means that trips to Mars can be carried out by using Venus to perturb the postencounter trajectory, such
  that it intercepts Mars (see Ref. 1)".
- Table 19 gives the Earth-Mars equivalents: "this planet does not have enough mass to produce good deep
  space trajectories".
- Section V: "The planets Mars and Venus can be used to obtain inclinations of about 10 to 15 deg" out of
  the ecliptic.
- The multi-flyby round-trip work (Earth-Mars with Venus, Mars, Earth, Mars, Earth and Venus flybys, as
  cited by Menning 1968) is the EARLIER report, Ref. 1: Minovitch, "The Determination and Characteristics
  of Ballistic Interplanetary Trajectories Under the Influence of Multiple Planetary Attractions", JPL
  TR 32-464, 31 Oct 1963. That report is the one relevant to R1, and it is NOT held.

## 1. Content summary (READ)

- Sect. I: launch-energy tables for the "high-energy missions" (Tables 1-4).
- Sect. II: the patched-conic flyby formulas for postencounter trajectories, given the two planets'
  positions and times (P1, T1; P2, T2) and the closest-approach distance d.
- Sect. III: deep-space postencounter trajectories, mainly Earth-Jupiter-escape (for example, 11 Oct 1978).
  Also Earth-Venus (Table 18), Earth-Mars (Table 19), and Earth-Moon-escape (Tables 20-24; the lunar flyby
  adds up to about 1.1 km/s to V_inf).
- Sect. IV: solar-probe postencounter trajectories via Jupiter.
- Sect. V: out-of-ecliptic trajectories via Jupiter (Earth-Jupiter 1967-1978, up to i = 90 deg, Table 39).
  Mars or Venus give 10-15 deg.
- Sect. VI: concluding comments. "Manned or unmanned missions to Mercury, Venus, or Mars are other examples
  where this approach can yield significant savings in launch energies"; and "In 1976 or 1977 high energy
  missions to Saturn, Uranus, Neptune, and Pluto could be carried out by means of a Jupiter flyby".
