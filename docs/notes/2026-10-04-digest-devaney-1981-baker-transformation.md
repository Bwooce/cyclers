# Digest: Devaney 1981, "The Baker Transformation and a Mapping Associated to the Restricted Three Body Problem"

R. L. Devaney, Communications in Mathematical Physics 80:465-476 (1981), DOI 10.1007/BF01941657 (received 4 December 1980; University of Maryland, then Boston University). Filed in the private paper corpus as `devaney-1981-baker-transformation-mapping-restricted-three-body-problem-cmp-80-465-doi-10.1007-BF01941657.pdf` (12 PDF pages, one per printed page 465 to 476, text layer good, formulas and the printed table-free statements read on page images).

This is the proof cited by Henon 2001 (chapter 18, Proposition 18.3.1) for the structure of the R-arc and R-orbit equations of type 2 bifurcations: digested in `docs/notes/2026-10-04-digest-henon-2001-generating-families-II-part-b-type-2.md` (sections 3 and 4; the R-arc and R-orbit tables and counts). Evidence tags: READ (printed page, equation read on the image), COMPUTED (my own run on 2026-10-04, scratch scripts not kept in the repository), INFERRED (my reading across sources).

## 1. What the paper does

A six-page core (pp.465-476 including figures and references) with no numerical table and no numbers other than the counts 2^n - 2 and the constants in the hyperbolicity bounds. It proves that the plane map

  F: x_1 = x_0 + 1/y_0, y_1 = y_0 - x_1 = y_0 - x_0 - 1/y_0     (abstract and p.465, p.467)

(Hénon's asymptotic form of the restricted problem; the book's own references are the unpublished "Notes on the restricted three body problem", mimeographed) is topologically conjugate to the baker transformation. F is real analytic and area preserving on R^2 minus the line y = 0 (singular set), with inverse (p.467) x_{-1} = x_0 - 1/(x_0 + y_0), y_{-1} = x_0 + y_0, undefined on y_0 = -x_0. Reading of the second formula: the abstract prints y_1 = y_0 - x_0 - 1/y_0, and the proof of Lemma 6 uses y_2 = y_1 - x_2, so y_{j+1} = y_j - x_{j+1}; I took both from the images.

Origin of the map (READ, p.465-466): the paper says only that it is "an asymptotic form of the equations of motion ... related to the ultimate behavior of families of periodic solutions as the mass-ratio tends to zero" and that Hénon had conjectured equivalence to the baker transformation from numerical work. There is no derivation from the restricted problem in this paper. The derivation is in Henon 2001 (chapters 17 to 22; part B digest section 2): the recurrence is the nu = 1/2 limit equations (18.1) after the scaling of 17.165 to 17.168. INFERRED identification, confirmed by direct comparison: with Henon's variables the encounter relation is y_i (x_{i+1} - x_i) = 1, i.e. x_{i+1} = x_i + 1/y_i, and y_i = y_{i-1} - x_i (18.1 and 18.6), identical to Devaney's F with (x_j, y_j) the same as Henon's (x_i, y_i). Henon's remark (p.196 of the book) that Devaney "uses x_i and y_i rather than y_i and y_{i+1} as coordinates" is consistent with this.

## 2. The coding and the baker map (READ pp.467-468)

For p in R^2 whose full orbit is defined, s(p) = (.. s_{-2}, s_{-1}, s_0; s_1, s_2, ..) with s_j = +1 if y_j(p) > 0 and -1 if y_j(p) < 0 (signs of y, not of x). Orbits that hit y_j = 0 going forward, or y_{-k} = -x_{-k} going backward, terminate; they receive terminating sequences written [0, s_{-j}, .., s_0; s_1, .., s_k, 0] (finite on either side or infinite on one side); the origin gets [0; 0]. The set Sigma is all such sequences except those ending (right or left) in an infinite string of +1's or of -1's. The map to the square is, with signs as digits:

  u = sum_{i=1..k} s_i / 2^i,   v = sum_{i=0..-k} s_i / 2^(1-i)

onto the open square |u|, |v| < 1 (the book prints "0 <= |u|, |v| < 1"; the boundaries are excluded, and the sequences ending in strings of +1 or -1 are identified with dyadic-rational terminating ones, "the usual identification which yields the dyadic rationals"). Terminating sequences [0; s_1..s_k, 0] map to v = 0, and [0, s_{-j}..s_0; 0] to u = 0. The shift sigma on Sigma (defined if s_0 is not 0) is "the well-known baker transformation" (refs. Moser, Arnold-Avez); it is a homeomorphism wherever it is defined, with range everything except sequences with s_1 = 0. The conjugacy diagram is F: R^2 minus {y = 0} -> R^2 minus {y = -x} with sigma: Sigma minus {s_0 = 0} -> Sigma minus {s_1 = 0}. Note the sign convention (digits plus or minus one, square (-1,1)^2) differs from Henon's 0 and 1 digits with v in (0,1) (book eqs. 18.64 to 18.71); the two are the affine maps u' = (u + 1)/2.

## 3. Results

- Theorem A (p.466): F is topologically conjugate to the baker transformation of the open square.
- Corollary B (p.466): periodic points are dense; **exactly 2^n - 2 fixed points of F^n for each n >= 1**; each periodic point is hyperbolic.
- Corollary C: a dense orbit exists (F is topologically transitive).
- Corollary D: the set S of points whose orbits terminate at the singular set is dense (though of measure zero).
- Open question (p.466): F preserves Lebesgue measure on the plane; the baker map is ergodic on the square with its Lebesgue measure, so the conjugacy induces a different, infinite measure on the square; ergodicity of F is left open.

## 4. The proof structure (READ pp.468-475) and its hypotheses

1. Non-uniform hyperbolicity (Proposition 1, p.469, read on the image). Sectors S^u = {xi*eta <= 0}, S^s = {xi*eta >= 0}; dF(S^u) in S^u, dF^{-1}(S^s) in S^s; for (xi, eta) in S^u at (x, y), ||dF(xi, eta)|| >= min(2, 1 + 2/y^2) ||(xi, eta)||; for (xi, eta) in S^s, ||dF^{-1}(xi, eta)|| >= (1 + 1/(x + y)^2) ||(xi, eta)|| (sup norm). The expansion rate tends to 1 as |y| goes to infinity (non-uniform).
2. Unstable and stable separatrices, strips and a horseshoe-type nesting argument (Propositions 2 and 3, Corollary 4, Lemma 5; pp.469-471): H and V nested families of stable and unstable strips indexed by finite sign strings; for every doubly infinite sequence that is not constant on a half-line (requiring s_i differs from s_j for some i, j > 0 and s_k differs from s_l for some k, l < 0) the intersection of nested compact sets contains a point; terminating sequences are handled separately (p.471).
3. Continuity of s at the singular set (Lemma 6 and the argument after it, pp.471-472): points near the x-axis are mapped arbitrarily far (x_1 > M and y_1 < -M if 0 < y_0 < epsilon), so a point near a terminating orbit has a sign sequence close to its terminating sequence.
4. Every orbit either changes the sign of y infinitely often or meets y = 0 (Lemmas 1 and 2, pp.472-473): an orbit with y_n > 0 for all n would need x_n increasing without bound with y_n bounded by y_0 - n x_0, or, if x_0 < 0, x_n larger than a partial sum of the divergent series sum 1/(y_0 - n x_0). This is why sequences ending in an infinite run of equal signs are disallowed.
5. Uniqueness (Lemmas 3 to 7 and the proof, pp.473-475): if two points have the same sequence, the straight segment joining them stays an unstable (or stable) curve under iteration, whose tangent cannot lie in the second quadrant after a sign change; Lemma 4 handles two-sided terminating sequences directly.

Hypotheses: the map is exactly the plane map above. Henon's equations reduce to it only in the range 1/3 < nu < 1/2 (system 18.1, no w term); for the transition 2.1 equation with w different from 0 the recurrence is different, y_{i-1} - 2 y_i + y_{i+1} = -2w/y_i + 2w^2, and Devaney's theorem does not apply to it; the book uses the conjugacy only for the w -> 0 limit, 18.3). INFERRED from the two statements; Henon's section 18.3 applies it to y_{i-1} - 2 y_i + y_{i+1} + 1/y_i = 0 (book eq. 18.59), which is Devaney's F in the coordinates (y_i, y_{i+1}) rather than (x_i, y_i). The theorem is topological: it proves existence and uniqueness of an orbit for each admissible sign sequence, not the location of the point or any smoothness of the conjugacy.

## 5. What it proves about the Henon 2001 counts

Dictionary (INFERRED, checked on the data below):
- An R-orbit of order n is a periodic point of F with period dividing n, written in Henon's cyclic variable y_0, .., y_{n-1}; Corollary B gives exactly 2^n - 2 fixed points of F^n; the two missing sequences are the constant-sign ones (all +1 or all -1), which the baker map excludes (they map to the corners of the square, the excluded boundary) and which Henon shows directly cannot occur (sum of 1/y_i = 0, 18.84). The count 2^n - 2 includes points of lower period dividing n and all n shifts of each orbit, which is how Henon counts (book section 18.2.3.5: 54 listed solutions with shifts plus 2 spurious sub-period-2 and 6 spurious sub-period-3 gives 62 = 2^6 - 2). So Devaney's Corollary B proves Henon's conjecture "exactly one solution for each choice of signs except the two constant sequences" (18.2.4), and Henon's maximal count 2^n - 2 from Bezout (18.2.1) is attained.
- Hyperbolicity (each periodic point hyperbolic) is Henon's Proposition 18.2.2 (R-orbits always unstable, z > 1, which Henon proved by a direct bound on the monodromy entries) for the same periodic orbits.
- An R-arc of order n-tilde, with y_0 = y_{n-tilde} = 0 and y_1 .. y_{n-tilde - 1} not zero, is an orbit that starts on the line y = -x (the left terminating end, y_1 = -x_1, where F^{-1} is undefined) and ends on y = 0: a two-sided terminating sequence [0; s_1, .., s_{n-tilde - 1}, 0] in Devaney's notation. Lemma 4 (a two-sided terminating sequence determines at most one point) gives uniqueness, and the existence argument for terminating sequences (p.471, "handled similarly") gives one point for each, so there are exactly 2^(n-tilde - 1) R-arcs, one per sign string, as Henon's Propositions 18.1.1 to 18.1.3. The existence part for two-sided terminating sequences is sketched rather than written out in the paper ("The other types of terminating sequences are handled similarly", p.471); Henon proves the R-arc count independently by the monotonicity table (18.1.2), so the two arguments are independent.

COMPUTED check of the dictionary (my solve of Henon's recurrence by multi-start Newton, not Devaney's construction): the cyclic recurrence y_{i-1} - 2 y_i + y_{i+1} + 1/y_i = 0 has exactly 2, 6, 14, 30, 62, 126 solutions for n = 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7 (after excluding the spurious solutions with all y_i huge and equal, which are the point at infinity of the homogeneous system, 18.21), each with a distinct sign sequence, which equals 2^n - 2. For n = 6, 7 this was 62 and 126 distinct sign sequences out of 64 and 128. The one-sided case was checked in the part B digest (Table 18.2: all 31 R-arc values reproduced, 2^(n-tilde - 1) solutions per order).

Two further COMPUTED checks on the map itself: (i) taking Table 18.3 row n = 4 (y_0..y_3 = 1.366025, 1.000000, -0.366025, 1.000000) and setting x_i = y_{i-1} - y_i, F carries (x_i, y_i) to (x_{i+1}, y_{i+1}) to better than 1e-5 at every step; the monodromy has determinant 1.0000000 (area preserving) and trace 146.0, so the orbit is hyperbolic with stability index z = 73.0 (Henon's z, half the trace); this is consistent with Corollary B and with Henon's Proposition 18.2.2 (z larger than 1) but the value of z is mine, not printed. (ii) Henon's n = 7 numerical orbit (book p.191: y_0 = 0.880142094, y_1 = 1.302150709, y_2 = 0.956199, y_3 = -0.435560, y_4 = 0.468576, y_5 = -0.761411, y_6 = -0.678047) satisfies the cyclic recurrence to 2.5e-6 and sum of 1/y_i = 3.3e-6 (the rounding of the six-digit entries), so it is a valid R-orbit with sign sequence +++-+-- ... as the book says, with no symmetry.

## 6. Printed numbers usable as sourced tests

The paper prints no table. What is testable:
1. Count: for each n at least 1, the number of period-n points (F^n fixed points, including lower periods) of F is 2^n - 2 (Corollary B); each is hyperbolic. For n = 1: zero fixed points (2^1 - 2 = 0): COMPUTED consistent, since F(x, y) = (x, y) needs 1/y = 0.
2. Inverse formulas: F^{-1}(x_0, y_0) = (x_0 - 1/(x_0 + y_0), x_0 + y_0), so F^{-1} composed with F is the identity wherever both are defined (identity test, area preservation: determinant of dF is 1: COMPUTED analytically, dF = [[1, -1/y^2], [-1, 1 + 1/y^2]] with determinant 1).
3. Proposition 1 bounds: ||dF v|| >= min(2, 1 + 2/y^2) ||v|| in the sup norm for v in the second or fourth quadrant and ||dF^{-1} v|| >= (1 + 1/(x + y)^2) ||v|| in the other two. Not verified numerically by me (I read them on the image). A numerical test of these two inequalities on random points is a cheap independent check of the stated sectors.
4. Sign-coding bijection: the number of finite terminating codes [0; s_1..s_k, 0] is 2^k, and one point of the line y = -x per code; compare Table 18.2 (2^(n-tilde - 1) per order).
5. Lemma 6 explicit inequality: if 0 < y_0 < epsilon and |x* - x_0| < epsilon with x* + 1/epsilon - epsilon > M then x_1 > M and y_1 < -M (a continuity bound near the singular set).
6. The sign-sequence orbit u(v) statement of Henon's section 18.3 (Fig. 18.1, v as a function of y_1 with devil's staircase values 1/8, 1/4, 3/8, 1/2, 5/8, 3/4, 7/8 at zeros of y_k) is an independent test of the coding with the book's 0 and 1 digits (not in this paper).

## 7. Techniques applicable to the project's problems

(a) `#899`, R-arc and R-orbit solvers with Henon 2001 Tables 18.2 and 18.3 as tests. Devaney adds a symbolic-dynamics enumeration that is better than the multi-start Newton I used for the check: since F is one-to-one and every admissible sign sequence has exactly one orbit, a solver can be built by **shooting on the coded boundary**, not by random starts. R-arc of order n-tilde with signs s_1 .. s_{n-tilde - 1}: pick x_1 and iterate x_{i+1} = x_i + 1/y_i, y_i = y_{i-1} - x_i with y_0 = 0 (so y_1 = -x_1); the baker coding says the end value y_{n-tilde} is a strictly monotone function (Henon's Table 18.1 and Proposition 18.1.1) of x_1 on each of the 2^(n-tilde - 1) intervals between poles, with exactly one root per sign string; bracket each root by the pole structure (the zeros of y_k as x_1 runs from -infinity to +infinity) and bisect or Newton. R-orbit: the periodic point of the baker shift with the binary periodic digit string has (u, v) of the form of repeating binary fractions of period n, but u and v are only a coding, not coordinates of the plane, so the plane point still needs a one-dimensional root find (on y_0 with y_1 solved by monotonicity: Henon's Table 18.4 shows that for fixed y_0, v is a continuous increasing function of y_1, so one bisects on y_1 until the first n sign digits equal the target string, then Newton-polishes against y_n = y_0, y_{n+1} = y_1). This makes the enumeration complete by construction, with a built-in count check (2^(n-tilde - 1) arcs, 2^n - 2 orbits, minus constant sequences) and no reliance on random starts, which can silently miss solutions for n of 8 or more (my random-start check needed 40 000 starts at n = 7). INFERRED design; not built. Precision caution from Henon: instability doubles errors per step, so use arbitrary-precision or at least extended precision for n above about 12 and restart each solve from the coded bracket.
Test data to use: Henon 2001 Table 18.2 (31 R-arc y_1 values), Table 18.3 (R-orbits n = 2 to 6, all rows satisfy the recurrence to 4e-6), the closed forms (n-tilde = 2: 1/sqrt(2); n-tilde = 3: 1 and 1/sqrt(3); the n = 5 polynomial 5 y^6 - 20 y^4 + 17 y^2 - 4 = 0), plus Devaney's counts above (2^n - 2 for n = 2..7 verified here), area preservation (determinant 1) and the inverse formulas. Devaney adds no new numbers beyond the counts; its value is the proof that the count is exact and the coding is a bijection, which makes "all solutions found" decidable.

(b) `#906`: nothing direct. It confirms that the sign sequence of y_i (the relative side of passage) is a complete invariant of an R-arc or R-orbit, so the gate's "side of passage" report is a faithful label for these chains.

(c) `#928`: the singular line y = 0 of F is the near-collision singularity of the regularised-time problem; Devaney's Lemma 6 (points near y = 0 are mapped arbitrarily far in a way fixed by the sign of y_0) is the discrete analogue of the sign of a near-collision flyby and the reason a plain integrator fails near the singular set: no new tool, but a statement of the sensitivity that a regularised propagator test should reproduce.

## 8. Limits of the paper

- Topological, not metric: no statement on the invariant measure, ergodicity, Lyapunov exponents (only the sector bounds of Proposition 1 with a rate tending to 1 for large |y|), nor on the speed of equidistribution.
- It concerns F with no parameter. The dependence on w and on the sign convention appears only through Henon's scalings, so it says nothing about the existence or counts at w different from 0 (Henon's Table 19.1 and 20.1 junctions are separate, found by his positional method).
- Existence for the terminating (R-arc type) sequences is sketched, not written out.
- No numerical example or table anywhere; the paper's four references that are not standard texts are Henon's unpublished mimeographed notes, Katok and Strelcyn (to appear), Pesin 1976, and Smale 1965.
