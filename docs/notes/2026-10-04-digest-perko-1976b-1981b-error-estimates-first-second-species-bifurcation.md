## Part D: Perko 1976b, "Application of singular perturbation theory to the restricted three body problem"

Rocky Mountain J. Math. 6(4):675-696 (Fall 1976), DOI 10.1216/rmj-1976-6-4-675. Filed in the private paper corpus as `perko-1976b-singular-perturbation-theory-restricted-three-body-problem-rocky-mountain-j-math-6-675-doi-10.1216-rmj-1976-6-4-675.pdf` (22 pages; PDF page n is journal page 674 + n; a text layer with garbled mathematics, so formulas were read from images).

### D1. What it is (READ pp.675-696)

A survey with proofs sketched, by the author of the 1964 thesis and the 1967 error-estimation paper (SIAM J. Appl. Math. 15:738-753, cited as [28]). Abstract: it "describes how the theory of singular perturbations can be applied to establish the existence and asymptotic approximation of those solutions of the restricted three body problem with small mass ratio mu > 0 which reach an O(mu) neighborhood of the perturbing body", and how this gives the periodic second species solutions. Its value for the project is that it states, in one place, the remainder estimates that the earlier parts (A, B, C) take for granted ("error estimates ... given by Perko (1976)" in Part A).

### D2. The estimates (READ pp.676-682)

Setting: inertial Earth-centred frame, outer expansion x = sum_{j=0..N} mu^j x_j(t) + r_N(t, mu) with x_0 the two-body orbit and x_j from the linear equations (7); the Moon-passage time t1 where x_0(t1) = x_m(t1) with relative velocity V1 = xdot_0(t1) - xdot_m(t1) not zero; initial variations of order mu.
- Outer terms (eq. 8, p.677): x_0(t) - x_m(t) = -V1 tau + O(tau^2) and x_1 = (i/V1^2) ln(tau_0/tau) + x_1b(t) with tau = t1 - t, tau_0 = t1 - t0, and x_2 = (i... /V1^2)(1/tau)[ln(tau_0/tau) + a_1 + ...] + x_2b: the logarithm in x_1 and the 1/tau growth in x_2 are the singular behaviour near the Moon.
- Theorem 1 (p.677): the x_j satisfy |x_1(t)| <= C_1 |ln tau| e^(c0 (t - t0)) and |x_j(t)| <= C_j |ln tau|^(j-1)/tau^(j-1) e^(c0 (t - t0)) for j at least 2 (printed bound read as in the text layer; the powers of 1/tau grow with j).
- Theorem 2 (p.679, read on the image): for all N at least 2 and every epsilon above 0 there is mu_1 such that for mu in [0, mu_1] the solution exists for t in [t0, t1 - mu^(1 - epsilon)] and
  |r_N(t, mu)| <= C_N mu^N |ln tau / tau|^(N - 1), |rdot_N(t, mu)| <= C_N mu^N |ln tau|^(N - 1)/tau^N.
  So the outer expansion is valid until the distance to the Moon is about mu^(1 - epsilon) (in time), and its error grows like mu^N/tau^(N-1) toward the passage.
- Inner expansion (section 3, pp.679-681): inner variables Y = (x - x_m)/mu, s = (t - t_p)/mu, Y_0 a Moon-centred hyperbola (eq. 13); Theorem 3 (p.680): |R_N| = O(mu^(N-1-epsilon)) and |R_N'| = O(mu^(N-1-epsilon)) hold at s = 1/mu^(1/2) imply |R_N(s, mu)| <= C_N mu^(N-1-epsilon) for s in [-1/mu^(1/2), 1/mu^epsilon] (text layer; the case N = 2 is carried out in the 1964 thesis). In outer variables the inner expansion has error O(mu^(N - epsilon)) in position and O(mu^(N-1-epsilon)) in velocity on [t_p - mu^(1/2), t_p + mu^(1 - epsilon)] (p.681).
- Matching (section 4, pp.681-682): second-order matching determines the Moon-centred hyperbola to within O(mu^(3 - epsilon)):
  (15a) Delta = mu j . x_1b(t1) + mu^2 j . x_2b(t1) - (mu^2/V1)[j . xdot_1b(t1)][i . x_1b(t1)] + O(mu^(3 - epsilon)),
  (15b) t1 - t_p = -(mu/V_inf) i . x_1b(t1) + (mu/|V_inf|^3)[ln(2 V_inf^2 tau_0/(mu e_1)) - 1] + (mu^2/V1^2)[j . xdot_1b][i . x_1b] + mu^2 i . x_2b(t1) + O(mu^(3 - epsilon)),
  (15c) V_inf = V1 + mu xdot_1b(t1) + mu^2(...) + O(mu^(3 - epsilon)),
  with e_1 = [1 + (V_inf^2 Delta/mu)^2]^(1/2); the second-order V_inf terms are "too lengthy to include" and are in Breakwell & Perko 1974. x_1b(t1) = Phi_rr(t1, t0)(dx0/mu) + Phi_rv(t1, t0)(dxdot0/mu) + integral of b_1(t1, t) dt with b_1(t, t') = Phi_rv(t, t') g[x_0(t'), t'] - i (t - t')/(V1^2 tau^3?) (printed as i(t - t')/(V1^2 (t1 - t')^2)); b_1(t1, t1) = 0.
- Theorem 4 (p.682, read on the image): with the non-collision condition |j . Phi_rr(t1, t0) dx0 + j . Phi_rv(t1, t0) dxdot0 + mu integral j . b_1(t1, t) dt| at least mu d0, for t in [t0, t1 + mu^(1 - epsilon) k0] the solution exists, is analytic in its variables and "is approximated uniformly on this interval to within an error of O(mu^(3 - epsilon)) by the outer expansion (6) with N = 5 on [t0, t1 - mu^(1/2) k0] and by the inner expansion (12) with N = 3 on [t1 - 2 k0 mu^(1/2), t1 + mu^(1 - epsilon) k0] provided eta_0 is a Moon centred hyperbola with the parameters given by equations (15)". Note p.683: "The appearance of O(mu^(3 - epsilon)) estimates is due to the fact that there are terms of O(mu^3 |ln mu|) in the error term."
- Lemma (p.685): if z(a, mu) = z_0(a, mu) + O(mu^(2 - epsilon)), z_0(a0, mu) = 0 and dz_0/da at least k_1 above 0, then there is a root a* = a0 + O(mu^(2 - epsilon)). This is the intermediate-value lemma that every periodic-orbit existence proof of Parts A to C uses.
- Theorems 5 to 7 (pp.685-689): existence of a unique time t* of closest approach with rho . rhodot = 0 and |rho| minimal (t* = t_p + O(mu^(2 - epsilon))); a distance Delta* = mu tan(gamma_1)/V_inf^2 + O(mu^(2 - epsilon)) giving a second perpendicular crossing; Theorem 7 gives the type A periodic family with a21 dx0 + a24 dxdot0 = mu[-tan(gamma_10)/V1^2 - j . (...)] + O(mu^(2 - epsilon)) (the same as Part A, eq. 2.6).
- Section 6 (pp.689-690): the loss-of-order rule, verbatim: "since the angle through which the velocity vector turns at a near-moon passage beta = 2 Tan^(-1)(mu/(Delta V_inf^2)) it follows that if Delta is determined to Nth order, then beta is only determined to (N - 1)st order; i.e., an order of accuracy is lost with each near-moon passage. And since it is necessary to know beta to O(1) in order to establish a second perpendicular crossing ..., it follows that to establish the existence and 1st order asymptotic approximation of a second species periodic solution with n near-moon passages, it is necessary to carry out (n + 1)st order asymptotic matching over the first arc, (n)th order matching over the second arc ..."
- Section 7 (pp.690-691): Guillaume's extension to variations of order mu^a, 1/3 <= a <= 1; for a = 1 the deflection is O(1), for 0 < a < 1 it is O(mu^(1 - a)) tending to zero: "two entirely different types of limiting orbits as mu tends to 0 for a = 1 and for 0 < a < 1 ... Guillaume in [12, p. 254] refers to this phenomena as a second species bifurcation." Also quotes Guillaume's local form at a type-A intersection (eq. (*), from Guillaume's 1971 thesis, eq. IV-13, p.114): "(W_0 delta a_0 + W_1 delta e_0)(...) = ..." a quadratic form that "describes a hyperbola with the tangents to the characteristics of the generating ellipse at the bifurcation point as asymptotes", compared with Deprit's numerical Hecuba-gap and Hilda-group computations (Guillaume Fig. IV-8). The right-hand side and the coefficients are not printed. Section 8 (p.691): "the restricted three body problem with |x - x_m| = O(mu^a) has the form of a singular perturbation problem only if a > 1/3; i.e., a = 1/3 is the lower limit for which singular perturbation theory can be, or need be for that matter, applied."
- Historical remark (p.693): "no numerical study of second species solutions with near-moon passages ... has been made due to the difficult nature of this problem".

## Part E: Perko 1981b, "Periodic orbits in the restricted problem: an analysis in the neighborhood of a 1st species-2nd species bifurcation"

SIAM J. Appl. Math. 41(1):181-202 (August 1981; received 15 August 1979, revised 31 July 1980), DOI 10.1137/0141013. Filed as `perko-1981b-periodic-orbits-restricted-problem-first-second-species-bifurcation-siam-j-appl-math-41-181-doi-10.1137-0141013.pdf` (22 pages; PDF page n is journal page 180 + n; text layer, formulas and digits read on the images of pp.184, 187, 188, 197, 198, 202).

### E1. What it establishes

- Abstract: "The existence and first order asymptotic approximation of two one-parameter families of periodic orbits in the neighborhood of a 1st species-2nd species bifurcation orbit are established for small values of the mass ratio mu > 0." Henon's three types (p.182, from Henon 1979 "in preparation"): type 1, an ellipse of rational mean motion meeting the unit circle at two points (treated in the 1977 and 1981 Celest. Mech. papers, Parts C and B); type 2, an ellipse of rational mean motion tangent to the unit circle (this paper); type 3, the retrograde circular orbit of radius one (this paper).
- Distance: "in which case the minimum distance to the moon d(mu) = O(mu^(1/2))" (p.181), and a bridge: d(mu) = O(mu^nu), 0 < nu < 1, between the first species d(mu) tending to d0 above 0 and the second species d(mu) = O(mu). Guillaume (1969, 1971, 1973) treated first species-first species and, without an existence theorem, first species-second species bifurcations.

### E2. The example (type 3, family m) and the numbers that are printed (READ pp.182-185)

The bifurcation is the junction of the family I_r (retrograde circular orbits around both primaries) with the second species family A_0 of Henon's consecutive-collision orbits, in the family m of Stromgren (family F of Broucke 1968). Figs. 1 and 2 (pp.183-184) sketch family m for mu = 0, mu = 0.012... (Broucke, "mu = .012...", JPL TR 32-1168 p.64; Szebehely p.505), mu up to 0.5 (Stromgren) and show the bifurcation point at (x0, C) = (-1, -1) in the (x0, C) plane (the axes of Fig. 2 are -x0 and -C with the tick 1.0 at the crossing, read on the page image). The characteristics of family m for small mu "pass through an O(mu^(1/2)) neighbourhood of the bifurcation point". There is a complementary family, h of Stromgren (A of Broucke), through the same neighbourhood. Printed numerical content: the point (-1, -1) and the statements above; the curve equations for I_r and A_0 are given by reference only ("the initial conditions defining the family I_r are given by" a formula lost from the text layer; A_0 by Henon 1968 eq. (30), tabulated there pp.394-402). COMPUTED: the retrograde unit circle has C = -1 (C = 2 sigma1 sqrt(a(1 - e^2)) + 1/a with sigma1 = -1, a = 1, e = 0), x0 = -1, and a Moon-relative speed V1 = sqrt(3 - C) = 2, so V1 = 2 for this bifurcation orbit.
Claim of the paper that the generating family structure of m and h is entirely first kind, second species or first species-second species bifurcation orbits (p.201), so that its theory plus Poincare/Arenstorf and Perko 1974 gives "a complete existence and approximation theory for any family which contains only 1st and 2nd species orbits and 1st species-2nd species bifurcation orbits".

- Lemma 1 (p.187): |R_1(t)| <= c_1 |ln mu| on [0, t1 + epsilon].
- Theorem 1 (p.188, eqs. 18 and 19, read on the image): |r - R_0 - mu R_1| <= c_2 mu^2 |ln mu|^2 / max[|t1 - t|, epsilon], and |rdot - Rdot_0 - mu Rdot_1| <= c_2 mu^2 |ln mu|^2 / max[(t1 - t)^2, epsilon^2], for t in [0, t1 + k0 epsilon]; "The error estimates in Theorem 1 can be improved so that they contain only a first power |ln mu|-term" (p.191).

COMPUTED scales at mu = 0.0121529529 (distances in units of the Earth-Moon distance 384,400 km, time unit 1/omega = 27.3217 d/(2 pi) = 4.348 d; the constants c_0, c_2 are not given, so these are scales): delta = mu^(1/2) = 0.1102 (42,400 km; in time 0.479 d) for every nu at or below 1/2, and delta = mu^nu above 1/2; delta^3 |ln mu|^2 = 0.00134 x 19.45 = 0.0261 (about 10,000 km); mu^2 |ln mu|^2/epsilon at epsilon = mu^(1/2) is the same 0.0261. So the a priori error scale of the matched hyperbola at the Earth-Moon mass is of order ten thousand kilometres in position, before the unknown constant: first-order matching can place the periapsis only to that accuracy, and is not an engineering-grade predictor at mu_EM (INFERRED reading of the scale; the paper states no numerical error).

### E4. The existence theorem and the hyperbola (READ pp.194-199)

- Theorem 4 (p.195, text layer checked on the image of p.197): let r_0(t) be a consecutive-collision orbit tangent to the Moon's circle at t1 (type 2; for type 3 the same with the unit circle), V1 not zero, perpendicular crossing at 0, and a_ij the components of the transition matrix Phi(t1, 0) R in (i, j) coordinates (i along V1, j perpendicular, oriented so the Moon's motion is direct; R a rotation through angle alpha[r_0(0), V1], which is plus or minus pi/2 for tangency). Then, with d_1 = a44 a21 - a24 a41:
  (1) nu = 1/2: for (1/2) k0 mu^(1/2) <= |a21 dr + a24 dv| <= 2 k0 mu^(1/2) there is a one-parameter family of periodic solutions, period 2 t_p, with (eq. 50, read on the image of p.198) (a21 dr + a24 dv)(a41 dr + a44 dv) - mu/V1 = 0, solved to O(mu^(3/2)) for dr(dv, mu) (if a21 a44 ... condition (51)) or dv(dr, mu) (condition (52)); the variation that is the parameter is of order mu^(1/2).
  (2) 0 < nu < 1/2: a41 dr + a44 dv = 0 to leading order and the product of the parameter with the other variation equals mu/(V1 d_1) + O(mu^(1+nu)) (text layer, signs not rechecked): the parameter dr_0 or dv_0 is of order mu^nu, the partner of order mu^(1-nu).
  (3) 1/2 < nu < 1: a21 dr_0 + a24 dv_0 = 0 to leading order and the corresponding product is of order mu with error O(mu^(2-nu)).
- Theorem 4' (p.197, read on the image): in the variables beta = alpha[V_inf, V_inf+] (the deflection) and eta = j_inf . Delta (Delta the directed distance to the incoming asymptote, j_inf the unit vector perpendicular to V_inf), for a21 and a24 not zero, beta in [(1/2) k0 mu^(1-nu), 2 k0 mu^(1-nu)], 0 < nu < 1, there is a one-parameter family with
  beta eta = -2 mu / V1^2 + O(mu^(1+nu), mu^(2-nu)),   (eq. 46)
  which is exactly the small-angle form of the hyperbola relation tan(beta/2) = mu/(V_inf^2 sigma Delta) (eq. 48 reads beta/2 = -arctan(mu/(V_inf^2 sigma Delta))). "Each branch of the hyperbola (46) (for k0 above 0 and below 0 respectively) approximates the characteristic of one family of periodic orbits. Theorem 4' therefore establishes the existence of exactly two one-parameter families of periodic orbits, for each sufficiently small mu above 0, generated by each 1st species-2nd species bifurcation orbit r_0(t) of type 2 or type 3 for mu = 0." A technical hypothesis: a21 V1 beta not equal to 2 a41 eta (excludes one point of each branch when a21 a41 is negative, as for a retrograde ellipse tangent on its inside).
- Statement on the transition matrix (p.197): "It follows from well-known formulas for the components of the transition matrix (cf., e.g., [3]) that a21 a24 not zero for 1st species-2nd species bifurcation orbits of the type being considered here" ([3] Deprit & Deprit-Bartholome 1969, Bull. Astron. 3:315-339, matrizants of the Keplerian motions, two-dimensional case; not held).
- Corollary (p.200): the side of passage is invariant: r(t_0, mu) minus 1 is not zero during a bifurcation (the distance to the Moon at the perpendicular crossing stays positive), with sign(beta) = -sign(eta) as a partial converse "established by Henon (in a private communication)".
- The bridge (pp.200-201): fixing a beta interval, Theorem 4' overlaps in domain of validity with the second species theory (the characteristic of Perko 1974 for a fixed beta, eq. 58 of the paper, which has the form eta = -mu cot(beta/2)/V_inf^2 up to the printed second-order term) and, for d0 above 0, with the first species theory, so a family containing only first and second species orbits and bifurcations has "a complete existence and approximation theory". It also introduces "orthogonal characteristics" in the (x0, C) plane and a definition of the species of a periodic orbit at mu above 0 through them (p.201), with Henon's reservation that it depends on the coordinates used.

### E6. Does either paper give the coefficients Guillaume 1973's local hyperbola and cubic need?

Context (from the Guillaume 1973 digest): his eq. (2) is a hyperbola f1 f2 = (constant) mu in the (x0, C) plane at the type 3 junction of I_r and A_0 at C = -1 (his example 1), and eq. (4'') is a cubic at a bifurcation with three basic characteristics (his example 2); the coefficients and the normalisation of f1, f2 are missing from the 1973 paper, and the digests of Guillaume 1975 and 1975a say they are not there either.
- The hyperbola (example 1): YES, in a different but equivalent form, from Perko 1981b. At a type 2 or 3 bifurcation orbit the local characteristic is (eq. 50) (a21 dr + a24 dv)(a41 dr + a44 dv) = mu/V1 in the variables (dr, dv) of a perpendicular crossing, to O(mu^(3/2)), the two linear factors being the tangent lines to the two generating characteristics (a21 dr + a24 dv = 0: the line Delta = 0, a collision at t1, i.e. the second species characteristic; a41 dr + a44 dv = 0: zero deflection, V_inf parallel to V1, i.e. the first species characteristic) and the right-hand side a pure constant mu/V1. The a_ij are the Kepler transition matrix Phi(t1, 0) R, which the project can evaluate from its two-body state transition matrix with V1 = 2 for the retrograde unit circle (a21 and a24 are not zero by Perko's statement). To turn it into Guillaume's (x0, C) form one needs the linear map from (dr, dv) to (dx0, dC) at the bifurcation point (the derivative of the Jacobi constant with respect to the initial velocity at the perpendicular crossing, which follows from the Jacobi integral at that point), which Perko does not print. So the coefficient of eq. (2) is determined, but only after a computation the project must do; it is not printed numerically in either Perko paper.
- The cubic (example 2): NO. Perko 1981b treats type 2 and 3 bifurcations that have exactly two families; Perko 1976b section 7 only quotes Guillaume's quadratic form without coefficients; Perko 1977 and 1981 (Parts C and B) treat the type 1 hyperbola (eq. 9 there) with two near-Moon arcs. Nothing in the five Perko papers gives a cubic with three asymptotic characteristics or its coefficients. INFERRED: the cubic is the case where three basic arcs meet, which Henon 1979 (cited, "in preparation") says needs the quantitative approach beyond Broucke's principle for more than three basic arcs.
- What 1976b adds for the coefficient question: nothing numerical; it gives the remainder estimates and the loss-of-order rule.

## Techniques applicable to the project's problems

### `#899` (second species seeds and continuation to the Earth-Moon mass)
1. The seed-quality scale. Parts D and E give the remainder of the matched approximation: the hyperbola parameters are accurate to O(delta^3 |ln mu|) with delta = max(mu^(1/2), mu^nu); at mu_EM that scale is about 0.026 Earth-Moon distances before the unknown constant (COMPUTED above). So a Barrabes-Gomez or Perko seed at the physical mass is a start for a corrector, not an orbit, and the project's pre-registered pass test for P2 (seven of nine Table 3 rows) should count a seed as "reached" only after correction. The three-step continuation of Casoliva (mass, then C, then mass) is then the correction path the error scale justifies.
2. The sqrt(mu) rule for where a flyby leaves the second species description: d(mu) = O(mu^(1/2)) at a bifurcation (0.110 lunar distances at mu_EM). A candidate whose periapsis is about 0.1 lunar distances or more at the first-species/second-species junction is in Perko's bridge regime (Theorem 4'), where the right local model is the hyperbola (46), and the second species O(mu) formulas do not apply.
3. A branch-selection rule for the continuation driver: at a type 2 or 3 junction exactly two families exist, one per branch of the hyperbola (46), and beta and eta have opposite sign (Corollary; sign(beta) = -sign(eta)); the side-of-passage invariant tells which branch continues. This is the "policeman" of Guillaume and the missing branch rule noted in the Guillaume 1973 digest.
4. A direct control for the driver: the avoided-crossing constant (50), (a21 dr + a24 dv)(a41 dr + a44 dv) = mu/V1, can be tested by continuing a family through the type 3 junction at C = -1 at two or three values of mu below mu_EM and comparing the fitted constant with the prediction from the Kepler transition matrix (not run; this is the test the Guillaume digest asked for).

### `#906` (turn gate)
- The loss-of-order rule (1976b section 6) is a statement about the accuracy with which a turn beta can be predicted from Earth-side initial conditions: each near-Moon passage costs one order, so for a chain with n passages a gate that uses first-order matching to predict beta has an error of order mu^(1 - epsilon) per passage after the first, relative to an O(1) turn, and for a small turn (nu above 0) the relative error is larger. At mu_EM and a chain of two or more passages the predicted turn is not reliable to better than a few percent times unknown constants; the gate should use the integrated turn (rotating-frame velocities), not a matched prediction, and treat the prediction only as a seed. 
- The identity (46) and Theorem 4' show that near a bifurcation the demanded turn and the miss distance are tied: beta eta = -2 mu/V1^2. A gate that sees a small demanded turn can test whether the matching miss distance is consistent with (46); an inconsistency flags a mis-measured velocity (inertial instead of rotating, Bolotin-MacKay's slip) rather than a physical case. INFERRED use.
- The side-of-passage corollary reinforces the proposal in the Hénon digest: a zero or small turn at a first species-second species bifurcation is legitimate, with an invariant sign; record the sign.

### `#928` (regularised propagator and transition matrix near a close approach)
- The error estimates say where the variational (outer) description fails: the first-order term R_1 has a logarithm in the time to closest approach and the second-order term grows like 1/tau (1976b eq. 8), so a state transition matrix propagated through a close passage by the outer (Kepler) variational equations is not valid within about delta = max(mu^(1/2), mu^nu) of the Moon in time (about 0.48 d at mu_EM for delta = mu^(1/2), COMPUTED); inside that window the Moon-centred hyperbola (inner solution) must be used, and the two are matched at the edge. For a regularised STM (`#928`) this gives a ready design: use the outer Kepler STM to t1 minus delta, the hyperbola with parameters (Delta, V_inf, t_p) (eqs. 15, or 26-28) across, the outer STM after, and compare the product with the regularised variational integration; the differences should scale like delta^3 |ln mu|, a quantitative test of the regularised code, with the scale constant fitted. Guillaume 1975a's remark (digest) that regularisation does not remove the singularity of the outer expansion agrees: regularisation fixes the integration, not the asymptotic non-uniformity.
- The transition matrix components a_ij of Phi(t1, 0) R are the objects Perko uses; the project's `core` two-body STM is the source (INFERRED; not checked against the code here).

Note 2026-10-05 (Breakwell & Perko 1974 now held; `docs/notes/2026-10-04-digest-breakwell-perko-1974-second-order-matching.md`): the "about 10,000 km" scale in section E3 and in the `#899` technique 1 comes from the crude uniform bound delta^3 |ln mu|^2 of Theorem 2 of Perko 1981b, which is for the nu-family regime (variations of order mu^nu near a bifurcation), and carries an unknown constant.
