## 2. What the new papers say is NOT done (the raw material)

**Heliocentric, early record (1956-1971).** Every paper is one-shot except Rall & Hollister 1971.
- Minovitch 1963 (JPL TR 32-464) p.15 `[P PDF p.25]`: "Exact numerical calculations of even the most
  simple types of these trajectories having only two planetary encounters have (so far as the author
  knows) never been carried out"; example 5 imagines E-V-M-E-V-M-E repeating free fall, not computed
  (recorded in the owner summary sec. 2.3). Legs capped at 540 degrees (one extra revolution) `[D]`.
- Crocco 1956 `[P scan p.29 = printed p.252]`: "the research is to be considered like a laying out of the
  problem and not like a solution"; a = 1 AU one-year ellipse with Mars and Venus flybys cancelling the
  return error; "the return point on the Earth will be however different from the departure point" `[D]`.
- Hollister 1963 Sc.D. `[P PDF p.15]`: priority claim for Venus powered-flyby Mars round trips; the
  repeat is of the launch opportunity "about every six years" (3 E-M synodic periods) `[D]`.
- NASA TM X-53049 (1964) Ehricke `[P PDF p.26]`: "bi-planet capture or capture/PFB missions ... will
  represent the actual future mode of interplanetary transportation. Unfortunately ... too ambitious".
- Breakwell, Gillespie & Ross 1961 `[P PDF p.4]`: round trips "will be investigated in a paper now in
  preparation"; appendix identity: two Lambert arcs with equal energy close on one ellipse of period
  2 dt `[D]`.
- Young 1966 `[P PDF p.10]`: "The relative geometry of Earth, Venus and Mars is approximately repetitive
  every 6.4 years" (4 E-V synodic = 3 E-M synodic = 2336-2340 d `[C]`); a 1977 E-V-M-V-E one-shot visits
  Venus twice via Mars.
- Manning 1967 `[P PDF p.7]`: "The pertinent Earth-Mercury geometry repeats itself every 4750 days (13
  Earth years)"; the digest's arithmetic adds 9 Venus orbits = 23 Mercury orbits (5.54 yr, -4.0 deg
  drift) `[C]`.
- VanderVeen 1969 `[P PDF pp.3-4]`: Earth-Venus opportunities repeat "at the end of five such synodic
  periods, or eight years" (2.4 d slip per 8 yr `[C]`); "In the case of Venus-Mars trajectories it has
  been demonstrated that the absolute repetitive period is 32 years" (no citation; probably a conflation
  with the Earth-Mars 15-synodic figure `[I]`); "Venus swingbys to Jupiter or other outer planet target
  missions have not been found practical".
- Hollister & Prussing 1965 `[P PDF p.2]`: "The method of analysis is applicable to any interplanetary
  flyby trajectory"; Fig. 1 rotating-frame availability rule (bodies inside 1 AU drift ahead of Earth).
- Rall & Hollister 1971 `[P PDF p.1]`: "finding a periodic orbit which includes encounters of [Mars] is
  more difficult and requires an approach different from that of Hollister and Menning"; "Both the method
  of Hollister and Menning and the method presented below do not guarantee that all periodic orbits
  connecting the planets of interest will be found"; "Additional types of direct returns exist but were
  not included in the investigation."
