## 0. Pre-registration (committed before any `#1048` run)

### 0.1 (a) Seeds whose Kepler skeleton never reaches the Moon: 3:1, 7:2 and 4:1

- **Why `#1000` missed them.** Its seed rule kept only skeletons with r_p < 1 < r_a. For 3:1
  (a = 0.481), 7:2 (a = 0.435) and 4:1 (a = 0.397), 2a < 1, so r_a < 1 for every r_p. Yet
  vaquero-31-c254 (symmetric 3:1 prograde, C = 2.54, T = 6.2696, perigee 792 km alt,
  periselene 31,521 km alt) exists, held by the lunar perturbation.
- **Seed.** The same p:q skeleton (`scripts/run_1000_shooting.py`: nodes at the p apocentres,
  N = p arcs, minimum-norm multiple shooting). The r_a > 1 filter is dropped for these three
  ratios. The lunar perturbation enters through the correction itself: the CR3BP arcs between
  nodes, solved by Newton. No separate analytic perturbation term is added.
  - Grid: p:q in {3:1, 7:2, 4:1}; sense prograde and retrograde; r_p at the 8 `#1000` values
    (6,578 to 42,164 km, log-spaced); omega at 36 values (10 deg).
- **Control first, hard stop: vaquero-31-c254.**
  - The 3:1 seeds (576: both senses, 8 r_p, 36 omega) are solved with C fixed at the row's
    C = 2.54.
  - **Pass:** a converged orbit has T within 1e-4 relative of the row's period
    (6.269604424886022) and a perpendicular x-axis crossing within 1e-3 (in x and ydot) of the
    row's (x0, ydot0) = (0.9013301668, -0.8462249954), or of its other perpendicular crossing.
  - **If the row is not recovered, method (a) is rejected for all three ratios and I stop.**
- **Then** the full grid with C free (1,728 seeds). Classification, the cycler-class test and the
  gates are as in `#1000` (sec. 0.4, 0.5, 5.1b/c). That includes the arc-by-arc asymmetry check
  and the arc-STM b. New asymmetric cycler-class families get the `#1000` literal collision
  table (p:q, sense, C, T, perigee and periselene, with the 5.1b tolerance).

### 0.2 (b) Lifting the continuation cap

- **Families:** A (5:2 prograde), B (5:3 retrograde) and D (7:3 prograde), plus any new
  asymmetric cycler-class family from (a).
- **Continuation:** the `#1000` fixed-C multiple shooting (`run_1000_continue.py --extend`), with
  at most 400 steps per direction. The adaptive step is unchanged: dC = 0.01, halved up to 3
  times on failure, grown x1.5 after an easy step.
- **Fold detection.** A fixed-C continuation cannot pass a fold in C; it shows up as loss of
  convergence at the smallest step. At such a stop I record |dT/dC| and |d(perigee)/dC| over the
  last 5 members. If |dT/dC| has grown by at least 10 times over those members, the stop is
  labelled "fold (suspected)"; otherwise "loss of convergence (unexplained)". The branch past a
  fold is NOT computed here.
- **Natural ends:** impact at a floor, a suspected fold, b crossing -2, a topology change, or the
  family becoming symmetric (a pitchfork).
- **Runs needed:**
  - A downward from 2.4109. A upward already ended at 3.0968 in `#1050`, by loss of convergence.
  - B downward from 0.6703. B upward ended at the Earth floor at 0.9418.
  - D downward already ended at the Earth floor at 2.4136. D upward ended by loss of convergence
    at 2.5601; it gets the fold label from its existing members.
- **Reported:** the full C range of each family, which floor ends it at each end, and the
  candidate range (both floors cleared, perigee at or below GEO, periselene inside the Hill
  radius).

### 0.3 (c) What the 54% impacting solves say about the seed grid

- An analysis of `data/1000_complement/shooting/grid.jsonl` (no new solves). For each seed I
  compute:
  - the skeleton's closest approach to the Moon over one period (a two-body ellipse in the
    rotating frame, with the Moon at (1 - mu, 0));
  - its perigee radius.
- Impact, convergence and iteration-limit rates are tabulated against:
  - p:q and sense;
  - r_p;
  - the skeleton's closest lunar approach, in bins of < 5,000, 5,000-20,000, 20,000-60,000 and
    > 60,000 km.
- **Expected outcome:** impacts concentrate in seeds whose skeleton passes the Moon closely and
  in the lowest r_p bins. If so, the seed grid wastes most solves on near-collision skeletons. A
  better seed would place the lunar pass at a set distance, for example by choosing omega so
  that the skeleton's closest lunar approach equals a target, rather than sampling omega
  uniformly. That is a recommendation only; it is not run here.
