## 0. The answer to the question that matters: hyperbolic and parabolic motion

Yes. The 1968 paper is general in the energy: "It is applicable to any type of motion (elliptic, parabolic or hyperbolic) without any modification of the formulae and integration procedures" (p.345). The device that removes the elliptic-only limit of the 1967 form is the choice of the fictitious time and of the elements. Instead of the eccentric anomaly with an energy-dependent scale, dt = sqrt(a) r dE (1967), the 1968 paper uses an energy-independent Sundman time, dt = (1/kappa) r ds, with omega^2 = 1/a as the element (positive for ellipses, zero for parabolas, negative for hyperbolas), and replaces cos/sin by the functions B0, B1, B2, B3 (Stumpff type), which are "well defined for all values of s and also for -infinity < omega^2 < +infinity" (p.350). So the 1967 form failing on a hyperbolic moon flyby is cured, and the cure is exactly the 1/a form that the 1967 digest had derived by hand (section 5 below confirms it equation by equation and numerically).

The regularised equations of the printed paper are exact for every sign of the energy. COMPUTED checks (section 6) reproduced a hyperbolic Kepler orbit (omega^2 = -0.38), a parabolic orbit (omega^2 = 0) and a radial hyperbolic fall through the centre from the printed closed form to 1e-13, and integrated a perturbed hyperbolic-energy close pass (omega^2 = -0.905, closest approach 0.025 of the starting distance, with velocity-dependent, time-dependent and position-dependent perturbation) in the s-form to 6.5e-13 against a Cartesian integration; a perturbed bound close pass (closest approach 0.033) was also checked for the element equations (section 6).

## 1. Chapter I: Kepler motion (READ pp.346 to 351)

Setting: origin at the central body, position x(t) of a particle of negligible mass, kappa^2 = gamma (m1 + m2), set to 1 by the choice of units. Scalar product (x, y), cross product [x, y], dots are d/dt, primes are d/ds.
- (101), (102): xdd + (kappa^2/r^3) x = 0.
- Energy (103), (104): omega^2 = 1/a = 2/r - v^2, "the element a ... is called the major semi-axis"; 1/a = omega^2 > 0 ellipse (circle), omega^2 = 0 parabola, omega^2 < 0 hyperbola (p.347).
- Second Laplace vector (105): C = [x, xdot] (angular momentum). First Laplace vector (106): A = (v^2 - 1/r) x - (x, xdot) xdot. Properties (pp.347 to 348): A and C orthogonal; A lies in the orbital plane and points to the pericentre; |A| = e. Hierarchy: omega^2 is defined for any conservative field, C for any central force, A only for U = mu/r.
- An element q is any function q(x, xdot) with qdot = 0.

The regularising time (109a to 109d): t = (1/kappa) integral of r ds, dt = (1/kappa) r ds, d/dt = (1/r) d/ds, d^2/dt^2 = (1/r^2) d^2/ds^2 - (r'/r^3) d/ds. Adding ((1/r - v^2)/r^2) x + (rdot/r) x to both sides of (102) and substituting (104), (106) gives (110): xdd + (rdot/r) xdot + (omega^2/r^2) x + (1/r^2) A = 0, and from (109), (110) the fundamental equations (111), boxed:

 x'' + omega^2 x + A = 0, t' = r.

"A three-dimensional harmonic oscillator with constant frequency omega^2; its centre is located at -A/omega^2" (p.349). Equation (112), for the distance: r'' + omega^2 r - 1 = 0. Both are linear with constant coefficients; neither contains r in a denominator. Note what makes this form global in energy: the oscillator coefficient is omega^2 = 1/a, which stays finite as a goes to infinity (parabola), whereas the 1967 coefficient was fixed at 1 by absorbing sqrt(a) into the time.

Natural elements (113) to (118) (pp.349 to 351): the initial-value problem (113a to 113e): x'' + omega^2 x + A = 0, t' = r, t(s0) = t0, x(s0) = x0, x'(s0) = r0 xdot0, r0^2 = (x0, x0). General solution (114), (115):
- x(s) = alpha B2(omega^2, s) + beta B1(omega^2, s) + gamma, x'(s) = alpha B1 + beta B0, with gamma = -(A + alpha)/omega^2 (114c);
- r(s) = delta1 B2 + delta2 B1 + delta3, with delta3 = (1 - delta1)/omega^2 (115b);
- the B-functions (p.350): B0 = cos(omega s), B1 = (1/omega) sin(omega s), B2 = (1/omega^2)(1 - cos(omega s)), B3 = (1/omega^2)(s - B1). Later: B5 = (1/omega^2)(3 B3 - B1 B2), B-tilde-3(omega^2, s) = 4 B3(4 omega^2, s) (p.353 and its footnote), B4(omega^2, X) = (1/omega) arctan(omega X) (Appendix I). Presumably for omega^2 < 0 these continue to cosh and sinh; the paper says only that they are well defined for all omega^2 and gives the formulae in the trigonometric form (INFERRED continuation; COMPUTED check in section 6 uses cosh and sinh and reproduces a hyperbolic orbit).
- Initial values at s0 = 0 (117), r0 not zero: alpha = -(omega^2 x0 + A), beta = r0 xdot0, gamma = x0, delta1 = 1 - omega^2 r0, delta2 = r'0 = (x0, xdot0), delta3 = r0, delta0 = t0 (with omega^2, A from (104), (106) at x0, xdot0). Ejection (r0 = 0): take-off direction lambda (direction cosines) and energy are given; alpha = -A = lambda, delta1 = 1, delta0 = t0, and beta = gamma = delta2 = delta3 = 0 (p.350 to 351). So ejection and collision orbits are handled by the same formulae.
- Kepler's equation (118): t(s) = delta1 B3 + delta2 B2 + delta3 s + delta0 (transcendental in s; solve for s given t by iteration).
- "In this theory the motion is determined with the help of alpha, beta, gamma, omega^2, delta_i (i.e. 14 constants); it is however completely determined by the seven constants t0, x0, xdot0" (p.351): the elements satisfy identities (Appendix I below).

## 2. Chapter II: the perturbed problem (READ pp.351 to 354)

Perturbing force P(x, xdot, t) lumps everything but the central attraction: other attracting centres, oblateness, radiation, drag, thrust (p.351). (200): xdd + (kappa^2/r^3) x = P. The same transformations as in the unperturbed case give the fundamental equations (201), where omega^2 and A are still DEFINED by (104), (106) but are no longer constants:

 (201a) x'' + omega^2(s) x + A(s) = r^2 P,
 (201b) r'' + omega^2(s) r - 1 = r(P, x) (the right side as printed is "r(P, x)", i.e. r times the scalar product),
 (201c) t' = r.

Perturbation equations (202), (203), "a straightforward differentiation" (p.352):
 d(omega^2)/ds = -2 (P, x'),
 dA/ds = 2 (P, x') x - (x, x') P - (P, x) x'.
The system (201), (202), (203) is "regular everywhere, even for r = 0", of total order 13 (counting x second order, r second order, t, omega^2 and the three A: 6 + 2 + 1 + 1 + 3 = 13; without the redundant r equation it is 11, since r = |x|). The velocity-dependent force is allowed: the dependence P(x, xdot, t) uses xdot = x'/r, so a velocity-linear force (Coriolis) gives r^2 P proportional to r x', which is regular.

Variable natural elements (pp.352 to 353). With alpha, beta, gamma, delta_i now functions of s, the solution has the same shape, (204), (205): x = alpha B2 + beta B1 + gamma; x' = alpha B1 + beta B0; r = delta1 B2 + delta2 B1 + delta3; t = delta1 B3 + delta2 B2 + delta3 s + delta0, with all B-functions at the running (omega^2(s), s). Substituting into (201) gives the element equations (206), where C1 = (P, x'), C2 = (x, x') and C3 = (P, x):

 (206a) alpha' = alpha (C3 B0 - C1 B1) B1 + beta (C3 B0^2 - C1 (B0 B1 + s)) + P (C2 B0 + r^2 omega^2 B1),
 (206b) beta' = alpha (C1 B-tilde-3 - C3 B1^2) + beta (C1 B1 - C3 B0) B1 + P (r^2 B0 - C2 B1),
 (206c) gamma' = alpha (C3 B1 + C1 B2) B2 + beta (C1 B3 + B2 (C3 B0 - C1 B1)) + P (C2 B2 - r^2 B1),
 (206d) delta1' = omega^2 r C3 B1 - C1 (delta1 omega^2 B2^2 - 2 delta3 B0 + delta2 B3 omega^2),
 (206e) delta2' = r C3 B0 - C1 (delta1 (B1 B2 - B3) + 2 delta3 B1 + delta2 B1^2),
 (206f) delta3' = -r C3 B1 + C1 (delta1 B2^2 + delta2 B-tilde-3 + 2 delta3 B2),
 (206g) delta0' = r C3 B2 - C1 (delta1 B5 + 2 delta3 B3 + delta2 B2^2),
 (206h) (omega^2)' = -2 C1,

as PRINTED. Two printing errors, found by COMPUTED check (section 6): in (206c) the alpha term must read alpha (C3 B1 - C1 B2) B2 (the printed + before C1 B2 is wrong), and in (206d) the last B3 must be B-tilde-3 (the tilde is missing on the page; Appendix II (4) and equation (315) have it). With these two corrections every element equation of (206), and the Chapter III forms (306) to (311) and (315) to (317), reproduces the numerical derivative of the elements along a reference trajectory to 1e-10 (section 6). The same sign error appears in (311) and in Appendix II (6): gamma' contains +C1 alpha B2^2 and must contain -C1 alpha B2^2. Hand derivation (COMPUTED, 2026-10-05): from gamma = -(A + alpha)/omega^2, (202), (203) and the verified alpha' of (206a), gamma' = P (C2 B2 - r^2 B1) + x' C3 B2 + C1 (-alpha B2^2 + beta (B3 - B1 B2)).

Remarks printed on p.353: since P is a factor in every term of (206) (except the Kepler part), the elements are "slowly varying", "the system supplies the solution to any perturbed two-body problem; its total order is 14"; element systems of (206) are regular at r = 0 and at e = 1.

Stability (pp.353 to 354, a short and useful analysis). Kepler motion is orbitally stable but the position on the orbit is unstable (period T proportional to a^(3/2), so an energy error produces a drift in phase); under isoenergetic variations of the initial conditions it is strictly stable (bounded). In the present theory all 14 elements satisfy q' = 0 for P = 0, so the orbital stability is immediate, and the only unstable item is the physical time: t(s) = s/omega^2 + periodic terms + constant, so with an error Delta(omega^2) in the energy the time error grows as -s Delta(omega^2) + periodic + constant. "This instability cannot be completely suppressed by any mathematical trick since the physical problem itself is unstable; however, it has completely disappeared from the differential equations (206) and is only present in the term s/omega^2, i.e. in the algebraic computation of the value of physical time." Hence minimal error propagation.

## 3. Chapter III: the numerical experiments (READ pp.354 to 363)

### 3.1 The test problem

The test is a perturbed two-body problem with an exact solution: the restricted three-body problem with the particle at the Lagrange point L4 (pp.354 to 355). m1 is much more massive than m2; m2 moves on a Kepler conic about m1; the massless particle m at L4 stays at the apex of the equilateral triangle on m1-m2, so its motion is the m2 orbit rotated by 60 degrees (Figures 1, 2: "the conic ... has the same shape ... whose major axis forms a 60 degree angle"). Perturbation of the two-body problem (m1, m) by m2 (300): P = -m2 ((1/rho^3)(x - x2) + (1/r2^3) x2), with rho the distance particle to m2, and r = r2 = rho at L4 (301). Mass ratio m2/m1 = 0.01 ("the motion in L4 is stable provided the ratio is < 0.03852").

### 3.2 Procedures

Procedure B (the present method) and its companion, plus the Stiefel et al. NUMPER KS programme "RS" (the book of NASA CR-769, reference [1], not held), and Encke's method "E". All used a fixed-step fourth-order Runge-Kutta on a CDC 1604 with an 11-digit mantissa. Equation (329) is the Encke-type deviation equation Delta-xdd = kappa^2 (x_k/r_k^3 - x/r^3) + P (INFERRED reading; the printed form is "Delta-x-double-dot = kappa^2 (1/r_k^3 x_k - 1/r^3 x) + P").

Procedure B, first part (302) to (309): initial elements at s = 0: alpha(0) = -(omega^2(0) x0 + A0) (302), beta(0) = r0 xdot0 (303), omega^2(0) = 2/r0 - (xdot0, xdot0) (304), delta0(0) = t0 (305), with A0 = [(xdot0, xdot0) - 1/r0] x0 - (x0, xdot0) xdot0. Equations (306), (307), (308), (309):
 alpha' = P (r^2 omega^2 B1 + C2 B0) + x' (C3 B0 - C1 B1) - C1 s beta,
 beta' = P (r^2 B0 - C2 B1) - x' C3 B1 + C1 (alpha B-tilde-3 + beta B1^2),
 delta0' = r C3 B2 - C1 (delta1 B5 + 2 delta3 B3 + delta2 B2^2),
 (omega^2)' = -2 C1.
Second part: gamma(0) = x0 (310), gamma' = P (-r^2 B1 + C2 B2) + x' (C3 B2) + C1 (alpha B2^2 + beta (B3 - B1 B2)) (311), with the sign error noted in section 2 (alpha term must be minus). Third part: delta1(0) = 1 - omega^2 r0 (312), delta2(0) = (x0, xdot0) (313), delta3(0) = r0 (314); (315) delta1' = r C3 omega^2 B1 - C1 (omega^2 (delta1 B2^2 + delta2 B-tilde-3) - 2 delta3 B0); (316) delta2' = r C3 B0 - C1 (delta1 (B1 B2 - B3) + delta2 B1^2 + 2 delta3 B1); (317) delta3' = -r C3 B1 + C1 (delta1 B2^2 + delta2 B-tilde-3 + 2 delta3 B2).

Companion procedure (318) to (321): integrate only alpha, beta, omega^2, delta0 (3 + 3 + 1 + 1 = 8 equations, p.363: "the total order of this system is 14, but it can be conveniently reduced to 8") and obtain the rest from gamma = -delta3 alpha + delta2 beta (318), delta3 = (beta, beta)/(1 + delta1) (319), delta2 = (1/delta1)(alpha, beta) = sqrt((1/omega^2)(1 - (alpha, alpha))) (320), delta1^2 = 1 - omega^2 (beta, beta) (321). For nearly circular orbits it is often better to replace only the second part by (318) and integrate the delta_i (p.357). Solution assembly (322) to (327): x = alpha B2 + beta B1 + gamma, x' = alpha B1 + beta B0, r = delta1 B2 + delta2 B1 + delta3, r' = delta1 B1 + delta2 B0, t = delta1 B3 + delta2 B2 + delta3 s + delta0.

### 3.5 Conclusions (p.363)

Printed advantages: the natural elements are always defined (no singularity at r = 0, e = 1 or critical inclination); a simple system for any perturbed motion, order 14, reducible to 8; the osculating-conic concept is present, so a first-order analytical theory is at hand; no transformation of coordinates or of forces is required, so improvements based on modifying P (the Runge-Kutta-Fehlberg method is named) apply; a fixed s-step corresponds to an automatic physical-time step control Gamma = h r(t); physical time is obtained from an element; the regularisation "can be generalized for the problem with two fixed centres; regularization of the three-dimensional restricted three-body problem can thus be established without resorting to a transformation of the physical coordinates ... will be presented in a later publication" (pp.363 to 364; not held, and no citation is given).

## 4. Appendices (READ pp.364 to 368)

Appendix I (identities between natural elements). With sigma the value of the B-function argument at t = t0 (i.e. s + sigma = 0 at the pericentre), the elements referred to the pericentre (subscript p) are related to the general elements by the linear map (4): alpha = alpha_p B0(omega^2, sigma) - beta_p omega^2 B1(omega^2, sigma), beta = alpha_p B1 + beta_p B0, delta1 = delta1p B0 - delta2p omega^2 B1, delta2 = delta1p B1 + delta2p B0. Standard form when started at pericentre (5): (x0, x'0) = 0, (alpha_p, beta_p) = 0, (alpha_p, alpha_p) = 1, (beta_p, beta_p) = (1/omega^2)(1 - e^2) = p (the parameter of the conic; the printed "= p" is as given, and p = (1 - e^2)/omega^2 = a (1 - e^2)), A = -e alpha_p, delta1p = e, delta2p = 0, delta3p = (1/omega^2)(1 - e) = p/(1 + e). Relations (6a, 6b): (alpha, beta) = e^2 B1(4 omega^2, sigma) = delta1 delta2; (alpha, alpha) - omega^2 (beta, beta) = e^2 B0(4 omega^2, sigma) = delta1^2 - omega^2 delta2^2; so e^2 = 2 - (alpha, alpha) - omega^2 (beta, beta) = delta1^2 + omega^2 delta2^2; delta1 = e B0(omega^2, sigma), delta2 = e B1(omega^2, sigma). The pericentre phase sigma follows from Y = delta2/delta1 or X = (alpha, beta)/((alpha, alpha) - omega^2 (beta, beta)) as sigma = B4(omega^2, Y) = B4(4 omega^2, X) (7), (8). gamma = -(A + alpha)/omega^2 = -(-e alpha_p + alpha)/omega^2 = -delta3 alpha + delta2 beta (9). Alternatives usable at e = 0 (where (8) is undefined) (10): delta1 = 1 - omega^2 (beta, beta), delta2 = sqrt((1/omega^2)(1 - (alpha, alpha))), delta3 = (beta, beta)/(1 + delta1); and (11): omega^2 = 2/delta3 - (beta, beta)/delta3^2 = (1 - (alpha, alpha))/((alpha, beta)^2 + (beta, beta)(1 - (alpha, alpha))). "All these identities ... furnish a variety of possible checks for the accuracy of the numerical integration" (p.365), and are the basis of the companion procedure.
Appendix II (derivation of (206)): differentiates x = (1/omega^2)(-alpha cos(omega s) + beta omega sin(omega s) - A), r = (1/omega^2)(-delta1 cos(omega s) + delta2 omega sin(omega s) + 1) and their s-derivatives (printed for omega^2 > 0), uses the identity B0^2 + omega^2 B1^2 = 1 to separate alpha' from beta' (1a, 1c), obtains (2) alpha', (3) beta', (4) delta1' and delta2', (5) delta3' (differentiation of delta3 = (1 - delta1)/omega^2), (6) gamma' (with the alpha-term sign error noted above), and the physical-time element delta0' (7a) to (8), with the new function B5 "also well defined for all values of s and omega^2 including omega^2 = 0".

May 1961 p.660; [4] Broucke, Regularized Special Perturbation Techniques Using Levi-Civita Variables, AIAA Paper 8 (1963); [5] Stiefel, in Dynamics of Rockets and Satellites (Groves ed.), chap. ## 5. Relation to the 1967 paper and to the hand-derived 1/a form

Comparison, 1967 against 1968 (all READ):
- Independent variable: 1967 dt = sqrt(a) r dE (eccentric anomaly, a > 0 only); 1968 dt = (1/kappa) r ds (kappa^2 = 1, energy-independent).
- Oscillator: 1967 d^2x/dE^2 + x + a A = a r^2 P; 1968 x'' + omega^2 x + A = r^2 P (omega^2 = 1/a). The two are related by E = omega s: x'' (1967 in E) = (1/omega^2) x'' (in s). So (6a)/(10a) of 1967 is (111)/(201a) of 1968 multiplied by a = 1/omega^2.
- Rate equations: 1967 (10c), (10d) in E; 1968 (202), (203) in s. 1968 (203) is dA/ds = 2 (P, x') x - (x, x') P - (P, x) x'. COMPUTED, 2026-10-05: this equals the form I derived by hand in the 1967 digest sections 3.2 and 6.1 ("d(1/a)/ds = -2 P.x', dA/ds = 2 (P.x_s) x - (x.P) x_s - (x.x_s) P, dt = r ds") term for term. The hand derivation is therefore confirmed by the printed source and by the numerical check of section 6, and the "check it symbolically" caveat can be dropped.
- Sign conventions: 1967 alpha = A + x0/a = A + omega^2 x0 and A = e alpha; 1968 alpha = -(omega^2 x0 + A) and A = -e alpha_p. Conversion (COMPUTED, comparing the printed solutions): alpha(1968) = -alpha(1967); beta(1968) = beta(1967)/omega, with E = omega s. The vector A is the same in both; the 1968 paper's p.365 formula x(s) = (1/omega^2)(-alpha cos(omega s) + beta omega sin(omega s) - A) matches the 1967 (7a) under this map.
- Time element: 1967 (14) is the physical time perturbation; 1968 has delta0 with the equations (206g)/(308) and t = delta1 B3 + delta2 B2 + delta3 s + delta0.
- The 1968 paper adds: the parabolic and hyperbolic cases, the ejection case, the identities of Appendix I with the companion (8-equation) procedure, and the numerical tests.

## 7. Comparison with KS, with Stiefel and Scheifele 1971, and with the #928 recommendation

State of #928 (read in `data/OUTSTANDING.md` and the Stiefel-Scheifele digest section 16.1): build one KS code about the moon (4-vector u, planar when u3 = u4 = 0 which is Levi-Civita), total-energy form (9,53), conservative part as V with V(0) = 0, Coriolis as P; the transition matrix projected from the 9 x 9 or 10 x 10 variational equations; Burdet "no longer needed for 3D" because the u-method is valid for hyperbolic passes. The 1971 book does not discuss Burdet's formulation (digest section 10).

What Burdet 1968 does and does not change.
- It removes the stated defect of the Burdet alternative: the printed general form is valid for all energies (section 0), so Burdet's route is a real option for a moon flyby, not an elliptic-only curiosity.
- It does not change the primary recommendation. KS with the total energy keeps h constant (the Jacobi-type energy in the time-independent CR3BP), so its oscillator frequency is constant and the perturbation is only Coriolis plus V; the Burdet form uses the Kepler energy omega^2 of the moon-centred two-body problem, which is NOT conserved (omega^2' = -2 (P, x')), so away from the moon the elements vary at a rate set by the other primary and the rotating frame, not slowly. Burdet's advantage (slowly varying elements, a few steps per revolution) holds for a weak perturbation; in the Earth-Moon problem it holds only in the neighbourhood of the moon. KS also gives a canonical structure (a symplectic check on the transition matrix, a free test), which the Burdet system, being non-canonical, does not.
- Burdet's real value for #928 is as the second, structurally different integrator. It uses Cartesian x (no map, no gauge to maintain, no 4-vector lift), is the same formula in 2 and 3 dimensions, takes a velocity-dependent perturbation without special treatment, has an 11-variable form (x, x', omega^2, A, t) that is a one-screen implementation and was checked here to 1e-12 on a hyperbolic-energy close pass, and has a free internal consistency monitor (the integrated omega^2 and A against their algebraic values (104), (106)). A KS propagator and a Burdet propagator agreeing at a pass 8.7e-6 from the moon would be a stronger test of the #896 tolerances than two integrators of the same Sundman structure; I would keep the order KS first, Burdet as the cross-check.
- Where Burdet 1968 might be preferred over KS: a quick first prototype (no map to derive), a time-dependent model where h is not a constant anyway (the elliptic CR3BP, #895 ephemeris models), or a 3D case where the KS fibre gauge is a nuisance. For the transition matrix the cost is a redundant 11-dimensional state to be projected onto the 6 + t physical components, versus KS's 9 or 10 with a fibre gauge; neither is easier.
- Physical time: both carry the same fictitious-time law dt = r ds (kappa = sqrt(mu_moon) in the project's units; the paper sets kappa = 1 by the unit of mass). The Burdet paper's remark that in the KS theory "the physical time t ... was computed without introducing the appropriate element" (p.345) is addressed in the 1971 book by the time element of section 18 (elliptic only per the Stiefel-Scheifele digest), so the time-element advantage is not decisive.

### 9.1 #928 (a regularised propagator and transition matrix for close passes)

Formulation to build: KS first (planar = Levi-Civita, 3D = the same code), as recommended in the Stiefel-Scheifele digest; Burdet 1968 as the second structure, in this form, for any energy:
- state: x (3), x' (3), omega^2, A (3), t (11 variables, or 8 with the companion identities, below); unit choice kappa^2 = mu_moon, or non-dimensionalise with the moon's mu = 1;
- equations (201a), (201c), (202), (203): x'' = -omega^2 x - A + r^2 P, t' = r, (omega^2)' = -2 (P, x'), A' = 2 (P, x') x - (x, x') P - (P, x) x';
- initial values: omega^2 = 2/r0 - v0^2, A = (v0^2 - 1/r0) x0 - (x0, v0) v0, x'0 = r0 v0;
- P in the moon-centred rotating CR3BP: every acceleration of the moon-centred frame except the moon's own central attraction (which is the Kepler part), namely the Earth's direct attraction, the centrifugal term and the Coriolis term 2 (v_y, -v_x, 0) in the form the project's frame gives it; P is a function of x, v = x'/r and t, with the velocity harmless because r^2 P contains r x' only. Unlike KS in the total-energy form, there is no additive potential constant to choose (V(0) = 0 does not arise), because the Burdet form takes forces, not a potential (INFERRED);
- all parts regular at r = 0, hyperbolic, parabolic and elliptic included.
Planar and 3D: the same formulae, with x a 2- or 3-vector (planar: drop the third component, A in the plane). No special planar version is needed (an advantage over KS and Levi-Civita, which need a map).
Analytic tests (all from printed closed forms; the closed forms need B0 to B3 evaluated with series near omega^2 s^2 = 0 and in cosh/sinh form for omega^2 < 0, since the printed 1/omega^2 forms cancel catastrophically near omega^2 = 0; COMPUTED: my check switched to a Stumpff series for omega^2 s^2 below 1e-4):

### 9.2 #924 (variational vectors through a close pass: FLI, MEGNO)

- Regularity: the variational equations of the Burdet system are regular through r = 0 (all coefficients polynomial in x, x', omega^2, A, r^2 P and its derivative), so the indicator is not swamped by a 1/r^3 growth at the pass (INFERRED).
- The paper's stability analysis says what an indicator should expect on a Kepler arc: with P = 0 the elements are constant; the phase error grows as -s Delta(omega^2) in physical time (p.354), i.e. a linear shear in the along-track direction for any energy error, so physical-time FLI and MEGNO on a regularised arc should be defined to either include or exclude the energy direction explicitly. This matches the point recorded in the Stiefel-Scheifele digest section 16.3 for KS.
- Convert to physical time with dt = r ds before averaging, as for KS.
- Control: the analytic transition matrix of a Kepler arc from (114), (118) against finite difference, then the perturbed L4 benchmark (an exact solution, so the variational equations can be checked against the analytic derivative of the rotated Kepler orbit with respect to the initial data).

### 9.3 #929 (integrator controls through one moon flyby)

- Add the Burdet form as one structurally different member of the three-way comparison (control (b)), because it shares neither the singular force law of the plain integrator nor the coordinate map of KS, and costs a few lines.
- Free monitors: omega^2 and A integrated against recomputed from the state; the elements' identities (Appendix I) if elements are carried; the time error enters only through s/omega^2 (p.354), so a time-dependent model (#895, #900 ephemeris tables, the elliptic problem) inherits an error in t that feeds the perturbation P(t): test with a time-shifted P.
- The paper's own lesson (Figures 4 to 8): in a Kepler-dominated arc the error is mostly along the track (Delta phi) and much smaller across it (Delta r); report along-track and cross-track errors separately in the #929 comparison rather than a single norm.
- Reversibility control (a): the s-equations with P(x, x', t) are not time-reversible when P depends on x' (the Coriolis term), as for any velocity-dependent force; the reversibility test needs the usual velocity reversal and the sign of P(x', t) to be handled (INFERRED, standard).
