## 0. Verdict

**This paper tabulates the two 2/3-resonant Earth-Moon periodic families found in Part I, six orbits each, to eight
digits. Every row reproduces.** It also makes a negative 3D search.
- **Direct family** (Table I): x0 from −0.39215 to −0.29215 (the Earth-side perpendicular crossing).
  - Period p1 is 11.64-12.17 TU, below 4π.
  - The far-side lunar distance is 0.088-0.199 L.
  - The orbits are unstable. Table III shows the period difference growing by a factor of about 8.5-10 per cycle.
- **Retrograde family** (Table II): x0 from −0.49215 to −0.39215.
  - p1 is 12.69-12.84 TU, above 4π.
  - The orbits are stable. Table IV shows a bounded oscillation of the period difference.

  with the wanted character: "none with the desired characteristic ... has been found" (abstract).

## 1. Content

**Method** (sec. I, pp.388-389, read on the image).
- The period p of a nearly periodic orbit is "the time interval between two successive crossings over the x axis by
  the third body at points near to each other". The periods of successive cycles are p1, p2, ..., pn.
- "Δ_{n+1,n} = p_{n+1} − p_n" (1).
- Start: "x = x0, y = 0, ẋ = 0, ẏ = ẏ0" (2). x0 is fixed and ẏ0 is varied until Δ2,1 vanishes, by interpolation.
- Six orbits per family were chosen so that "when the third body is on the far side of the moon, the closest distance
  between them lies between 0.08 and 0.20". Our far-side distances are 0.0884-0.1989 (direct) and 0.0956-0.1735
  (retrograde).
- Integration:
  - fourth-order Runge-Kutta, double precision, IBM 7090, Δt = 0.0078125;
  - the Jacobi constant holds "for at least six significant figures for a time interval of 4π";
  - p_n is found by Lagrange four-point inverse interpolation of y(t).
- "For each value of x0 in both tables, two values of ẏ0 are given — one with Δ2,1 being positive and the other with
  Δ2,1 negative". The x0 = −0.43215 retrograde row has three values, the middle one with Δ2,1 = 0.000000.

**Stability** (sec. II, pp.389-390).
- A stable orbit has Δ_{n+1,n} oscillating "without increasing its amplitude". An unstable one has it growing.
- Table III: direct, x0 = −0.31215, both bracketing ẏ0. The printed ratios |Δ_{n+1,n}/Δ_{n,n−1}| are 8.5-10.3. The
  sign alternates, which is a flip-type growth.
- Table IV: retrograde, x0 = −0.43215, four ẏ0 values from 2.2645060 to 2.2645075 (Δẏ0 = 0 to 7.5e-7), n = 1..13.
  The amplitude stays bounded and shrinks with Δẏ0.
- The paper cites Poincaré's characteristic exponents and Message (1959) for the formal route, which it does not take.

**Three-dimensional case** (sec. III, pp.390-391).
- Start: "x = x0, y = 0, z = 0, ẋ = 0, ẏ = ẏ0, ż = ż0" (3).
- Two sequences (4):
  - (1) x0 = −0.39215 with ẏ0 varied;
  - (2) x0 = −ẏ0, a "velocity perpendicular to the plane of orbit of the moon" in the fixed frame.
- p_n(z=0) and p_n(y=0) are the intervals between passages through the xy and xz planes at x near x0. Tables V and
  VI give, for each ẏ0 (or x0), the ż0 that makes p1(z=0) = p1(y=0) (5).
- These were computed in single precision, so fewer digits are given.
- (5) is not sufficient for periodicity. Periodicity also needs p1 = 4π and x(4π) = x0, or equal periods for every
  cycle.
- Result:
  - Sequence 1 perhaps has a solution near ẏ0 = 0.135, but those orbits "do not pass around the far side of the
    moon".
  - Sequence 2 has two ż0 roots for each x0 and neither gives 4π over x = −0.29215 to −0.39215.
  - Conclusion: no 3D orbit "of enclosing both the earth and the moon" was found.
