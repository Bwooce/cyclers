## What it is
A graph-based framework for computing impulsive low-energy transfers in the spatial CR3BP in a
multi-objective (flight time vs fuel) fashion. Two phases: a general precomputing phase (grid
search plus graph generation, independent of any mission) and a specific mission-analysis phase
(add boundary arcs, shortest-path search, optimisation). Abstract: it "finds new Pareto
solutions in the halo-to-halo transfer problem as well as the high-energy excursion technique
that enhances the flyby effect in the multi-flyby transfer problem as by-products."

## Q1. The graph
**Model and constants (Table 1, Earth-Moon CR3BP, source cited as Topputo [11]), as printed:**

| Name | Value | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| Mass parameter mu | 1.21506683e-2 (printed as 1.21506683 x 10^-2) | - |
| Length unit | 3.84405000e5 | km |
| Time unit | 4.34811305 | day |
| Earth radius Re | 6378 | km |
| Moon radius Rm | 1738 | km |

Equations in the standard rotating frame (P1 at [-mu, 0, 0], P2 at [1-mu, 0, 0]); Jacobi energy
C = -|v|^2 - 2U-bar(r) (Eq. 7), so "low energy" means high C. The paper studies "the Jacobi
energy level higher than that of the libration point L3 (C_L3)". The numerical value of C_L3 is
not stated. Configuration space is split into interior, P2, exterior and forbidden realms.

**Nodes.** "initial periapsis states of the p2p arcs represent the graph nodes" (Sec 3.2).
A p2p arc is a periapsis-to-periapsis arc of the spatial CR3BP flow with respect to a central
body c (c = 1 Earth in the interior realm, c = 2 Moon in the P2 realm). Nodes are generated by
a grid search over 3-dimensional subsets of the 6D state defined by "special apsis conditions"
(all satisfy the general apsis condition Rdot_c = 0, i.e. (x - x_c*) vx + y vy + z vz = 0):

- (12) vx = vy = vz = 0 (the zero velocity surface)
- (13) vx = vy = z = 0
- (14) vx = y = vz = 0 (includes states on xz-planar symmetric periodic orbits)
- (15) vx = y = z = 0 (includes states on axi-symmetric periodic orbits)
- (16)-(18) x - x_c* = vy = vz = 0; x - x_c* = vy = z = 0; x - x_c* = y = vz = 0
- (19) x - x_c* = y = z = 0 is excluded as a singular point at the centre of a body.

Initial conditions on these grids are propagated forward and backward to periapsis (starting
from apoapsis states, or from periapsis states via an apoapsis). Stored per arc: STM over the
apoapsis-to-periapsis interval, the apsis states and the flight time between apsides. Periodic
and libration-point orbits are NOT inserted as explicit nodes; they are only captured because
the special apsis conditions include their states (the paper says (14)/(15) "include states on"
such orbits). Energy range kept: example 1 C_L3 < C < 4 (Eq. 42); example 2 C_L3 < C < 3.3
(Eq. 43). Distance filters: example 1 Re < R1 < 1 and R2 > R_Hill, inclination i < 90 deg;
example 2 Rm < R2 < 2 R_Hill.

**Edges.** A directed edge n_i to n_j exists if the fixed-time-of-arrival (FTA) method (Battin
[17]) joins the preceding arc's final periapsis state to the succeeding arc's initial periapsis
state with two impulses: dv_a at the apoapsis of the preceding arc and dv_p at the patch
periapsis, using the preceding arc's STM:
dv_a = Phi_rv^-1 (r_p+ - r_p-)  (Eq. 20); dv_p = v_p+ - v_p- - Phi_vv dv_a  (Eq. 21).
dv_a cancels the position error, dv_p the velocity error; flight time dt is invariant; the
nodes are unaffected. Edge approval requires |dv_a| < dv_a_tol and |dv_p|/|v_p+| < dv_p_tol
(Eqs. 23-24; second one is normalised). Edge weight (Eqs. 25-27):
w = alpha dt + beta (1 - alpha) dv; dv = gamma |dv_a| + delta (1 - gamma) dv_p_min;
dv_p_min is the theoretical-minimum velocity change at r_p+ to reach the Jacobi energy of the
succeeding arc. alpha and gamma are each stepped 0.1 from 0 to 1 (11 values each; no gamma
needed at alpha = 1), giving 110 + 1 = 111 graphs. beta and delta are scaling constants.
Graphs are stored as edge lists (MATLAB `digraph`), not adjacency matrices.

**Search.** Dijkstra's algorithm (single-source single-target via MATLAB `shortestpath`;
multi-source single-target via `shortestpathtree`). Run in each of the 111 graphs; duplicate
shortest paths are removed. Boundary arcs: the source node is the periapsis state of an initial
p2p arc whose start is the end of the initial boundary arc; the target node is the periapsis
state of the final boundary arc; they are added to the precomputed graphs with `addnode` and
`addedge`. Objectives: flight time and fuel (total delta-v), traded by alpha/gamma (a Pareto
sweep by weighted sum over 111 graphs). Shortest paths are then optimised by direct multiple
shooting (regularised impulse variables |dv_j| = |U_j|^2 from Oshima 2024 [22]; objective sum
|U_j|^2; fmincon interior-point; tolerances 1e-7 on constraint violation, 1e-2 on first-order
optimality, initial barrier 1e-10; minimum-radius constraint at periapsis nodes; nodes
subdivided into three equal-time segments).

## Q2. Transfers designed and published results
Two examples. Results are given only as Pareto scatter plots (Figs 8 and 14) and trajectory
and time-history figures; **no numeric result table of delta-v, time of flight or Jacobi
constant per solution is printed.**

**Example 1 (Sec 4.1), multi-flyby transfer:** Earth-bound orbit (C approx 3.6) to a southern
L1 halo orbit (C = 3.1), targeting a stable manifold of the halo into the interior realm
(Earth central, c = 1). Initial state "satisfies perigee and apogee radii of 35,000 km and
230,000 km, i = 30 deg, and argument of perigee ... 178 deg ... similar to those at the first
lunar flyby in the SMART-1 mission [27]". Final state is the periapsis at i approx 10.5 deg of a
stable manifold with perturbation magnitude 1e-4. Theoretical minimum dv approx 60 m/s.
Counts: 605,764 p2p arcs; 144,555,222 approved node pairs (FTA tolerances dv_a_tol = 200 m/s,
dv_p_tol = 0.5; beta = 100, delta = 10); 33 unique solutions among 111; 2 discarded for total
transfer time over 200 days; 31 optimised with t1 = 0 and R1_min = 20,000 km + Re
(radiation belt); all converged. Pareto plot (Fig 8) axes read as total dv approx 60-140 m/s
(horizontal) against transfer time approx 300-550 days (vertical) (read from axis tick labels
only; axis titles and units are not in the text layer). Stated findings: "distinct families are
observed in an orderly manner in terms of the lunar orbital period"; dv values are "much larger
than the theoretical minimum value (approx 60 m/s) possibly due to the substantial inclination
change"; multiple high-altitude lunar flybys plus impulses "pump up the energy and crank down
the inclination"; the "high-energy excursion" (drop below target C = 3.1 via perigee manoeuvres,
exploit greater lunar flyby effects, then raise back) reduces total dv, especially for
inclination.

**Example 2 (Sec 4.2), halo-to-halo transfer:** southern L2 halo orbits, period 7.5 days to
period 13.6 days (Moon central, c = 2). Compared against Davis [29,30], Tsirogiannis-Markellos
[31], Tsirogiannis-Davis [32] in Fig 14. Counts: 1,233,538 p2p arcs; 285,880,138 approved
pairs (dv_a_tol = 30 m/s, dv_p_tol = 0.5; beta = 300, delta = 3); 222 solutions (two source
nodes), 36 unique, 2 discarded for time over 120 days, 34 optimised with t1 = T and
R2_min = 1000 km + Rm; all converged. Initial boundary arcs are "accelerated" unstable
manifolds (perturbation 1e-4 plus 5 m/s, both perturbation signs, 5 m/s counted in total dv);
final boundary arc a stable manifold of the final halo orbit (perturbation 1e-4). Theoretical
minimum dv approx 11.5 m/s. The method explored approximately 40-100 days; "the minimum-dv
solution almost reaches the theoretical minimum"; shorter than 40 days not explored (attributed
to boundary-arc choice). The Pareto front mixes solutions from both source signs. Conclusion:
"Pareto solutions that are not dominated in terms of the transfer time and dv by those found in
the earlier works". Fig 14 solutions (i)-(iii) are representative Pareto points; their numeric
values are not in the text.

## Q4. Anything periodic, repeating or cycler-like
Text search of the preprint (pdftotext, case-insensitive): "cycler" 0; "round trip" 0;
"cycle" 0; "loop" 0; "periodic" 11 lines; "resonan" 4 lines (including 1 reference title).
There are no cycler results, no round trips, and no closed loops in the graph (the graph is a
directed weighted graph searched for a single-source to target shortest path). The "periodic"
and "resonan" lines are all background or bibliography, except one observation:

- "the initial guess solutions choose the periodic orbit-like transfer paths as shortest paths
  without explicit inputs of such dynamical objects to the graphs." (Sec 4.2, example 2)
- "symmetric periodic orbits including libration point orbits and resonant orbits govern
  transport phenomena in the CR3BP." (Sec 3.1, as motivation; conditions (14) and (15) include
  states on xz-planar symmetric and axi-symmetric periodic orbits [16].)
- Intro: "periodic/quasi-periodic orbits, stable/unstable manifolds, or resonances" as special
  solutions; and the framework is "applicable to distinct phase-space regions ... regardless of
  the existence of various dynamical objects ... such as the libration point orbits and
  resonant orbits".
- Reference [16] title contains "periodic orbits ... near the retrograde co-orbital resonance
  with the Moon" (Oshima 2022); [14] "quasi-periodic orbits"; [30]-[32] "unstable periodic
  orbits".

Repeated lunar encounters occur only as multi-revolution high-altitude flybys within the
one-way example 1 transfer, and extra revolutions near the halo orbits in example 2 solutions
(ii) and (iii). Neither is a repeating or periodic trajectory. No exterior 1:3 or 1:4
resonances are mentioned. The exterior realm is explicitly not treated: "In the exterior realm
... arcs connecting from apoapsis to apoapsis (a2a arcs) may be more convenient ... Switching
over to the a2a arcs within the developed framework would be straightforward and its
demonstration may be considered in future work."

## Q5. Methods reusable by a periodic-orbit-network project (facts as stated)
- Graph nodes as apsis states on 3D slices of the CR3BP phase space, chosen so that symmetric
  periodic orbits (xz-planar and axi-symmetric) and the zero velocity surface are included
  (Eqs. 12-18); reduces the 5D search to 3D "at the cost of disregarding those not satisfying
  any of the special apsis conditions".
- Arc-to-arc connectivity by the two-impulse FTA correction from the stored apoapsis-to-
  periapsis STM (Eqs. 20-22); normalised velocity-error and absolute position-error thresholds
  prune edges (Eqs. 23-24); tolerances were set "by trial and error" (200 m/s and 0.5 in ex. 1;
  30 m/s and 0.5 in ex. 2).
- Edge weight blending time and dv with a Jacobi-energy-change term (Eqs. 25-27); 111 graphs
  for the (alpha, gamma) sweep; trade-off studied by Pareto front.
- Dijkstra single-source single-target and shortest-path-tree (multi-source) searches in
  MATLAB; sources and targets added to precomputed graphs after the fact.
- Edge-list storage and the stated memory cost (76 GB and 142 GB for 111 graphs).
- Regularised multiple-shooting dv optimisation from the shortest-path initial guess
  (reference [22]); all 31 and 34 initial guesses converged.
- Limits stated: spatial CR3BP only; impulsive only; interior and P2 realms only; boundary arcs
  held fixed during optimisation; non-autonomous models would add a dimension and make storage
  harder.

## What it does NOT contain
- No cyclers, no periodic or repeating trajectories as results, no round trips, no closed
  loops or cycle analysis in the graph (shortest-path search only).
- No resonant-orbit nodes, no exterior 1:3 or 1:4 resonance work, no exterior-realm results
  (a2a arcs proposed as future work).
- No lunar capture or Earth-to-Moon landing transfer; the targets are halo-orbit stable
  manifolds (L1 halo from an Earth-bound orbit; L2 halo to L2 halo).
- No tabulated per-solution dv, time of flight or Jacobi constants (Pareto scatter figures
  only), no halo-orbit initial conditions, no numerical C_L3, no Jacobi constants for the
  L2 halo orbits, no source code or data link.
- No non-autonomous or ephemeris model, no low-thrust results (both discussed as future work).
- The published Acta Astronautica version was not checked.
