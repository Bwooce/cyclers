## 0. What this paper is, in eight lines

1. Scope (READ p.255, abstract): planar circular restricted three-body problem at small mu. Symmetric periodic solutions of the second species (passing near the body of mass mu, called P2) and "their distance from the center of the body of mass mu" are studied by perturbing arc-solutions (solutions with consecutive collisions, which exist at mu = 0). "Orbits which also pass near the body of mass 1 - mu are studied in detail." Applied to the Earth-Moon system and to the Sun-Jupiter system.
2. Arcs: the symmetric arcs with consecutive collisions are the families S (A0, A1, ...; B1, B2, ...; C12, C23, ...) of Henon 1968; non-symmetric ones are families T. The structure of phase space at mu = 0 is in Bruno 1972 (Preprint 18/1972, English translation Celest. Mech. 18:9-50).
3. A first-order formula for the distance of closest approach to P2 (section 2): the smallest distance of the periodic orbit from P2 is xi = mu W, where W is a closed-form function of the arc's (a, e, eps') only, so the whole Hénon table is converted into "distance of closest approach to the Moon" without computing any orbit at mu > 0.
4. Validity (section 2, eq. 15): the first approximation holds where |W| is at most about 1 and at most about V (the excess speed); a Newton-polyhedron argument gives the domain.
5. The Earth-Moon result (section 4): from Henon's tables, orbits with e = 1 (passing Earth's centre in the limit) and a below 0.725 pass the Moon outside its surface; 11 such arcs are found in the printed range tau/pi <= 5.5, eta/pi <= 6.5; periods about one sidereal month for B1 and about two months for C23, C24, C25. The text calls them "10 families" (count discussed in section 2.3).
6. The Sun-Jupiter result (section 5): the A0 family below tau/pi = 0.5 contains orbits passing the Sun inside the Earth's orbit; the one with the maximum Jacobi constant C = -2H = -0.39913 (tau/pi about 0.213, period about 2.5 years) lies in an interval of stable orbits for small mu > 0.
7. Stability statement (READ p.258): periodic solutions generated by S are generally strongly unstable (trace of the stability matrix Tr near plus or minus infinity), but where the Hamiltonian has an extremum along the family Tr jumps from +infinity to -infinity (or back) and an interval of stability |Tr| <= 2 appears. Credit: Henon & Guyot 1970, Bruno 1973, Hitzl & Henon 1977.
8. What it does not do: no orbit at mu > 0 is computed anywhere (the paper has no integration and no continuation); the matching of the hyperbola to the arc is done "in the most rough manner" (p.260). It is a formula-and-tables paper. Existence at mu > 0 is by Perko 1976 (simple and composite second-species solutions exist for arcs of the families S).

## 1. Definitions and equations (READ pp.255-263)

### 1.1 Setup (READ pp.255-256)

P1 and P2 of masses 1 - mu and mu rotate in a circle about their common centre of mass; P3 has infinitesimal mass. Sidereal and synodic frames as in Bruno 1972 (Bruno's own notation). For mu = 0 the problem reduces to P1-P3 two-body motion, and collisions of P2 with P3 split the solutions into arcs: "Those arcs, which are contained between two collisions with the body P2, are called by Henon (1968) orbits with consecutive collisions."

Let the semi-major axis be a and eccentricity e of the ellipse carrying a symmetric arc, collisions at times t1 < t2 with eccentric anomalies E1, E2, t0 = (t1 + t2)/2. By symmetry, at t0 the three bodies P1, P2, P3 lie on the X1 axis (sidereal; Figure 1, p.256). Switches (READ p.256, same as Henon 1968, here with Bruno's symbols):
- eps'' = +1 if the apse of P3 at t0 (on the positive X1 semi-axis) is the pericentre, -1 if it is the apocentre.
- eps = +1 if X1(P2) > 0 at t0, -1 if X1(P2) < 0.
- eps' = the sign of the direction of motion of P3 on the ellipse (the paper says "Recall that eps' is the sign..."; +1 direct, -1 retrograde by Henon 1968 eq. 4).
- tau = (t2 - t1)/2, eta = |E2 - E1|/2.

### 1.2 The system and the elimination (eqs. 1 and 2, READ p.256)

- cos tau = eps a (cos eta - eps'' e),
- sin tau = eps eps' a sqrt(1 - e^2) sin eta,
- tau = a^(3/2) (eta - eps'' e sin eta). (eq. 1)

First two together: 1 = a (1 - eps'' e cos eta) (eq. 2). Printed difference from Henon 1968 eq. 27: the first equation is written cos tau = eps a (cos eta - eps'' e) here, whereas Henon 1968 (and Hitzl-Henon) have eps a (eps'' cos eta - e). The two forms coincide only when eps'' = 1; I used Henon's form, which closes every row of his tables (COMPUTED, 324 rows), so the printed first line of eq. (1) is, I believe, a slip in this translation (or a different eps'' convention for eta; not confirmed). Henon eliminated a and e and got a transcendental equation in tau, eta, eps, which he solved numerically (Figure 2 = Henon's diagram, p.257).

### 1.3 Figure 2 and Figure 3 (READ pp.257-258)

- Figure 2: "Henon's diagram. Each curve represents one family of symmetric orbits with consecutive collisions", plotted in (tau/pi, eta/pi) for tau/pi in 0 to 5.5 and eta/pi in 0 to 6.5 with the families A0, A1, A2, A3, A4; B1 to B5; C12, C23, C24, C25, C34, C35, C36, C45, C46, C56 labelled and the sign triples (eps, eps', eps'') beside branches.
- Egorov 1958 (section 10) also sought arcs with consecutive collisions near the Moon, with two conditions: (1) eta < 2 pi (collision on the first circuit) and (2) eps'' = +1 (pericentre at t0). He found one piece each of A0 and B1 (his C^B_H and D) and a countable set of pieces of A1, A2 and B2 (D'_k, D_k) (Figure 3, p.258). For those pieces "periodic orbits around the Moon with near-Earth passages are not possible", and Egorov concluded that this type of orbit is generally impossible; "it is shown below that that type of orbit is possible for three solutions of Henon which were not found by Egorov."
- Perko 1976 (READ p.258): simple solutions (one arc) and composite (several arcs) second-species periodic solutions exist for arcs of the families S. Bruno restricts himself to simple ones. Composite ones (S arcs and/or T arcs) "lead to many more possible periodic orbits around P2 but their analysis is complicated."

### 1.4 Collision velocity (eqs. 3 and 4, READ pp.258-259)

From Bruno 1972 part 4-B, the synodic velocity of P3 at collision:
- V1 = xdot1 = e eps'' sqrt(a) sin eta,
- V2 = xdot2 = eps' sqrt(a (1 - e^2)) - 1. (eq. 3)
- V^2 = V1^2 + V2^2 = 3 - C = 3 - 2 eps' sqrt(a (1 - e^2)) - 1/a, so C = 2 eps' sqrt(a(1 - e^2)) + 1/a (eq. 4); the exit velocity from P2 is V1' = -V1, V2' = V2.

COMPUTED check: with V1 and V2 as in eq. 3, V1^2 + V2^2 = 3 - C identically using 1 = a (1 - eps'' e cos eta) (verified numerically on the Table IV rows, errors below 1e-4).

### 1.5 Perturbation near the small body (section 2, eqs. 5 to 15, READ pp.259-262)

Near P2 the pull of P1 is neglected and P3 moves on a symmetric hyperbola with focus P2 (Figure 4), semi-major axis a_*, eccentricity e_*, true anomaly v, local coordinates xi1, xi2:
- r = a_* (e_*^2 - 1)/(1 + e_* cos v), xi1 = r cos v, xi2 = r sin v (eq. 5); nearest point on the xi1 axis: |xi1^0| = a_* (e_* - 1) (eq. 6).
- At infinity 1 + e_* cos v_inf = 0, so e_* = -1/cos v_inf > 1 (eq. 7); energy gives V^2 = mu/a_*, so a_* = mu/V^2 (eq. 8); from Figure 4, |cos v_inf| = |V1|/V (eq. 9).
- Closest approach on the xi1 axis: xi1^0 = (mu/V^2)(V/|V1| - 1) sgn V1. Define W = xi1^0/mu = (1/V^2)(V/|V1| - 1) sgn V1 (eq. 10).
- Rough matching: the incoming velocity at infinity on the hyperbola equals the collision velocity (V1, V2) of the arc and the outgoing one equals the exit velocity; "more accurate matching forms the object of many investigations (e.g. Breakwell and Perko, 1974)." So the smallest distance of the periodic orbit from P2 is xi1^0 = mu W with (eq. 11) W = (1/V^2)(V/(sqrt(a) e |sin eta|) - 1) eps'' sgn(sin eta), and by eq. 2 (eps'' e cos eta = 1 - 1/a, so e|sin eta| = sqrt(2a - 1 + a^2 e^2 - a^2)/a) (eq. 12): W = (1/V^2)(V/sqrt(2 - 1/a + a e^2 - a) - 1) eps'' sgn(sin eta). Hence |W| is a single-valued function of (a, e, eps').
- Egorov's naming (READ p.261): solutions with W < 0 are "interior orbits" and with W > 0 "exterior orbits" (Figure 7: an interior orbit passes P2 on the side towards P1, an exterior orbit on the far side).
- Validity (READ pp.261-262): expand the Hamiltonian H (eq. 13, in local coordinates xi = x - 1, eta1 = y1, eta2 = y2 - 1; the Hamiltonian is H = (1/2)(y1^2 + y2^2) + x2 y1 - x1 y2 - (1-mu)/sqrt(x1^2 + x2^2) - mu/sqrt((x1-1)^2 + x2^2) + mu x1) in the exponents q1 = ord(xi), q2 = ord(eta), q3 = ord(mu) and take the Newton polyhedron (Figure 5). The two-body problem P2-P3 is the edge Gamma_1^(1) spanning (0, 2, 0) and (-1, 0, 1); the short-cut Hamiltonian is H_hat = (1/2)(eta1^2 + eta2^2) - mu/sqrt(xi1^2 + xi2^2) (eq. 14). It is a first approximation where mu/|xi| ~ |eta|^2 >> |xi|^2. With |xi| = mu |W| and |eta| = V this gives 1/|W| ~ V^2 >> mu^2 W^2, that is |W| << mu^(-2/3) and |W|/V << mu^(-1). "For small mu these inequalities are satisfied if |W| <~ 1, |W| <~ V" (eq. 15). Also valid for a symmetric orbit generated by a pair of arcs M and M-bar belonging to a family T_N.

### 1.6 Large eccentricity, e = 1 (section 3, READ pp.262-263)

The minimal distance from P1 is rho = a (1 - e); for arcs of order-unity a the orbits that pass near P1 have e near 1, so only small pieces of the families S near e = 1 matter, and W can be approximated by its value at e = 1, where V^2 = 3 - 1/a and (eq. after 15): |W(a)| = (1/(3 - 1/a)) [ sqrt(3 - 1/a)/sqrt(2 - 1/a) - 1 ]. "A not too difficult analysis shows that on the half line 0.5 < a <= infinity the function |W| monotonically decreases from +infinity to gamma = (sqrt(3)/sqrt(2) - 1)/3 = 0.0749149." Table I (below) gives |W(a)| and V(a); Figure 6 plots them. |W| < 1 < V for a > 0.57045 (eq. 16), so (15) is satisfied and eq. (12) applies.

Body of radius rho2 in the normalised units: put |W0| = rho2/mu; a periodic orbit with |W| < |W0| passes through P2 and is not realisable. Launch at a distance rho0 from P1: realisable orbits have rho = a(1 - e) > rho0 if rho0 is the radius of the body P1, and rho <= rho0 if rho0 is the radius of a satellite orbit round P1. (READ p.265.)
