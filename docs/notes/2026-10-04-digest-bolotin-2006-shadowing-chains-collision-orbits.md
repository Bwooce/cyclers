## 1. The setting, exactly

### 1.1 Motivating system (section 1, pp.235-236)
Plane elliptic restricted three-body problem: Sun mass 1, Jupiter mass mu, Asteroid of negligible mass in R^2; centre of mass of Sun and Jupiter fixed at 0; Jupiter's orbit is an ellipse with focus at 0 and eccentricity epsilon in [0, 1); period 2 pi and major semi-axis (1 + mu)^(-1/3); positions u(t) (Jupiter) and -mu u(t) (Sun). Eq. 1.1:
  H(q, p, t, mu, epsilon) = |p|^2/2 - 1/|q + mu u(t)| - mu/|q - u(t)|,   p, q in R^2, t in T = R/2 pi Z.                                         (1.1)
"For the circular problem with eccentricity epsilon = 0, system (1.1) is autonomous in the rotating coordinate frame, and hence has Jacobi's integral h = H - G, where G = q ^ p is the angular momentum." For mu = 0 it is the Kepler problem E(q,p) = |p|^2/2 - |q|^(-1) with energy E < 0 (ellipses) and angular momentum G not equal to 0.
Verbatim motivation: "For small mu > 0 solutions of system (1.1) are close to the solutions of the Kepler problem, because the influence of Jupiter is small. However, when the Asteroid enters a O(sqrt mu)-neighborhood N_mu of Jupiter, the gravitational force of Jupiter becomes large and hence it can not be ignored. Near Jupiter the orbit of the Asteroid will be close to the hyperbolic orbit of the Kepler problem Jupiter-Asteroid. After interacting with Jupiter, the Asteroid exists N_mu, and then moves again along almost Kepler orbit, until the next almost collision with Jupiter. Hence the orbit of the Asteroid shadows a chain of orbits of the Kepler problem Sun-Asteroid. Periodic almost collision orbits of this type were studied by Poincare who named them second species solutions."
Stated goal (p.236): "to prove the existence of many almost collision periodic and chaotic orbits for the elliptic restricted 3 body problem with eccentricity epsilon not equal to 0. We will consider systems with more general Hamiltonians than (1.1)."

### 1.2 The general Hamiltonian (section 2, p.236), Eq. 2.1
  H(q, p, t, mu) = |p|^2/2 - V(q, t, mu) - mu/|q - u_mu(t)|,   p, q in R^2, t in T,                                                            (2.1)
"depending on a small parameter mu in [0, mu_0). We assume that u_mu : T -> D and V in C^3(D x T x [0, mu_0)), where D is an open set in R^2. Then system (H_mu) is a singular perturbation of system (H_0). A particular case is the Hamiltonian of the elliptic 3 body problem. Then V has a singularity at the Sun, so D is the complement of a neighborhood of 0 in R^2."
So the answers to "what is the singularity" in the main setting are:
- ONE Newtonian singularity mu/|q - u_mu(t)| with coefficient mu (so its strength is the small parameter), located at u_mu(t): MOVING, time-periodic (u_mu : T -> D, period 2 pi), allowed to depend on mu (C^3 smooth in what is stated for V; u_mu is assumed only "u_mu : T -> D").
- A smooth (C^3 in D x T x [0, mu_0)) time-periodic potential V(q, t, mu), whose own singularities (the "Sun") lie outside D; "All orbits we consider lie in D" and "Since we will not be using any global methods, the behavior of V in the complement of D does not matter."
- Time: periodic with period 2 pi (normalised), t in T. Phase space: planar (d = 2).
- The unperturbed system (H_0) is mu = 0: the singularity disappears; for the elliptic problem it is Kepler.
Change of variables (2.2): (q, p) -> (q - u_mu(t), p) puts the singularity at the origin:
  H_mu(q, p, t) = (1/2)|p - u'_mu(t)|^2 - (1/2)|u'_mu(t)|^2 - V_mu(q, t) - mu/|q|,   V_mu(q, t) = V(q + u_mu(t), t, mu).                                       (2.2)
"Note that H_mu not equal to H: the Hamiltonian is not invariant under time dependent symplectic transformations. The physical meaning of H_mu is energy relative to Jupiter." Action (2.3): A_mu(gamma) = integral_(t_0)^(t_1) ( (1/2)|gamma' + u'_mu|^2 + V_mu(gamma, t) + mu/|gamma| ) dt.

### 1.3 Remarks on generality (p.238), verbatim
Remark 1: "The results of this paper hold also for systems with Hamiltonian
  H(q, p, t, mu) = (1/2) a(q, t, mu) |p|^2 + b(q, t, mu) . p - V(q, t, mu) - mu sum_(i=1)^k a_i(q, t, mu)/|q - u_i(t, mu)|,                              (2.9)
with a and a_i positive functions. This means that the Asteroid moves in the gravitational field of several planets u_i(t, mu). The proofs in this more general case are essentially the same. However, for simplicity of notation we consider only H of the form (2.1)."
Remark 2: "Theorem 2.1 also holds for systems with d > 2 degrees of freedom. The only difference is in the proof of Proposition 5.1. The proof we give is based on the Levi-Civita regularization which does not work for d > 2. For d = 3 a similar KS-regularization works (see [7]), while for d > 3 another method is needed."
Remark 3: "A result analogous to Theorem 2.1 is proved in [7] for autonomous systems with H of the form (2.9). For autonomous systems nondegeneracy of a collision chain is defined for fixed Hamiltonian H = h in terms of the Maupertuis action functional. The constructed periodic shadowing orbits all lie in the same level H = h. The period T can not be prescribed, but depends on h. Our Theorem 2.1 gives shadowing orbits with fixed period T = 2 pi m and does not apply to autonomous systems.
The circular restricted 3-body problem with eccentricity epsilon = 0 and small mu > 0 has autonomous Hamiltonian of the form (2.9) in the rotating coordinate frame. The Hamiltonian equals the Jacobi integral h. The results of [7] apply for h in (-3/2, sqrt 2) and give an infinite number of orbits periodic with respect to the rotating coordinate frame and having the prescribed value h of the Jacobi integral. ... Orbits obtained in these papers are quasiperiodic with respect to the fixed coordinate frame except when T/2 pi is rational. If T/2 pi is rational, the periodic orbits fill a torus T^2 in R^4 x T. By the perturbation methods of Poincare, for the elliptic problem with small eccentricity 0 < epsilon << mu, this torus disintegrates into at least 2 periodic orbits. On the other hand, if T/2 pi is Diophantine, then for 0 < epsilon << mu we can apply KAM theory and get quasiperiodic solutions of the elliptic problem. For fixed epsilon > 0 and small mu such approach does not work.
The results of the present paper work for fixed epsilon > 0 and small mu > 0. It is essential that Jupiter's orbit is non-circular."
COMPUTED cross-check of the range: Bolotin's h in (-3/2, sqrt 2) with the Casoliva/Barrabes-Gomez relation C_J = -2h (Casoliva Eq. 3, 5; INFERRED to apply to this h = Jacobi integral, since H in the rotating frame equals the Jacobi integral) gives C_J in (-2 sqrt 2, 3) = (-2.8284, 3), which is the interval of Barrabes & Gomez 2002 Eq. 27 for all p/q. The upper bound 3 is the same as "the collision arc needs relative speed squared 3 - C_J > 0" (project note `2026-10-04-897-technique-synthesis-papers-to-problems.md` section 0 item 2); so Bolotin's theory, like Barrabes & Gomez, is confined to C_J < 3.

## 2. The theorems with their hypotheses

### 2.1 Collision orbits and chains (pp.236-237)
Collision orbit: Gamma = set of W^(1,2) curves gamma : [t_0, t_1] -> R^2 with gamma(t_0) = gamma(t_1) = 0, gamma(t) not equal to 0 for t in (t_0, t_1); "A curve gamma in Gamma which is an orbit of system (H_0) such that gamma'(t_0) not equal to 0, gamma'(t_1) not equal to 0, will be called a collision orbit." (Orbits of (H_0), i.e. in coordinates relative to the moving singularity.)
Goal: "to find for small mu > 0 almost collision 2 pi m-periodic orbits of system (H_mu) shadowing a chain of n collision orbits of the system (H_0)."
Chains: Pi = set of chains sigma = (sigma_i)_(i=1)^n in Gamma^n, collision curves sigma_i : [t_(i-1), t_i] -> R^2, t_0 = t_n - 2 pi m < t_1 < ... < t_n; action A_0(sigma) = sum_i A_0(sigma_i). Critical points of A_0 are chains of collision orbits of (H_0) with |v_i^+| = |v_i^-|, where v_i^+ = sigma'_(i-1)(t_i) and v_i^- = sigma'_i(t_i) (the sign convention: v_i^- = incoming? see below). Equivalently h_i^- = h_i^+ = h_i with
  h_i^+/- = H_0(0, v_i^+/- + u'(t_i), t_i) = (1/2)|v_i^+/-|^2 - (1/2)|u'(t_i)|^2 - V_0(0, t_i).                                                       (2.5)
First variation (2.6): dA_0(sigma_i) = h_(i-1)^- dt_(i-1) - h_i^+ dt_i, dA_0(sigma) = sum_i (h_i^- - h_i^+) dt_i.
(Sign and index convention. The text under (2.4) prints v_i^+ = sigma'_(i-1)(t_i) and v_i^- = sigma'_i(t_i) for chains with sigma_i defined on [t_(i-1), t_i]; the index i - 1 looks off by one. From (2.6), (3.1) and (3.3), v^+ is the ARRIVAL (incoming) velocity of a collision arc at the singularity, v^- the DEPARTURE (outgoing) velocity of the next arc, so h_i^+ is the arrival energy and h_i^- the departure energy. INFERRED, from those equations.)
Nondegenerate chain (p.237), verbatim: "We say that sigma is a nondegenerate periodic collision chain if it is a nondegenerate critical point of the functional A_0 : Pi -> R. Hence the time moments t_(i-1) < t_i are not conjugate along sigma_i. Then for any (tau_1, ..., tau_n) close to (t_1, ..., t_n) and any i = 1, ..., n, there exists a unique collision orbit gamma_i : [tau_(i-1), tau_i] -> R^2 of system (H_0) which is close to sigma_i. Then A_0(gamma_i) = S_i(tau_(i-1), tau_i) is a C^2 function, and the action of the chain gamma = (gamma_i) is
  A_0(gamma) = sum_(i=1)^n S_i(tau_(i-1), tau_i) = S^(n)(tau_1, ..., tau_n).                                                                    (2.7)
Thus sigma is a nondegenerate critical point iff: Each sigma_i : [t_(i-1), t_i] -> R^2 is a collision orbit of system (H_0) and t_(i-1) < t_i are not conjugate along sigma_i. (t_1, ..., t_n) is a nondegenerate critical point of S^(n)."
In words (INFERRED, translation): S_i(tau_(i-1), tau_i) is the action of the unique Kepler-type arc from the singularity at time tau_(i-1) to the singularity at time tau_i, i.e. a Lambert-type two-point boundary solution between the two moon positions; the chain is a sequence of such arcs, closed over time 2 pi m, whose switching times are a critical point of the summed action with a nonsingular Hessian. Critical point means h_i^- = h_i^+ at every encounter: equal relative energy (equal flyby speed in and out).
Changing direction condition: v_i^+ not equal to +/- v_i^- at each collision.
THEOREM 2.1 (p.237), verbatim: "For any nondegenerate periodic collision chain sigma of system (H_0) satisfying the changing direction condition, there exists mu_0 > 0 such that for any mu in (0, mu_0) there exists a unique 2 pi m-periodic orbit gamma_mu : R -> R^2 \ {0} of system (H_mu) which is O(mu)-shadowing the chain sigma and avoids collisions with Jupiter. More precisely, there exist constants a, b > 0 such that
  |gamma_mu(t) - sigma_i(t)| <= a mu,   |gamma_mu(t)| >= b mu   for t_(i-1) <= t <= t_i."                                                          (2.8)
"An analog of Theorem 2.1 holds for collision chains which correspond to topologically nondegenerate critical points of S^(n), but then the shadowing orbit will be non-unique in general."
Note on hypothesis size (INFERRED): the theorem is for any n and m but mu_0 depends on the chain (no quantification); "uniform" version below removes the dependence on the chain.

### 2.2 Infinite chains (section 3, pp.238-240)
Sigma = collision orbits gamma : [t_0, t_1] -> R^2 of (H_0) with t_0 < t_1 non-conjugate; 2-dimensional manifold, projection pi : Sigma -> R^2_+, gamma -> (t_0, t_1) a local diffeomorphism; finite subcovering {Sigma_k}_(k in K), U_k subsets W_k bounded away from the boundary by delta (compact modulo T translation); constants M, L > 0 with
  ||S_k||_(C^2(U_k)) <= M,   L < |v_k^+| < L^(-1) in U_k.                                                                                          (3.2), (3.3)
First derivatives (3.1): D_1 S_k(t_0, t_1) = H_0(0, gamma'(t_0) + u'(t_0), t_0), D_2 S_k(t_0, t_1) = -H_0(0, gamma'(t_1) + u'(t_1), t_1). Relative Hamiltonian at a collision (3.4): h_i^- = h_i^+ = h_i = -D_2 S_(k_i)(t_(i-1), t_i) = D_1 S_(k_(i+1))(t_i, t_(i+1)).
Hessian T_kappa(tau) = A''_kappa(tau) is tridiagonal with entries (3.5): D_12 S_(k_i)(t_(i-1), t_i); D_22 S_(k_i)(t_(i-1), t_i) + D_11 S_(k_(i+1))(t_i, t_(i+1)); D_12 S_(k_(i+1))(t_i, t_(i+1)); ||T|| <= 4M (3.6). "c-nondegenerate": ||T_kappa^(-1)(tau)|| <= c (3.7). Uniform changing direction (3.8): |cos angle(v_+, v_-)| < 1 - nu. "(c, nu)-nondegenerate".
THEOREM 3.1 (p.240): "There exists mu_0 = mu_0(c, nu, U) > 0 such that for any mu in (0, mu_0), any n, m in N, any kappa in K^n, and any (c, nu)-nondegenerate critical point tau in X_kappa of A_kappa, system (H_mu) has a unique 2 pi m-periodic orbit gamma_mu : R -> R^2 \ {0} which is O(mu)-shadowing the collision chain sigma corresponding to tau." (a = a(c, nu, U), b = b(c, nu, U).)
THEOREM 3.2 (p.240): "There exists mu_0 = mu_0(c, nu, U) > 0 such that for any mu in (0, mu_0), any kappa in K^Z, and any (c, nu)-nondegenerate critical point tau in X_kappa of A_kappa, system (H_mu) has an orbit gamma_mu : R -> R^2 \ {0} which is O(mu)-shadowing the collision chain sigma corresponding to tau." (bi-infinite chains; chaotic orbits.)
Key point: mu_0 is independent of the length n and period m; the uniform nondegeneracy (3.7)-(3.8) is what the infinite version needs.

### 2.3 Twist-map reformulation (section 4, pp.240-243)
Twist condition: D_12 S_k not equal to 0. g_k(t_0, t_1) = (t_0, h_0), h_0 = D_1 S_k(t_0, t_1); twist map f_k : V_k -> A (annulus T x R), f_k(t_0, h_0) = (t_1, h_1), h_1 = -D_2 S_k(t_0, t_1): the collision-to-collision map (time and relative energy at one collision to the next), symplectic.
LEMMA 4.1 (p.241): for a periodic orbit of the twist maps, f_(k_n) o ... o f_(k_1)(x_0) = x_0, "the corresponding sequence tau is a nondegenerate critical point of A_kappa iff the periodic orbit is nondegenerate: det(D(f_(k_n) o ... o f_(k_1))(x_0) - I) not equal to 0." So nondegeneracy of a chain = nonsingular Poincare map of the composed collision-to-collision twist maps. This is the computable form of the hypothesis.
Changing direction in these terms: w_(k_i)^+(x_i) not equal to +/- w^-_(k_(i+1))(x_(i+1)).
LEMMA 4.2 (p.241): if the skew product F over the Bernoulli shift has a hyperbolic compact invariant set Lambda, then every orbit gives a c-nondegenerate critical point. THEOREM 4.1 (p.241-242): hyperbolic compact invariant set plus the changing direction condition (3.8-type) gives mu_0, a, b with a shadowing orbit for each orbit of F: "Thus system (H_mu) has a chaotic invariant set Lambda_mu. One can show that one pair of Lyapunov exponents of any solution in Lambda_mu is of order |ln mu|, while the other pair is close to the Lyapunov exponents of an orbit in Lambda."
Example, the elliptic problem (p.242), verbatim in substance: "For the elliptic 3 body problem with small eccentricity epsilon, system (H_0) is O(epsilon)-close to an autonomous system in the rotating frame. Then V_k = T x J_k and each map f_k : V_k -> A is almost integrable:
  f_k(t, h) = (t + omega_k(h), h) + mu (D_2 F_k(t, h), -D_1 F_k(t, h)) + O(epsilon^2),     [as printed, with omega_k'(h) not equal to 0 in J_k]
For the circular 3 body problem h is the Jacobi integral." Existence of a hyperbolic invariant set is a transcendental problem related to exponentially small splitting of separatrices; no finite Taylor expansion in epsilon is sufficient. Poincare: if F_k(t, h) = sum_n e^(int) a_kn(h) is an infinite series, f_k has many nondegenerate periodic orbits for small epsilon; but in applications F_k is a Fourier polynomial (in the elliptic problem F_k has 2 harmonics), so one uses RANDOM sequences of maps: approximate first integral Phi_k = h + epsilon phi_k, phi_k = sum_(n not equal to 0) e^(int) b_kn(h), b_kn = i n a_kn/(e^(i n omega_k(h)) - 1).
THEOREM 4.2 (p.243): "Suppose that J_k intersect J_l is not empty for a pair k, l in K and the integrals Phi_k, Phi_l are independent in V_k intersect V_l: there is n not equal to 0 such that b_kn(h) is not identically b_ln(h) in J_k intersect J_l. Then for small epsilon not equal to 0 the skew product map F has a chaotic hyperbolic invariant set in {k, l}^Z x (V_k intersect V_l) with Lyapunov exponents of order sqrt(epsilon)." (Proved in [6]; in [5] the conditions are verified for the elliptic 3 body problem with small eccentricity.) (The hypothesis is printed as J_k intersect J_l not empty, "J_k cap J_l not equal to empty set"; I read it as: the two energy intervals J_k, J_l overlap. The printed words "Suppose that J_k ∩ J_l ≠ ∅ for a pair k, l in K" are all the paper says about it; INFERRED that J_k is the energy range of the twist map f_k.)
COROLLARY 4.1 (p.243), verbatim: "For any h_0 in (-3/2, sqrt 2) there exist C > 0, epsilon_0 > 0 such that for any epsilon in (0, epsilon_0) there exists mu_0 > 0 such that for mu in (0, mu_0) the elliptic 3 body problem (1.1) has chaotic and periodic almost collision orbits in the domain |h - h_0| < C epsilon." Also: "Using the ideas of [18, 17] one can also show that there exist 'diffusion' orbits with drifting Jacobi constant."
(Here mu_0 depends on epsilon, so mu must be small compared with a power of epsilon not stated; INFERRED from the quantifier order "for any epsilon there exists mu_0".)

## 3. The method (sections 5-11, pp.243-259)
1. Local connection (section 5): Lemma 5.1 and 5.2: for q_+/- on a small circle S_r, and t_+ < t_0 < t_-, unique collision orbits gamma_+/- of (H_0) from/to the origin; action (5.1) A_0(gamma_+/-) = r^2/(2 tau) -/+ q . u'(t_0) + tau(|u'(t_0)|^2/2 + V_0(0, t_0)) + O_2(r) and relative energy (5.2). Midpoint time and energy of a passage O(mu)-close to the collision: t_0 = (t_+ + t_-)/2 + O(r), h_0 = 2 r^2 (t_- - t_+)^(-2) - |u'(t_0)|^2/2 - V_0(0, t_0) + O(r) (5.8) (COMPUTED: consistent with the Lemma 5.1 relation h = r^2/(2 tau^2) - ... with tau = (t_- - t_+)/2). Velocity at the collision: gamma'_+/-(t_0) = -/+ r^(-1) lambda(x_0) q + O(r), lambda(t_0, h_0) = sqrt(2 (V_0(0, t_0) + h_0) + |u'(t_0)|^2) (5.10): speed at the collision equals sqrt(2 (V_0 + h) + |u'|^2), i.e. relative speed of the Kepler arc at the moving singularity.
2. Proposition 5.1 (p.245): for small r > 0 and mu_0, for (q_+, q_-, t_0, h_0) in P x N (angle condition (5.13): |cos angle(q_+, q_-)| <= 1 - nu/2, i.e. no reflection or exact pass-through), unique orbit of (H_mu) connecting q_+ to q_- inside B_r with relative energy h_0; endpoints and action depend C^2 on mu up to mu = 0 ((5.14)); nondegeneracy (5.16). Proved in sections 9-11 by Levi-Civita regularisation (q = z^2/2, p = p_z/z-bar), extension to an autonomous system by adding (t, h) as conjugate variables (9.2), time reparametrisation by |z|^2, a normally hyperbolic manifold N of equilibria with eigenvalues 0, +/- lambda (Lemma 10.1), a boundary-value problem solved with a contraction on the stable/unstable manifolds (Lemma 10.2, 11.1, 11.2 (Shilnikov-type integral equation method)), and the passage time T = -ln(mu)/(2 lambda(x_0)) + bounded (10.14): the time spent near the singularity scales as |ln mu|. 
3. Section 6-7: periodic chain as a critical point of an action functional J_mu = J_0 + mu J^_mu on chains of connecting orbits (6.3); Lemma 6.1: a critical point with (6.4) "reflections forbidden" defines a 2 pi m-periodic orbit; Lemma 6.2 relates nondegenerate critical points of A_0 and J_0; implicit function theorem gives Theorem 2.1. Section 7: finite-dimensional approximation Y_kappa of Omega; Lemma 7.1-7.5 (Lyapunov-Schmidt reduction, quantitative implicit function theorem, Lemma 7.4) give uniform bounds ||(f_0'')^(-1)|| <= C_4, hence Theorem 3.1. Section 8: infinite chains in l_infinity, formal action; same proof.
So the method is variational: periodic orbits of the time-periodic system as critical points of the action, with the almost-collision passages replaced by exact local solutions from the regularised normally hyperbolic structure.
Regularisation is for d = 2 (Levi-Civita); the proof of Proposition 5.1 is "independent of all previous sections except section 5."

## 4. Checks and consistencies (COMPUTED / READ)
1. The Jacobi-integral range h in (-3/2, sqrt 2) equals C_J in (-2 sqrt 2, 3) = the Barrabes & Gomez interval bounds (section 1.3).
2. Statement quantifiers: Theorem 2.1 mu_0 depends on the chain; Theorem 3.1 mu_0 depends only on (c, nu, U); Corollary 4.1 mu_0 depends on epsilon.
3. Distances: (2.8) says the shadowing orbit passes within O(mu) of the singularity (constant b), so "closest approach of order mu in units of the orbit's radius". INFERRED consequence: for Titania and Oberon at mass ratios of order 1e-5 (the Uranian moons' GM are about 2e2 km^3/s^2 against about 5.8e6 for Uranus; from general knowledge, to be checked against the project registry) and orbit radii 4.4e5 and 5.8e5 km, mu a_moon is of order 10 km, far inside the moons' radii (788 km and 761 km, general knowledge). The theorem's O(mu) almost-collision orbit is therefore not a physical flyby (altitude 1.4e3 to 2.0e3 km in `#890`, which is of order 3 to 5e-3 of the orbit radius, about 100 times mu a); this is the same statement as the project's note that the first-order chain is outside validity (`2026-10-04-897-technique-synthesis-papers-to-problems.md` T3).

## 5. What this does and does not cover for #890

### 5.1 The question
Can the planar problem of a planet with TWO moons on circular orbits of different periods be put in Bolotin's setting, for example in the frame rotating with one moon, where the other moon becomes a time-periodic perturbation that is a second Newtonian singularity and not a smooth term?

### 5.2 Answer from the paper (READ)
Yes for the form of the Hamiltonian, by Remark 1 (p.238, Eq. 2.9): the paper explicitly allows "several planets u_i(t, mu)", moving, time-periodic, each entering as mu a_i(q, t, mu)/|q - u_i(t, mu)| with positive a_i, together with a general kinetic term (1/2) a |p|^2 + b . p, whose b . p term is the form a rotating frame produces. It says "the proofs in this more general case are essentially the same" and does not give them; the only worked chain nondegeneracy is for the single-planet Kepler case (ref [5], Bolotin, "Second species solutions of the elliptic restricted 3 body problem", Celest. Mech. Dyn. Astron., in print 2006), which I could not find in the corpus index (grep of `docs/notes/CORPUS_INDEX.md` 2026-10-04: no entry).
### 5.3 The two-moon model in this setting (INFERRED unless marked)
- Frame rotating with moon 1 (angular rate n_1): moon 1 is fixed (u_1 = constant, hence time-independent, nothing wrong with Bolotin's u_i(t)), moon 2 moves on a circle with angular rate n_2 - n_1 relative to the frame; the Hamiltonian is time-periodic with the SYNODIC period 2 pi/|n_2 - n_1| (normalised to 2 pi). This holds for ANY pair of circular periods; commensurability of n_1 and n_2 is not needed for the Hamiltonian to be periodic in that frame. The Coriolis term is a b . p term (Eq. 2.9); the central planet is the "Sun" singularity of V outside D; J2 and other smooth effects are in V (C^3 in D x T x [0, mu_0)); moons that the orbit never approaches can also be in V.
- The orbits the theorem produces are 2 pi m-periodic in THE ROTATING FRAME (period m synodic periods). Periodic in the inertial frame needs in addition that m synodic periods contain an integer number of moon-1 revolutions (the same caveat as Remark 3: periodic in the rotating frame, quasi-periodic in the fixed frame unless the rational condition holds). READ for the circular single-moon remark, INFERRED for two moons.
- Both moons' masses must enter with the same small parameter scaling (the sum in (2.9) has a common factor mu). Two moons with different mass ratios can be written mu a_i with a_i = (mass_i/mu); INFERRED fine as long as the ratio is fixed.

### 5.4 What is missing (INFERRED; each is a gap between the paper and a result about the physical Uranian system)

2. The chain must be nondegenerate at mu = 0 with all collisions at moving singularities. For a chain visiting ONE circular moon the Hamiltonian in the corotating frame is autonomous, the collision-to-collision map has an energy-and-phase symmetry (a time-shift symmetry), and the Hessian of S^(n) has a null direction; INFERRED degenerate, and Remark 3 states the reason non-circularity is essential in the single-moon case. ### 5.5 Four-sentence answer (also in the hand-back message)
Yes, as to the form of the Hamiltonian: Remark 1 (p.238, Eq. 2.9) of Bolotin 2006 allows several moving planets u_i(t) as separate Newtonian singularities mu a_i/|q - u_i(t)| together with a b . p term, so the two-moon problem in the frame rotating with moon 1 (moon 1 fixed, moon 2 on a circle at the synodic period) is of that form, while the paper proves the single-singularity case and says the multi-planet proofs are "essentially the same". What is missing for a theorem about the real system is: a proof or at least a numerical check that the project's two-moon chain is nondegenerate (nonsingular Hessian of the summed action, det(D(composed twist map) - I) not equal to 0, and the changing-direction condition at each flyby), a quantified mu_0 (the theorem is for small enough mu), and a link between the O(mu) almost-collision passages (inside the moons' bodies at Uranian mass ratios) and physical flybys above the turn-gate floor. Periodicity is in the rotating frame with period m synodic periods; periodicity in the inertial frame needs an extra rationality condition on the moons' periods. The theory therefore supports "orbits of this class exist for small enough masses" and the nondegeneracy diagnostics, not the existence of the `#890` orbit at the physical masses, which remains a numerical result.

## 6. Use for the project (suggestions, INFERRED)
- A diagnostic for any patched-conic or four-body chain (`#890`, `#899` chains with several flybys): the tridiagonal Hessian of the summed Lambert action and the changing-direction angle at each encounter; a vanishing determinant flags a degenerate chain, which explains a failure of continuation in the moons' masses (the `#890` continuation from mass 0.01 to 1 converged: a check that the Hessian is nonsingular along it would be informative).

- n collision orbits per period are covered (any n, m in Theorem 2.1 and 3.1): the published theory for more than one flyby per period that Casoliva et al. (one flyby per period in the cases they continue) do not provide.
