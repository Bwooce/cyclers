## 0. What the paper is

A method paper. Abstract (p.185), compressed: an eight-dimensional regularisation of the general three-body problem, "based on Kustaanheimo-Stiefel regularization of a single binary". Properties claimed: (i) the equations of motion are regular for the two-body collisions R1 tending to 0 or R2 tending to 0 (R1 = |r1 - r3|, R2 = |r2 - r3|, R = |r1 - r2|); (ii) provided that R is at least of the order of R1 or R2, the equations are numerically well behaved for close triple encounters. The condition may need occasional re-labelling of the particles, but "all integrations are performed in regularized variables". Numerical comparison with standard KS shows "improved accuracy per integration step at no extra computing time", and time-reversal tests "indicate that critical triple encounters may now be studied with confidence". A perturbed three-body Hamiltonian formulation is also given (section 6).

So it regularises ONE reference body (m3) against both of the others by two simultaneous KS transformations. The pair m1-m2 is not regularised: it is singular as R tends to 0 but "numerically well behaved" provided the reference body is chosen so that R is not the smallest separation. Section 8 states plainly that the method cannot be used if a particle is massless. This is the "two-pair" form of the task description (two KS maps, one shared reference body); Heggie's form (three KS maps) removes the re-labelling at the cost of more equations.

## 1. The formulation, written out (READ pp.187-193)

### 1.1 Reduction of the order (section 2, pp.187-189)

Masses m1, m2, m3 with coordinates (q'_1, q'_2, q'_3), (q'_4, q'_5, q'_6), (q'_7, q'_8, q'_9) and momenta p'_r (Fig. 1). G = 1. Hamiltonian (eq. 1):
H' = sum_{r=1..9} p'_r^2/(2 m_k) - m1 m3/R1 - m2 m3/R2 - m1 m2/R, with k the integer part of (r + 2)/3,
R1 = |r1 - r3|, R2 = |r2 - r3|, R = |r1 - r2| (eq. 2). Canonical contact transformation (eqs. 4, 5) from the generating function W = sum_{r=1..6} p'_r q_r + sum_{r=1..3} (p'_r + p'_{r+3} + p'_{r+6}) q_{r+6}. Meaning (p.188): (q1, q2, q3) and (q4, q5, q6) are the coordinates of m1 and m2 RELATIVE TO m3; (q7, q8, q9) are the coordinates of m3 (so, with the inverse, the centre of mass appears via p7..p9 as the momenta of the centre of mass); (p1, p2, p3) and (p4, p5, p6) are the momenta of m1 and m2 (unchanged, p_k = p'_k). The coordinates q7..q9 are ignorable; choose p7 = p8 = p9 = 0 (centre-of-mass frame, eq. 6). Hamiltonian (eq. 7):
H = sum_{r=1..6} p_r^2/(2 mu_k3) + (1/m3) sum_{r=1..3} p_r p_{r+3} - m1 m3/R1 - m2 m3/R2 - m1 m2/R,
with mu_k3 = m_k m3/(m_k + m3), R1 = (q1^2+q2^2+q3^2)^(1/2), R2 = (q4^2+q5^2+q6^2)^(1/2), R = |(q1,q2,q3) - (q4,q5,q6)| (eq. 8). Twelve first-order equations remain (eq. 9; p.189). The cross term (p_r p_{r+3})/m3 is the "indirect" term of a heliocentric (here m3-centric) description; it is why the masses appear in denominators.

### 1.2 Eight dimensions (section 3, pp.189-191)

KS needs 4-vectors, so the 3 + 3 coordinates are embedded in 4 + 4 by relabelling q_r to q_{r+1}, p_r to p_{r+1} for r = 4, 5, 6 (eq. 10) and adding redundant variables q4 = q8 = p4 = p8 = 0 (eq. 11). The Hamiltonian (12) is (1) with sums over 8 components, the cross term sum_{r=1..4} p_r p_{r+4}/m3, k the integer part of (r + 3)/4 and R2 = (q5^2+q6^2+q7^2)^(1/2) (eq. 13). Canonical transformation with a separable generating function W = sum_r p_r f_r (eq. 17), f_r = f_r(Q1..Q4) for r at most 4 and f_r(Q5..Q8) for r above 4 (eq. 18); hence q_r = f_r (19) and P_r = sum_j p_j df_j/dQ_r (20), i.e. P = A p with A block diagonal of two 4 x 4 blocks A_1, A_2 of the partial derivatives (eqs. 21 to 23). Orthogonality condition A_k A_k^T = D_k I (eq. 25) gives p_k = A_k^T P_k/D_k (26) and p_k^2 = P_k^2/D_k (28). New Hamiltonian (eq. 29):
H~ = sum_{k=1,2} P_k^2/(2 mu_k3 D_k) + P_1^T A_1 A_2^T P_2/(m3 D_1 D_2) - m1 m3/R1 - m2 m3/R2 - m1 m2/R,
with R1 = (f1^2+f2^2+f3^2)^(1/2), R2 = (f5^2+f6^2+f7^2)^(1/2) (eq. 30). Extended phase space: Q9 = t, P9 = -H, Gamma = P9 + H~ (eq. 31), Hamilton's equations in the new independent variable w (eqs. 32, 33) with dQ9/dw = 1 and dP9/dw = 0, w = t + const.

### 1.3 The regularisation (section 4, pp.192-194)

Time transformation dt = g dtau with g = g(Q1..Q8) (eq. 34), Gamma* = g Gamma (eq. 35), canonical equations in tau (eq. 36). The condition P9 + H~ = 0 on the solution path simplifies the P equations (footnote: "equivalent to the introduction of a control term in the equations of motion (Baumgarte and Stiefel, 1974)", p.192). Choice g = lambda D1 D2 (eq. 37): singularities in Gamma* then sit in D1 D2/R1, D1 D2/R2 and D1 D2/R. Two are removed by D_k = lambda_k R_k (eq. 38), so A_k A_k^T = lambda_k R_k I (eq. 39). KS transformations (eq. 40, p.192):
f1 = Q1^2 - Q2^2 - Q3^2 + Q4^2, f2 = 2(Q1 Q2 - Q3 Q4), f3 = 2(Q1 Q3 + Q2 Q4), f4 = 0, and the same in Q5..Q8 for f5..f8.
Properties (41): sum_{r=1..4} Q_r^2 = R1, sum_{r=5..8} Q_r^2 = R2. Orthogonality for the 4th column (42) gives lambda_k = 4, so D_k = 4 R_k and the time transformation with lambda = 1/16 is
dt = R1 R2 dtau   (eq. 43),
and the Hamiltonian (eq. 44, p.193):
Gamma* = R1 R2 P9 + sum_{k=1,2} (1/(8 mu_k3)) R_l P_k^2 + (1/(16 m3)) P_1^T A_1 A_2^T P_2 - m1 m3 R2 - m2 m3 R1 - m1 m2 R1 R2/R,
l the integer part of (k + 1)/k (so l = 2 for k = 1 and l = 1 for k = 2). COMPUTED check: multiplying (29) by R1 R2 with D_k = 4 R_k reproduces each term of (44) (the kinetic terms become R_l P_k^2/(8 mu_k3), the cross term 1/(16 m3), the potentials m1 m3 R2, m2 m3 R1, m1 m2 R1 R2/R). Equations of motion (eq. 70, p.198), the derivative of (44) with respect to the momenta:
dQ_k/dtau = (1/(4 mu_k3)) R_l P_k + (1/(16 m3)) A_k A_l^T P_l, k = 1, 2.
Regularity (p.193): "These equations are regular for R1 tending to 0 or R2 tending to 0, since Equation (28) implies that |P_k| tends to a constant as R_k tends to 0, hence the mass-point m3 can have collisions or close encounters with either m1 or m2 without causing numerical difficulties." The case R tending to 0 "is still singular", handled by re-labelling so that the reference body is changed; the form of the last term of (44) shows that triple encounters can be integrated provided R is at least of the order of min(R1, R2), where the critical term R1 R2/R is well behaved even for small R.

Remark on why both coordinate and time transformations are needed (p.194): a regular Hamiltonian containing terms R_k^alpha with alpha below 2 gives singular equations of motion; and the time transformation dt = R1^3 R2^3 dtau removes the singularities but is not a regularisation because "the new time becomes infinite at two-body collisions". (Both statements as printed; I have not re-derived them.)

### 1.4 The transformations in practice (section 5, pp.194-195)

Relative coordinates q_k = q'_k - q'_3, p_k = p'_k (45), relabelling (46), redundant variables (47). For q1 non-negative (eq. 48): Q1 = [(|q1| + q1)/2]^(1/2), Q2 = q2/(2 Q1), Q3 = q3/(2 Q1), Q4 = 0; for q1 negative (eq. 49): Q2 = [(|q1| - q1)/2]^(1/2), Q1 = q2/(2 Q2), Q3 = 0, Q4 = q3/(2 Q2) (where in the paper q1 stands for the first component of the vector and |q1| for its length, as the printed bars show). Momenta P_k = A_k p_k (50). Only the 4 x 3 submatrix of A_k is needed (eq. 51, read on the image):
A_1 = 2 [ [Q1, Q2, Q3], [-Q2, Q1, Q4], [-Q3, -Q4, Q1], [Q4, -Q3, Q2] ],
"apart from the factor two ... identical to the transpose of the generalized Levi-Civita matrix used in numerical applications of the standard KS regularization (Aarseth, 1971)". COMPUTED check: the columns are the gradients of f1, f2, f3 of (40) with respect to Q1..Q4. Inverse: q_k = (1/2) A_k^T Q_k (52) and p_k = A_k^T P_k/(4 R_k) (53); relabel back (54); q'_3 = -sum m_k q_k / sum m (55). The first four components are the KS 4-vector with the fourth component of the physical vector set to zero by the gauge Q4 = 0 or Q3 = 0 above; KS has a one-parameter gauge freedom and this is a fixed choice at the start (INFERRED; the paper does not say so).

## 2. The perturbed form and its equations (section 6, pp.195-199)

N particles, with m1, m2, m3 the regularised sub-system and the other N - 3 perturbers. H_N = H_3 + script-R (eq. 56), with script-R (eq. 57) the kinetic energy of the centre of mass of the triple, the kinetic and mutual potential energies of the other particles and the potential coupling the triple to them (v0 the centre-of-mass velocity, eq. 58). Regularisation constraint P9 = -H_3 along the path gives (eq. 59):
Gamma* = R1 R2 (script-R - H_N) + R1 R2 H~_3,
with the equations of motion (60): dQ_k/dtau = d(R1 R2 H~_3)/dP_k; dP_k/dtau = -d(R1 R2 H~_3)/dQ_k - (script-R - H_N) d(R1 R2)/dQ_k - R1 R2 d script-R/dQ_k. "This set of equations is analogous to the two-body formulation of Peters (1968) and is also formally similar to the classical variation of parameters method, where only the equations for the velocities are modified by the perturbation" (p.196). The constraint dP9/dtau = 0 no longer applies: replaced by dP9/dtau = d script-R/dtau (61); alternatively script-R - H_N equals -H_3 (energy relation) and dH_3/dtau can be integrated instead, avoiding an O(N^2) force sum for large N. The perturbing acceleration is computed in Cartesian coordinates: r_i from (63) (r1 = r0 + (m2 + m3) q1/M - m2 q2/M, r2 = r0 - m1 q1/M + (m1 + m3) q2/M, r3 = r0 - m1 q1/M - m2 q2/M, r0 the centre of mass), F_i = -sum_{j=4..N} m_j (r_i - r_j)/|r_i - r_j|^3 (66), d q_k/dQ_k = A_k (67), and (eq. 68)
d script-R/dQ_k = -(A_k/M) [m1 m2 (F2 - F1) (-1)^k + m_k m3 (F_k - F3)], k = 1, 2,
dscript-R/dtau = -(1/4) sum_{k=1,2} R_l P_k^T A_k (F_k - F_3) (71), generalising Aarseth (1971) for the perturbed two-body problem. The centre of mass and the other bodies are integrated in Cartesian time (74): d^2 r0/dt^2 = (1/M) sum m_i F_i; d^2 r_i/dt^2 = -sum_{j != i} m_j (r_i - r_j)/|r_i - r_j|^3 for i = 4..N, "if desired" multiplied by R1 R2 to run in regularised time. The unperturbed formulation reduces the 18 first-order equations to 12; "more than one triple system may be treated at the same time" (pp.198-199), generalising Aarseth 1971.

## 4. Discussion points (section 8, pp.202-204) and the massless-particle statement

- READ p.202: "At first sight it may seem surprising that the new method cannot be used to study three-body systems if one of the particles is massless." Reason given: the eight-dimensional method is closely related to standard KS, which permits one of two particles to be massless; for the isolated pair (m1, m3), dropping all m2 terms from (44) including the factor R2 gives "a Hamiltonian which is identical to the unperturbed KS formulation of Peters (1968)", where the mass singularity 1/mu13 becomes a common factor by using the regularised velocity V1 = P1/mu13 and the binding energy per unit mass h = -P9/mu13, and the first of eqs. (36) gives V1 = 4 dQ1/dtau. In the restricted problem with m2 = 0 the same treatment "fails to make m2 a common factor of all terms involved in the differentiation with respect to Q2". "The special case of a massless reference body is equally critical, since the Hamiltonian appears to contain a non-removable singularity associated with m3, which can only be eliminated by introducing additional equations" (p.203; this is the route Heggie 1974 took, see section 6). "Moreover, these difficulties indicate that the numerical results may be less reliable if the mass ratios are very large" (p.203).
- Why it works (p.203): the time transformation (43) regularises the terms in R1 and R2, leaving the singularity in m1 m2/R; the P_k equations contain two singular terms of the type Q_k R_l/R and R1 R2 A_k R/R^3; from the geometry R is about R1 if R is greater than R2 and R1 is greater than R2, so the condition R greater than min(R1, R2) keeps the critical terms smaller than the regularised ones: "mainly responsible for the improved treatment of close triple encounters". When the masses differ the heaviest particle is the natural reference body. When masses differ significantly "the geometrical re-labelling condition employed here should be replaced by an appropriate dynamical criterion" (p.204).
- Cost (p.203): 24 equations of motion per three-particle sub-system against 22 in the standard KS treatment; force calculations unchanged.
- Generalisation (p.204): add KS maps between further particles and the reference body, but "it is unlikely that more than two particles would normally share the same natural reference body" because of the inverse cube singularity; the formulation is "practically useful only in special systems containing one dominant member". "This difficulty is not present in the new global regularization of Heggie (1974)." Simultaneous sub-systems can use a common time transformation (Bettis and Szebehely 1971, more accurate but less efficient for large numbers) or separate treatment (Aarseth 1971). Two simultaneous binary collisions are related to triple collisions because dt = R1 R2 dtau gives an improper integral if both distances tend to zero together, "of no consequence in practical calculations".

## 5. Comparison with Heggie's global form and with Burdet 1967

Against Heggie 1974 (`docs/notes/2026-10-04-digest-heggie-1974-global-regularisation.md`):
| Item | Aarseth and Zare 1974 | Heggie 1974 |
|---|---|---|
| Relative vectors | two, relative to a chosen body m3 (q1 = q'_1 - q'_3, q2 = q'_2 - q'_3) | three, q1 + q2 + q3 = 0, symmetric in the bodies |
| KS maps | two | three |
| Time transformation | dt = R1 R2 dtau (eq. 43), the product of the two regularised distances | dt = R1 R2 R3 dtau (his eq. 20), or the (22) to (24) variant |
| Hamiltonian | Gamma* eq. 44, cross term P1^T A1 A2^T P2/(16 m3) | polynomial of degree six in Q, degree two in P, three cross terms |
| Regular for | R1 or R2 tending to 0 | collisions between any pair |
| Singular remainder | m1 m2 R1 R2/R; handled by choosing the reference body so R is at least about min(R1, R2) and re-labelling | none (except simultaneous collisions) |
| Massless body | cannot be used (p.202-203) | separate construction: section 4, massless particle with primaries on prescribed Kepler motion, KS on both primary-relative vectors |
| Equations (three-body, unperturbed, centre of mass removed) | 16 (Q, P) + time and energy | 25 for N = 3 (his section 6) |
| Re-labelling | needed in general | not needed |
Both rest on the same idea (enlarging the phase space until the singular distances are coordinates of a KS 4-vector). Heggie states his section 2 "owes much to that of Aarseth and Zare" (acknowledgements). The extension that matters for a massless spacecraft is Heggie's.

Against Burdet 1967 (`docs/notes/2026-10-04-digest-burdet-1967-regularization-two-body-problem.md`): Burdet regularises the two-body problem by changing only the independent variable (dt = sqrt(a) r dE) and keeps Cartesian position with constant elements a and A_i, giving three linear oscillator equations; it is elliptic-only as printed and has no coordinate map. Aarseth and Zare keep the canonical, Hamiltonian form with a KS coordinate map and dt = R1 R2 dtau. In the two-body limit (drop m2) A and Z's equations (44), (70) reduce to the KS Hamiltonian Gamma* = R P9 + P^2/(8 mu) - m1 m3 with R = Q.Q; COMPUTED from it: dQ/dtau = P/(4 mu) and dP/dtau = -2 P9 Q, so d^2Q/dtau^2 = -(P9/(2 mu)) Q, a harmonic oscillator with squared frequency (-H)/(2 mu) for a bound orbit (H the internal energy, negative), hyperbolic (exponential) motion when H is positive. Unlike Burdet's elliptic-only form this covers all energies in one set of equations (this is a statement about the KS oscillator that follows from the printed Hamiltonian; the paper itself does not print it). Burdet's advantage is that it works the same in 2 and 3 dimensions with no map and handles velocity-dependent forces; KS needs the 4D map in 3D and reduces to Levi-Civita in the plane.

## 6. Printed numbers usable as tests

All READ on the page images.
1. Table III (p.201) is the main control; it is cross-checked against Heggie's Table III (his p.226; digest section 2 of the Heggie note). The two papers use different integrators and tolerances' effect on the stopping point; the physical quantity C/3 (one third of the minimum perimeter of the triangle) is therefore an independent two-source quantity: rows 1, 3, 4, 5 agree exactly (4e-4, 1e-7, 3e-4, 6e-6), row 2 differs in the last digit (8e-6 here, 7e-6 in Heggie; the printed digits are single-figure so the difference is within one unit of the rounding), and the symmetric row differs (1e-12 here, 2e-13 in Heggie, the stopping point at which the symmetric collapse is halted depends on the integrator). Heggie's own text says his last case takes fewer steps (139 against 200 here) and "approaches more closely" (2e-13 against 1e-12), which these numbers confirm. R_f/a_f differs between the two papers (this paper 20, 18, 13, 18, 15; Heggie 32, 30, 28, 31, 29) because it depends on when the forward run is stopped (a fixed step count of 250), so R_f/a_f is NOT a portable expected value; the earlier statement in the Heggie digest that it is portable is corrected by a dated note there. The rms return errors (this paper 1e-9, 2e-8, 9e-8, 2e-10, 2e-9, 1e-6) are properties of the integrator, usable only as upper bounds for a similarly tuned code.

## 8. Techniques applicable to the project's problems

All uses INFERRED; nothing built or run.

**#928 (a float regularised propagator and transition matrix for close passes; formulation choice so far: planar Levi-Civita first; KS or a Burdet-type 1/a form for 3D).**
- Does this paper change the formulation choice? No change to the choice; it supports it and adds constraints. (a) The paper states that the eight-dimensional two-KS form cannot treat a massless particle (pp.202-203), so it is not a candidate for the project's spacecraft problem; Heggie's section 4 form is the massless-particle extension and is in any case heavier than needed for one moon. (b) For a massless particle passing one moon, the relevant statement in this paper is that standard KS handles a massless second particle (p.202), with the perturbing bodies entering as accelerations F_i evaluated in Cartesian coordinates, which is the structure of (60), (66), (68) and (71) with the Earth as the perturber. That is the "perturbed KS about the moon" route; its working equations in the form that the project needs are in Aarseth 1971 (Astrophys. Space Sci. 14:118) and Peters 1968 (Bull. Astron. 3:167), which are cited here and in Heggie but are NOT held. The planar-first choice is consistent: KS in the plane is Levi-Civita, and the matrix (51) restricted to the planar block is the Levi-Civita matrix (A and Z say so for the transpose, p.195). (c) For 3D, this paper gives a complete, printed, checkable KS map: forward (40), inverse (48), (49), momentum map (50), (53), the 4 x 3 matrix (51), and the Hamiltonian structure (44), (70). This is a usable alternative to a hand-derived Burdet 1/a form (the #928 entry says that form is hand-derived and unchecked; Burdet's printed form is elliptic-only), because the KS oscillator is valid for all energies (section 5 above). The transition matrix of a redundant-state form must still be projected onto the physical state, and the KS map carries a one-parameter gauge (the fixed 4th-component gauge of eqs. 48 and 49), so the projection is needed for 3D (as the #928 entry already requires). (d) The control term of footnote p.192: the constraint P9 + H~ = 0 is used to simplify the equations, and Baumgarte and Stiefel's control term is the stabilisation of it; for #928 this means the energy-like constraint should be monitored (and possibly stabilised) in the regularised integration. A recorded observation of the paper (p.201) is that the treatment of time contributed greater systematic errors than the orbit equations, so the fictitious-time to physical-time recovery should be tested separately from the orbit.
- Tests (concrete). (i) The KS map round trip: apply (48) then (52) and (40), residual at machine precision, both branches (first component positive and negative) and at |q| tending to 0; (ii) the planar block of (51) against the existing `#670` Levi-Civita map, same orbit; (iii) a Kepler hyperbola and ellipse passage at 1e-8 of the moon radius against `core/kepler.py` (the two-body KS equations are exactly harmonic in regularised time, section 5), error to 1e-12 independent of the passage distance; (iv) the control that the pure time transformation fails: the same passage under the Sundman option of `core/cr3bp_regularized.py` for comparison; (v) the three-body KS (eight-dimensional) code is not needed; but if a three-body regularised code is ever built, Table III of this paper and Heggie's is the control, with C/3 (not R_f/a_f) as the comparison quantity, stopping rule recorded.

**#899 (second species continuation to Earth-Moon, a Casoliva 2010 reproduction, with the Henon enumerator).** The paper adds one piece: the reference-body switching rule (re-label when R is below min(R1, R2)) is the three-body analogue of choosing which primary to regularise around when a trajectory passes between two attractors. For the Earth-Moon restricted problem there is only one small body and the particle is never near the Earth in the cases at issue, so no switching is needed; the rule becomes relevant only for the two-moon cases (#890 and #895 below). For the continuation through near-collision seeds (the #928 use), see the #928 tests above.

**#924 (chaos indicators FLI and MEGNO as a screen).** Variational vectors grow as 1/r^3 through a close pass in Cartesian coordinates. In regularised variables the variational equations are regular (the Hamiltonian (44) is polynomial in Q, P for the regularised pairs). The paper has no variational equation, but the structure supports computing the transition matrix in KS variables and mapping it back, which is the #928 transition matrix; FLI or MEGNO values near a moon should be computed from it. The control already recorded in #929 item (e) (analytic against finite-difference transition matrix through one flyby) applies unchanged. Note also the paper's remark (p.201) about the instability growing in both forward and backward integration after a critical encounter: for an indicator, a finite-time value depends on the stopping rule, a point the digests of FLI and MEGNO should be re-read for (not done here).

**#929 (integrator controls through one moon flyby).** Table III's time-reversal test (stop, reverse the velocities, integrate the same fixed number of steps back, compare coordinates) is the control #929 item (a) proposes, with a published precedent: use it with fixed step counts and record the stopping point, and compare the physical quantity (closest approach), not the return error alone, between integrators (Table III and Heggie's differ in R_f/a_f and the return errors for the same initial conditions). The paper's finding that return errors are smaller if the reversal is stopped slightly earlier means a reversal test should be run at several stopping points and the worst reported.

**#890 and #895 (Uranian two-moon; methods only).** The re-labelling rule R greater than min(R1, R2) (p.193, p.204) is the one published mechanism for a particle that has close passes with two different bodies in turn: transform to physical variables, change the reference body, and re-regularise. For a spacecraft between Titania and Oberon the two moons are the "m1" and "m2" and the planet is far away; the planar or spatial KS map about the nearer moon at each pass, switching at a defined separation, is the natural scheme; the paper's cautions are (i) the switching introduces uncertainty (p.202: "not important unless transformations from regularized to physical variables occur during close encounters", and in Table III the standard switching deteriorated time-reversed deviations by factors 10 to 10^5, so switching should be done well away from the close approach), (ii) with unequal masses a dynamical criterion replaces the geometric one (p.204); for the Uranian moons the planet dominates all motion, so the reference body is not "heaviest particle", and the criterion would have to be about which moon the spacecraft is nearer. No published number applies to that case.

**#899, #906 and the rest of #913 to #918, #921 to #923.** Not applicable beyond the above (no close passes or no regularisation need).
