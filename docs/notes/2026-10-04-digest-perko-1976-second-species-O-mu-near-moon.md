## 0. What the two papers are

- Part A (1976) shows, by second-order asymptotic matching (Breakwell & Perko 1974, Celest. Mech. 9:437-450), that there are one-parameter families of second species periodic solutions of the planar circular restricted problem with ONE near-Moon passage at distance of order mu per half period, and gives their initial conditions to first order in the variations. It does so for five generating-orbit types and states the pattern for more passages. It carries no numerical orbit at a stated mass ratio. READ p.395 (abstract): "Second species solutions with near-moon passages have not been studied numerically due to the difficult nature of this problem."
- Part B (1981) extends the same theory to near-Moon passages at distance of order mu^nu for all 0 < nu < 1, corrects and improves the 1977 asymptotic formulas, applies it to two worked bifurcations with printed numbers, and proves a corollary: the side of passage is invariant. Its abstract (READ p.155) also corrects the stability claim: "the periodic orbits established in (I) for small mu > 0 approach bifurcation orbits as mu tends to 0, and not critical generating orbits. Thus, the results of (I) do apply to a study of certain types of bifurcations that occur in families of periodic orbits in the restricted problem, but not to a study of stability as was stated in (I)."

## 1. Definitions (Part A, READ pp.395-398)

- Moon on the unit circle, r_m(t) = (cos t, sin t); the equation of motion (1.4) is the restricted problem with the Moon's pull as perturbation; a "periodic solution" means periodic in the rotating frame; by Birkhoff (1915), a solution crossing the Earth-Moon line perpendicularly twice is periodic in the rotating frame, and that is the closing condition used (p.397).
- Generating ellipse: a two-body solution r_0(t) with r_0(t1) = r_m(t1) and r_0(t1 + T0) = r_m(t1 + T0). Type B and ratio m/k: semi-major axis a0 = (m/k)^(2/3), T0 = 2 pi k a0^(3/2) = 2 m pi (this is a resonant return, Henon's T-arc, INFERRED from the book's correspondence note: "there is a one-to-one correspondence between my type A arcs and Henon's S-arcs, and between my type B arcs and Henon's T-arcs", Part B p.170, READ). Type A and ratio m/k: an ellipse through the Moon's position at t1 satisfying the timing condition T_1P2 + 2 pi (k - 1) a0^(3/2) = 2 m pi - T_2Q1, equivalently (eq. 1.2, p.396, read on the image)
  F_{m/k}(a0, e0) = a0^(3/2) [ k pi - arccos((1 - a0)/(a0 e0)) - sqrt(e0^2 - ((1 - a0)/a0)^2) ] - m pi + arccos[(1 - a0 (1 - e0^2))/e0] = 0,
  with period T0 = 2 m pi minus or plus T_2Q1 (eq. 1.3; upper sign direct, lower retrograde). "These curves are arranged in families which are dense in the region a0(1 - e0) < 1 < a0(1 + e0), 0 < e0 < 1 of the (a0, e0)-plane; cf. Henon (1968), p. 388." The apogee or perigee of a type A ellipse lies on the Earth-Moon line at the time t0 = t1 - T0/2.
- Near-Moon passage versus symmetric conjunction (p.397-398): a solution relative to the Moon passing through an O(mu) neighbourhood of the Moon without hitting it has a unique time t_p of closest approach (rho dot-product rho-dot = 0); if the point lies on the Earth-Moon line it is a "symmetric conjunction", otherwise a "near-moon passage". A solution is of type (i, n) if, in [t0, t0 + T/2], it has i symmetric conjunctions (i = 0, 1 or 2) and n near-moon passages in an O(mu) neighbourhood of the Moon (definition due to Guillaume 1971). Labels such as AB(1, 1) say which generating-ellipse types carry the arcs: one arc of type A, a corner at the Moon, one arc of type B, ending in a symmetric conjunction. "In order to establish the existence and to derive asymptotic formulas which uniquely determine a family of second species solutions of type (i, n), it is necessary to use (n + 1)st order asymptotic matching" (p.398).
- Hyperbolic variables (p.399): the motion near the Moon is a Moon-centred hyperbola with Delta the directed distance to the asymptote, V_inf the speed at minus infinity (V_+ at plus infinity) and t_p the time of perilune; beta1 = angle[V_inf, V_+] is the deflection. The "elementary formula of hyperbolic motion" (eq. 2.3 and eq. 3.8): tan(beta/2) = mu/(Delta V_inf^2), i.e. beta = 2 arctan(mu/(Delta V_inf^2)) (eq. 3.8, read as printed in the text layer). COMPUTED consequence: with e = csc(beta/2) and perilune distance d = Delta sqrt((e - 1)/(e + 1)) (Part B p.170), d = mu (e - 1)/V_inf^2, which is exactly the project's turn-gate relation r_p = mu (e - 1)/v_inf^2 with sin(turn/2) = 1/e. So the gate relation is the hyperbola relation, not an expansion.
- Symmetric conjunction condition (eq. 2.5, p.401): the hyperbola has a symmetric conjunction at t_p iff Delta = sigma (mu / V_inf^2) tan gamma_1 (sigma = sgn(...) as in Fig. 3, gamma_1 the angle between V_inf and r_m(t_p)); the nondegeneracy ("non-collision") condition is tan gamma_10 not zero, which with gamma_10 = arcsin[(1 - sqrt(a0 (1 - e0^2)))/V_1] is a0 (1 - e0^2) not equal to 1: "the angular momentum of r_0(t) not being plus or minus 1" (p.401).

## 2. The matching and the existence statements (Part A, READ pp.398-419, 419-427)

First-order matching (Appendix A, pp.419-423; Breakwell & Perko 1965): outer expansion r = r_0 + mu rho_1 + ... away from the Moon, inner expansion r - r_m = rho_0 (hyperbola) + rho_1* + O(mu^2) within O(mu^(1/2)) of the Moon; the outer first-order term has a logarithm, rho_1(t) = (i/V_1^2) ln(tau_0/tau) + rho_1b(t) with tau = t1 - t (eq. a.3), and the matching gives (eqs. a.8 to a.10): j . Delta = j . rho_1b(t1) + O(mu^2); V_1 delta t_p = mu i . rho_1b(t1) + (mu/V_1^2) [ln(2 V_1^3 tau_0/(mu e_1)) - 1] + O(mu^2) up to the printed bracket (text layer garbled; the logarithm of mu in the time of perilune is the point); V_inf = V_1 + O(mu). The transition matrix Phi is that of the Earth two-body variational equation (a.6).
Second-order matching (Appendix B, pp.423-426; Breakwell & Perko 1974): j* . Delta = mu j . rho_1b(t1) + mu^2 j . rho_2b(t1) minus a product term of the first-order pieces, plus O(mu^3) (eq. b.2), with rho_2b given by an integral of b_2 (eq. b.5) and a V-term (b.4); the V_inf and t_p corrections at order mu^2 are in Breakwell & Perko (not reproduced). For Moon-to-Moon legs (type B) the first- and second-order matrices are (a.12) and (b.6), "too lengthy to give here".
Existence statements, all of the form: for all but possibly a finite number of (a0, e0) in any compact subset of E0 = {a0 (1 - e0) < 1 < a0 (1 + e0), 0 < e0 < 1}, with the listed non-collision conditions, for all mu in (0, mu_1) there is a one-parameter family, analytic in the parameter and in mu, with initial conditions r(t0) = r_0(t0) + dr, rdot(t0) = rdot_0(t0) + dv (eq. 1.5) at a perpendicular crossing, where dr is along r_0 and dv along rdot_0. The mass bound mu_1 is never quantified (READ; "there is a mu_1 > 0").
- Theorem 1 (p.401), type A(1, 0): condition a0 (1 - e0^2) not equal to 1, and a21 a24 not equal to 0 except at a finite set; the family is fixed to first order by a21 dr + a24 dv = mu k V_1^2 K_1... (eq. 2.6, printed as a21 dr + a24 dv = mu k V1^2/(tan gamma_10) K_1... partly garbled; equal to Guillaume 1975a eq. 5.1); period T = T0 + 2 delta t_1 + O(mu^2). This is the 1974 first-order case (n = 0). Example orbit: Fig. 4.
- Theorem 2 (p.403), type B(2, 0): a0 = (m/k)^(2/3), two symmetric conjunctions; example Fig. 5.
- Theorem 3 (pp.405-408), type AB(1, 1): a type A arc to a type B arc with ratio m/k, the second ellipse's elements fixed by the Jacobi constant: V_1^2 = 3 - 1/a0 - 2 sqrt(a0 (1 - e0^2)) (p.405), e_+ = {1 - [(1/a0 - 1/a_+)/2 + sqrt(a0 (1 - e0^2))]^2/(a_+)}^(1/2)... (eq. 3.3, garbled in the text layer; read the structure: e_+ is determined by a0, e0 and a_+); a_+ = (m/k)^(2/3) must lie in the range (3.4). A countable infinity of families, one per admissible a_+. Conditions: sqrt(a0 (1 - e0^2)) + (1/a0 - 1/a_+)/2 not equal to 1 and sqrt(a0 (1 - e0^2)) + (1/a0 - 1/a_+)/4 not equal to 1 (the second is the near-Moon passage's own non-collision condition, "the average of the angular momenta of r_0 and r_0+ is not plus or minus 1"). The first-order choice of dr_1 is eq. 3.10 and the second-order dr_2 is eq. 3.11; note dr = mu dr_1 + mu^2 dr_2 with dv = mu dv_1 free, and the second-order term dr_2 is quadratic in dr_1, dv_1 through K_2 (p.405). Period T = T0 + 2 (delta t_1 + delta t_2) + O(mu^2). Example orbit: Fig. 8.
- Theorem 4 (pp.409-415), type AA(0, 1): two type A arcs joined at the Moon, the second ellipse having its apogee or perigee on the Earth-Moon line at t2 (Fig. 9); there are infinitely many such ellipses because the curves F_{m/k} = 0 are dense (Henon 1968 Fig. 9, reproduced as Fig. 10). Condition: sqrt(a0(1 - e0^2)) + (1/a0 - 1/a_+)/4 not equal to 1, and a21 a24 A_+ not equal to 0; dr_1 by eq. 4.6, dr_2 by eq. 4.12 (with the perpendicularity condition 4.10 and the input-output equations 4.11 of Breakwell & Perko 1965, p.54, called Guillaume's "input-output equations"). Example: Fig. 13.
- Theorem 5 (pp.416-418), type BB(2, 1): two type B ellipses of ratios m1/k1 and m2/k2, one near-Moon passage between two symmetric conjunctions; dr_1 by eq. 5.5; period T = T0 + 2 (delta t_2 + delta t_3) with T0 = 4 (m1 + m2) pi. Example: Fig. 15.
- Conclusions (p.418): "the same type of analysis could be used together with the results of nth order matching ... to establish the existence and uniform first-order approximation of various families of second species periodic solutions of type (i, n - 1), i = 0, 1 or 2", with a three-arc example of type ABB(1, 2) needing third-order matching (p.418-419).

### Which generating orbits are covered and excluded (READ)

- Covered: generating orbits made of one or two arcs (types above), with the near-Moon passage at a corner where the deflection angle beta10 is NOT zero: "The condition beta10 not equal to 0, which has been assumed throughout this paper, means that the angle angle[V_inf1, V_inf1+] does not approach zero as mu tends to 0" (p.418-419).
- Excluded, in the author's words (p.419): "As was pointed out by Guillaume (1971, 1975a), to have beta1 tend to 0 as mu tends to 0 would require that Delta_1 = O(mu^nu), 0 < nu < 1 ... in which case ... beta1 = O(mu^(1 - nu)). But to obtain Delta_1 = O(mu^nu) would require O(mu^nu)-variations in the initial conditions. Guillaume (1975b) has carried out asymptotic matching ... with O(mu^nu)-variations in the initial conditions with 1/2 <= nu <= 1 ... This type of second species solution and also the second species bifurcations referred to by Guillaume (1975a), pp. 253-254, will be treated in a later paper." That later paper is the 1977 paper, corrected by Part B.
- Excluded by hypothesis: orbits through a finite set of exceptional (a0, e0) where a21 a24 vanishes, and orbits with a0 (1 - e0^2) = 1 (relative velocity radial at the Moon). More than one near-Moon passage per half period is outlined only.

## 3. Part B: the extension and its worked examples (READ pp.155-170)

- Setting: near-Moon passage of size e = mu^nu with 0 < nu < 1; deflection beta = O(mu^(1 - nu)) tending to zero; the generating orbit is a type 1 bifurcation orbit of Henon (1979): "Type 1: the generating orbit is an ellipse of rational mean motion which intersects the Moon's position on the unit circle in two distinct points. Type 2: ... tangent to the unit circle at a point. Type 3: ... a retrograde circular orbit of radius one." (p.156). The existence theory for first species-second species bifurcations of types 2 and 3 is in Perko 1981 (SIAM, not held) and Guillaume 1971, 1973.
- Three cases (section 4): 0 < nu < 1/2, 1/2 < nu < 1 and nu = 1/2, with different scalings of the variations: dv_1 of order mu^nu (case i), of order mu^(1 - nu) (case ii; "This was pointed out in a private communication by Dr Michel Henon"), of order mu^(1/2) (case iii). For nu = 1/2 the characteristic is a hyperbola: eq. (9): (a21 dr_1 + a24 dv_1)(A21 dr_1 + A24 dv_1) = 2 mu a2+4 / V_1 + O(mu^(3/2)) (type AA(1, 1)), eq. (21) for AA(0, 1); "In either case the characteristics are approximated by the two branches of a hyperbola. The characteristic in the neighbourhood of the bifurcation is approximated by the equations with nu = 1/2" (p.166). The cases nu below 1/2 and above 1/2 "serve as bridges" to the first species and to the O(mu) near-Moon theory (p.166).
- Corrections to the 1977 paper: equations (10), (14), (16) and (19) of that paper "are in error" (p.162), plus a sign error in its eq. (11) (p.165); the matching formulas are improved to third order (p.162: j* . Delta_1 = ... + O(mu^(3 nu), mu^4)). Consequence for anyone using the 1977 paper: use the 1981 formulas.
- Theorems 1 and 2 (pp.165-169): two one-parameter families for each type AA(1, 1) and AA(0, 1) bifurcation orbit, with the initial conditions for the three cases. Corollary (p.169, proof p.169-170): the side of passage of the particle relative to the Moon, at a near-Moon passage or at a symmetric conjunction, is invariant along the bifurcation; proof from Delta = mu cot(beta/2)/V_inf^2 > 0 or < 0 (beta in (-pi, 0) or (0, pi)) and perilune distance d = Delta ((e - 1)/(e + 1))^(1/2) greater than 0, e = [1 + (V_inf^2 Delta/mu)^2]^(1/2). Perko: "this corollary establishes a local form of Broucke's principle, extended to include the invariance of the side of passage with respect to the Moon at a near-Moon passage, for the first species-second species or second species-second species, type 1 bifurcations."
- Conclusion (p.170): "an analysis of all second species-second species bifurcations would be a monumental undertaking. Henon (1979) has made substantial progress ... having determined the proper connections of the branches for all second species type 1 bifurcations in second species families with up to and including six basic arcs. ... Henon (1979) finds that Broucke's principle, extended to include near-Moon passages as in this paper, suffices to solve all second species type 1 bifurcations with up to and including three basic arcs, but that for more than three basic arcs, a quantitative approach must be used." Also the note (p.170): for non-bifurcation orbits type A arcs are Henon's S-arcs and type B are T-arcs; for bifurcation orbits the correspondence fails because Henon's arcs may contain several basic arcs, so the paper's second example is better called AA(1, 1) than AB(1, 1).

## 4. Techniques and answers to the three questions

### (a) What the second-order matching adds to the first-order formulas the project's gate uses

- The gate relation r_p = mu (e - 1)/v_inf^2, sin(turn/2) = 1/e is the exact Kepler-hyperbola relation (section 1 above, COMPUTED from Perko's eq. 3.8 and the perilune formula on p.170). Neither paper modifies it. What the matching determines is the set (Delta, V_inf, t_p) of that hyperbola from the Earth-side initial conditions: first order gives j . Delta = O(mu) terms, V_inf = V_1 + O(mu) and a time shift delta t_p with a logarithm, (mu/V_1^3) ln(1/mu); second order adds the mu^2 terms of eqs. (b.1) to (b.3), including mu^2 ln mu pieces (carried in the constant C_1, which "contains a ln mu term", p.403).
- Why it is needed. With a near-Moon passage at distance of order mu AND a nonzero deflection, the O(mu) variations dr_1, dv_1 of the initial conditions fix the deflection, so the closing condition at the second conjunction must be written to O(mu^2) to determine dr_2; hence "(n + 1)st order matching for n near-Moon passages" (p.398). In other words the second-order terms select which family member closes; they do not change the periapsis for a given turn and speed.
- Size at the Earth-Moon mass. The papers give no number. COMPUTED scales: the expansion parameter of the logarithmic terms is mu |ln mu| / V_inf^3; at mu = 0.0121529529 it is 0.0535 divided by V_inf^3, where V_inf is the Moon-relative speed in units of the Moon's orbital speed (the same validity number as in the Gomez & Olle digest and in the 1977 digest). For V_inf of order 1 the second-order correction to the initial conditions is of order 5 percent times an unknown coefficient; for the slow, near-resonant orbits (V_inf^2 = 0.03 to 0.1) it is not small, and the series is not usable. INFERRED: whether the second-order term changes the predicted periapsis enough to matter at the Earth-Moon mass cannot be answered from either paper; the paper that quantifies the neglected terms is Perko's error-estimate paper (Rocky Mountain J. Math. 6:130-145 (1976), cited on p.427; the reference list of Henon 1997 prints it as 675-696; not held) and the numerical comparisons of Casoliva, Gomez & Olle (their table of exact versus asymptotic).
- Practical use for the project. The first-order relation can be kept as the gate's periapsis estimate; but a prediction of WHICH initial conditions close (what the P2 seed provides) should use Barrabes & Gomez's matching or Perko's eqs. 3.10-3.11 and 4.6-4.12, and its error is of order mu |ln mu| in the first-order terms, as above.

### (b) Is the Earth-Moon mass ratio treated numerically

No. Part A states that near-Moon-passage solutions "have not been studied numerically" and that Henon "has carried out a numerical study of second species solutions of type (1, 0)" only (p.418). Its figures (4, 5, 8, 13, 15) are sketched example orbits with no stated mu. One numerical example is given: a type A generating ellipse a0 = 0.813 284 53, e0 = 0.815 565 11 "of ratio 1/2" (p.411, read on the image), for which solutions a_+ of F_{m/k}(a_+, e_+(a0, e0, a_+)) = 0 are plotted in Fig. 12 (a plot of F against a_+ with the resonance ratios m/k = 2/3, ..., 7/8 marked; no table). COMPUTED: eq. 1.2 as printed does not vanish at this (a0, e0) for m/k = 1/2 (F = 0.4185) or for any integers with m between -6 and 8, k between -3 and 7; and Henon's S-arc equation (his eq. 4.28, with Z = beta A - alpha) gives non-integer lines there. I could not reproduce the example's membership of a type A family; it should not be used as a control without the original or a convention check (a sign convention in eq. 1.2, or a misprint in the digits, are possible). Part B mentions mu = 0.012999 for Broucke's family l computation (a likely misprint of 0.012155; Henon 1997 p.206 gives 0.012155 for the same Broucke study) and gives no orbit at a mass ratio.

### (c) Relation to Hitzl & Henon's critical generating orbits

- Different objects. Hitzl & Henon's critical generating orbits are extrema of C along a family of consecutive-collision orbits (with one collision per period), at which the stability index jumps through infinity. Perko's families are generated by orbits at which the deflection angle beta10 is not zero (Part A) or by bifurcation orbits with beta tending to zero (Part B). Part B says explicitly that the second kind approaches "bifurcation orbits as mu tends to 0, and not critical generating orbits", and withdraws the 1977 paper's claim that it could be used for stability.
- Where they meet (INFERRED). The Hitzl & Henon critical orbits sit next to the integer points (tau/pi, eta/pi) = (i, j), where two branches of the timing condition cross: these are the first/second species bifurcation orbits that Perko's Part B treats with nu below 1 (the two worked examples are the (I, J) = (2, 1) bifurcation at C = -0.406767). The hypotheses of Part A that exclude cases are (i) zero deflection (beta10 = 0, treated in Part B), (ii) a0 (1 - e0^2) = 1, (iii) a finite set where a21 a24 = 0. I did not find a statement relating (iii) to the critical orbits (C extremal), and I cannot tell from the papers whether a21 a24 = 0 corresponds to a Hitzl-Henon critical orbit. At a critical generating orbit the deflection is generally nonzero (Hitzl & Henon digest, section 5(b): 0.1 to 65 degrees), so Part A applies there (INFERRED), and the stability index at mu above 0 near such an orbit is a separate calculation (Hitzl & Henon 1977b, not held).
- Relation to the turn gate. Part B's corollary (side of passage invariant at a near-Moon passage) is the physical reason a zero-turn bifurcation junction cannot be simply "rejected" or "accepted" by sign: the branch that continues has the same side of passage, the sign of the demanded deflection is conserved along the bifurcation. This supports the proposed check in the Henon 1997 digest (report bifurcation junctions, do not discard them), and adds that the sign of the turn (not only its magnitude) is an invariant to record.

### Other techniques (INFERRED uses)

- The five-type table (A(1,0), B(2,0), AB(1,1), AA(0,1), BB(2,1)) is a catalogue of one-passage and two-passage chains with explicit first-order initial conditions: a direct model for P2-type seeds of chains with a lunar flyby between two resonant legs, with the resonance ratios m/k in the admissible range (3.4) for a type B leg. For a chain of A and B legs the Jacobi constant fixes e_+ from (a0, e0, a_+) by (3.3), so the chain closes on the Jacobi-constant condition of Henon's arcs.
- The mass-order bookkeeping is the useful rule: n passages need matching of order n + 1; at the Earth-Moon mass the series in mu cannot be assumed convergent for chains with several passages.
