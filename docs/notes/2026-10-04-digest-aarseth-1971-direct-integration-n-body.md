## 0. What the paper is

A methods paper for stellar-dynamics N-body codes. Abstract (p.118): "A fourth-order polynomial method for the integration of N-body systems is described in detail together with the computational algorithm. Most particles are treated efficiently by an individual time-step scheme but the calculation of close encounters and persistent binary orbits is rather time-consuming and is best performed by special techniques. A discussion is given of the Kustaanheimo-Stiefel regularization procedure which is used to integrate dominant two-body encounters as well as close binaries. Suitable decision-making parameters are introduced and a simple method is developed for regularizing an arbitrary number of simultaneous two-body encounters."

Two parts: (A) sections 2 and 3, a fourth-order divided-difference force-polynomial integrator with a different time step for each particle; (B) sections 4 to 7, perturbed two-body KS regularisation of close pairs inside that code, with criteria for starting and ending it and a step-size rule. Part B is the form of KS the project needs (perturbed KS with accelerations from the other bodies, written for the particles' masses with reduced mass mu) and is the "Aarseth 1971" cited in the Peters and Aarseth-Zare digests. The paper carries NO table; all numbers are in the text (section 5 below).

## 1. The fourth-order polynomial method (READ pp.118-123)

### 1.1 The force polynomial (section 2, pp.118-121)

Per body, expand the force per unit mass about the reference time t0 with t_r = t - t0 (eq. 1): F = F0 + B' t_r + C' t_r^2 + D' t_r^3 + E' t_r^4. B', C', D' are fitted at three previous times and expressed through divided differences weighted by the three preceding steps dt1, dt2, dt3 (dt3 the most recent); the fourth coefficient E' is "not known until the end of the fourth step dt4 when its contribution is added" ("a semi-iteration ... almost one extra order of integration is included at very little additional effort and only one force calculation is needed for each interval", p.119). Practical tests "indicate that there is no significant gain in efficiency when going to higher orders" (Wielen 1967 for the general derivation). In the convenient form (eq. 2): F = F0 + B t_r + C (dt3 + t_r) t_r + D (dt2 + dt3 + t_r)(dt3 + t_r) t_r + E (dt1 + dt2 + dt3 + t_r)(dt2 + dt3 + t_r)(dt3 + t_r) t_r. Divided backward differences (eq. 3): F^_0 = (F0 - F_-3)/dt3, with the second and third differences F^^_0, F^^^_0 in the same pattern (F_-3 the force at t_r = -dt3). The adopted coefficients (eq. 4): B = F^_0; C = dt3 F^^_0/(dt2 + dt3); D = [dt2 dt3 F^^^_0 / ((dt1 + dt2 + dt3)(dt1 + dt2))] - [(dt2^2 - dt1 dt3) F^^_0 / ((dt1 + dt2 + dt3)(dt1 + dt2)(dt2 + dt3))]; E combines the divided difference F^^_4 equivalent to F^^_0 at t_r = dt4 with the earlier ones and D, divided by (dt1 + dt2 + dt3 + dt4) (p.119-120, as printed; read on the page images).

### 1.2 Start-up and Taylor derivatives (section 3, pp.120-121)

Equation of motion (5): r_i'' = - sum_{j != i} m_j (r_i - r_j)/|r_i - r_j|^3 (F0 = r_i''). The first three Taylor-series derivatives of F0 are written out explicitly in terms of r_ij, rdot_ij, rddot_ij (eq. 6, from Gonzalez and Lecar 1968): "the second and third derivatives are readily determined once all the current forces and the corresponding first derivatives have been calculated." Initial steps for each particle from the criterion (eq. 7)
dt4^3 = eta F0 / ((1/6) F0'''), with F0 = |F0| etc. (the denominator is one sixth of the magnitude of the third derivative of the force, three dots printed),
"the parameter eta specifies the permissible relative change of force during the new step as contributed by the last known term ... The definition (7) is independent of mass and has the desired property of preserving the relative accuracy of each orbit during close encounters by reducing the integration interval." For the start put dt_k = dt4 for k = 1, 2, 3 (eq. 8) and convert the Taylor derivatives to polynomial ones by (9).

### 1.3 Individual time steps and the scheduling (pp.121-122)

Each particle carries the time t_i of its last force computation and an auxiliary interval (eq. 10) dt~_i = t_i + dt_i - t (the time remaining to the particle's next update). The particle alpha with the smallest dt~ is advanced next (eq. 11): dt~_alpha = min_i (dt~_i); the time advances to t + dt~_alpha and all dt~_i are reduced by dt~_alpha. The body alpha needs a new force, but first all coordinates are predicted by low-order extrapolation (eq. 12): Delta r_i = [((1/6) Fdot_0 dt'_i + (1/2) F_0) dt'_i + rdot_0] dt'_i, with dt'_i = t - t_i. If dt_alpha is small, "one extra order" is included in the coordinate prediction for particles with small steps. A more accurate position for alpha is obtained by integrating twice the increments from the coefficients C and D; then the new force is computed (the sum (5)), the fifth term of (2) is added, and the velocity follows by integrating the force polynomial once. The next step is predicted from the relative criterion (eq. 13)
dt_alpha^3 = eta (F0 + Fdot_0 dt3) / ((1/6) F0''' + (1/24) F0^(IV) dt3)  (magnitudes),
"where two terms have been added to the expression (7) in order to ensure proper convergence in exceptional cases... No upper limit is used but the new value is not allowed to increase by more than a factor of 1.4 in order to safeguard the numerical stability" (p.122). The velocity is available only at the end of each interval, so sensitive quantities (integrals of motion) must be evaluated with care when the particles are at different times.

### 1.4 Cost and accuracy figures (p.122, text, no table)

- Coordinate prediction costs "only 20-30 percent of the total computing time depending on the order used" (p.118-119).
- Storage: "A minimum of 28 N storage locations" is required; extended precision for r0, rdot0 and the times and positions adds 10 N locations (IBM 360/44); a 32000-word store would still allow about 600 particles; additional time under 30 percent for the same number of steps.
- "A value [eta] of 2 x 10^-4 leads to satisfactory solutions in the absence of extremely close encounters"; about one hour per mean crossing time for N = 100 with a basic addition time of 3.8 microseconds; the time per individual step is about 1.0 x 10^-3 N seconds for N much greater than 2.
- Footnote p.122: for a binary with eccentricity 0.92 "the relative energy errors ... are then DE/E = 2 x 10^-5 and 7 x 10^-6 per revolution, using the two alternative orders of prediction with 234 steps for each component."
- Controls named (p.122-123): the relative error of total energy, time reversibility of the equations ("a more detailed check"), eccentric binary orbits over many revolutions as the test of dealing with close encounters without special treatment, and the critical three-body case of Szebehely and Peters (1967), "a substantial part of the evolution may also be reproduced reasonably well by the ordinary method."

## 2. The KS treatment of close pairs (sections 4 and 5, READ pp.123-128)

Motivation (p.123): Levi-Civita has been generalised to three dimensions (Kustaanheimo and Stiefel 1965), the new equations are non-singular, and Peters (1968) showed it efficient in three-body problems; here the extension to large systems needs a formulation of the basic equations suitable for arbitrary numbers of simultaneous two-body encounters, plus criteria to combine it with the direct method. "In the following we make full use of the Hamiltonian formulation of Peters, preserving some of the notation."

### 2.1 The equations (READ pp.123-125)

Four-dimensional coordinates u and momenta v of the pair (m_k, m_l), reduced mass mu, fictitious time tau (primes d/dtau); total energy of the system E (eqs. 14 to 17):
- u' = (1/(4 mu)) v   (14)
- v' = 2 (E - V) u + 2 mu R L^T (F_k - F_l)   (15)
- V = (1/2)(m_k + m_l) Qdot^2 + (1/2) sum_{i != k,l} m_i rdot_i^2 - (1/2) sum_{i != k,l} sum_{j != i,k,l} m_i m_j/|r_i - r_j|   (16)  (the kinetic energy of the centre-of-mass motion, of the other bodies and their mutual potential, but not the pair's mutual term m_k m_l)
- F_k - F_l = - sum_{j != k,l} m_j [ (r_k - r_j)/|r_k - r_j|^3 - (r_l - r_j)/|r_l - r_j|^3 ]   (17)  (the relative Newtonian perturbation: the force on the pair's relative motion from every other body)
with the transposed generalised Levi-Civita matrix (eq. 18, 3 x 4)
L = [ [u1, -u2, -u3, u4], [u2, u1, -u4, -u3], [u3, u4, u1, u2] ],
and the separation R = u1^2 + u2^2 + u3^2 + u4^2 = u.u (eq. 19).
To avoid the double sum in V for large N, E and V are combined (eq. 20): E - V = P.P/(2 mu) - m_k m_l/R, with P the relative momentum. This also follows from the regularised Hamiltonian being zero (Peters). Define the binding energy per unit mass (eq. 21) h = (E - V)/mu; with P.P = v.v/(4R) (eq. 22) and v = 4 mu u' from (14):
h = [2 u'.u' - (m_k + m_l)] / R   (eq. 23),
"at the expense of introducing a division by the particle separation, and this procedure does not allow cases to be studied where R tends to 0. The probability of near collisions, however, is extremely low in simulated clusters" (p.124). The eight first-order equations reduce to four second-order ones (eq. 24):
u'' = (1/2) h u + (1/2) R L^T (F_k - F_l).
COMPUTED checks of this note: (i) from (14), (20), (22) one gets E - V = (2 mu u'.u' - m_k m_l)/R and hence (23) after dividing by mu (m_k m_l/mu = m_k + m_l); (ii) differentiating (14) and using (15), (21) gives (24). SIGN CONVENTION: h here is the physical (specific) energy of the relative motion, so h is negative for a bound pair, and the oscillator u'' = (h/2) u + ... has squared frequency -h/2; this is the same sign as Peters's h and OPPOSITE to the h of the Stiefel and Scheifele book (h positive for an ellipse). A hyperbolic pass has h positive and exponential u.

Time transformation (eq. 25): dt = R dtau. "In the present derivation each close pair is treated independently and all fictitious intervals dtau are converted to ordinary time in order to provide a common frame of reference. This approach differs significantly from that of Peters and allows the introduction of multiple regularizations without affecting the integration of other particles."

### 2.2 The binding-energy instability and the regular alternative (READ p.125)

"Actual calculations show that the binding energy formulation leads to a numerical instability in special cases of rapidly varying separations", because the predicted binding energy appears in (24). For the unperturbed expression "the relative error in binding energy continues to grow if the parameter (eq. 26) epsilon ~ u0 u0' dtau / u_p^2 exceeds unity" (u0, u0' the transformed coordinate and velocity at the start of the interval, u_p the predicted coordinate; a scalar first-order analysis). "It is usually sufficient to ensure that the predicted integration step does not violate the adopted stability condition epsilon <~ 0.5, but growing oscillations of the binding energy still appear at extremely small separations." Fix: use the previously calculated binding energy rather than the predicted one. An alternative, "as yet untried" in the text and adopted by the footnote of p.132: integrate h itself (Stiefel 1967, NASA CR-769); differentiating (23), using (24) and R' = 2 u.u',
h' = 2 u'.L^T (F_k - F_l)   (eq. 27),
"completely regular", with the perturbation contribution well behaved at small separations (the classical h-dot = Rdot.(F_k - F_l)), and it "gains one order of accuracy compared to Equation (23) which involves the regularized velocity", the initial value from (23). COMPUTED check: differentiating h = (2 u'.u' - M)/R (M = m_k + m_l): h' = 4 u'.u''/R - (2 u'.u' - M) R'/R^2 = 4 u'.u''/R - h R'/R; inserting u'' from (24), (1/2) h u and the perturbation, the h u term gives 4 u'.(1/2) h u/R = h R'/R (since R' = 2 u.u'), which cancels the second term, leaving 4 u'.(1/2) R L^T(F_k - F_l)/R = 2 u'.L^T(F_k - F_l): (27) confirmed.

### 2.3 Transformations (section 5, READ pp.126-128)

Pair relative coordinates and momenta (eqs. 28, 29): R = r_k - r_l; P = mu (rdot_k - rdot_l). The footnote of p.126 reads: "The definition of relative co-ordinates and momenta used by Peters should be reversed in order to be consistent with the final equation of motion. A correct derivation is given in the original Ph.D. thesis, Yale University, 1968." COMPUTED confirmation of what that means: with the reference body's vector defined as in Aarseth (R = r_k - r_l) the perturbation term of the equations is +2 mu R L^T (F_k - F_l) (eq. 15; I verified this by differentiating the perturbation Hamiltonian, dR_pert/dR = mu (F_l - F_k)), and Peters's printed gradient dR/du = -2 mu A* (P_k'/(r m_k) - P_l'/(r m_l)) (his p.172) has exactly that sign, which corresponds to r = r_k - r_l, whereas Peters's text defines r = r_l - r_k (his p.168). An implementer should use Aarseth's convention (R = r_k - r_l) throughout. For X > 0 (X, Y, Z the components of the separation vector R) (eq. 30): u1 = [(R + X)/2]^(1/2), u2 = Y/(2 u1), u3 = Z/(2 u1), u4 = 0; for X < 0 (eq. 31): u2 = [(R - X)/2]^(1/2), u1 = Y/(2 u2), u3 = 0, u4 = Z/(2 u2). Inverse (eq. 32, both cases): X = u1^2 - u2^2 - u3^2 + u4^2, Y = 2 (u1 u2 - u3 u4), Z = 2 (u1 u3 + u2 u4), implying (19). Momentum (eq. 33) v = 2 L^T P with (eq. 34, 4 x 3)
L^T = [ [u1, u2, u3], [-u2, u1, u4], [-u3, -u4, u1], [u4, -u3, u2] ],
and the "non-holonomic condition" (eq. 35): u4 v1 - u3 v2 + u2 v3 - u1 v4 = 0 (the same relation as Peters's p.172 control, overall sign reversed: COMPUTED comparison). The matrix (34) is the 4 x 3 matrix A* of Peters and one half of A_1 of Aarseth and Zare 1974 eq. 51 (Aarseth and Zare say "identical to the transpose of the generalized Levi-Civita matrix used ... (Aarseth, 1971)", p.195 of that paper: confirmed here).
Accuracy of the integration can be checked with (27) or (35). The polynomial method integrates u and u' with (24) in the role of (5) (p.127).

Centre of mass (eqs. 36, 37): Q = (m_k r_k + m_l r_l)/(m_k + m_l), Qddot = (m_k F_k + m_l F_l)/(m_k + m_l): "only the two perturbations enter in the centre of mass acceleration since the dominant terms cancel analytically." The pair needs 16 equations instead of 12 (p.127). Back-transformation (eq. 38): R = L u, R = u.u, P = 2 mu L u'/R, r_k = Q + mu R/m_k, r_l = Q - mu R/m_l, rdot_k = Qdot + P/m_k, rdot_l = Qdot - P/m_l, mu = m_k m_l/(m_k + m_l) (eq. 39). COMPUTED check: P = 2 mu L u'/R follows from v = 4 mu u' and v = 2 L^T P with L L^T = 4R... as in Peters (A A* = 4 r I).

Fictitious to ordinary time (section 5, READ pp.127-128). Taylor series in dtau (eq. 40): Dt = t0' Dtau + (1/2) t0'' Dtau^2 + (1/6) t0''' Dtau^3 + (1/24) t0^(IV) Dtau^4 + (1/120) t0^(V) Dtau^5, with the derivatives from successive differentiation of the second of (38) using dt = R dtau (eq. 41): t0' = u.u, t0'' = 2 u'.u, t0''' = 2 u''.u + 2 u'.u'. All derivatives of u needed are available from the integration scheme "hence the conversion to ordinary time is very efficient". The inverse (extrapolation within an interval, less accuracy needed, eq. 42): Dtau~ = tau~0' Dt + (1/2) tau~0'' Dt^2 + (1/6) tau~0''' Dt^3 with tau~0' = 1/R (eq. 43) and (eq. 44) tau~0'' = -t0''/R^3, tau~0''' = [3 t0''^2/R - t0'''] (1/R^4); "the division by small values of R may be permitted since it does not effect the integration of the relative motion", the procedure being needed only for the centre-of-mass and other particles' accelerations.
Starting procedure for the regularised components (eqs. 45, 46): the explicit scheme of (6) to (10) is not available for the relative motion, so a fitting procedure is used: expansion G = G0 + G0' tau_r + (1/2) G0'' tau_r^2 + (1/6) G0''' tau_r^3, with G_j (j = 1, 2, 3) computed from (24) at three equal intervals dtau advanced with all coordinates (centre of mass included), with 3 dtau = 0.5 Dtau0 (Dtau0 the initial step). The printed coefficients (eq. 46) are G0' = [-(11/6) G0 + 3 G1 - (3/2) G2 + (1/3) G3]/dtau; G0'' = [G0 - (5/2) G1 + 2 G2 - (1/2) G3]/dtau^2; G0''' = [-(1/6) G0 + (1/2) G1 - (1/2) G2 + (1/6) G3]/dtau^3. FLAG (COMPUTED): the first line is the standard four-point forward-difference formula for the first derivative, but the second and third lines are exactly one half and one sixth of the standard formulas for the second derivative [(2G0 - 5G1 + 4G2 - G3)/dtau^2] and the third [(-G0 + 3G1 - 3G2 + G3)/dtau^3]; i.e. as printed they are the Taylor COEFFICIENTS (G0''/2, G0'''/6), not the derivatives that (45) multiplies by 1/2 and 1/6 again. Either the printing or the symbols are inconsistent; an implementer should derive the coefficients independently (and the paper then applies the conversion of (8), (9) "in analogy"). The same fit is applied to H = h' (eq. 27) for the binding energy, integrated once.

## 3. When to regularise, how long, and the step size (sections 6 and 7, READ pp.129-132)

Selection (p.129): a critical step Dt_min and a corresponding separation R_min. When a particle m_k has dt_k < Dt_min and its step is decreasing, search for the body m_l with the largest force contribution, noting all other particles j inside about 3 R_min. The pair is accepted if R < R_min and the relative force is dominant (eq. 47): (m_k + m_l)/|r_k - r_l|^2 > (m_l + m_j)/|r_l - r_j|^2 for all j, k, l (this allows m_k to be close to another regularised pair; including only approaching particles is prudent).
Termination: the perturbation parameter (eq. 48) gamma = |F_k - F_l| R^2/(m_k + m_l), the ratio of the perturbing to the central force of the pair; when a third particle gives gamma of order 1 or more "the original binary motion is then no longer dominant" and the pair is replaced; "Regularization of hyperbolic cases or wide binaries is normally terminated by the combined criterion" (eq. 49) R > R0 and gamma > gamma_max, R0 the initial separation; the second condition lets the special treatment continue beyond the initial separation, "particularly useful for hyperbolic orbits with R0 much less than R_min, or when dealing with eccentric binaries where the apocentre distance exceeds the critical value". "The regularization criteria discussed above are completely general."
Regularised step (eq. 50): Dtau = (2 pi/N) [1/(2|h|)]^(1/2) (1/(1 + 1000 gamma)^(1/3)), N the number of steps during one unperturbed revolution; the correction term reduces the step when strong perturbations change the binding energy rapidly; "Equation (50) is equally suitable for treating hyperbolic motion"; the unperturbed part is replaced by a constant beta in all cases that would give Dtau greater than beta; as a safety measure half the predicted value is used for the first step Dtau0 and later intervals may increase by a factor of 1.2; "the adopted time-step definition is not unique". "It may be noted that the period of the regularized equation of motion (24) corresponds to twice the Keplerian value" (p.130). Close binaries over many revolutions: neglect the small perturbation by using only nearest neighbours (eq. 51): include a particle if m_i/|Q - r_i|^3 is at least 1/(kappa^3 R^3), kappa R the limiting separation for unit mass, consistent with the tidal limit of (48) (gamma of order kappa^-3); neighbour list updated at every apocentre passage (sign change of R').
Numbers (p.131): "One example with eccentricity 0.7 shows that N = 50 is sufficient to keep the relative binding energy error per revolution below 1 x 10^-6, while N = 63 improves the accuracy to 3 x 10^-7, when all calculations are performed in extended precision." Parameters of the first large regularised N-body integration: Dt_min = 1 x 10^-5, R_min = 0.01, gamma_max = 0.01, N = 50, beta = 0.01, kappa = 100 for N = 500 particles with energy scaling E = -(1/4) N^2 and masses summing to N; the fast perturbation calculation only for close binaries with h < -N. The extra transformations for particles inside kappa R "partly offset the advantages of regularizing large separations, hence the conservative choice of the first three parameters."
Footnote p.132: the treatment based on (27) "has now been adopted. Energy errors for a binary with eccentricity 0.92 then improve from DE/E = -5 x 10^-6 to -8 x 10^-8 per revolution, if N = 50."
Section 7 (pp.131-132): organisation of arrays so that regularised components come first, centre-of-mass arrays g_{N+j} appended, arbitrary numbers of pairs by moving entries; arrays ordered by decreasing mass initially (heavy bodies are in most encounters); "considerable programming effort is required"; "Some efficiency is lost ... if there are no dominant pairs", where Heggie (1972, in the same volume) is the alternative.

## 4. Does it cover a massless particle and hyperbolic passes?

Not stated. Both are my reading (INFERRED, partly COMPUTED).
- Massless: the paper's pair has masses m_k, m_l and a reduced mass mu, and the statements it makes use mu throughout (14, 15, 39), so it is written for massive pairs. The SECOND-ORDER form (24) with h from (23) or (27), the perturbation (17), and the back-transformation do not contain mu in a singular way: (24) has no mu; (23) has (m_k + m_l) which stays finite when m_k tends to 0; (17) and (27) are accelerations, which carry no mass of the pair; and (38) gives r_k = Q + (m_l/(m_k + m_l)) R, tending to Q + R, and r_l = Q - (m_k/(m_k + m_l)) R, tending to Q (the moon at the centre of mass), with Qddot tending to F_l (the moon's acceleration). COMPUTED: mu/m_k = m_l/(m_k + m_l) and mu/m_l = m_k/(m_k + m_l). So the u-form (14)/(24)/(27)/(38) is regular for a massless particle k and a massive moon l, if the working variable is u' (not v = 4 mu u', which tends to zero; P/mu = 2 L u'/R stays finite). The momentum v and the energy E - V of (15) and (20) should not be used (they vanish with mu).
- Hyperbolic passes: the equations are regular for either sign of h; step rule (50) is stated suitable for hyperbolic motion and uses |h|, with the cap beta for |h| tending to zero; termination criterion (49) is written for hyperbolic cases. The paper gives no hyperbolic numerical example.
- It does not address a force that is not a sum of Newtonian pulls: the project's frame has Coriolis and centrifugal terms. The S and S treatment (Coriolis as a force P, centrifugal plus other-primary direct attraction as V) covers that; Aarseth's F_k - F_l is a difference of two gravitational accelerations.

4. The h evolution: the paper's finding that evolving h from the predicted value is unstable (epsilon above about 0.5, p.125) and that integrating h' = 2 u'.L^T (F_k - F_l) is regular and gains an order is relevant when h is NOT constant, i.e. in time-dependent models (the elliptic restricted problem, the #895 ephemeris model; S and S section 12 caveat 3 gives the same h' with the other sign convention for h). In the autonomous circular problem h is constant (Jacobi) and none of this arises. The instability analysis (26) is a first-order scalar estimate; it is a monitor to record, not a result to rely on.

Where to start and stop the KS stage (a decision rule the S and S book leaves to the user): the perturbation ratio (48), gamma = |F_k - F_l| R^2/(m_k + m_l), start KS when R < R_min (a pair-dominance test (47), here only one candidate pair) and terminate with (49) (R above the entry radius and gamma above gamma_max = 0.01).
