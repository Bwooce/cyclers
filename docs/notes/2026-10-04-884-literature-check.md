## 1. Verdict

Same class as prior work; these specific families not found in the BCR4BP.

- The METHOD (continue a CR3BP periodic orbit whose period is commensurate with the Sun's synodic
  period into a Sun-perturbed model by raising the solar coupling from 0 to 1, collecting several
  "dynamical equivalents" per CR3BP orbit) is not new. For the BCR4BP specifically it is Boudad,
  Howell & Davis (2020), who apply it to the 3:1, 4:1 and 9:2 NRHO. For the Hill four-body problem it
  is Brown et al. (2025).
- The PROBLEM (extend Earth-Moon planar periodic transfer/cycler-type orbits from the CR3BP to a
  Sun-perturbed model) is also not new: Leiva & Briozzo (2005, 2008) did it in the quasi-bicircular
  problem for 80 families, with analytic continuation in a parameter, getting 11 periodic orbits and 25
  periodic arcs. This is the closest prior work and the project does not hold it (see section 3).
- A member of the Ross-Roberts-Tsoukkas (3,2)-cycler family (the project's C32) is stated by Ross &
  Roberts-Tsoukkas to have a perturbed version that persists under solar perturbation (Leiva &
  Briozzo). So "C32 survives the Sun" is already stated in print for a member of that family.
- Not found: a BCR4BP (or HR4BP) survey of the Ross-RT C11/C21/C31/C32 cyclers, Braik-Ross families,
  Casoliva resonant cyclers or Vaquero orbits with the Melnikov zero structure, the fold-and-reconnect
  behaviour, the stability classification (only forced orbits whose CR3BP parent is stable are stable),
  or the periselene-below-lunar-radius finding for Casoliva 2:1(b). Absence in what I could reach is
  conditional on the search, and the Komachi paper could not be read (section 4).

Do not label any #884 result novel. Frame it as an application of Boudad et al. / Brown et al. to
the Earth-Moon cycler catalogue, with Leiva & Briozzo as the prior work on the same orbit class in the
QBCP. Labelling under spec 16.4 is the coordinator's call.

## 2. Most relevant papers

1. Leiva & Briozzo, "Extension of fast periodic transfer orbits from the Earth-Moon RTBP to the
   Sun-Earth-Moon Quasi-Bicircular Problem", Celest. Mech. Dyn. Astron. 101:225-245 (2008),
   DOI 10.1007/s10569-008-9134-9. ABSTRACT (via ADS snippet in a search result): "Starting from 80
   families of low-energy fast periodic transfer orbits in the Earth-Moon planar circular Restricted
   Three Body Problem (RTBP), we obtain by analytical continuation 11 periodic orbits and 25 periodic
   arcs with similar properties in the Sun-Earth-Moon Quasi-Bicircular Problem (QBCP)." Contains the
   same kind of result (Earth-Moon periodic orbits continued into a Sun model, some survive, some do
   not). Model is QBCP, not BCR4BP; orbit class is their own 80 low-energy families, not the Ross/Braik
   catalogue. Whether it prints initial conditions, and whether lunar flybys are included, I could not
   see. Companion: Leiva & Briozzo, "Fast Periodic Transfer Orbits in the Sun-Earth-Moon Quasi-Bicircular
   Problem", CMDA 91:357-372 (2005), DOI 10.1007/s10569-004-7818-3. ABSTRACT snippet: a family of fast
   periodic transfer orbits in the planar RTBP, "using analytic continuation techniques, finds two
   unstable periodic orbits" in the QBCP, period about 29.5 days.
   The held 2006 preprint (arXiv astro-ph/0612386, VERIFIED) states the programme in its Sec. 6.2: "We
   recently showed (Leiva and Briozzo 2005) how under suitable conditions some UPOs can be extended from
   the Earth-Moon CR3BP to the Sun-Earth-Moon QBCP by analytical continuation in an adequate parameter.
   In work soon to be submitted for publication, we will present a systematic way to extend some POs
   from families in the Atlas to equivalent POs in the Sun-Earth-Moon QBCP". It also says of the
   bicircular model that it "is not a self-consistent model (Andreu 1998) and is less accurate than the
   QBCP". The 2008 paper is that promised systematic extension (INFERRED from the title and abstract).

2. Boudad, Howell & Davis, "Dynamics of synodic resonant near rectilinear halo orbits in the bicircular
   four-body problem", Adv. Space Res. 66(9):2194-2214 (2020), ADS 2020AdSpR..66.2194B. NOT read; I
   could not open the ScienceDirect, ADS or full text pages (403/405). What I know is from Brown et
   al. 2025 (JAS 72:43), which I read in full: "Examining the diagrams depicting the continuation of the
   3:1 NRHO and 4:1 NRHO in the BCP (shown in Fig. 24 and Figs. 25-26, respectively, in the work by
   Boudad et al.), there are a clear number of dynamical equivalents that can be continued up from the
   CR3BP when introducing the effect of the Sun by increasing the value of eps_B from eps_B = 0 to
   eps_B,SEM. However, examining Figs. 27-29 in the work by Boudad et al., a large number of SEM BCP
   dynamical equivalents were identified for the 9:2 NRHO." (Sec. 2.3, JAS 72:43 p. 8.) So the exact
   homotopy in a BCR4BP solar parameter, with multiple equivalents per CR3BP orbit, is in Boudad et al.
   for Earth-Moon halo/NRHO resonances. No cyclers, no lunar-flyby orbits as far as Brown et al. report
   (INFERRED; I did not see the paper).

3. Brown, Peterson, Henry & Scheeres, "Behavior of Symmetric Periodic Orbits with High-Order Resonance
   in the Sun-Earth-Moon System", J. Astronaut. Sci. 72:43 (2025), DOI 10.1007/s40295-025-00517-3.
   VERIFIED (open access PDF, full text). Abstract: "we use Melnikov theory and a continuation
   algorithm to transition this orbit into the Sun-Earth-Moon (SEM) system using the Hill Restricted
   4-Body Problem (HR4BP) ... we extend our analysis to other resonant periodic orbits and dynamical
   models. We find that the SEM HR4BP dynamical equivalents to the L2 7:2 NRHO and 5:2 halo orbit also
   exhibit similar behavior, and the 9:2 NRHO in the SEM Bicircular Restricted 4-Body Problem exhibits
   this behavior as well." This is the BCR4BP Melnikov continuation from the same group as the SIADS
   paper the #884 note follows. It confirms the #884 note's Tg: "In the BCP Tg = 2 pi (1.0808)", while
   "Tg = pi in the HR4BP" (Sec. 2.3). It covers NRHO/halo/L4 orbits only; no cyclers, no lunar flybys,
   no printed initial conditions seen in the pages I searched (grep for cycler, flyby: no hits).
   Not held in the corpus (CORPUS_INDEX has no entry).

## 4. What the literature says about the #884 note

- The note's "Published reference" is Brown et al. SIADS; it does not cite Boudad et al. (2020), which
  did the BCR4BP solar-parameter continuation, or Brown et al. JAS 2025, which did the BCR4BP Melnikov
  version. The note should cite both. In particular the note's sentence that the BCR4BP is "same primary
  and model class as Brown et al." understates it: the BCR4BP half is covered in Brown et al. JAS 72:43
  App. 2 (BCP equations and Melnikov function) and in Boudad et al.
- The note's Sec. 2 claim "Tg = 2 x theirs" (BCR4BP versus HR4BP) is confirmed by Brown et al. JAS 72:43
  Sec. 2.3 ("In the BCP Tg = 2 pi (1.0808) ... as Tg = pi in the HR4BP"). VERIFIED.
- The note's own claim for C32 (all cycler-class equivalents unstable, periselene stays in the SOI)
  does not contradict the Leiva-Briozzo statement relayed by Ross & Roberts-Tsoukkas that a perturbed
  member of the (3,2) family persists under the Sun. Different models (QBCP vs BCR4BP) and the note
  tests only members at commensurate periods. Whether Leiva-Briozzo's persisting orbit is at or near
  the note's commensurate members is unknown to me.
- Nothing found contradicts the note's numerical results. Nothing found reproduces them either: no
  paper I could reach prints initial conditions for Sun-forced equivalents of Earth-Moon cyclers.
- Genova & Aldrin's statement that a 3:1 free-return cycler with close Earth passes "was not shown to
  exist in the restricted three-body problem" predates Ross-RT C21 (the project's 3:1 member), so it is
  not a contradiction, but Genova & Aldrin's cycler should not be described as "found first in the
  Sun-forced model" without noting that C21 now exists in the CR3BP.
- Unconfirmed novelty-adjacent claims in the note that need the 2008 Leiva-Briozzo text before being
  stated publicly: any wording that Sun-continuation of Earth-Moon cycler-type orbits is a new
  capability.
