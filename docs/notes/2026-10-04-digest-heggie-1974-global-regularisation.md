## 0. What the paper is

A method paper in celestial mechanics. Abstract (p.217): the work of Aarseth and Zare (1974) is extended "to provide a global regularisation of the classical gravitational three-body problem: by transformation of the variables in a way that does not depend on the particular configuration, we obtain equations of motion which are regular with respect to collisions between any pair of particles. The only cases excepted are those in which collisions between more than one pair occur simultaneously and those in which at least one of the masses vanishes. However, by means of the same principles the restricted problem is regularised globally if collisions between the two primaries are excluded." It then generalises to perturbed three-body motion (section 5) and to N bodies (section 6), and gives, in an appendix, a general way of increasing the number of degrees of freedom of a Hamiltonian system, which is "central to the method".

Why it matters to this project: it is the standard reference for a regularisation that is regular at a close approach to EITHER primary, in Hamiltonian form, with a stated restricted-problem limit that allows the primaries to move on any Kepler orbit (circular, elliptic, parabolic or hyperbolic). The project's Earth-Moon second species work (#899) is a restricted problem with repeated passes at distances of 1e-5 or less of the Moon-Earth distance, which is the regime the paper is about.

## 1. The three-body construction (READ pp.217-223)

Units: G = 1. Masses m1, m2, m3; M = m1 + m2 + m3; reduced masses mu_ij = m_i m_j/(m_i + m_j). Inertial Cartesian coordinates q'_i, momenta p'_i, Hamiltonian (eq. 1):
H' = sum_i p'_i^2/(2 m_i) - m2 m3/|q'_2 - q'_3| - m3 m1/|q'_3 - q'_1| - m1 m2/|q'_1 - q'_2|.

Step 1, relative vectors (eqs. 4 to 5). New coordinates
q1 = q'_2 - q'_3, q2 = q'_3 - q'_1, q3 = q'_1 - q'_2 (the last two by cyclic interchange; the asterisk in the paper marks the cyclic rule), and <q> = (1/M) sum m_i q'_i. The three q_i are not independent: q1 + q2 + q3 = 0 (eq. 9). This is the key difference from Aarseth and Zare, who used two relative vectors and a third body as reference: here all three pair separations are put on an equal footing. The momenta are defined by
p'_i = sum_j p_j^T (dq_j/dq'_i) + <p>^T (d<q>/dq'_i), which gives, for example, p'_1 = -p_2 + p_3 + (m1/M) <p> (eq. 5, with cyclic versions).

Step 2, the Hamiltonian (eq. 6, read on the image):
H = p1^2/(2 mu_23) + p2^2/(2 mu_31) + p3^2/(2 mu_12) + <p>^2/(2M) - (p2.p3/m1 + p3.p1/m2 + p1.p2/m3) - m2 m3/q1 - m3 m1/q2 - m1 m2/q3,
with q_i = |q_i|. "This looks like a Hamiltonian for three independent Keplerian systems, save for the coupling term represented by the crossed momentum products", which is linear in each momentum (p.221).

Equations of motion (eq. 7): qdot_1 = p1/mu_23 - p3/m2 - p2/m3 (and cyclic), <qdot> = <p>/M, pdot_1 = -m2 m3 q1/q1^3 (and cyclic), <pdot> = 0.

Initial conditions (eq. 8): q1(0) = q'_20 - q'_30, <q>(0) = (1/M) sum m_i q'_i0, <p>(0) = sum p'_i0, and p1(0) = (1/3)(p'_20 - p'_30 - ((m2 - m3)/M) sum p'_i0) (eq. 8b; the factor 1/3 is consistent with p'_2 - p'_3 = 3 p1 when p1 + p2 + p3 = 0, COMPUTED check from eq. 5). Equation (9), sum q_i = 0, is an integral of the enlarged system and is the condition that the map (4) be invertible. Inversion (eq. 10 to 11): q*_1 = (m2 q3 - m3 q2)/M + <q>, so that q1 = q*_2 - q*_3, and p*_1 = -p2 + p3 + (m1/M) <p> (eq. 12). Equations (13) to (16) show that q*, p* satisfy the original equations (2) with the original initial conditions (3): so solving the new Hamiltonian system recovers the original motion. The device is called enlarging the dynamical system (nine degrees of freedom become twelve, with sum q_i = 0 as a constraint preserved by the flow); the general statement is in the appendix (section 6 below).

Step 3, KS transformation (eq. 17 to 19). In the centre-of-mass frame the <p> term is dropped. Each vector q_i = (q_i1, q_i2, q_i3) receives the Kustaanheimo-Stiefel map to a 4-vector Q_i = (Q_i1, ..., Q_i4):
q_i1 = Q_i1^2 - Q_i2^2 - Q_i3^2 + Q_i4^2,
q_i2 = 2 (Q_i1 Q_i2 - Q_i3 Q_i4),
q_i3 = 2 (Q_i1 Q_i3 + Q_i2 Q_i4),   (eq. 17)
with R_i = q_i = Q_i^T Q_i, the 4 x 3 matrix A_i = 2 [[Q_i1, Q_i2, Q_i3], [-Q_i2, Q_i1, Q_i4], [-Q_i3, -Q_i4, Q_i1], [Q_i4, -Q_i3, Q_i2]] (eq. 18, read on the image of p.221), momenta p_i = A_i^T P_i/(4 R_i) (eq. 19) and p_i^T p_i = P_i^T P_i/(4 R_i). The notation R1, R2, R3 here corresponds to Aarseth and Zare's R2, R1, R (p.222).

Step 4, the time transformation (eq. 20): dtau = dt/(R1 R2 R3), so dt = R1 R2 R3 dtau, with the Hamiltonian Gamma* = (script-H - h) R1 R2 R3 where h is the value of the Hamiltonian on the path of interest. Written out (p.222, read on the image):
Gamma* = (1/8) (R2R3/mu_23 P1.P1 + R3R1/mu_31 P2.P2 + R1R2/mu_12 P3.P3)
 - (1/16) (R1/m1 P2^T A2 A3^T P3 + R2/m2 P3^T A3 A1^T P1 + R3/m3 P1^T A1 A2^T P2)
 - m2 m3 R2 R3 - m3 m1 R3 R1 - m1 m2 R1 R2 - h R1 R2 R3,
"a polynomial of degree six in the regularised coordinates and degree two in the conjugate momenta". Hamilton's equations (eq. 21): Q_i' = dGamma*/dP_i, P_i' = -dGamma*/dQ_i (prime = d/dtau). They are "regular with respect to collisions between any pair of bodies" and linear in each of P_i and Q_i for each i (p.222-223). The transformation is global because it is one composition (q' to q to Q, plus t to tau), not one chosen per pair.

Limitation: terms with reciprocals of the masses appear, so the equations do not apply if any mass vanishes. The restricted problem needs section 4.

## 2. Numerical experience (READ pp.223-226; tables read on both copies)

Both Heggie's and Aarseth-Zare's programmes used the Runge-Kutta-Fehlberg 7(8) algorithm (Fehlberg 1968) on the same computer with about sixteen significant figures. Cost per step: 1.6 times that of Aarseth-Zare for eqs. (20), (21) (p.223), and about 15 percent more again for the (22) to (24) system (p.226).

Alternative time transformation (eq. 22, p.224), with n = 3/2: dtau = (R1 + R2 + R3)^n/(R1 R2 R3) dt. Equations (23) to (25): Q_i' = (R1+R2+R3)^(-3/2) dGamma*/dP_i; P_i' = -(R1+R2+R3)^(-3/2) {dGamma*/dQ_i - (3/2) Gamma* d ln(R1+R2+R3)/dQ_i} (eq. 24), replaceable by P_i' = -(R1+R2+R3)^(-3/2) dGamma*/dQ_i (eq. 25) because Gamma* = 0 on the solution path. With tolerance 1e-10 on the realistic problem, eqs. (22), (23), (25) gave |dE/E| = 1e-11 in 63 steps (against 83 steps and 3e-11 for eq. 21).

Triple collisions (p.224-225): R_i ~ alpha_i t^(2/3) as t tends to 0 from below (Siegel and Moser 1971, section 12). With (22), the regularised time is bounded as t tends to 0 if and only if n is greater than 3/2, while a necessary and sufficient condition for the equations of motion to be regular is n less than 3/2: "a compromise was sought by the choice n = 3/2." In practice, for a very close triple encounter (all mutual distances simultaneously below about 1e-7 of their initial values) integrations with (20), (21) or with (22), (23), (25) are "very unsatisfactory". Using (24) instead of (25) removes this: the two are formally identical on the solution path but differ in numerical stability. The linearised analysis about the analytic three-body collision orbits (Siegel and Moser section 13) found that the extra degrees of freedom do not create unstable modes, but the time transformation can: for (20), (21) and for (22), (23), (25), modes grow as 1/R_i, and "No such unstable mode is present if Equations (22)-(24) are adopted." The stabilising term is likened to Baumgarte's (1972) control term.

## 3. The restricted problem (READ pp.226-231; equations (26) to (46) read on images)

This is the section that bears on the project. It is explicitly for a massless third body whose primaries may have any Kepler relative motion.

### 3.1 The limit m3 tends to 0 of the three-body equations (pp.226-229)

The three-body Hamiltonian cannot be used with m3 = 0. Set V_i = P_i/m3 (i = 1, 2) (eq. 26); equations (27), (28) (read on the image) give Q_1', Q_2', Q_3' in terms of P3, V1, V2 and R's, "well-behaved in the limit m3 tends to 0". A new variable k is defined by (eq. 29) m3 k = h - p3^2/(2 mu_12) + m1 m2/q3, with m3 kdot = q3^T (m2 p2 + m1 p1)/q3^3 (eq. 30) and, in regularised variables, k' = (1/(8 R3^2)) Q3^T A3 (m2 R1 A2^T V2 + m1 R2 A1^T V1) (eq. 31), "which is independent of m3 but singular as R3 tends to 0, i.e. in case of collision between the primaries." V1', V2', P3' are given by (32) and (33), with P3' simplifying enormously for m3 = 0. New initial variables (eq. 34 and the display on p.228): p'_3 = m3 v'_3, p_i = m3 v_i; v10 = (p'_20 - m2 v'_30)/M, v20 = (-p'_10 + m1 v'_30)/M, p30 = (m2 p'_10 - m1 p'_20)/M, <p>_0 = p'_10 + p'_20 + m3 v'_30 taken as zero in the chosen frame; the initial value of k for small m3 is computed from (p.229)
k = (1/2) ((m2 + m3)/m2 v1^2 + (m1 + m3)/m1 v2^2) - (v2.p3/m1 + v1.p3/m2 + v1.v2) - m2/q1 - m1/q2
in the centre-of-mass rest frame.

Printed remark (p.229): these equations "may be used to treat three-dimensional motion of the massless particle, and the relative motion of the primaries may be hyperbolic, parabolic or elliptic, as well as circular; collision orbits alone being excepted." They were derived "motivated by the search for a global regularisation of the restricted problem", and given for m3 not zero as a likely better form when one mass is very small but non-zero.

### 3.2 The alternative derivation for a massless particle (pp.229-231)

Hamiltonian per unit mass of the third body (eq. 35): H' = p'^2/2 - m1/|q' - q'_1| - m2/|q' - q'_2|, with the primary positions q'_i(t) prescribed functions of time. New coordinates q_i = q' - q'_i (eq. 36), i = 1, 2; generating function W = (q' - q'_1)^T p1 + (q' - q'_2)^T p2 (eq. 37), so p' = p1 + p2 (eq. 38). The new Hamiltonian is H = H' + dW/dt:
H = (1/2)(p1^2 + p2^2) + p1.p2 - m1/q1 - m2/q2 - qdot'_1.p1 - qdot'_2.p2   (eq. 39),
explicitly time-dependent through the primaries. Initial conditions (eqs. 40, 41): q_i(0) = q'_0 - q'_i0, p_i(0) = p'_0/2. Equations of motion (eq. 42): qdot_i = p1 + p2 - qdot'_i, pdot_i = -m_i q_i/q_i^3. A first integral: q1 + q'_1 = q2 + q'_2, so q' = q1 + q'_1 (eq. 43) recovers the particle's inertial position. The value h of H along the path is NOT constant: hdot = -qddot'_1.p1 - qddot'_2.p2 (eq. 44), and in the centre-of-mass frame with Kepler relative motion q3 = q'_1 - q'_2, qddot_3 = -(m1 + m2) q3/q3^3, so hdot = q3^T (m2 p1 - m1 p2)/q3^3 (eq. 45), "identical with (30) apart from changes of notation"; "The quantity h is not quite the energy per unit mass of the third body, but differs from this by the value of dW/dt." (p.231).

KS transformation and time transformation (eq. 46): dt = R1 R2 R3 dtau (R_i = q_i; R3 = |q3| = the primary separation), with
Gamma* = -h R1 R2 R3 + (1/8) R3 (R2 P1^2 + R1 P2^2) + (1/16) R3 P1^T A1 A2^T P2 - R3 (m1 R2 + m2 R1) + (1/4) (R3/(m1 + m2)) qdot_3^T (-m2 R2 A1^T P1 + m1 R1 A2^T P2),
and Q_i' = dGamma*/dP_i, P_i' = -dGamma*/dQ_i, i = 1, 2. "The equations of motion remain regular even if we omit the factor R3 from (46) and from Gamma*" (p.231): so for primaries on a prescribed orbit the time transformation can be taken as dt = R1 R2 dtau, with R1 and R2 the distances of the particle to the two primaries. The equation for h' is regular during collisions of the particle with either primary.

Remarks printed on this section (p.231): "For most investigations into the restricted problem it would be preferable to work in a system of coordinates in which the primaries are at rest. The principles used in the second half of the present section are easily applied to treat the corresponding Hamiltonian, but we shall not stop to give the details, for an alternative global regularisation of this problem already exists (Waldvogel, 1967)" (Waldvogel, Bull. Astron. 3:295; not held). And: the extension to any number of massive bodies "should be of value in the computation of orbits for a probe suffering successive close encounters with different planets, for example."

How it reduces to the restricted problem: the reduction is the m3 tends to 0 limit of section 2's regularisation (3.1), and, independently, the direct massless-particle construction (3.2), with both primaries given by prescribed motion. Neither is the standard rotating-frame planar CR3BP; the frame is inertial and the primaries' motion enters through qdot'_i in (39) and (46). Planar motion is a special case (q_i3 = 0 in the 3-vector, KS 4-vector with the planar restriction); the paper does not say this and does not give a planar (Levi-Civita) version, so the planar reduction of KS is my reading (INFERRED).

## 4. Perturbed three-body motion and N bodies (READ pp.231-237; text layer for pp.233 to 237)

- Perturbed three-body (section 5): add N - 3 further bodies; the perturbation enters through R' = -sum_{i<=3, j>=4} m_i m_j/|q'_i - q'_j|, T'_1 = kinetic energy of the other bodies, R'_1 = their mutual potential (eqs. 47, 48, read on image); the coordinates are (49) to (51), the momenta (52) to (53), and the inverse q'_1 = (m2 q3 - m3 q2)/M + <q> (54). The centre of mass of the triple moves under the perturbation (equations (58)); an "internal energy" h3 of the triple is integrated with a regular equation (written in terms of Q using (17), (18)) so that the cost does not grow as N^2. The appendix result is used in place of a direct proof (p.233). "In comparing the numerical results of Section 3, it is therefore justifiable to take the number of integration steps as a realistic measure of the computational effort" when most of the effort is in the perturbing forces for large N.
- N bodies (section 6): q_ij = q'_i - q'_j for i < j, momenta P_i = sum_j p_ij - sum_j p_ji + (m_i/M) <p> (eq. 63), a KS map on each of the N(N-1)/2 vectors, time transformation dtau = dt/(prod_{i<j} R_ij) (eq. 64), Gamma* = (script-H - h) prod R_ij, Hamilton's equations (65): "regular with respect to collisions between any pair of the N bodies, provided that two or more collisions do not occur simultaneously" (p.237). For N = 2 it is the KS regularisation; for N = 3 it is section 2. Cost (p.237): N(N - 1)/2 pairs, eight first-order equations per pair, so 4N(N - 1) + 1 equations in all, against 6N or 6(N - 1) unregularised; for N = 2 the count rises from 6 to 9, for N = 3 from 12 to 25 (COMPUTED check: 4 x 3 x 2 + 1 = 25 and 4 x 2 x 1 + 1 = 9, as printed). The number of terms grows faster with N than for the Newtonian equations: "these equations rapidly become inefficient as N increases". The case with a massless body is "a straightforward generalisation", and simultaneous regularisation of subsystems of any size is said to be possible "but whether the efficiency of a computer programme developed along such lines would justify the effort ... is, perhaps, another matter."

## 5. The appendix: enlargement of a Hamiltonian system (READ pp.238-241, text layer)

Setting: a system with n' coordinates q'_i, momenta p'_i, Hamiltonian H'(q', p'). Suppose there are n >= n' functions q_i(q') such that (A1) H'(q', p') = H''(q(q'), p') for some H'' and (A2) the Jacobian matrix {dq_i/dq'_j} has maximal rank n'. (The three-body example: n' = 9 and n = 12 with q the functions (4); the matrix is printed and has rank 9.) Choose a left-inverse q*(q) with q*_i(q(q')) = q'_i (A2, A3) and the relation sum_j (dq*_k/dq_j)(dq_j/dq'_l) = delta_kl (A4). Define new momenta p_i by p'_i = sum_j (dq_j/dq'_i) p_j (A5; n' equations in n unknowns, so the choice is wide), the new Hamiltonian H(q, p) = H''(q, p*(q, p)) (A6, with p* the transformed momenta), and integrate Hamilton's equations qdot_i = dH/dp_i, pdot_i = -dH/dq_i (A9, A12) with initial conditions satisfying (A3) and (A5). Theorem (summary p.241): "solution of these equations allows us to recover p' and q' by (A3) and (A5)", the solution of the initial value problem is unique, but not every solution of the enlarged system comes from the original: the initial conditions must satisfy q_i = q_i(q') and (A5). In the three-body case the integral sum q_i = 0 (9) gives the invertibility directly, "and this step is difficult to generalise completely", so the appendix proof is a different, more general argument. The central idea for the project: extra variables can make a force law polynomial and the transformation global.

## 7. Reconciliation against project code

Searched `src/cyclerfinder` on 2026-10-04 for Levi-Civita, Kustaanheimo, KS, Heggie, Waldvogel, Sundman and universal variables.
- `core/cr3bp_regularized.py`: a Sundman time-regularised CR3BP propagator with dt/ds = r1 r2, r1 or r2 ("Regularization = Literal['r1r2', 'r1', 'r2']"), augmented state (x, y, z, vx, vy, vz, t) integrated in s. This is the time transformation only; it keeps Cartesian coordinates and the singular force law. Its docstring cites Szebehely 1967 section 3.7 and Stiefel and Scheifele 1971. Heggie's dt = R1 R2 dtau with R3 omitted (section 3.2) is the same time transformation; what Heggie adds is the coordinate map (KS) that makes the equations regular and, in the two-body limit, linear (harmonic oscillator), which Sundman time alone does not do. (Statement that Sundman time alone leaves the equations singular for rectilinear collisions is the textbook result; INFERRED here, not checked in the code.)
- Levi-Civita: the project has a Levi-Civita regularised planar CR3BP in interval arithmetic in `scripts/certify_670_wz_oterma_regularized.py`, `scripts/_validated_taylor_integrator.py` and `tests/scripts/test_670_levi_civita_regularization.py` (task #670, Jupiter and the Oterma proof lane, built with fictitious time dt = r2 dtau on the extended-phase-space Hamiltonian Gamma = r2 (H - h), using the secondary-centred complex-square map z = w^2). It is a rigorous-enclosure tool, not a float propagator in `src/`, and it is not wired to any Earth-Moon or second-species work. The `#896` check (`tests/core/test_cr3bp_font_nunes_simo_second_species.py`, listed in `docs/notes/2026-10-04-896-published-checks-added.md`) says "The project has no Levi-Civita integrator, which is what the papers used": it means no float Levi-Civita integrator in `src/`, which is correct; the interval one in `scripts/` is the only one. That test bounded the integrator error by comparing plain DOP853 with the Sundman integrator, 1e-11 for passages farther than 1e-4 from the Moon and about 1e-9 for the closer ones, with orbit 12i coming within 8.7e-6 (in the test's CR3BP length units; COMPUTED, if the unit is the Earth-Moon distance of 384,400 km this is about 3.3 km from the Moon's centre, i.e. inside the Moon, a mathematical passage). The bound at the closest pass is therefore a bound by agreement of two non-independent integrators in the same coordinates, which is the gap a regularisation of different structure (KS or Levi-Civita) would close.
- Universal-variable Kepler propagation: `core/_stumpff.py`, `core/kepler.py`, `core/lambert.py`; this regularises two-body propagation (a different thing from regularising the restricted problem) and is unrelated to Heggie beyond the shared idea.
- No code or constant in the project is sourced from Heggie 1974, and no grep hit for Heggie, Waldvogel or Kustaanheimo exists in `src/`. Nothing in this paper contradicts project code. The project has no KS or LC float integrator, no regularised variational equation (the `genome/multi_shooting.py` header says "regularised STM is not implemented in the project; the dual integration" is used), and no integrator for primaries on elliptic orbits through a close pass.

## 8. Techniques applicable to the project's problems

All uses are INFERRED; nothing was built or run.

**#899 (second species continuation to Earth-Moon; reproduction of Casoliva 2010; with the Henon enumerator).**
1. A regularised integrator with a different structure from the Sundman one, to settle the "integrator error at a pass 8.7e-6 from the Moon" question. Build the planar Levi-Civita regularised CR3BP in `src/cyclerfinder/core` as a float propagator (the derivation and an interval-arithmetic implementation already exist in the #670 scripts; reuse them, do not rederive). Heggie's paper supplies the justification of the construction (section 3.2: coordinates relative to the secondary, KS or LC map, dt = R2 dtau) and the stated limitation (collisions between the two primaries excluded, which does not arise). The rotating-frame planar circular problem can use the secondary-centred LC map with the Jacobi constant fixed, as #670 does; Heggie's inertial, time-dependent Hamiltonian (39) is the alternative if the primaries are not on a circle (see #917 below). Tests, in order: (i) LC map round trip and a radial fall through the secondary against the closed form (the #670 tests already do this in intervals); (ii) a Kepler ellipse about the Moon at small mu: in the regularised time the motion is harmonic, so the LC-integrated orbit must agree with the universal-variable `core/kepler.py` solution to 1e-13; (iii) agreement with plain DOP853 and with the Sundman propagator on the `#896` Font-Nunes-Simo orbits at passages above 1e-2, 1e-3, 1e-4 from the Moon, and at 12i (8.7e-6) the three-way difference; (iv) the control `#896` already used. If (iii) shows plain and Sundman agree with LC to 1e-9 at 12i then the existing bound stands as an independent bound; if not, the `#896` tolerances were a coincidence of the two same-structure integrators. The expected outcome is not known; record it either way (it is a clean negative or positive on the existing bound).
2. Regularised variational equations (the STM through a close pass). The multi-shooting and continuation of #899 need the monodromy through each lunar pass; `genome/multi_shooting.py` records that a regularised STM is not implemented. Heggie's Hamiltonian is smooth at collision, so its variational equations are regular; the #670 interval implementation already builds the variational equation of the Levi-Civita flow (`V' = Df_reg(y) V`, noted in `data/OUTSTANDING.md`). Control: the STM through a pass must reproduce the finite-difference Jacobian of the same regularised flow to the usual 1e-6 and its determinant must equal 1 (symplectic in the extended space; Heggie notes the transformations are canonical, so a symplectic-form check on the regularised STM is a free test).
3. Where this fits with the second species theory (Guillaume 1973, Henon 1997, Perko): the second species orbits have collisions at M at mu = 0; at mu = 0.0121529529 the passes are at periselene distances of order mu (3e-3 to 1e-2 in Earth-Moon units), not at 1e-5. A regularised integrator is not needed for those; it is needed for the continuation in mass from small mu, where r_p = mu (e - 1)/v_inf^2 shrinks with mu and the pass is at 1e-5 or closer, and for passes with demanded turn near 180 degrees. So its use in #899 is the mass-continuation driver (the Casoliva three-step), not the Earth-Moon orbits themselves.

**#906 (turn-gate hardening).** Heggie provides no turn criterion. It provides an independent route to the demanded turn actually flown: integrate through the pass in regularised variables and measure the relative-velocity direction change, then compare with the first-order relation r_p = mu (e - 1)/v_inf^2 and with the Henon demanded turn. The check to add: for each gated junction with a periapsis below a threshold, report the actual (integrated) deflection against the gate's, to tolerance 1e-9. Control: a Table 4.4 single-arc orbit of Henon 1997 at mu = 1e-8, where the demanded turn is known to the printed digits (Henon digest sections 2.4 and 4(d)).

**#890 and #895 (Titania-Oberon, a Uranian orbit; methods only).** A massless particle with two moons and a planet is a restricted problem with three attractors. Heggie's section 3.2 is for two attractors with prescribed motion, and his remark (p.231) that the extension to any number of massive bodies suits "a probe suffering successive close encounters with different planets" is the relevant statement: for #895, where the real-ephemeris arcs have close flybys of two moons, regularise with respect to the nearer moon at each pass (KS with respect to the moon being passed; this is the standard per-encounter KS that section 1 calls not global for N > 2, which is fine for a particle passing one body at a time). Section 6's global N-body form costs 4N(N-1)+1 equations and is not warranted. Not testable against published numbers (none).

**#913 to #915, #918.** Zero-size flybys on real planet positions (#913, #915, #918) need no regularisation. #914 (Russell and Strange moon cyclers with every flyby integrated): the integrated flybys are at altitudes far above the singular scale; a regularised integrator is a precision check, not a requirement (the existing `verify/flyby_integrate.py` was not read for this note).

**#916 and #917 (persistence conjecture; Casoliva rows in the elliptic problem with the fold count).** #917 needs the elliptic restricted problem near the Moon: Heggie's (39) and (46) are written for primaries on ANY Kepler motion (printed p.229), including elliptic. This is the only paper in the corpus I have read that regularises the elliptic restricted problem at a primary in Hamiltonian form; it is a candidate integrator for the #912 and #917 close passes. The cost is the time dependence (h varies, eq. 44) and an inertial frame. #916 needs invariant curves of the one-period map, which need the monodromy through the passes: see #899 item 2.

**#921 to #924.** #924 (FLI and MEGNO screens): both indicators integrate variational equations along the trajectory and are corrupted by a close pass in the unregularised variables (the variational vector grows as 1/r^3 through the pass); computing them in regularised variables is the standard fix. This is an INFERRED requirement, not a result: the FLI and MEGNO digests were not re-read for this note. #921 and #923 do not use regularisation.

- Its Table III is the same experiment as Heggie's Table III (three unit masses at rest, same perturbations, tolerance 1e-12, time reversal) with a different integrator.
