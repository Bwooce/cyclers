## Headline

1. **`#835` is resolved, and the answer was not the suspected one.** `5.30ggF3`'s
   141.244° node-2 demand is a **loop cyclic-ORDER** defect. Every one of the row's legs
   reproduces its sourced conic; the designated-arc split is innocent. Under the other
   traversal of the same legs the closure is bend-feasible at **every** node and measures
   TR **1.255** against the published **1.27**.
2. **The discriminator is exactly the probe `#835`'s registration proposed** — the
   **vector** residual mode, whose per-flyby bend hinge sees what the magnitude residual
   cannot. It agrees with magnitude mode on 10 of 12 rows.
3. **`#829`'s fix is bigger than "also report a flag": the filter must precede the
   ranking.** With that one change the blind 256-epoch grid goes from **2** CLOSE-AND-MATCH
   verdicts to **6**, and all 8 converging probes become admissible (7 strictly ballistic
   + `6.44Gg3` faithfully near-ballistic) — correcting `#826`'s "6 admissible + 2 not".
4. **Every historical CLOSE-\* verdict is re-classified**: 27 retracted, 9 stand.
   `#820`'s two CLOSE-AND-MATCH headlines survive; all ten of its CLOSE-OFF-ANCHOR labels
   do not.
5. **`#830` lands on an honest negative about the INSTRUMENT.** The structural obstacle is
   genuinely gone — a continuous full-period multi-arc trajectory now exists and
   propagates — but the V2 lap-drift gate is **degenerate** for this construction, proven
   with a negative control. A `stable` verdict there measures period commensurability, not
   ballistic periodicity. **No promotion is proposed.**

## 1. `#833` — the measured turn ratio as a named instrument

`src/cyclerfinder/search/turn_ratio_check.py` (+ `tests/search/test_turn_ratio_check.py`,
8 tests). `measure_turn_ratio(nodes, sequence)` returns a per-flyby report (required bend,
max ballistic bend, ratio, feasible) plus the binding node and
`turn_ratio = min(max_bend / required_bend)` — a like-for-like reproduction of
Russell-Ocampo 2003 p.13's published TR, because our `_max_bend_deg` uses the same sourced
200 km safe altitude. `closure_turn_ratio(result, ...)` does the same from a
`BallisticClosureResult`. `agrees_with_published(tr)` compares at `TURN_RATIO_TOL = 0.05`
(`#826` measured a 0.001–0.024 spread).

Golden discipline: the expected side is always the row's own published
`invariants.turn_ratio`; the bends emerge from the converged chain. `#826`'s test file now
imports the instrument instead of carrying its own copy, so there is exactly one
implementation.

Also added (during `#830`, §5): **`wrap_node_turn`** — the periodicity wrap flyby that
`_bend_feasible` never checks.

## 2. `#835` — the defect is the loop cyclic order, not the designated arc

The `#835` registration suspected the `F(3:2,82.487,180.000)` designated-arc split. It is
innocent, and the evidence is direct.

**The Mars node's required bend is 0.000°** — legs 0 and 1 are literally one conic, i.e.
the split is faithful. So the per-leg osculating elements were compared against the `#794`
written-back segment values:

| leg | genome | converged (a AU, e) | sourced | note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0+1 | designated `F(3:2)` | **1.3104, 0.2682** | a = 1.5^(2/3) = **1.3104**; aphelion 1.6618 vs published **1.66** | exact |
| 2 | `loop-ee-1` = `g(1.4646,527.25,Ll)` | 1.0541, 0.1796 | 1.0545, 0.1807 | exact |
| 3 | `loop-ee-2` = `g(1.9416,338.97,U)` | 1.5831, 0.3708 | 1.5828, 0.3711 | exact |

Every leg IS Russell's leg. What was wrong was the ORDER in which the two E-E loops are
traversed. `build_genome` reads them in the descriptor's printed order (`#794`'s cyclic
mapping); with the other traversal of the same legs:

| loop order | bend feasible | per-node TR | wrap node (frame-corrected) |
|---|---|---|---|
| printed `[0,1]` | **no** | M:∞ **E2:0.613** E3:1.255 | 1.198 |
| reversed `[1,0]` | **yes** | M:∞ E2:2.357 **E3:1.255** | 2.683 |

published TR = **1.27**. The reversed order reproduces it (Δ 0.015) and is flyable at every
node; the printed one demands 141.244° where 86.582° is available.

**Why the campaign could not see it.** The magnitude residual constrains only |V∞|
continuity, which both orders satisfy identically. Truth residuals: printed 0.0736,
reversed 0.0831 — magnitude mode *prefers the wrong one*, by 0.01 km/s. In vector mode
(bend hinge inside the residual) the same pair reads **1.1242 vs 0.0831** — a 13× margin
the right way.

**Blast radius, measured on all 12 rows.** Vector and magnitude select the same
`(loop order, leg-1 topology)` on 10. The two that differ:

- `5.30ggF3` — vector picks the reversed order (feasible, TR 1.255 vs 1.27).
- `5.75ggF3` — magnitude **ties exactly** (0.3489 both orders, so its "choice" is an
  arbitrary tie-break); vector picks the printed order (feasible, TR 1.347 vs 1.34). Its
  reverse measures 0.678 — infeasible. The correct answer is row-specific, and only the
  bend-aware residual gets both right.

**Control preserved:** `6.44Gg3` still measures TR 0.956 against its published 0.95 and is
still bend-infeasible. That is a published property (Russell Table 4.13 near-ballistic) and
must not be "fixed" away.

`select_leg1_topology` is accordingly renamed **`select_topology`** and now discriminates
leg 1's `(n_revs, branch)` AND the loop cyclic order jointly, in vector mode, reporting
both residual modes for every candidate.

**Not done here, deliberately:** the campaign-side selection now *contradicts* the
`loop-ee-*` segment notes, which assert the printed cyclic order as a `#794`
primary-source mapping. That is a catalogue data claim and needs its own primary-source
adjudication — registered, no `catalogue.yaml` edit made.

## 3. `#829` — filter before ranking

`BallisticClosureResult` exposes `constraints_satisfied` (= converged AND bend_feasible AND
vinf_cap_ok), and spec §14 **V0** requires the hard constraints on top of the residual.
Pre-`#829`, `run_row` filtered on `converged` and then took `min` by residual. That is not
merely an under-report — it is **systematically wrong**: magnitude mode drives the
powered/degenerate basin to ~1e-14 while the bend-feasible (usually vector-mode) solution
sits at a comparable residual, so the `min` always returned the infeasible one. In `#820`'s
runlog, every bend-infeasible "best" is `mode=magnitude` and both surviving
CLOSE-AND-MATCH rows are `mode=vector`.

Changes: `run_row` filters on `constraints_satisfied` **before** ranking; the fallback
among inadmissible closures is the **least-violating** (highest measured TR), not the
lowest residual, so a near-ballistic family is not hidden by the degenerate basin; new
outcome `CLOSE-INADMISSIBLE` names the violated constraint; `_classify` now also checks
the published `turn_ratio` as a match anchor; `probe_at_truth` reports the per-node
required/max bend, the measured vs published TR, and an **admissibility** axis
(`ADMISSIBLE` / `NEAR-BALLISTIC-AS-PUBLISHED` / `BEND-INFEASIBLE` / `NOT-CONVERGED`) kept
orthogonal to `#820`'s unchanged stayed/walked verdict — one is about the closure reached,
the other about the seed.

### Historical re-classification (`[[feedback_bugfix_invalidates_past_searches]]`)

`scripts/reclassify_russell12_runlogs.py` →
`data/runs/russell12-829-reclassification.json` (+ 7 tests). Over all 5 runlogs, 44
records: **27 RETRACTED, 9 STANDS, 8 N/A**. Every CLOSE-\* verdict of the June-07 run and

**Scope, stated honestly in the script itself:** a runlog stores only the best result, so
this retracts a *recorded verdict*; it cannot conclude that no admissible closure existed
at some other epoch of that run. The pre-`#820` runs cannot be meaningfully re-run at all
(mis-posed genome), which is exactly why record-level retraction is the right instrument
there and a re-run is the right instrument for the current posing. **A retraction is not a
validation-level downgrade.**

## 4. `#830` prerequisite — the exact synodic period

`period_sec_for_row(row, "exact-synodic")` = `k × T_syn(pair)` from the bodies' own mean
motions (`synodic_period_days`), wired through `build_genome(period_mode=...)`; default
unchanged. `T_syn(E,M) = 779.9286 d`, so k=2 → 4.270657 yr (printed 4.27, **+0.24 d**) and
k=3 → 6.405985 yr (printed 6.41, **−1.47 d**).

The rounding is secular, and now measured rather than estimated — the same closure
propagated 3 laps drifts **2.8e6–3.2e7 km** (56×–641× the 50,000 km tolerance) on the
printed period and ~1e-5 km on the exact one. `#820`'s ~3e6 km/lap estimate was right.

A side benefit: the exact posing *improves* published-TR agreement on 4 of the 7 rows
(3.78Gg3 1.786 → **1.812** vs 1.81; 5.30ggF3 1.255 → **1.273** vs 1.27; mcconaghy and
4.991gG2 2.658 → **2.652** vs 2.65).

## 5. `#830` — the V2 gate is degenerate here (negative control)

`src/cyclerfinder/search/multiarc_cycler.py::build_multiarc_cycler` is the missing adapter
from a converged N-arc `BallisticClosureResult` to a `model.Cycler` the §12 / §14-V2
machinery can propagate. With it, `test_free_return_v2_ballistic`'s recorded structural
ground — "no continuous ≥3-lap trajectory to propagate" — **is obsolete**.

But the gate cannot answer the question. `verify_long_term_stability` reconstructs each lap
from the cycler's **leg template** at lap-shifted planet positions (`propagate_lap` step 3)
instead of integrating across the wrap. On an exactly commensurate period with the circular
ephemeris the planets return to the same configuration, so **any** template repeats.

**Negative control** (`[[feedback_verify_gauntlet_with_positive_control]]`), on
`9.353Gg2` and `5.30ggF3`:

| chain | max |Δ|V∞|| at a node | 3-lap drift | `stable` |
|---|---|---|---|
| converged closure | 0.000 km/s | 2.7e-5 km | True |
| broken ±5 d | 0.67 km/s | 2.8e-5 km | True |
| broken ±20 d | 2.87 km/s | 2.7e-5 km | True |
| broken ±100 d | **9.12 km/s** (34.98 on 5.30ggF3) | 3.5e-5 km | **True** |
| broken t0 +37 d | 2.18 km/s | 9.0e-6 km | True |

A chain requiring a 35 km/s impulsive burn at a flyby passes identically. So `stable` here
is a statement about **period commensurability**, not about the trajectory, and must never
be cited as V2 evidence for this construction. The driver reports `GATE-DEGENERATE` with
the `(drift, all-node feasibility)` pair rather than a "PASS".

**What is load-bearing instead.** In an idealized-flyby model, once single-period closure
holds and every flyby's required turn is deliverable, the multi-lap trajectory *is* the
single lap repeated — so ≥3-lap boundedness follows from all-node feasibility. Which makes
the **periodicity wrap node** decisive, and `_bend_feasible` never checks it (it loops over
intermediate encounters only). `wrap_node_turn` measures it, rotating `vinf_out(b0)` by the
home body's own advance over the period. Construction validated: on single-loop rows the
wrap ratio comes out *equal* to the intermediate node's (9.353Gg2: 1.702 = 1.702), as the
cycle's symmetry requires.

Measured over the 7 admissible rows on the exact period
(`data/found/830_v2_ballistic_multiarc/results.json`): **all seven are feasible at every
node including the wrap.** That is a real, new statement — and it is an input to an
adjudication, not a promotion. No `catalogue.yaml` edit was made.
