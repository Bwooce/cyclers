## 0. What the paper is, and what it is not

An eight-page conference survey. The abstract (p.199, READ): "An approximate analytic description is given of the families of periodic symmetric solutions of the planar circular restricted three-body problem for small values of mu." The conclusion (p.206) says what the reader should expect: "We have given a brief survey of our analysis of the P.S. families ... All this work will be exposed in a series of papers in preparation." So the paper gives (i) a complete classification of the limits of periodic symmetric (P.S.) solutions as mu tends to 0, (ii) the qualitative picture of bifurcation, and (iii) two worked second species bifurcations as illustrations, each with a printed local equation but with the coefficients left unspecified. There is no table, no numerical orbit, and no stated mass ratio other than the labels "mu about 10^-3" (Fig. 3) and "mu about 10^-2" (Fig. 4) on the numerically computed curves of other authors. The matching scheme itself is not derived here: it is cited to Breakwell and Perko (1965, 1966) "which however we had to adapt to the case, where the zero order deflexion angle beta^(0) ... is 0" (p.203). The full theory is in the papers listed as "Celes. Mech. (to appear)" which are Guillaume, Celest. Mech. 11:213-254 and 449-467 (1975), as cited by Perko; not held.

Nothing in the paper is a usable numerical control. Section 4 lists the few printed numbers and says why none can be a test of a computed orbit.

## 1. Definitions (READ pp.199-200)

- Frame: synodic axes centred at the Earth E (mass 1 - mu), the Moon M (mass mu, small) at distance 1 on the x axis, the satellite S of negligible mass; the synodic frame is obtained from the sidereal one by a rotation through angle t (Fig. 1, p.199). Note the origin is in the Earth, the same convention as Henon 1997.
- P.S. solution (p.199): a solution with initial conditions (x0, 0, 0, ydot0) is symmetric about the synodic axis EM. If it crosses EM orthogonally again at t = T it is 2T-periodic: a periodic symmetric solution. It is a point of the (x0, C) plane, C the Jacobi constant; "two points of this plane (with same ordinate C) correspond to a given P.S. orbit, since there are two orthogonal crossings".
- Wintner (1932), as quoted (p.200): (1) for fixed mu the P.S. solutions form continuous one-parameter P.S. families, each represented by two curves of the (x0, C) plane, the P.S. characteristics; (2) families close on themselves or terminate only when x0, C or T becomes infinite (principle of natural termination). (The reference list prints the name as "Winther, A.: 1932, Math. Zeits. 34"; the text spells it Wintner. This is a typesetting slip on the page, the same author.)
- Basic orbit (p.200): the limit r^(0)(t) of a P.S. solution r(t, mu) as mu tends to 0. The classification turns on whether the distance rho(mu) from M to r(t, mu) tends to 0.

## 2. The basic case mu = 0+ : the classification of basic orbits (READ pp.200-201)

Case (1), rho(0+) not zero: the basic orbits are two-body P.S. solutions, and form
- I_d and I_r: direct and retrograde circular orbits, each a one-parameter family represented by two curves of the (x0, C) plane, called the basic characteristics of the first kind (Poincare 1899 terminology), designated I_d and I_r.
- E_ij: elliptical orbits of rational mean motion n, "i and j being all the positive relatively prime integers", one family for each pair, "along which |n| has constantly the value j/i", each represented by two ellipses of the (x0, C) plane: the basic characteristics E_ij of the second kind.

Case (2), rho(0+) = 0: the second species solutions (S.S.S., Poincare 1899). A basic S.S.S. is a finite union of conic arcs "with consecutive collisions" (Henon 1968): they begin and end at M. Henon's classification, as stated:
- the conics S, which meet M at two different points of the sidereal frame. In the synodic axes they are symmetric about EM, which they cross orthogonally; "they form an infinite number of continuous one parameter families, each represented by one curve of the (x0, C)-plane (: there is only one point of orthogonal crossing)". These curves are "basic characteristics of second species", given Henon's names A0, A1, ..., B0, B1, ..., C12, C24, ...; Guillaume calls them collectively the basic characteristics S.
- the conics T, with rational mean motion, whose collisions with M take place at one and only one point of the sidereal frame. "To each value j/i of |n| corresponds a continuous one parameter family of such conics T."

The map to the limit (p.200-201): if (x0'(mu), C(mu)) and (x0''(mu), C(mu)) are the two points representing r(t, mu), the "basic points of symmetric conjunctions" are their limits as mu tends to 0, and the set of them is made of I_d, I_r, E_ij and S. The completing feature (p.201): x0'(mu) or x0''(mu) can tend to 1. Then the orthogonal crossing can disappear on the basic orbit, at the same time as a discontinuity appears in the direction of the relative velocity. Fig. 2 sketches this: the basic orbit passes through M and its relative velocity jumps, characterised by the angle beta^(0), "the zero order deflexion angle" (the project's demanded turn, in the rotating frame, evaluated at mu = 0). Hence (p.201): "This is the essential reason why the two body problem has not to be considered in itself but as the limiting case of the restricted problem as mu tends to 0. And thus, in order to complete the set of basic points of symmetric conjunctions, we must add the straight line of equation x0 = 1 as a supplementary basic characteristic of second species." The line x0 = 1 is the characteristic of orbits that cross EM at the Moon's position.

INFERRED relation to Henon 1997: this is the 1973 form of Henon's (1997) definition of generating orbits: the species of Henon's Definitions 2.10.1 to 2.10.3; E_ij corresponds to Henon's T-arcs and type 1 supporting ellipses with mean motion j/i (Henon 1997 labels the same ellipse by (I, J) with A = a^(3/2) = I/J, so Guillaume's (i, j) = Henon's (I, J) when the same ratio is meant, the particle making J revolutions in I Moon revolutions). Guillaume's S characteristics are Henon's S-arc families (one curve in the (x0, C) plane because there is one orthogonal crossing). I did not check that the two papers' E_ij naming matches letter for letter; treat the mapping as INFERRED.

## 3. The case mu not 0: bifurcation and the "policeman" (READ pp.202-203)

- Fig. 3 (p.202): a portion of P.S. characteristic computed numerically by Deprit (1967, 1968) in the Sun-Jupiter case (mu about 10^-3), drawn with the basic characteristics (I_d, E12, E23, C12, B1, ...) as dashed, dotted and dash-dotted lines; boxed regions A, B, C (regular behaviour) and D, E, F (bifurcations). The (x0, C) axes run x0 from -1.8 to 1.0 and C from 0 to about 3.5 (axis labels as printed).
- Two behaviours (p.202): either the P.S. characteristic follows a basic characteristic ("regular behaviour"), or, "when it approaches the intersection of two basic characteristics, it often happens that it leaves the former basic characteristic and begins to follow the other one. We have called this phenomenon 'bifurcation' and 'bifurcation orbit' the basic orbit corresponding to the intersection of the two basic characteristics."
- The programme (p.202), quoted: "if we know that a P.S. characteristic follows a basic characteristic till the first bifurcation it will encounter, if at this bifurcation, a 'policeman' can tell which way it must go, and if such a policeman can be found at each bifurcation, it will be possible to follow this P.S. characteristic till its natural termination." This is the goal that Henon 1997 reports unreached in general (Henon digest section 3 point 3: junctions are decided only by symmetry and Broucke's principle, which "is not sufficient" in general).
- Regular portions: "done extensively (Uno, 1937; Arenstorf, 1963; Guillaume, 1969, 1971, 1972)" (p.202).
- Two classes of bifurcation (p.203): by whether the bifurcation orbit passes through M. If not, the bifurcation is at the intersection of basic characteristics of the first and second kinds, "completely described" (Uno 1937; Guillaume 1969, 1971). The paper restricts itself to the second class, "second species bifurcations", where the bifurcation orbit passes through M and the zero order deflexion angle beta^(0) is 0. In Perko 1981's terms these are the type 1, 2 and 3 bifurcations and Perko's nu in (0, 1) regime (Perko digest Part B), where the deflection tends to zero with mu.

## 4. The two worked second species bifurcations (READ pp.203-205)

Both are described by Guillaume as "an illustration of the general theory"; the approach is to write the symmetry equations for the perturbed orbit, eliminate the crossing-time shift, and obtain a local relation among the variations.

### 4.1 Example 1: the retrograde circular orbit at (x0, C) = (-1, -1) (pp.203-204, Fig. 4)

- Setting: Broucke (1962, 1968) found, for the Earth-Moon case, portions of P.S. characteristics that he called A1 and F (Fig. 4, mu "about 10^-2", full lines). They follow the two basic characteristics I_r (first kind, retrograde circular) and A0 (second species) except near I_r intersect A0.
- The intersection has coordinates (-1, -1) and "corresponds to a retrograde circular orbit of periods 2 pi and pi respectively in the sidereal and synodical frames" (p.203).
- Local analysis (p.204): the symmetric orbit through (-1 + dx0, -1 + dC) is adjusted so that it again crosses EM orthogonally at a time about pi/2, written pi/2 + dT; after eliminating dT from the symmetry equations y(dT, dx0, dC, mu) = xdot(dT, dx0, dC, mu) = 0 (eq. 1) one gets, to O(mu^(3/2)),
  f1(-1 + dx0, -1 + dC) . f2(-1 + dx0, -1 + dC) = mu + O(mu^(3/2))   (eq. 2)
  where f1(x0, C) = 0 and f2(x0, C) = 0 are the equations of the curves A0 and I_r. "The curve of Equation (2) has an hyperbolic shape asymptotic to A0 and I_r (Figure 4). The agreement with the numerical results is, as can be seen, very good." (The agreement is visual, on Fig. 4: the dash-dotted hyperbola against Broucke's full-line curves A1 and F; no residual is printed.)
- Reading: a first species / second species type 3 junction at C = -1; the characteristic that reaches the crossing along I_r leaves along A0 or stays on the other branch, an avoided crossing (the two branches A1 above and F below the crossing in Fig. 4). Equation (2) says the product of the two distances to the basic curves is fixed by mu, with the coefficient of mu equal to 1 in the normalisation printed (the f1, f2 are not normalised in the paper, so the 1 is not a checkable number). Guillaume adds: "It is possible to explain by similar methods all the second species bifurcations which are known at this day."
- Relation to the others (INFERRED, checked against the digests): Perko 1981 digest Part B (section 3, example 2) says "There is another second species-second species (type 3) bifurcation at C = -1 in the family a"; Henon 1997 type 3 arcs are the retrograde unit circle with C = -1 (digest section 1.3). This is the same object. Perko's nu = 1/2 characteristic is "the two branches of a hyperbola" (digest section 3), the same hyperbolic form as eq. (2) here. The hyperbola eq. (2), f1 f2 = mu, is the earliest printed form I have found of the nu = 1/2 characteristic.

### 4.2 Example 2: the n = -2 ellipse (pp.204-205, Fig. 5)

- Orbit: "the elliptical orbit of n = -2 which orthogonally crosses EM once outside M and once at M. The t-interval between two consecutive collisions with M is 2 pi." Take the orthogonal crossing outside M as t = 0, initial conditions (x0, 0, 0, ydot0). (So the mean motion is 2 in magnitude, in the family E_12 of section 2, and the arc is a T-conic; the collision interval 2 pi means one Moon revolution per return, so in Henon's labelling (I, J) = (1, 2), not the (2, 1) orbit of Perko's two worked examples.)
- Perturbation: a second species solution with initial conditions (x0 + dx0, 0, dxdot0, ydot0 + dydot0), chosen to have two orthogonal crossings near M at t = 2 pi + dT1 and t = -2 pi + dT2. Eliminating dT1 and dT2 from the four symmetry equations y(dT_k, ...) = xdot(dT_k, ...) = 0 (k = 1, 2) gives two equations that split into two systems, as printed (p.204):
  (3') dxdot0 = 0 + O(mu)
  (3'') (alpha dx0 + beta dydot0)(alpha' dx0 + beta' dydot0) = gamma mu + O(mu^(3/2))
  (4') dx0 = lambda dxdot0^2 + nu dydot0^2 + O(mu)
  (4'') dydot0 (a dydot0 + b dxdot0)(a dydot0 - b dxdot0) = delta mu + O(mu^(4/3))
  The Greek and Latin coefficients (alpha, beta, alpha', beta', gamma, lambda, nu, a, b, delta) are not given.
- Family I, systems (3'), (3''): orbits that cross EM orthogonally at t = 0, so a second crossing at t = 2 pi + dT1 implies one at t = -(2 pi + dT1). Broucke (1968) computed this family numerically and called it "BD". Its characteristic in the (x0, C) plane is obtained by expressing dydot0 through dx0 and dC in (3''). The characteristic is again hyperbola-like (the product of two linear forms equals gamma mu), Fig. 5a upper curve with branch pairs A1 = C2 and A2 = C1 exchanging at the bifurcation: the P.S. solution A crosses EM orthogonally once far from M and once near M; near the bifurcation the first crossing approaches M, coincides with the second, and they exchange (Fig. 5 caption).
- Family II, systems (4'), (4''): "The curve of Equation (4'') is a cubic whose asymptotes are the tangents at the bifurcation point to three basic characteristics of second species: the basic characteristic T12 which represents the family T12 of conics T with consecutive collisions (this family cannot be represented in the (x0, C)-plane), and two arcs of basic characteristics C24." Along II (Fig. 5c), the basic orbits E1E2, F1F2, G1G2 are successively an arc of conic S (E) and the union of two arcs of conics T (G), F being the intermediate orbit; "the SSS of basic orbit (G) jumps from an arc T to the other at each symmetric conjunction".
- The paper states the error orders as printed: O(mu) in (3'), O(mu^(3/2)) in (3''), O(mu) in (4'), O(mu^(4/3)) in (4''). The 4/3 is not explained here (it presumably comes from the cubic scaling dydot0 of order mu^(1/3); INFERRED, not stated).
- This is a bifurcation of order three in Henon 1997's sense (three basic characteristics meeting: one T-arc family and two C24 arcs), the case where Perko's Part B nu = 1/3 scaling would be expected to appear (INFERRED; Perko's regime nu below 1/2 has delta v1 of order mu^nu, and a cubic has all three variations of order mu^(1/3)).

## 6. Relation to the digested papers

- Henon 1997: Guillaume's 1973 classification is the mu = 0+ skeleton that Henon 1997 builds out in full (arcs, (A, Z) plane, Table 4.4 critical arcs, Tables 6.2 and 6.3 bifurcation ellipses) and Guillaume's "bifurcation orbit" with beta^(0) = 0 is Henon's total or partial bifurcation orbit. Henon credits Guillaume (1973b, 1975) in his reference list as "Not held" (his digest). Guillaume's gap, which Henon 1997 acknowledges as unfinished, is the general junction rule.
- Perko 1976 (Part A): handles beta10 not zero; Guillaume 1973 handles beta^(0) = 0 locally. Perko's Part A (p.419) cites Guillaume 1971, 1975a, 1975b for the excluded zero-deflection case and says it is "treated in a later paper": that is Perko 1977 and 1981. So the logical order is: Guillaume 1971 and 1973 announce the zero-deflection analysis by Breakwell-Perko matching adapted to beta^(0) = 0; Guillaume 1975 gives it in full; Perko 1977 and 1981 give a second, independent treatment with the nu-regimes.
- Perko 1977 and 1981 (Parts B and C): the hyperbola of eq. (2) here is the nu = 1/2 characteristic of Perko 1981 eq. (9) (a21 dr1 + a24 dv1)(A21 dr1 + A24 dv1) = 2 mu a2+4/V1; the cubic of eq. (4'') is the non-hyperbolic case where three basic arcs meet. Guillaume's example 1 (C = -1, the retrograde unit circle) is Perko's type 3 bifurcation (Perko 1981 p.156, "a retrograde circular orbit of radius one"); Guillaume's example 2 has two crossings near M, i.e. two collisions per period of a T-arc, so a type 1 bifurcation in Perko's classification; Perko's own worked examples are the (I, J) = (2, 1) ellipse (mean motion 1/2), not Guillaume's mean motion 2. INFERRED: they are not the same orbit.
- Breakwell-Perko matching (Perko digest section 2): Guillaume 1973 says it is the basic tool, adapted for beta^(0) = 0; in Perko's notation the adaptation is the O(mu^nu) variation in the initial conditions with the same hyperbolic inner expansion. The hyperbola relation tan(beta/2) = mu/(Delta V_inf^2) is not printed in this paper; it is in Perko and is the project's gate relation r_p = mu (e - 1)/v_inf^2.
- Hitzl and Henon 1977: critical generating orbits are on the S characteristics, which are the A_k, B_k, C_ij of this paper's section 2; the "policeman" question here is the stability and junction problem of those papers.
- Gomez and Olle 1991: the same bifurcation neighbourhoods at positive mu and in the elliptic problem; Guillaume 1973 is where the neighbourhood structure first appears in print.

## 8. Techniques applicable to the project's problems

Everything below is INFERRED use; nothing was built or run.

**#899 (second species continuation to Earth-Moon; reproduction of Casoliva 2010, with the Henon enumerator).**
1. Seed catalogue in the (x0, C) plane. Build the basic characteristics of section 2 as a data structure (I_r, I_d, E_ij for small i, j, the S families of Henon 1997 Table 4.4 and the line x0 = 1) and intersect them: each intersection is a bifurcation orbit, and each Casoliva resonant row near it is an orbit on a branch that follows a basic characteristic until its first bifurcation. Test: the intersection of I_r and A0 at (-1, -1) must be produced by the intersection code (a printed number, p.203), and E_ij ellipses must reproduce Henon 1997 Table 6.2 first row (I, J, L = 2, 1, 0, C = -0.406767) as the second control.
2. Hyperbola local model. Fit the eq. (2) form f1 f2 = c mu to a computed avoided crossing at the Earth-Moon mass (a one-parameter fit of c from a few computed orbits) and check the 1/2 power scaling by repeating at two or three smaller mu (control for the continuation driver: the minimum gap between the branches should scale as mu^(1/2) times a constant). The expected exponent is a structural statement of the paper (the product f1 f2 equals mu to O(mu^(3/2))).
3. Fold and junction bookkeeping. The "policeman" is exactly the missing branch-selection rule of the continuation driver. For a continuation in mu from the (a, b) generating family at Earth-Moon mass, use the rule from the three papers: nearest basic characteristic until the next intersection, then decide by symmetry and Broucke's side-of-passage invariant (Henon 1997 chapter 8; Perko 1981 corollary), and when the rule fails use the local hyperbola or cubic. Control: Broucke's families A1 and F of Fig. 4 at mu about 10^-2 near (-1, -1).
4. Not usable: no printed orbit data, so the control for #899 remains Casoliva's and Henon's tables, not this paper.

**#906 (turn-gate hardening).** Section 2 gives the formal reason for the zero-turn cases: beta^(0) = 0 junctions are the second species bifurcation orbits (Henon's bifurcation junctions). The gate should classify a zero-turn junction as "bifurcation orbit" and not reject it, which is the existing recommendation of the Henon and Perko digests. Additional rule from this paper: the characteristics near such a junction have a hyperbolic or cubic structure, so a candidate near it has a minimal separation of order mu^(1/2) (or mu^(1/3) at order three) from the basic characteristic. At the Earth-Moon mass mu^(1/2) = 0.110 (COMPUTED, mu = 0.0121529529), i.e. 42,000 km at a lunar distance of 384,400 km, which gives a quantitative statement of how far a node must be from a bifurcation orbit before the first-order periapsis can be trusted. Test: compute the demanded turn for Casoliva orbits near the (I, J) = (2, 1) resonance and check that it falls as the distance to the bifurcation orbit falls, at a rate set by the hyperbola.

**#890 and #895 (Titania-Oberon, a Uranian orbit, not Earth-Moon; methods only).** The classification of basic orbits is for one secondary. For two moons the analogue is that the basic orbit passes through two small bodies, and a periodic symmetric orbit is again a point in a plane of Jacobi-like constants; the x0 = 1 characteristic becomes two lines (x0 at each moon). The local hyperbola model is a possible check on the closeness of a #890 closure to a bifurcation of the two-moon basic orbit: the #890 finding that the patched-conic closure misses by a large phase slip is of the kind a fold or an avoided crossing would produce. Test: not available (no printed numbers for a two-moon case).

**#913 to #918, #921 to #924.** Not applicable: these concern heliocentric cycler reproduction, optimiser, persistence, chaos indicators and correction budgets, none of which this paper addresses. The one transferable idea, for #916 (persistence conjecture of Ross and Roberts-Tsoukkas) and #924 (chaos indicators): a hyperbola-structured gap at each bifurcation means the stable members near a bifurcation orbit of a given family have a computable width of order mu^(1/2), which is a quantity to compare against the FLI or stability screen on the same orbits; this is a calibration idea, not a result.
