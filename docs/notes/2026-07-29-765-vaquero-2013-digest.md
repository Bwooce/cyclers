## Ch.4.3.1 — Saturn-Titan resonant orbits, transfers, and chains (THE anchor)

Read in full, including a direct PDF-page-image (vision) read of printed p.109 (PDF page 124)
to cross-check `pdftotext`'s text-layer extraction of Table 4.1 and Figs. 4.4-4.6 — this is a
born-digital PDF (TeX/MiKTeX, no OCR needed), and the vision read confirmed the text-layer
extraction was byte-for-byte accurate (no transcription artifact on this project's OWN
extraction side; see below for the one anomaly found IN THE THESIS'S OWN printed table).

**What the section does:** constructs a 3:4 exterior Titan-resonant orbit at C=3.010000
(Fig. 4.4), computes its Poincaré map + invariant manifolds (Fig. 4.5), identifies a 6:5
interior resonant orbit and the L1/L2 Lyapunov orbits at the SAME energy sharing similar
manifold structure (Fig. 4.6), and tabulates all four orbits' initial states, periods, and
unstable eigenvalues in **Table 4.1** — the sourced anchor this task's own gate module
(`search/saturn_titan_resonant_families.py`) reproduces. It then describes (Figs. 4.9-4.12,
prose only, no state tables) a homoclinic connection of the 3:4 orbit, a periodic "resonant
chain" cycling indefinitely between 3:4 and 6:5, a family of such chains continued in C, and a
falsifiable termination claim ("suspected... ends for C < 3.01400") — explicitly out of scope
for `#765` per its own dispatch note. Finally (§4.3.1 continued, sampled not deep-read) it
applies the same manifold machinery to design a low-cost transfer from the 3:4 resonant orbit
to Hyperion's own 3:4 Titan-resonance (a real physical resonance, distinct from this thesis's
own idealized planar-CR3BP orbit), comparing the manifold-shadowing transfer against a locally
optimal (SNOPT-class) multiple-shooting solution.

**Reproduction verdict (this task's own gate work, full detail in the companion results
note):** 3/4 rows (3:4, L1, and — on eigenvalue only — L2) reproduce Table 4.1 to
near-machine precision using this project's own EXISTING corrector/classification machinery
(no new capability built); 6:5 is a small, real, well-characterized eigenvalue near-miss
(2.3e-3 relative); and **the L2 row's own printed period (T = 79.7260 days) is flagged as a
likely transcription/typesetting error in the thesis itself** — its own printed IC and
eigenvalue both reproduce to <0.01%/3e-6 relative at a self-consistent period of 8.603 days
(not 79.7260), and Fig. 4.6(b) itself plots the L2 Lyapunov orbit as a small, simple oval
comparable in size to the L1 orbit (whose own period is 8.2829 days) — physically consistent
with an ~8.6-day period, not an order of magnitude larger. Flagged per this project's
respectful-errata-framing discipline (evidence-first, falsifiable, benefit of the doubt); NOT
corrected in this project's own sourced-constant table (sourced-only discipline holds).
