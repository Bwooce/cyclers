## 1. What the paper is (READ pp.738-739)

A general method for bounding the remainder R_N of a perturbation series x = sum_{j=0..N-1} eps^j x_j(t) + R_N(t, eps) for xddot = f(x) + eps g(x, t) when g is singular at some point, plus its application to the restricted three-body problem along trajectories whose unperturbed (two-body) orbit meets the small primary at a time t1. The emphasis is on the OUTER perturbation approximation, not on the matched composite solution (the matching is in Breakwell & Perko 1965 and Perko's 1964 thesis, "[3]", and "to second order in a three dimensions analysis in [4]", i.e. the 1974 paper, cited there under its 1966 preprint reference). Stated results (p.739): the estimates "are valid to within an O(epsilon) distance of the singularity (where the error R_N is O(epsilon) for all N at least 2) and show that at a distance of O(epsilon^(1/2)) from the singularity the error in the outer perturbation approximation, |R_N|, is O(epsilon^((N+1)/2))" (my reading of the garbled text, confirmed by the theorem: COMPUTED below), and that "the outer perturbation series is asymptotic (uniformly in t) to the actual solution which is shown to exist in this region."

## 2. The method (READ pp.739-745)

- Section 1 (Taylor's formula, pp.739-742): the generalised perturbation scheme. The terms x_j satisfy linear equations with a common homogeneous part (eq. 1.5), x_0 the unperturbed solution, x_1 the first variation with the nominal initial variations eps xi(t0), eps eta(t0); the solution of each is by the fundamental matrix Phi(t, t0) of xddot = f_x(x_0) x (eq. 1.3 form, with Phi(t0, t0) = I). The remainder R_N satisfies a nonlinear equation with Taylor remainders of f and g, giving integral inequalities for |R_N| and |Rdot_N| (p.742): |R_N(t)| <= |R_N(t0)| + |Rdot_N(t0)|(t - t0) + double integral of F(|R_N|, t'') dt'' dt', |Rdot_N(t)| <= |Rdot_N(t0)| + integral of F dt' (eq. 2.1).
- Section 2 (estimation lemmas, pp.742-745). The difficulty stated (p.742-743): F contains a term like eps/[a(t1 - t) - |R|]^3, so a bound on |R| of the form |R| < K(t) = a(t1 - t) - ... is needed before the integral inequality means anything, and there is no a priori reason for it near the singularity. The remedy is a majorising function.
  Estimation Lemma 1 (p.743): let K(t) be a given real function on [t0, T] and R(t) continuous with values in E^n such that on every subinterval [t0, t*] on which |R| < K: (i) the integral inequalities hold, (ii) F(|R|, t) is monotone increasing in |R|. If there is M in C^2[t0, T] with (iii) Mddot >= F(M, t), (iv) M(t) < K(t), (v) M(t0) > |R(t0)| and Mdot(t0) >= |Rdot(t0)|, then |R(t)| < M(t) and |Rdot(t)| <= Mdot(t) on [t0, T]. Proof by the first time t* at which |R| = M and the monotonicity of F (p.743-744). For K = infinity it is Gronwall's lemma generalised to the nonlinear case (Quirino 1946, Redheffer 1964).
  Estimation Lemma 2 (pp.744-745): the same with the existence of the solution not assumed: if |R| < K on [t0, t*) implies continuity of R on [t0, t* + delta) and the integral inequalities hold, then existence, continuity and the same bounds follow on [t0, T]. It is used to prove existence up to O(eps^(1-delta)) of the singularity.
- "The method of constructing the majorizing function M(t) depends on the problem, but quite often, as is the case in the problem discussed in section 3, the singular nature of the function majorizing R_N(t, eps) can be deduced from the singular nature of the Nth term in the perturbation series" (p.745).

## 3. The restricted-problem result (READ pp.746-753)

- Setting (eqs. 3.1-3.3, p.746): coordinates normalised by a distance d and the time by (d^3/GM)^(1/2), d chosen "large enough so that t1 - t0 < 1" (a normalisation of the time interval, which matters for the constants); mass ratio eps = m/M; xddot = f(x) + eps g(x, t), f(x) = -x/|x|^3, g(x, t) = -(x - x*)/|x - x*|^3 - x*/|x*|^3 (as printed up to sign conventions), x*(t) the small primary on a two-body orbit; unperturbed trajectory x_0 with x_0(t1) = x*(t1) and relative velocity v1 = xdot_0(t1) - xdot*(t1) of order one, so x_0(t) - x*(t) = v1(t1 - t) + O((t1 - t)^2) (p.747).
- Lemma (p.747, read on the image): suppose there are positive constants a, b, h with |x_0(t) - x*(t)| > a(t1 - t), |x_0(t)| > b, |x*(t)| > h on [t0, t1]. Then there are constants c_j such that |x_1(t)| <= c_1 |log(t1 - t)| exp[c_0(t - t0)] and |x_j(t)| <= c_j |log(t1 - t)/(t1 - t)|^(j-1) exp[c_0(t - t0)] for j = 2, ..., N - 1, all t in [t0, t1]. "It is shown in [3] that c_j = O(2^((j-1)^2))" (read on the image; the text layer shows "O(2^(j-1)^2)"). The count behind it: the number of terms in the sum over i_1 + ... + i_k = j of |x_i1| ... |x_ik| is binomial(j - 1, k - 1), and the sum over k of these is 2^(j-1).
- Explicit constants in the proof (p.748, read on the image): c_0 = (3!/b^3)^(1/2) and c_1 = max(a^(-2), h^(-2)); the first-term majorant is M_1(t) = c_1 |log tau| exp[c_0 (t - t0)] with tau = t1 - t, built from the bounds ||df/dx(x_0)|| <= 2!/|x_0|^3 <= 2!/b^3 and |g(x_0, t)| <= |x_0 - x*|^(-2) + |x*|^(-2) <= (a tau)^(-2) + h^(-2) (and the 3!b^(-4), 3^2 sqrt(3) b^(-4) c_1^2 |log tau|^2 e^(2 c_0) terms for j = 2). For j = 2 the constants c_2 are only "some constant"; the c_j for j at least 2 are not given.
- Theorem (p.750, read on the image): "Let the hypothesis of the preceding lemma be satisfied. It then follows that given N at least 2 and delta above 0 there exist an epsilon_1 above 0 and real numbers c_0-bar and C_N such that
  |R_N(t, eps)| < C_N eps^N |log(t1 - t)/(t1 - t)|^(N-1) exp[c_0-bar (t - t0)]
  and |Rdot_N(t, eps)| < C_N eps^N |log(t1 - t)|^(N-1)/(t1 - t)^N exp[c_0-bar (t - t0)]
  for all t in [t0, T] and eps in [0, eps_1], where t1 - T is at least O(eps^(1 - delta))." And "the perturbation series is asymptotic": lim over eps tending to 0 of |R_{N+1}|/|R_N| = lim |Rdot_{N+1}|/|Rdot_N| = 0 for N = 2, 3, ..., uniformly on compact subintervals of [t0, t1) (p.750). Because c_j = O(2^((j-1)^2)) "the series of upper bounds ... is a divergent series for any given epsilon above 0" (p.750).
- Proof (pp.750-753): a priori bound function K(t) = min[b', a'(t1 - t)...] with the constants a', b', c' defined by the smallness of eps (the three inequalities at the start of the proof, p.750-751); the monotone function F_0(|R_N|, t) is written down (p.751) and the majorising function is M(t, eps) = c eps^N |log tau/tau|^(N-1) exp[c_0-bar (t - t0)]; the requirement F_0(M, t) <= Mddot reduces to a condition on c whose right-hand side "approaches a value less than ..." as eps tends to 0 (the final expression is garbled and not recoverable; it is not a number the reader can use) because M/tau and |log tau| tend to zero. Conclusion (p.753): the solution exists and is continuous for t1 - t above O(eps^(1 - delta)) and "|R_N| = O(eps^(N + (N-1) delta ...))" at that distance (garbled; consistent with the theorem: at tau = eps^(1-delta) the bound is eps^N eps^(-(1-delta)(N-1)) = eps^(1 + (N-1) delta) times logarithms).
- The paper contains no numerical example, no table, no value of eps and no figure.

## 4. Are any constants explicit? Can the Earth-Moon seed error be given a number?

No usable number can be obtained from this paper. Details:
- Explicit: c_0 = (6/b^3)^(1/2) and c_1 = max(a^(-2), h^(-2)) for the first-order term |x_1| (READ p.748). For j at least 2 only the growth law c_j = O(2^((j-1)^2)) is stated, with the proof deferred to the 1964 thesis ("The details of the algebra involved in this computation are quite lengthy and will not be given here"). In the Theorem, C_N, c_0-bar and eps_1 are existence constants ("there exist"), not given.
- Even the explicit part is useless numerically. COMPUTED: with b the minimum distance of the unperturbed orbit from the large primary in units of the primary separation, c_0 = sqrt(6/b^3) and the bound carries the factor exp[c_0 (t - t0)] with t - t0 in the paper's normalised time (below 1 by the choice of d, so the exponent is c_0 times a number below one in those units; in the Earth-Moon units where the unit time is 1/omega = 4.348 d the exponent is c_0 (t - t0) with t - t0 up to a few radians): for b = 1 and t - t0 = pi, exp = 2.2e3; for b = 0.3 it is 2e20; for b = 0.1 it is 5e105. These are properties of a Gronwall-type majorant, which is exponentially pessimistic by construction. So the constants cannot certify an actual seed.
- What the theorem does give, as scales (COMPUTED, mu = eps = 0.0121529529, tau = mu^(1/2) = 0.1102 the matching distance, |ln tau| = 2.205, 1 Earth-Moon distance = 384,400 km, constants C_N unknown): the bound eps^N |ln tau/tau|^(N-1) at tau = eps^(1/2) is eps^((N+1)/2) |ln tau|^(N-1): N = 2: 2.95e-3 (1,140 km); N = 3: 7.18e-4 (276 km); N = 5: 4.24e-5 (16 km); each multiplied by the unknown C_N exp(c_0-bar (t - t0)). These are the sizes of the OUTER series' remainder in position at the matching distance, not the error of the matched hyperbola parameters (see the Breakwell & Perko digest, section 9, for those: mu^2 versus mu^3 scales).
- The distance below which the outer series is not usable at all: the bound is small only if eps |ln tau|/tau is small, i.e. tau much greater than mu |ln mu| = 0.0536 (0.233 d), so in time the outer (Kepler plus variational) description fails within about a quarter of a day of the Moon at the Earth-Moon mass; the matching distance mu^(1/2) = 0.1102 (0.479 d, 42,400 km at unit speed) is about twice that. INFERRED reading of the validity condition; the factor 1 depends on the unknown constants.
- Conclusion for the question "can the matched-seed error at the Earth-Moon mass be given a number rather than an order": not from Perko 1967 (nor from the later Perko papers). A number needs a calibration against computed orbits (see section 6).

## 5. Printed numbers usable as tests

None. The structural items that can be tested numerically without a printed value: the power law of the remainder in distance, |R_N| proportional to |ln tau/tau|^(N-1) at fixed eps (checkable by comparing a truncated outer series with an integrated orbit as a function of tau); the scaling eps^N at fixed tau; and the asymptotic-series property R_{N+1}/R_N tending to zero as eps tends to zero. The theorem's exponents are testable as log-log slopes; the constants are not.

## 6. Techniques applicable to the project's problems

### `#899` (seed accuracy, corrected by the Breakwell & Perko digest)
1. The outer series is valid only for tau above about mu |ln mu| (0.23 d) from the Moon, and the matching region of the standard theory sits at mu^(1/2) (0.48 d); a seed built from matched asymptotics is as good as the neglected terms at that radius: eps^((N+1)/2) |ln|^(N-1) in position (COMPUTED above, scales 1,140 km, 276 km, 16 km for N = 2, 3, 5 before constants). The second-order seed of Breakwell & Perko (N = 3 outer in effect) is therefore the right level for the Earth-Moon mass, and a fifth-order outer series (Perko 1976b Theorem 4, N = 5 outer, N = 3 inner) gives O(mu^(3 - epsilon)).
2. Calibration instead of certification: since no constant is explicit, determine C_N empirically. Recipe: integrate a Casoliva Table 3 flyby in the project's CR3BP code from t0 to a point tau before the Moon, compare with the outer series truncated at N = 2, 3 (using the transition-matrix quadratures), fit C_N from the ratio at tau = mu^(1/2), and check the tau dependence |ln tau/tau|^(N-1). The result is a measured error scale, to put beside the pre-registered test of the Breakwell & Perko digest. Not run.
3. The divergence of the series of upper bounds (c_j = O(2^((j-1)^2)), "a divergent series for any given epsilon") means that adding orders helps only until the terms stop decreasing; at mu = 0.0122 the practical limit is the second to third order, consistent with Henon's observation that generating families stop following the periodic family at mu_EM.

### `#906` (turn gate)
- The majorising-function lemma is a rigorous tool for a posteriori certification of an ODE solution when the right-hand side has a monotone majorant; it gives no number here. For the gate the useful content is the validity radius of the outer description: a turn computed from outer-series (patched-conic) velocities at a distance below about mu |ln mu| in time from the Moon (0.23 d) is outside the theorem's range, so velocity vectors taken from a Kepler arc must be sampled at least that far from the Moon (or be taken from an integrated trajectory, which the gate already prefers).

### `#928` (regularised propagator and transition matrix)
- The singular behaviour is quantified: |x_j| grows like |ln tau/tau|^(j-1) and the velocity like |ln tau|^(j-1)/tau^j (Lemma and Theorem). A regularised variational integration should reproduce the j-th variation within these envelopes; a check: integrate the first and second variations (x_1, x_2) of the Kepler orbit with the regularised code through a flyby at small mu and verify that |x_1| grows like |ln tau| and |x_2| like |ln tau/tau| as tau tends to zero, with the constants c_1 = max(a^(-2), h^(-2)) (explicit, READ p.748) as a loose upper envelope for |x_1| in the paper's units (a test of the form |x_1| <= c_1 |log tau| exp(c_0 (t - t0)), which is a true statement and trivially satisfied by a correct code; a failure flags a bug). The use is as a sanity envelope, not as an accuracy check.
- The lemma's technique (construct M with Mddot >= F(M, t)) can certify the regularised propagator's own variational error if explicit sup-norm bounds on f and g along the orbit are computed numerically; the constants would be as pessimistic as those above, so INFERRED: not recommended as an accuracy certificate.

## 7. Open points and follow-ups
- The values of C_N, c_0-bar, eps_1 and c_j (j at least 2) are not in the paper; "[3]" (Perko 1964 Stanford thesis, "Asymptotic matching in the restricted three body problem") has the algebra for c_j and is the only possible source of explicit constants; not held and hard to obtain.
- The final inequality of the proof (p.752-753) is not legible in the text layer; the image of p.752-753 was not transcribed digit by digit (it gives a condition that holds as eps tends to 0, not a number).
- Quirino (1946, Atti Accad. Naz. Lincei 1:1264-1271) and Redheffer (1964, Amer. Math. Monthly 71:656-659, "Stability by freshman calculus") are the sources of the lemma in the linear case; not needed for the project.

Note 2026-10-05 (Lancaster & Allemann 1973, `docs/notes/2026-10-05-digest-lancaster-allemann-1973-asymptotic-two-point-boundary-value.md`): a printed calibration exists for one class of Earth-Moon flybys (eight Earth-to-Moon cases): first-order pericynthion-radius errors 74 to 170 km, second-order 3.7 to 554 km, so a number for the matched-seed error is available as a prior for that class, but it is not a certified bound and covers one flyby geometry only (see that digest, section 5).
