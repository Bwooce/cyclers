## 1. The setting, exactly
Plane elliptic restricted three-body problem (p.343): Sun mass 1, Jupiter mass mu << 1, Asteroid massless, all in R^2; Sun and Jupiter on ellipses with foci at 0 and eccentricity epsilon in (0,1); period 2 pi; Jupiter's major semi-axis (1 + mu)^(-1/3); Jupiter's position u(t, mu, epsilon) is 2 pi-periodic in t in T = R/2 pi Z, analytic in (mu, epsilon).
Hamiltonian (1.1): H(p, q, t, mu, epsilon) = |p|^2/2 - 1/|q + mu u(t,mu,epsilon)| - mu/|q - u(t,mu,epsilon)|.
Singularities: ONE small Newtonian singularity (Jupiter, coefficient mu) which MOVES on a 2 pi-periodic orbit (u), plus the Sun's Newtonian singularity (coefficient 1, near the origin, which the orbits avoid). Time-periodic with period 2 pi. Two small parameters, mu (mass) and epsilon (Jupiter's eccentricity); "small mass ratio AND small eccentricity" (abstract). Unperturbed (mu = 0): Kepler problem H_0 = |p|^2/2 - 1/|q|, a = -1/(2E), e = sqrt(1 + 2 E G^2) (1.3), for E < 0, G not 0. Circular case epsilon = 0: autonomous in the rotating frame with Jacobi integral h = H^0 - G ("we prefer to use the relative Hamiltonian h", p.344, sign opposite to the usual C_J: C_J = -2h, COMPUTED from relative speed below).
Prior art stated (pp.344-345): circular-case second-species orbits for given Jacobi integral h (Henon 1977, Perko 1981, Bruno 1990, Gomez & Olle 1991); they exist for h in (-3/2, sqrt 2), when "there are Kepler ellipses with E - G = h crossing Jupiter's orbit which is the unit circle. The corresponding values of angular momentum G lie in (2 - sqrt(4h + 6), -h)." Marco & Niederman 1995 proved a symmetric 2-chain of collision orbits; Bolotin & MacKay 2000: infinitely many periodic (rotating frame) and chaotic orbits for any h in (-3/2, sqrt 2), a hyperbolic invariant set with Lyapunov exponents of order |ln mu| (also Font et al. 2002).
Goal (p.345): "to describe second species periodic orbits of the elliptic 3 body problem with small eccentricity epsilon in (0,1). These solutions are periodic with respect to the fixed coordinate frame, and their period is an integer multiple of 2 pi. Our results are similar to the results of (Bolotin and MacKay 2000) for the circular 3 body problem, but we consider only periodic collision chains. It turns out that the proofs for the elliptic problem are considerably harder." Chaotic orbits are deferred to a subsequent paper (that is Bolotin 2006, item D).

## 2. Main results with every hypothesis
Notation (p.345-346): Gamma = C^1 collision curves gamma : [t_0, t_1] -> R^2 \ {0} with gamma(t_0) = u(t_0), gamma(t_1) = u(t_1), gamma(t) not equal to u(t) in between; collision orbit = such a curve that is a Kepler orbit. Action A(gamma) = integral (|gamma'|^2/2 + 1/|gamma|) dt (2.2); "non-degenerate" = nondegenerate critical point, i.e. t_0, t_1 not conjugate along gamma. First variation (2.3): dA = h(gamma'(t_0), t_0) dt_0 - h(gamma'(t_1), t_1) dt_1, with the relative energy (2.4) h(p, t) = H_0(p, u(t)) - p . u'(t) = |p - u'(t)|^2/2 - |u'(t)|^2/2 - 1/|u(t)|.
Periodic n-chains: Pi_nm = 2 pi m-periodic chains sigma = (gamma_i)_(i=1)^n, t_n = t_0 + 2 pi m, switching times t_1 < ... < t_n free. A critical point of A(sigma) = sum A(gamma_i) satisfies (2.5) h(p_i^-, t_i) = h(p_i^+, t_i) = h_i, p_i^+ = gamma'_(i-1)(t_i), p_i^- = gamma'_i(t_i) (same off-by-one print as in item D; arrival/departure per (3.1)); equivalently |v_i^+| = |v_i^-| for the relative velocities v_i^+/- = p_i^+/- - u'(t_i).
"We call sigma a non-degenerate periodic collision chain if sigma is a non-degenerate critical point of the functional A and satisfies the changing direction condition: v_i^+ not equal to +/- v_i^- for i = 1, ..., n." Remark (p.346): "Collision chains are break solutions of Kepler's problem (1.2), and they depend on Jupiter only through velocity changes at times t_i. Then solutions undergo elastic reflections from Jupiter."
THEOREM 2.1 (p.346), verbatim: "For any non-degenerate periodic collision chain sigma = (gamma_i)_(i=1)^n of the Kepler problem there exists mu_0 > 0 such that for all mu in (0, mu_0) there exists a unique 2 pi m-periodic orbit gamma : R -> R^2 \ {0} of the elliptic 3 body problem which is O(mu)-shadowing the chain sigma and avoids collisions with Jupiter. More precisely, there exist constants a, b > 0 independent of mu such that |gamma(t) - u(t, mu, epsilon)| >= a mu, |gamma(t) - gamma_i(t)| <= b mu for t_(i-1) <= t <= t_i." (Proof in Bolotin 2006; "Theorem 2.1 holds for systems more general than the elliptic 3 body problem. What is important is the presence of a small Newtonian singularity in the potential.")
Degeneracy of 1-chains (p.347), verbatim: "Formally, the assertion of Theorem 2.1 holds also for 1-chains consisting of one collision orbit ... with t_1 - t_0 = 2 pi m. However, all 1-chains are degenerate, because the action of an elliptic orbit is a function of period only. Note that for the circular 3 body problem, there are many non-degenerate 1-chains, because the notion of non-degeneracy is different (Bolotin and MacKay 2000)."
THEOREM 2.2 (p.347), verbatim: "For any h in (-3/2, sqrt 2) there is a dense set {G_k}_(k in L(h)) in (2 - sqrt(4h + 6), -h) such that for any N >= 2, any sequence (k_i)_(i=1)^N with k_i in L(h) and k_(i-1) not equal to k_i for i = 1, ..., N [k_0 = k_N], and any sequence (l_i)_(i=1)^N of positive integers with sufficiently large sum l_i, there exists an integer sequence (m_i)_(i=1)^N, |m_j - l_j| <= 1, such that for sufficiently small epsilon > 0 the Kepler problem has 2 non-degenerate periodic collision n-chains sigma_+/- of the form
  gamma_11 ... gamma_(1 m_1) ... gamma_(i1) ... gamma_(i m_i) ... gamma_(N1) ... gamma_(N m_N),   n = sum m_i,                              (2.7)
where gamma_ij is an elliptic collision orbit with angular momentum G_ij and energy E_ij which are O(epsilon, n^(-1))-close to G_(k_i) and E_(k_i) = h - G_(k_i), respectively." Each gamma_ij "performs several rotations along a Kepler ellipse".
COROLLARY 2.1 (p.347): for small mu there exist periodic orbits gamma_+/- of the elliptic problem shadowing the chains sigma_+/- of Theorem 2.2; Poincare multipliers alpha_+/-, alpha_+/-^(-1), beta_+/-, beta_+/-^(-1) with alpha_+/- = exp(a_+/- sqrt(+/- epsilon) + O(mu, epsilon)), beta_+/- = b_+/- mu^(-1) + O(epsilon, mu), a_+/-, b_+/- not equal to 0. "gamma_- has a pair of multipliers on the unit circle, and the other on the real line. All multipliers of gamma_+ are real."
Limits stated (p.348), verbatim: "A drawback of Theorem 2.1 is that mu_0 depends on the collision chain sigma. Hence for fixed mu we are unable to conclude the existence of an infinite number of periodic orbits of the elliptic 3 body problem. ... For applications one has to estimate how small epsilon, mu need to be, but we do not address this problem in the present paper." Half of the orbits are linearly stable in some directions; the infinite family is hyperbolic.
Why not perturb the circular-case orbits (Remark, p.348): "One may hope to find almost collision periodic orbits of the elliptic 3 body problem with small eccentricity epsilon by perturbing the periodic orbits with given Jacobi's integral h in (-3/2, sqrt 2) found by Marco and Niderman (1995) and Bolotin and MacKay (2000) for the circular 3 body problem. ... As shown by Poincare, for 0 < epsilon << mu such torus generically disintegrates into a pair of periodic solutions. However for fixed epsilon > 0 and small mu such approach does not work. Hence it seems impossible to obtain our results by using similar results for the circular problem."

## 3. Method
### 3.1 Twist-map reformulation (section 3, pp.348-351; mu = 0, epsilon fixed)
Sigma = nondegenerate collision orbits (2-dimensional manifold, projection (t_0, t_1) a local diffeomorphism); covering {Sigma_k}, U_k invariant under (t_0, t_1) -> (t_0 + 2 pi, t_1 + 2 pi). Relative velocities (3.1) v_k^-(t_0,t_1) = gamma'(t_0) - u'(t_0), v_k^+(t_0,t_1) = gamma'(t_1) - u'(t_1); S_k(t_0,t_1) = A(gamma) (3.2); D_1 S_k = h(gamma'(t_0), t_0), D_2 S_k = -h(gamma'(t_1), t_1) (3.3). For k = (k_i) in K^n, X_k = {t = (t_i): (t_(i-1), t_i) in U_(k_i), t_n = t_0 + 2 pi m}, A_k(t) = sum S_(k_i)(t_(i-1), t_i) (3.4); h_i = -D_2 S_(k_i)(t_(i-1), t_i) = D_1 S_(k_(i+1))(t_i, t_(i+1)) (3.5); changing direction (3.6): v^+_(k_i)(t_(i-1), t_i) not equal to +/- v^-_(k_(i+1))(t_i, t_(i+1)). Remark: topologically nondegenerate critical points (nonzero degree of grad A_k) also give shadowing orbits, nonunique, without the O(mu) estimate.
Twist condition D_12 S_k not equal to 0 (3.7); g_k(t_0, t_1) = (t_0, h_0), h_0 = D_1 S_k; twist map f_k : (t_0, h_0) -> (t_1, h_1), h_1 = -D_2 S_k (3.8), on the annulus A = T x R: the collision-to-collision map in (collision time, relative energy).
LEMMA 3.1 (p.350): t is a nondegenerate critical point of A_k iff the fixed point x_0 of f_k = f_(k_n) o ... o f_(k_1) is nondegenerate: det(f_k'(x_0) - I) not equal to 0 (3.9); moreover det(f_k'(x_0) - I) = -det(A_k''(t)) prod_(i=1)^n (-D_12 S_(k_i)(t_(i-1), t_i))^(-1) (3.10). Morse index and stability (p.351): if all twists have the same sign, e.g. D_12 S < 0, sign det(f' - I) = -sign det A''; "if m(t) is even, the fixed point x_0 is hyperbolic, and if m(t) is odd, it is elliptic." PROPOSITION 3.1 (p.351): multipliers alpha = lambda + O(mu), beta = b mu^(-1) + O(mu) for the shadowing orbit, lambda the multipliers of the fixed point of f_k, provided the changing direction condition holds.
### 3.2 Almost autonomous maps (section 4, pp.351-358)
If S_k(t_0,t_1) = Psi_k(s) + epsilon psi_k(t_0, s) + O(epsilon^2), s = t_1 - t_0 (4.3): f_k is a perturbation of an integrable twist map (t, h) -> (t + rho_k(h), h) (h is the Jacobi integral). Twist conditions Psi_k'' not equal to 0; J_k = -Psi_k'(I_k); rho_k is the inverse of -Psi_k' with rho_k'(h) = -1/Psi_k''(s). Perturbed map (4.5): h_0 = h_1 + epsilon D_1 phi_k + O(epsilon^2), t_1 = t_0 + rho_k(h_1) + epsilon D_2 phi_k + O(epsilon^2), phi_k(t,h) = psi_k(t, rho_k(h)) (4.4).
Only first harmonic in the elliptic problem (4.7): phi_k(t,h) = a_k(h) e^(it) + conj(a_k)(h) e^(-it). Consequences (p.353): one map alone has fixed points only at rho_k(h) = 2 pi m (resonance with the single harmonic), which correspond to 1-chains, all degenerate. Therefore COMPOSE two or more DIFFERENT maps f_k, f_l ("random sequences"): approximate first integral H_k = h + epsilon D_1 chi_k, with the homological equation chi_k(t + rho_k(h), h) - chi_k(t, h) = phi_k(t, h) (4.8), chi_k = sum b_kn(h) e^(int), b_kn = a_kn/(e^(i n rho_k) - 1) (4.9); invariant curves Gamma_k(epsilon, h) = {(t, h - epsilon D_1 chi_k(t, h))}; if J_k intersect J_l not empty and H_k, H_l are independent, chi_kl = chi_k - chi_l not identically 0 (4.10), so the invariant curves of f_k and f_l intersect without coinciding (transversally for the elliptic problem, two points).
Alternating sequences k = (k^m1, l^m2, k^m3, ..., l^mN), N even (4.11). THEOREM 4.1 (p.354): for any even N >= 2 there is an infinite set M_N of integer sequences m with h_m in J such that, for small epsilon, f_k = f_l^(m_N) o f_k^(m_(N-1)) o ... o f_k^(m_1) has a pair of topologically nondegenerate fixed points x_+/- in T x (h_m - c epsilon, h_m + c epsilon) with multipliers lambda_+/-, lambda_+/-^(-1), lambda_+/- = exp(c_+/- sqrt(+/- epsilon) + O(epsilon)), c_+/- >= 0; {h_m} dense in J; M_N relatively dense in N^N (constants a, b with for any (l_j), sum l_j >= b, an m with max |m_j - l_j| <= a). x_- elliptic-type (on the unit circle), x_+ hyperbolic (generically).
THEOREM 4.2 (p.355): with Omega_k(h) = (m_1 + m_3 + ... + m_(N-1)) rho_k(h) + (m_2 + m_4 + ... + m_N) rho_l(h), c_k(h) = 1 - e^(i m_1 rho_k) + e^(i(m_1 rho_k + m_2 rho_l)) - ... (4.14); if for some h in J: Omega_k(h) = 0, Omega_k'(h) not equal to 0, c_k(h) not equal to 0 (4.15), then f_k has a pair of nondegenerate fixed points x_+/- as in (4.12). (The Omega_k(h) = 0 condition is read modulo 2 pi in the paper's use: e.g. N = 2, (4.17) m_1 rho_k + m_2 rho_l in 2 pi Z with (4.18) m_1 rho_k, m_2 rho_l not in 2 pi Z.) Density of such h by an interval-length argument (spacing d about 2 pi (m_1|rho_k'| + m_2|rho_l'|)^(-1) versus d' about 2 pi (min{...})^(-1), d' > d).
THEOREM 4.3 (p.357) and THEOREM 5.1 (p.360) give uniform versions with constants b, c, d depending on a closed interval Delta of h and a finite set Lambda of admissible pairs (k_(i-1), k_i) in D(h) = {(k,l) in L(h) x L(h): b_k(h) not equal to b_l(h)}, L(h) = {k in K(h) : rho_k' < 0 in J_k}. Proof idea (section 5): for epsilon = 0 A_k has a one-dimensional nondegenerate minimal critical manifold Z = {t_j = t + sum_(i<=j) rho_(k_i)(h)}; the Poincare function phi_k(t,h) = sum psi_(k_j)(t_(j-1), rho_(k_j)(h)) on Z (zero average, 2 pi-periodic); a nondegenerate critical point of it gives, by Lyapunov-Schmidt, a nondegenerate critical point of A_k near Z with the same Morse index; two critical points (minimum and mountain pass) give the pair x_+/-. det A_k'' = O(epsilon) so multipliers are e^(+/- O(sqrt epsilon)). The chain must close: Omega_k(h) = 2 pi m (5.2), (5.4) (sum of the times equals 2 pi m), obtained by choosing h near h~ with Omega_k(h~) = 2 pi m; since Omega_k' < -n rho < 0 the needed shift in h is O(1/n).
### 3.3 Computing the generating functions (sections 6-7, pp.361-370)
Circular Jupiter u(t) = e^(it) (section 6): a collision orbit with the Asteroid ellipse of semi-axis a, eccentricity e crossing the unit circle at true anomalies -theta (collision at t = 0) and theta (collision at t = s), s = 2 theta + 2 pi n. With eccentric anomaly eta and mean motion omega = a^(-3/2) (sign +/-), the system (p.362, Eq. 6.1-6.3):
  omega (theta + pi n) = eta - e sin eta + pi m,   cos theta = a (cos eta - e),   1 + e cos theta = a (1 - e^2).
Solve for e: e = (2a)^(-1) ( -cos theta +/- sqrt( cos^2 theta + 4 a (a - 1) ) ) (6.4), with domain conditions for a > 1, 1/2 < a < 1 (needs cos^2 theta > 4 a (1 - a)), a = 1. For large n and rational nu = m/n, omega = nu + (pi n)^(-1) R_k(theta) + O(n^(-2)), a = nu^(-2/3) + (2/3) n^(-1) nu^(-5/3) R_k(theta) + ..., with R_k(theta) = -theta + eta_(alpha beta) - e_beta sin eta_(alpha beta) at a = nu^(-2/3) (6.7-6.8); e = (1/2) nu^(2/3)( -cos theta + beta sqrt(cos^2 theta + 4 nu^(-2/3)(nu^(-2/3) - 1)) ) + O(1/n) (6.9). Index set K = {k = (nu, alpha, beta)}, nu = m/n rational, relatively prime, n > N, delta < |nu| < 1 - delta or 1 + delta < |nu| < 2^(3/2) - delta, alpha = +/-, beta as stated. Energy and angular momentum (6.11): E = -omega^(2/3)/2, G = omega^(-1/3) (1 - e^2)^(1/2). LEMMA 6.1 (p.364): unique nondegenerate elliptic collision orbit sigma : [0,s] -> R^2 for s in I_k; action Psi_k(s) satisfies the twist condition; Psi_k'(s) = -h = omega^(2/3)/2 + G (6.12), sign Psi_k'' = iota_k = alpha beta sign(nu) (6.10). Relation (6.13) G = -nu^(2/3) - h + O(1/n); the collision time s = rho_k(h) = 2 pi n + 2 alpha arccos( (G^2 - 1)(1 - nu^(2/3) G^2)^(-1/2) ) + O(1/n) (6.14). LEMMA 6.2: rho_k' not equal to 0, sign rho_k' = -iota_k; the intervals J_k cover any closed Delta in (-3/2, sqrt 2) and each h lies in infinitely many J_k; {G_k(h)} dense in (2 - sqrt(4h + 6), -h) except near the boundary. Early collisions (a collision orbit must not hit Jupiter earlier) are excluded by deleting finitely many s values (the condition R_k(theta) = pi (n q - m p)/p, Eq. in p.366; p < n - N).
Relative collision velocities (6.16-6.17, p.367): tangential component v_2 = G_k(h), radial v_1 = V_k(h) = +/- sqrt( 2 - nu^(2/3) - (h + nu^(2/3)/2)^2 ) + O(1/n) (sign +, if alpha nu > 0, else -); relative velocities v_k^- = V_k + (G_k - 1) i, v_k^+ = (-V_k + (G_k - 1) i) e^(is); "The absolute value is |v_k^+/-| = sqrt(2h + 3)."
COMPUTED checks: (i) |v|^2 = v_1^2 + (v_2 - 1)^2 = (2 + 2E - G^2) + G^2 - 2G + 1 = 3 + 2(E - G) = 3 + 2h, matching the printed value; with C_J = -2h this is 3 - C_J, the relative speed squared at the Moon in the Casoliva/Barrabes & Gomez notation, positive only for C_J < 3. (ii) h in (-3/2, sqrt 2) corresponds to C_J in (-2 sqrt 2, 3), the interval of Barrabes & Gomez 2002 Eq. 27 (also in item D's digest). (iii) The G range (2 - sqrt(4h + 6), -h): at h = -1 it is (0.586, 1).
LEMMA 6.3 (changing direction, p.367), verbatim: "The changing direction condition holds for the link sigma_k, sigma~_l of a collision chain except maybe in one of the following cases: alpha_k nu_k = -alpha_l nu_l; nu_k^(2/3) + nu_l^(2/3) = -4h - 2 and alpha_k sign nu_k = -alpha_l sign nu_l. For example, the case nu_k = nu_l and alpha_k = -alpha_l corresponds to continuing after the collision along the same Kepler ellipse. We have to avoid such trivial collision chains. If k = l then the changing direction condition always holds."
Small eccentricity (section 7): u(t, 0, epsilon) = e^(it) + epsilon xi(t) + O(epsilon^2), xi_1 = -cos t, xi_2 = 2 sin t in the moving basis (7.4) (r = 1 - epsilon cos t, theta = t + 2 epsilon sin t). LEMMA 7.1: psi_k(t_0, s) = 2 G_k(h)(sin(t + s) - sin t) + V_k(h)(cos(t + s) + cos t) (7.1) with t = t_0 (first variation of the action). LEMMA 7.2: chi_k(t, h) = B_k(h) sin t (7.5), B_k(h) = V_k sin rho_k/(1 - cos rho_k) + 2 G_k; for fixed h and k, the set {l : B_l(h) = B_k(h)} is finite. ## 5. What this does and does not cover for #890
### 5.1 How far is the setting from two moons of different periods
The setting here is time-periodic with ONE small singularity that MOVES on a given 2 pi-periodic orbit (an ellipse of small eccentricity epsilon) plus the central body's singularity that the orbit avoids. Two moons of different circular periods in the planet frame: both are small moving singularities, on circles with different angular rates (n_1, n_2). In the frame rotating with moon 1: moon 1 is FIXED and moon 2 moves on a circle with relative rate n_2 - n_1; the problem is time-periodic with the synodic period (INFERRED; also derived in the item D digest, where Bolotin 2006 Remark 1, Eq. 2.9, allows several moving singularities). So relative to this 2005 paper:
- the number of small singularities goes from 1 to 2 (one fixed, one moving), which Bolotin 2006 Remark 1 allows ("proofs essentially the same"), but this 2005 paper's Theorem 2.1 (cited from 2006) is stated for one;
- the small parameter epsilon (eccentricity) has no counterpart: the 2005 method needs the unperturbed twist maps f_k to be integrable (circular case, with an integral h) and perturbed by a small time-periodic term. For the two-moon problem there is no small parameter making the collision-to-collision maps nearly integrable: a chain arc from moon 1 back to moon 1 (a "k" arc in the planet frame) is nearly autonomous only in the frame of moon 1 if moon 2's effect is a small smooth perturbation, which it is not (it is a second singularity); arcs between the two moons depend on the moons' relative phase and are genuinely nonautonomous with an order-one dependence on t.
### 5.2 Could the method plausibly extend (INFERRED)
- The GENERAL part of the method does extend in principle: sections 2 to 3 (Theorem 2.1, the action A_k, Lemma 3.1, det(f_k' - I) = -det(A_k'') prod(-D_12 S)^(-1), the changing direction condition, Proposition 3.1 for multipliers) use nothing about almost-autonomy; they only need collision-to-collision twist maps (t, h) -> (t', h') with generating functions S_k, here one family per ordered moon pair (moon i to moon j), each arc a Kepler (Lambert-time) solution. The existence of nondegenerate periodic orbits of the composed twist map then follows from a numerical check of (3.9) instead of from an epsilon expansion.
- The SPECIFIC existence proof (section 4 onward: first harmonic phi_k = a e^(it) + c.c., Poincare function, invariant curves Gamma_k intersecting transversally, two maps f_k, f_l, density of h_m) relies on small epsilon and would not carry over unchanged. Two moons without a small parameter would need the numerical check or a different small parameter, for example a small ratio of moon masses to the planet (already the theory's mu) together with hyperbolicity of the composed maps (Bolotin 2006 Theorem 4.1 needs a hyperbolic invariant set, "a transcendental problem related to exponentially small splitting"). I do not see in either paper a route to existence for two moons without verifying hyperbolicity or nondegeneracy numerically.
- The role of small eccentricity in the paper (explicit): it breaks the time-shift symmetry of the circular case so that 1-chains become degenerate but compositions of different maps generate transversal intersections of invariant curves (Section 4). INFERRED parallel: in the two-moon problem the second moon's phase breaks the same time-shift symmetry without any small parameter, which is why a chain that visits both moons is a natural nondegenerate candidate while a chain visiting one circular moon only is degenerate (a null direction along the time shift). The paper states this degeneracy for the case of 1-chains of the elliptic problem and says non-degeneracy is "different" in the circular case.
### 5.3 Conditions under which a chain survives that a numerical search could test
Both papers give explicit finite conditions on a chain of Kepler (collision) arcs for a shadowing orbit to exist for small mu:
1. Nondegenerate critical point of the summed action A_k = sum S_(k_i)(t_(i-1), t_i): the tridiagonal Hessian in the switching times is nonsingular (equivalently det(f_k'(x_0) - I) not equal to 0, Lemma 3.1, Eq. 3.10).
2. Relative speeds equal in and out at every encounter: h_i^- = h_i^+, |v_i^+| = |v_i^-| (Eq. 2.5), a condition of the chain itself.
3. Changing direction at every encounter: v_i^+ not equal to +/- v_i^-: the turn is not 0 and not pi (item D quantifies it as |cos angle(v_+, v_-)| < 1 - nu, Eq. 3.8 there, uniform).
4. No early collisions: the Kepler arc between two encounters must not hit the singularity earlier (the paper deletes those arcs).
5. Twist condition D_12 S not equal to 0 along each arc (the Lambert-type arc has a time-of-flight derivative that does not vanish).
6. Multipliers of the shadowing orbit, from Proposition 3.1: alpha = lambda + O(mu) from the composed twist map (stability of the chain), beta of order 1/mu from the passages.
7. Size conditions: mu below an unquantified mu_0; both papers say outright that how small epsilon and mu must be is not estimated.
All except 7 are computable on a numerical chain; none requires a small epsilon, and a numerical search can test them at the project's patched-conic or four-body chains (INFERRED as a suggested use; the papers do not do it).
### 5.4 Relation to the project's demanded-turn gate (INFERRED)
- Condition 3 is a condition on the turn angle between v^+ and v^- only as an EXCLUSION (not zero, not 180 degrees); the theory has no upper bound on the turn: with |v^+| = |v^-| fixed, an arbitrary turn angle is attained by an impact parameter of order mu (the O(mu) closest approach: |gamma - u| >= a mu in (2.6)). The turn the chain needs, the angle between v^+ and v^- at each encounter with equal speeds, is exactly the project's "demanded turn" (the angle that the available bend at the allowed periapsis must cover).
- The gate compares demanded turn with the maximum bend available at a physically allowed periapsis (an altitude floor, Laplace-sphere conditions). The theory contains no such limit: it holds at distances O(mu) from the moon, below any physical floor at real mass ratios (item D digest, section 4.3). So the theory's conditions are necessary-for-the-chain-to-exist-at-small-mu conditions and the demanded-turn gate is a separate PHYSICAL condition at the actual mass. Neither paper provides a bound relating them; the demanded turn computed from the chain's relative velocities (Eq. 6.17 gives the circular-case values: |v| = sqrt(2h + 3), radial V_k, tangential G_k - 1) is the quantity both share.
- For a circular single-moon chain the demanded turn is computable in closed form from the printed (6.17): the angle between v^+ = (-V_k + (G_k - 1) i) e^(is) and v^- = V_k + (G_k - 1) i for the same k, which gives a direct comparison for a project gate test (INFERRED suggestion; a COMPUTED example is not done here).
### 5.5 Short answers
- The setting differs from two moons by: one more singularity (a second moving singularity, which Bolotin 2006 Remark 1 allows without proof) and the loss of a small perturbation parameter (eccentricity), so the paper's existence argument does not carry over, while the nondegeneracy machinery does.
- The conditions that a chain survives (nondegenerate action Hessian, equal speeds, changing direction, no early collision, twist, mu small) are all computable; none bounds the turn; the demanded-turn gate is the physical condition that the theory does not contain.
