## 1. What the paper is for
Mixed-variable symplectic integrators (Wisdom and Holman 1991) have no secular energy drift and are about an order of magnitude faster than conventional N-body codes when one body dominates, but they lose accuracy whenever two bodies approach closely, because the pair's interaction term becomes comparable to the Kepler terms (abstract, p.793). Changing the step size during an encounter breaks symplecticity and each change shifts the integration Hamiltonian, so many encounters accumulate an energy error (p.793, p.795; Michel and Valsecchi 1997 show it can give spurious trajectories). Chambers' answer is a hybrid: the close-encounter term is integrated with a conventional integrator while everything else is solved symplectically, and a smooth changeover function K(r) makes the split fixed in time so the scheme remains symplectic overall (p.793, p.795).

## 2. Method, with equations
### 2.1 Operator view (section 2, pp.794-795)
Hamilton's equations (1), H = sum p_i^2/(2 m_i) - G sum_i m_i sum_{j>i} ... as printed: H = sum_{i=1}^N p_i^2/(2 m_i) - G sum_i sum_{j>i} m_i m_j / r_ij (two sums as printed). dq/dt = F q, solution q(t) = exp(t F) q(t - t). Split H = H_A + H_B, with Lie operators A, B, which do not commute. First-order step q(t) = exp(t A) exp(t B) q(t - t) + O(t^2) (eq. 6 area). Second-order: q(t) = exp(t B/2) exp(t A) exp(t B/2) q(t - t) + O(t^3) (eq. 6). The integrator solves exactly a Hamiltonian H_integ close to H; for the first-order scheme H_integ = H + (t/2) sum_i (dH_B/dx_i dH_A/dp_i - dH_B/dp_i dH_A/dx_i) + O(t^2) (eq. 7, Saha and Tremaine 1992). H_integ is conserved to round-off, so H differs from it by a small bounded amount and there is no secular energy drift. If B ~ eps A, the error per step is proportional to eps, so the dominant part goes in A.

### 2.2 Democratic-heliocentric split (eq. 8, p.795)
Heliocentric positions, barycentric momenta ("mixed-centre", called democratic heliocentric by Duncan et al. 1998; the Sun is index "sun", N counts the bodies other than the Sun):
- H_A = sum_{i=1}^N ( p_i^2/(2 m_i) - G m_sun m_i / r_i,sun ): independent Kepler orbits about the Sun, advanced analytically.
- H_B = -G sum_{i=1}^N sum_{j=i+1}^N m_i m_j / r_ij: mutual interactions, a kick in momentum with positions fixed.
- H_C = (1/(2 m_sun)) ( sum_{i=1}^N p_i )^2: the Sun's kinetic energy, a drift in position with momenta fixed. This is the extra third piece that mixed-centre variables need.
Each piece is analytically solvable. Second-order step with the third piece (p.795): q(t) = exp(t B/2) exp(t C/2) exp(t A) exp(t C/2) exp(t B/2) q(t - t). Error per step is O(eps t^3) with eps = sum m_i / m_sun, provided H_A >> H_B and H_A >> H_C, i.e. all bodies stay far apart. Barycentric variables fail this for the Sun itself; Jacobi variables work for the plain scheme but, in an encounter, force every term with indices between alpha and beta into the numerical part (p.795), so mixed-centre variables are chosen "when close encounters can occur".

### 2.3 Why a naive transfer is not enough, and the changeover split (eq. 9, p.795)
Moving the pair term r_ab from H_B to H_A for the duration of an encounter keeps the error small but changes H_integ at each switch, which is a smaller version of the step-size-change defect (p.795). The fix is to split every pair interaction permanently:
- H_A = sum_i ( p_i^2/(2 m_i) - G m_sun m_i / r_i,sun ) - G sum_i sum_{j>i} (m_i m_j / r_ij) [1 - K(r_ij)]
- H_B = -G sum_i sum_{j>i} (m_i m_j / r_ij) K(r_ij)   (eq. 9)
with K -> 1 at large separation and K -> 0 at small separation. Then H_B stays small even during an encounter and H_A contains the encounter term only when K < 1. H_A is advanced analytically (Kepler) for non-encountering bodies; for bodies in an encounter the Kepler terms plus the (1 - K)-weighted interaction are integrated numerically. The split is fixed in time, so H_integ does not change when a pair enters or leaves an encounter, and the scheme is symplectic in the formal sense even though the numerical piece is not. Inspired by the separable potential method of Duncan, Levison and Lee (1998), but only two pieces are needed instead of many (p.796).

### 2.4 Step sequence (p.796)
Second-order hybrid step of length tau:
1. Coordinates fixed. Each body receives an acceleration from the other bodies (not the Sun), weighted by K(r_ij), for tau/2.
2. Momenta fixed. Each body shifts position by tau sum_i p_i / (2 m_sun) (the H_C drift, half step).
3. Bodies not in an encounter advance on a Kepler orbit about the Sun for tau. Bodies in an encounter: the Kepler terms and the interaction terms weighted by (1 - K) are integrated numerically for tau.
4. As step 2.
5. As step 1.

### 2.5 The changeover function (section 4.1, eq. 10, Figure 1, p.796)
Requirements: K -> 1 at large r_ij, K -> 0 at small r_ij; smooth enough that the numerical integrator follows it; fast to evaluate. The form found "after some trial and error":
K(y) = 0 for y < 0;  y^2 / (2 y^2 - 2 y + 1) for 0 < y < 1;  1 for y > 1,
y = (r_ij - 0.1 r_crit) / (0.9 r_crit), r_crit a free parameter. So K = 0 for r_ij < 0.1 r_crit and K = 1 for r_ij > r_crit. The author admits derivative discontinuities at y = 0 and 1, argues the numerical integrator only samples K at finitely many points so it need only be "fooled", and notes difficulty only with a very strict tolerance combined with a small symplectic step (p.796).
COMPUTED: K(y) + K(1 - y) = (y^2 + (1 - y)^2)/(2 y^2 - 2 y + 1) = 1 exactly, so K(0.5) = 0.5 (the curve in Figure 1 passes through 0.5 at r_ij/r_crit = 0.55, matching the plot); K'(0) = K'(1) = 0 and K''(0) = 2, K''(1) = 2 (so the first derivative is continuous and the second is not). This is one K-values golden available without the paper: K(0.25) = 0.0625/0.625 = 0.1, K(0.75) = 0.9 (COMPUTED, exact).

### 2.6 Changeover distance (section 4.2, p.796)
Compromise: too small and the encounter is not calculated properly, too large and the cost outruns the accuracy. Levison and Duncan (1994) use 3 Hill radii; Duncan et al. (1998) recommend 3 mutual Hill radii for two massive bodies. For small masses what matters is the number of steps used to sample K, not the number of Hill radii: "a pair of objects with lunar mass can easily travel 3 mutual Hill radii in a single time-step", making the switch effectively instantaneous and giving large errors. Two-fold rule: for each object r_crit = max(n1 R_H, n2 tau v_max), v_max the maximum velocity expected (say the initial orbital speed of the innermost body); for a pair take the larger of the two bodies' values. Suggested ranges n1 = 3 to 10, n2 = 0.3 to 2.0 (p.796).

### 2.7 Which numerical integrator for the encounter (section 4.3, p.796)
Bulirsch-Stoer (robust, the usual referee); Press et al.'s Stoermer-rule variant for force depending on x only is about twice as fast; Everhart's RADAU is faster still "but occasionally runs into difficulty when objects undergo very close encounters, so it is probably safer to use Bulirsch-Stoer". The MERCURY package includes Bulirsch-Stoer and a RADAU (p.799).

### 2.8 Predicting encounters (section 4.4, eq. 11, p.797)
The integrator must know before each step which pairs will be in an encounter at some time within the step; end-of-step separations can miss a minimum in between. The predictor need not be accurate but must err on the safe side (a false alarm only costs a numerical instead of analytic Kepler advance). Procedure: advance each body one step along a crude Kepler orbit about the Sun, take separations Delta_0, Delta_1 and time derivatives Delta_dot_0, Delta_dot_1 at the ends, and use the cubic Hermite interpolant, normalised time t in [0, 1]:
Delta(t) = (1 - t)^2 (1 + 2t) Delta_0 + t^2 (3 - 2t) Delta_1 + t (1 - t)^2 tau Delta_dot_0 - t^2 (1 - t) tau Delta_dot_1   (11).
Minimum separation: a t^2 + b t + c = 0 with a = 6 (Delta_0 - Delta_1) + 3 tau (Delta_dot_0 + Delta_dot_1), b = 6 (Delta_1 - Delta_0) - 2 tau (2 Delta_dot_0 + Delta_dot_1), c = tau Delta_dot_0. COMPUTED: differentiating (11) with the standard Hermite basis reproduces these a, b, c exactly (the printed accuracy "O(tau^3)" is as printed; a cubic Hermite is formally fourth order, which I did not test). A "pre-checker" that eliminates pairs which cannot meet during the step, plus this routine, takes "a few per cent" of total run time. Higher-order interpolants using accelerations at the ends "have an unfortunate habit of giving spurious additional minima", so the cubic is preferred. The same interpolation can be applied after the step to estimate the minimum separation reached (p.797).

### 2.9 Error behaviour claimed
- Plain second-order scheme: error per step O(eps tau^3), eps = sum m_i / m_sun, until a close approach raises it to O(tau^3) (p.795).
- Hybrid: no secular energy drift in the energy-error plots (Figures 2, 3) and symplectic in the formal sense; larger short-period energy fluctuations than a conventional code (p.797-798).
- Open caveat, verbatim in substance: there is no guarantee the series for H_integ converges for the hybrid scheme; there may be cases where errors exceed the leading O(eps tau^3) term; to be investigated in a later paper (p.796).
- Formal symplecticity is claimed with the explicit proviso that the numerical piece is not symplectic itself (abstract); the bodies' energy error is therefore bounded only to the extent that the Bulirsch-Stoer tolerance is tight (INFERRED from the BS-driven behaviour in Figure 3).

## 4. Reconciliation against the project's integrators
Checked by grep of `src/cyclerfinder` and `scripts` on 2026-10-04.
- REBOUND is used only with IAS15 (`sim.integrator = "ias15"`): `nbody/propagator.py` (restricted n-body on DE440), `nbody/jovian.py` (Jupiter-central, Galilean moons via an additional-forces callback on a rails cache), `data/validation/v3_3d.py`, `v3_3d_periodic.py`, and the verify scripts under `scripts/` (run_392/393 v3 and v4, branch_c32 b0, cr3bp_family_search, cr3bp_silver_inertial_crosscheck). No use of MERCURIUS, WHFast, WH or any symplectic integrator anywhere (grep for mercurius, whfast, janus returned nothing). So the project does NOT use the descendant of this method.
- scipy DOP853/Radau/LSODA: the CR3BP/BCR4BP/ER3BP/QBCP cores, the V4 lanes (`v4_uranus.py`, `v4_saturn.py`, strict variants: DOP853 with J2 and third-body moons), and `v3_bcr4bp.py` (LSODA as the independent integrator).
- A compiled adaptive DOP853 (own implementation with scipy's coefficients) with a state transition matrix in `search/titania_oberon_realeph_895.py` for the URA111 force model of #895, plus a scipy verification path.
- Structural fact that decides applicability (INFERRED from reading the module docstrings): in every lane the spacecraft is a massless test particle and the moons and planets follow prescribed motion (ephemeris tables, or a prescribed circle, or the planar circular-concentric `#890` four-body model with no moon-moon force). The massive bodies never evolve under their mutual gravity in the project's code. Hence the specific problem of Chambers' paper, N massive bodies perturbing each other with close pair encounters, does not arise. The related problem that does arise is a test particle passing a massive body inside its Hill sphere on a long arc.
- Where the project meets close encounters: the moon-tour and flyby lanes (`#895`, `#890`, the V4 lanes, `jovian.py`, the CR3BP families near a primary, `cr3bp_regularized.py`), where IAS15 or DOP853 are adaptive and shrink their step, as a conventional integrator does. The energy-drift mechanism the paper addresses (secular growth from step changes in a fixed-step symplectic scheme) does not occur in adaptive Runge-Kutta or IAS15, which have their own, different error growth (a linear drift in the paper's Figure 3 BS curve is this behaviour).
- Energy is not an invariant of most project models: the Jacobi constant is in the autonomous CR3BP only. The `#890` model and the `#895` model are explicitly time dependent (a moon moving on a circle or table), so a Chambers-style energy-error plot is not available there. Use time-reversal closure and tolerance convergence instead (section 5).
- Sources of non-integrator error that dominate in the project today and which the paper does not address: the V4 lanes zero or soften each moon's gravity inside its Hill sphere and double-count the moons' mass (#894, #900). Chambers' point that an encounter must be integrated with the full unsoftened interaction at the pair separation is the principle `#895` already follows ("Nothing here zeroes or softens a force near a body").

## 5. Techniques applicable to the project's problems
The honest summary: the symplectic split itself buys nothing for the project's present computations (massless particle, prescribed bodies, adaptive integrators with no step-size-symplecticity problem), and the hybrid scheme would need massive moon-moon evolution to be needed. What transfers is smaller: the encounter predictor, the sampling rule for the switching radius, the principle of an unsoftened full-interaction integration through the encounter, and the paper's test-design discipline (a secular-drift control, a step-size-independence control, an interacting-body reference).

### 5.1 #900 (rebuild the V4 moon-tour validation lane)
Read the entry: the lanes zero a moon's gravity inside its Hill sphere, restart every leg from a Lambert departure, and double-count the moons' mass; rebuild on the `#895` force model with a positive control on the moons' own motion and continuity through every flyby.
- Directly supported by the paper (p.795, p.796): Chambers integrates the unsoftened Newtonian interaction through the encounter with a tight-tolerance conventional integrator, and shrinks the symplectic part's role. That is the same design as the `#895` model, which already exists. Nothing to port from the paper's code path.
- Bookkeeping lesson from section 2.2: the democratic-heliocentric split attributes the Sun's reflex motion to the H_C term (sum of momenta squared over 2 m_sun) rather than to a second central body, so central mass is counted once. The double-count in `#894` is the planet-centred analogue (the planet GM already contains the inner moons; a system GM plus an added moon mass counts that moon twice). The `#895` model's direct plus indirect terms are the equivalent bookkeeping. A test for `#900`: for a spacecraft far from every moon, planet-centred acceleration from the lane must equal the same quantity computed in a barycentric frame from the same GM set to the working tolerance (an N-body Newton's-third-law identity, not a golden from any code).
- Test the encounter integration the way the paper tests its encounter piece: Figure 4 (T5) shows trajectory insensitivity to the outer step. For a rebuilt lane, run one fixed flyby arc (for example the `#895` Titania flyby) at rtol 1e-10, 1e-12, 1e-13 and with the moon table sampled at 2 x and 4 x finer; the miss distance and post-flyby state must agree to a stated tolerance. A lane that changes its answer under table spacing has the paper's "number of steps sampling K" problem in another form.
- The `#895` table is a cubic Hermite sample of SPICE positions, so the paper's section 4.2 argument applies in its table form: a table step must resolve the moon's motion over the time the spacecraft spends inside r_crit, otherwise the encounter sees a stepwise-wrong moon. Rule to adopt as a check: table step times the moon's speed must be a small fraction of the flyby periapsis distance.

### 5.2 #895, #890 (Titania-Oberon arcs on real ephemeris, long arcs with close moon passes)
Read the entries: `#890` found a periodic Titania-Oberon-Titania orbit in the planar circular four-body model that does not fly in the real system without correction; started in the four-body model the patched-conic closure misses Oberon by 967,000 km, attributed to the flyby changing the orbit's energy by a few percent over 5.5 revolutions. `#895` supplies the force model and corrector.
- That miss is a physical flyby effect, not an integrator error, so Chambers' method would not change it. What the paper adds is a way to rule an integrator in or out as a contributor: a reversibility control. In the symmetric `#890` model (symmetry (tau, x, y, vx, vy) -> (-tau, x, -y, -vx, vy)) integrate forward through one flyby and back; the return error is a direct integrator error measure through the encounter, valid with no energy invariant. It plays the role of Figure 3's energy plot (T4) in a time-dependent model.
- Use the paper's three-way comparison pattern on one flyby: the project's DOP853 core, REBOUND IAS15 with the same force model through the `jovian.py`-style callback (the existing `v3_3d_periodic` parity test is the template; its floors are the project's), and a reference at a much tighter tolerance. Agree to a stated figure or the flyby result is not trusted. This is T5's pattern (BS at 1e-12 as referee).
- The paper's section 4.4 predictor is directly usable for flyby detection and periapsis-passage bookkeeping in the shooting corrector: given states and radial-distance derivatives at the two ends of a step, equation 11 plus the quadratic returns the minimum separation to a moon without a root finder on dense output. The `#895` code currently finds periapses with brentq on rdot (`titania_oberon_realeph_895.py` near line 808); the predictor is an alternative that screens candidate steps cheaply and reports the minimum even when the step ends before periapsis. Test: the predicted minimum against the brentq periapsis on a known flyby, and a false-alarm rate on arcs with no encounter.
- Sampling rule applies to multi-shooting node placement: nodes spaced such that tau v_max is a small multiple of a flyby periapsis would put a node inside r_crit (n2 = 0.3 to 2.0 in section 4.2); a segment boundary inside the changeover region is the same discontinuity the paper warns about, applied to a corrector instead of a symplectic step. Keep nodes outside 3 to 10 Hill radii (n1 = 3 to 10) where that is feasible.

### 5.3 #908 (capture-sweep sampling; add speed and Sun phase as variables)
Read the entry: the capture sweeps sample speed through a fixed osculating eccentricity and ran at one Sun phase; add speed and Sun-phase coverage and rerun.
- The sweeps are thousands of long propagations of a massless particle with periodic perturbation, where fixed-step symplectic integration would be fast. A hybrid scheme in the paper's form (Kepler about the primary, kicks from the Moon or the Sun, a numerical integrator only during close lunar passes) is exactly what REBOUND's MERCURIUS is, restricted to a massless particle. It would not be valid for the cislunar capture case without checking, because the Sun's perturbation is not small compared with the Earth-Moon term at lunar distance, and Chambers' scheme assumes H_B << H_A (section 2.2); a Sun-perturbed particle near the lunar distance violates that for the Moon, which is the reason that case is not a Wisdom-Holman problem. For the Uranian case the planet dominates (the moons sum to roughly 1e-4 of the planet GM, INFERRED from the registry GMs, not computed here), so the split is valid there.
- Whether to adopt it: INFERRED, a speed-up, not a correctness gain. Do it only if the corrected rerun is compute-limited. The adoption test is the paper's T3 control transcribed: the Jacobi constant in a pure CR3BP case over the sweep time (printed figure 3e-6 over 1e6 yr at tau = 5 yr, r_crit = 10 Hill radii), plus a trajectory-agreement test against the existing DOP853 on 100 random initial conditions with a miss-distance criterion, and a positive control that the capture classification flips nowhere against the existing code. Per the project's memory on isolated flips in sweeps, any isolated disagreement is first suspected to be a numerical artefact in the new integrator.
- More relevant to `#908` than the splitting: the paper's lesson on the sampling of a switching function. A capture-sweep classification threshold evaluated at coarse output times can miss a crossing between steps. The predictor of section 4.4 (cubic Hermite minimum between samples) applies to the capture criterion (minimum energy or minimum distance in a step) without refining the step.

### 5.4 #924 (chaos indicators FLI and MEGNO; need accurate variational equations over long times)
Read the entry: ten lines on top of the transition matrices the models return; calibrate on a regular and a hyperbolic case; "Unproven for close encounters and short arcs".
- Important negative: this paper does not treat variational equations, tangent vectors or transition matrices at all. It gives no evidence that the hybrid scheme's tangent map is symplectic or accurate; the paper itself flags that convergence of the series for H_integ is not guaranteed (p.796). Any claim that the hybrid scheme gives accurate chaos indicators would be unsupported from this source.
- What does transfer: both FLI and MEGNO grow with the tangent vector, so their value after a close encounter depends on the tangent map through the encounter, where the paper's concern (an integrator that changes its effective Hamiltonian when a step changes) is real. The project's own memory note on REBOUND variational particles: order-1 variational particles do not differentiate a Python `additional_forces` callback, so the STM is wrong near a perturber unless the gravity-gradient tensor is applied by hand. The `jovian.py` and `propagator.py` paths use callbacks, so they carry that defect for chaos indicators. Recommended control from the paper's philosophy: compare the transition matrix through a flyby by (a) the model's analytic STM and (b) finite differences of the flow, at the tolerance used in the sweeps, as a gate before any FLI or MEGNO value near a moon is trusted. This is the "positive control" the `#924` entry already asks for, specialised to encounters.
- The paper's insistence on evaluating the changeover (here: the encounter) at enough steps is the same as requiring the tangent map to be stable under step refinement; test FLI at tolerance 1e-10 and 1e-13 on the same orbit and require agreement within a stated fraction.

### 5.5 #913 to #918
Read each entry. `#913` and `#915` are heliocentric Earth-Mars real-ephemeris cycler reproduction (planets prescribed, zero-size flybys, no encounter integration); `#914` carries the moon cyclers to multi-cycle trajectories with integrated flybys using the `#895` corrector; `#916` tests persistence under eccentricity and the Sun as invariant curves of the one-period map; `#917` the Casoliva rows in the elliptic problem with the fold count; `#918` a graph search for zero-radius cyclers.
- `#913`, `#915`, `#918`: no application (patched-conic, prescribed planets, no encounter integration).
- `#914`: the same recommendations as 5.2 (reversibility control, three-way integrator comparison, predictor-based periapsis detection, node placement outside r_crit) apply, because it is the integrated-flyby stage of the moon tours; this is where the paper's test design is most useful.
- `#916` and `#917`: invariant curves of a one-period map in a time-periodic model. Symplectic integrators preserve the structure of such a map (no spurious dissipation), which is the property that matters for Fourier invariant-curve computation. The paper offers a general argument for H_integ being conserved, but nothing about invariant tori, and the project already has collocation GMOS in Grossi and Topputo's approach (separate digest). Not an application of this paper.

## 7. Defects and limits noted
- Equations are garbled in the text layer (tau rendered as "t", missing minus signs); the digest uses the page images.
- Section 5.2 says "a Bulirsch-Stoer integration (using Stoermer's method) with tolerance 1e-9 gave similar results, requiring about 50 per cent more computer time", without stating which of the two (hybrid or BS) is the baseline for the 50 percent; read as 50 percent more than the hybrid run (INFERRED).
- The printed prediction accuracy "O(tau^3)" for a cubic Hermite interpolant is as printed; not tested.
- No order-of-convergence test, no run-time table for the symplectic part, and no test with more than a handful of bodies in the Wisdom-Holman regime: the author says the inherent speed advantage would only show with more planets (p.799).
- The paper states, but does not prove, that the scheme is symplectic given a non-symplectic numerical piece; it is symplectic in the sense that the Hamiltonian split is fixed in time, and the numerical error of the BS piece is the controlled remainder.

## 8. Update 2026-10-04: Duncan, Levison and Lee 1998 is now held
Duncan, Levison and Lee 1998 (SyMBA, AJ 116:2067) is now filed in the private corpus and digested in `docs/notes/2026-10-04-digest-duncan-levison-lee-1998-symba.md`. Can test T2 now be reproduced? No. That paper (section 6.2, p.2074) prints only: Sun plus four giant planets, masses x50, "nearly circular" initial orbits, step 0.03 yr, and outcomes (Neptune-Saturn crossing at about 30 yr, Saturn ejected at 1920 yr, Uranus about 60 yr later, energy change oscillating about zero). The planets' initial elements and epoch are printed in neither paper, and the ejection sequence differs between the two papers (Chambers: Neptune first, near 1000 yr; the system is chaotic and step-dependent). Only a qualitative control is available (no secular energy trend, same order of magnitude), from a reconstruction of the planets at a documented epoch. The fully specified pair is Chambers T3 with SyMBA's test 6.3 (Jacobi constant: 3e-6 over 1e6 yr here, one part in 29 000 worst case over up to 1e9 yr there). Follow-up 7 above is therefore partly resolved (Duncan 1998 held, T2 conditions still missing); follow-up 7's Michel and Valsecchi table IV remains unheld.
