## Verdict: NOT content-identical. The upload is a different version of the paper.

The upload has no AIAA conference header and no copyright line. Its footer reads "n of 10 American Institute of Aeronautics and Astronautics".

Examples read on the page image: Earth-1 launch V_inf 4.79; Mars-2 2 July 2014, 7.11 km/s, 26,419 km; Earth-6 7 April 2021, 4.51, 22,923; Earth-7 21 Sept. 2022, 4.51, 9,813; Earth-10 21 Jan. 2027, 8.04, 10,449; Mars-14 15 July 2031 (no V_inf).
- Table 8 "Powered outbound S1L1-B cycler with short Earth-Mars legs" (upload p.9). Earth-7 flyby altitude 10,000 km; maneuver of 0.96 delta-V on 24 Sept. 2022 (footnote b says delta-V maneuver); Earth-10 altitude -604 km and Mars-11 -519 km (negative, footnote c "subsurface flybys will be removed in a future work"); Earth-13 18 April 2031, V_inf 11.94, altitude 79,371 km.
- A section "Verification of the S1L1-B Cycler" (upload p.8). STOUR (Satellite Tour Design Program, ref 30 Rinderle, JPL D-263) found a ballistic cycler very similar to the S1L1-B cycler. The only difference stated: S1L1-B has a 153-day Earth-Mars leg, while the STOUR cycler has some legs almost twice as long.
- A paragraph introducing Table 8 ("we used a maneuver and allowed for subsurface flybys ... optimization will be addressed in a future work").
- Two more bullets under "Other Possible Extensions" (Rall-Hollister and Byrnes et al. cyclers can be built inside this framework; half-year Earth-to-Earth "backflips"), a paragraph on estimating total delta-V of powered cyclers, and Conclusions text.
- References 30 (Rinderle, STOUR user guide, JPL D-263) and 31 (Byrnes, McConaghy, Longuski, the two-synodic-period paper, item d). The held list has only 30 entries, and its ref 30 is the Byrnes, McConaghy, Longuski paper (the STOUR guide is not cited).
- Fig. 8 caption "The S1L1-B cycler." appears in both. The S1L1-B naming ("B stands for ballistic") is in the upload.
