## 0. Verdict

**This is the origin of the Pythagorean (Burrau) problem, the standard test case for general three-body
integrators. It is history and an integrator control, not a periodic orbit or a cycler.**
- Problem: masses m1 = 5, m2 = 4, m3 = 3, at rest at the vertices of a 3-4-5 right triangle. Each
  mass is opposite the side of equal length: rho1 = |m2 m3| = 5, rho2 = |m3 m1| = 4, rho3 = |m1 m2| = 3.
  So the right angle is at m1. G = 1 (implicit).
- Why this case: (i) a three-body case with comparable masses and distances, of which Burrau knew only one
  earlier example (Strömgren, A.N. 4356); (ii) a recollection of an 1893 remark by Meissel that these
  conditions should give a periodic motion; (iii) a deliberate test of Thiele's variable-step
  numerical integration (Interpolationsrechnung pp. 21-29), because masses 4 and 5 must fall together
  to a close periastron.
- Result: integrated by hand to t = 3.35 (with S. Kristensen). Burrau says that Meissel's periodicity
  conjecture "has not been confirmed in the computed part of the motion". He values the paper for the
  variable-step method.
- **Modern check (mine):** the table agrees with an accurate integration to 2e-5 up to t = 1.65, 5e-5 up
  to t = 1.84 and 1e-4 up to t = 1.874. It degrades through the 5-4 close approach at t = 1.879 (separation 0.0097) and is only qualitative
  afterwards (error 0.1 by t = 2.3, 1.7 at t = 3.35). Section 3.
- Catalogue implication: none. PROPOSAL only: if #931 (the general three-body control, see the
  Szebehely-Peters 1967a digest) is built, use Burrau's table rows for t up to 1.84 as a cheap
  published early-time check, to 5e-5 (2e-5 up to t = 1.65). Do not use the post-encounter rows as a golden.

## 1. Content (READ on the page images)

- Equations of motion (1): Newtonian, planar, written for m1 with m1 m2 (x2 - x1)/rho3^3 +
  m1 m3 (x3 - x1)/rho2^3, "and four analogous equations".
- Relative coordinates: x2 - x1 = rho3 cos theta3, y2 - y1 = rho3 sin theta3, "and the analogous expressions
  for x1 - x3 etc.". This is the cyclic set rho1 = r3 - r2, rho2 = r1 - r3, rho3 = r2 - r1. Equation (2)
  for rho1 is d^2(rho1 cos theta1)/dt^2 = -M cos theta1/rho1^2 + m1 (cos theta1/rho1^2 + cos theta2/rho2^2
  + cos theta3/rho3^2), with M = m1 + m2 + m3. I derived (2) from (1) and it holds only with the cyclic
  choice (INFERRED from the algebra; also confirmed by the numerical check below).
- Method: all three rho_n (12 second-order quadratures) are integrated, although 6 would do. The
  redundant three (rho1 + rho2 + rho3 = 0) serve as a running check. Burrau prefers this to computing
  everything twice. Integration and computing scheme as in Thiele.
- Table: t, rho2 cos theta2, rho2 sin theta2, rho3 cos theta3, rho3 sin theta3; 160 rows.
  - Six decimals to t = 1.3, five from 1.4, four from 1.93.
  - Step: 0.1-0.2 at first, 0.05 to 0.025 from t = 1.4, 0.01 to 0.001 from 1.8, and 1e-4 then
    5e-5 from 1.8773 to 1.8796 (the periastron). It then returns to 0.05 by 2.1, and is 1/80 to 1/160
    after 2.9.
  - First rows as printed (image): t = 0.0: 0.000000, +4.000000, -3.000000, +0.000000;
    t = 0.1: -0.001743, +3.996859, -2.994637, +0.000457 (dotted).
  - Last row: t = 3.35: -1.6966, -0.2942, -0.0000 (dotted), -0.1055 (dotted).
  - A raised dot follows the last digit of many entries. It is not explained. It is probably the old
    half-unit mark; the evidence is weak. Section 3, item 3.
- Barycentric formulae (p.117): X = (4 rho3 cos theta3 - 3 rho2 cos theta2)/12, Y likewise;
  m1 at (-X, -Y), m2 at (rho3 cos theta3 - X, ...), and m3 printed as (rho2 cos theta2 - X, rho2 sin
  theta2 - Y). **The m3 line has a sign slip.** With the cyclic convention it must be -rho2 cos theta2
  - X. Checked numerically at t = 1.0: the printed form gives (0.577, 4.582), but m3 is at
  (0.950, -2.773), which equals -rho2 - X. The X, Y and m2 lines are correct.
- Narrative (p.118): masses 4 and 5 fall together and make "the rapid periastron motion" near t = 1.88,
  shown in an inset at tenfold scale. Then mass 5 goes towards mass 3, with a periastron "around
  t = 2.9". Mass 3 then moves off to the right, and masses 4 and 5 approach again.

## 2. Comparison with Szebehely & Peters 1967

Held: `szebehely-peters-1967a-complete-solution-general-problem-three-bodies-aj-72-876-doi-10.1086-110355.pdf`
(digest `docs/notes/2026-10-05-digest-szebehely-peters-1967a-pythagorean-three-body.md`) and
`szebehely-peters-1967b-new-periodic-solution-problem-three-bodies-aj-72-1187-doi-10.1086-110398-ads-scan.pdf`
(digest `...-1967b-periodic-pythagorean.md`).
- Same problem, but the bodies are numbered in reverse. S&P body 1 has mass 3, body 2 mass 4 and
  body 3 mass 5, at (1, 3), (-2, -1), (1, -1). So Burrau's (m1, m2) = (5, 4) pair is S&P's (3, 2) pair.
  The S&P positions differ from mine only by a rotation and translation; the triangle and the masses
  are the same. Energy: S&P E = -769/60 = -12.81667. My integration: E0 = -12.8166666667 (agrees).
- S&P state that Burrau stopped at t = 3.35. S&P first close approach is t1 = 1.879, r23 about 1e-2. My run gives 0.00970
  at t = 1.87934; the S&P digest's run gave 9.700e-3 at 1.879343. Burrau's table gives 0.0096 at
  1.87935. So all three agree on the first approach.
- S&P's m1-m3 approach "t4 about 3" (masses 3 and 5) is Burrau's "periastron around t = 2.9". The
  accurate time is t = 3.024 with distance 0.571. Burrau's table puts it at about t = 2.96 with distance
  0.29. So Burrau's second encounter is early and too deep, a result of the error carried out of
  the first encounter.
- S&P carried the problem to the end: escape of the mass-3 body, with masses 4 and 5 left as a binary
  (an elliptic-hyperbolic final motion). The 1967b paper found a nearby periodic orbit with a 4-5
  binary collision at T/2 (T = 31.8229622453). That orbit is a modified-position solution, not
  Burrau's initial conditions. So Meissel's conjecture fails for these exact conditions, but a
  periodic orbit exists nearby.

   - Reference convergence: against DOP853 at rtol 1e-14, DOP853 at 1e-10 differs by at most 1.5e-10,

   - Transcription spike check: for every cell, a cubic through the residuals (table - calc) of the 4
     neighbouring rows predicts the cell; 13 of 640 cells deviate by more than 2.5 units of the last
     printed digit. I re-read on a 400 dpi image the flagged cells at t = 2.85, 2.875, 2.9125 and all of
     page 3 (3.2125 to 3.35, including the largest flag, 3.3375 col. 4, 7.7 units). All match my
     transcription. The other flags sit at t = 1.3 (where the precision changes from 6 to 5 decimals)
     and at 1.8791-1.8792 (the periastron, where the residual changes fastest), both expected artefacts of
     the fit. No misread was found. Before about t = 1.87 the integration checks every digit directly.
4. `burrau_dotmark.py` (output `burrau_dotmark_output.txt`). At t = 0.1 and 0.2, before the hand
   error grows, both dotted entries are 0.46 and 0.42 units of the last digit below the accurate
   magnitude. The six undotted entries are -0.13 to +0.48 units. But the undotted +0.48 overlaps the dotted values, so this is consistent with an "add about half a
   unit" mark but does not prove it. Later the drift of the hand integration (4-6 units by t = 1.3) hides it.

The table's agreement up to the first close approach (5e-5 to t = 1.84 with a hand variable-step method) and its
loss of accuracy afterwards are what one expects. A close encounter at d = 0.01 amplifies small
errors, and the motion is chaotic.
