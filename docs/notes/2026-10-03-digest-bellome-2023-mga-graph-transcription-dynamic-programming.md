## What the paper is
A heliocentric (planet-to-planet) multiple-gravity-assist (MGA) trajectory design method. Abstract: it "describes a methodology based upon a trajectory model to transcribe the mixed-integer space into a discrete graph made by grids of interconnected nodes. The model is based on Lambert arc grids obtained for a range of departure dates and flight times between two planets. A Tisserand-based criterion selects planets to pass-by. Dynamic programming is extended to multi-objective optimization of MGA trajectories and used to explore the graph, guaranteeing Pareto optimality with only moderate computational effort." Test cases named in the abstract: "Missions to Jupiter and Saturn, alongside challenging comet sample return transfers involving long MGA sequences".

The "Jupiter and Saturn" in this paper are the DESTINATION PLANETS of Earth-departure interplanetary transfers (Cassini-like Earth-to-Saturn). The paper treats no planetary moons as bodies in any of its computations (see the answer to question 3).

## The method (question 1)
Four steps (Sections II-VI):

1. **Problem statement (Sec. II).** MGA design is posed as a mixed-integer nonlinear programme (MINLP / hybrid optimal control). The integer vector x is the sequence of bodies; the continuous vector y holds epochs and manoeuvre/flyby variables (MGA-DSM model: Table 1 lists t0, v_inf,dep, theta, k, T, alpha for the first leg and r_p, gamma, T, alpha for later legs). Two objectives (Eq. 2): f1 = v_inf,dep + sum of delta-v_i + v_inf,arr (propellant-like cost, km/s) and f2 = sum of leg times of flight T_i (total transfer time).
2. **Sequence generation by Tisserand graph (Sec. III).** A Tisserand graph (Strange and Longuski [41]) is explored by breadth-first search, "a maximum of 4-5 swing-bys is sufficient to reach any object in the Solar System". For Earth-Jupiter with v_inf 3 to 5 km/s at Earth, 1 km/s contour steps, "an automatic exploration of the Tisserand graph finds 27 sequences with 3 to 5 fly-bys". The text says resonant orbits at Venus, Earth and Mars are used to connect planets and gives EVEEJ, EVEMEJ and EVEMMMJ as examples; EVEMMMJ is stated to use "2:1 and 3:1 resonant ratio on each consecutive MM leg" (Figs. 3, 4). The Tisserand step is used in "explicit" (pre-generated list) form in the paper; an "implicit" variant is described but not the one used.
3. **Graph transcription (Sec. IV).** A DSM is assumed immediately after each planetary encounter, so each leg is one Lambert arc between two encounter epochs. The cost of a leg is the "velocity defect" at the flyby (Eq. 3): if the required deflection delta <= delta_max, defect = | |v_inf+| - |v_inf-| |; otherwise sqrt(|v+|^2 + |v-|^2 - 2|v+||v-| cos(delta_max - delta)). delta_max = 2 arcsin( (1 + r_p,min |v_inf|^2 / mu_pl)^-1 ) (Eq. 4). Decision variables reduce to y = [t1, T1, ..., T_(n_int-1)], applied on grids of departure dates and transfer times. A graph node is a pair of (object, epoch) points, node A = (a_t1, b_t2) etc.; the cost of a connection between nodes depends on the previously visited object through the incoming v_inf-, giving a unique cost per triplet (P_i, P_j, P_k). Resonant legs (same planet consecutively) are "not generated by standard Lambert solvers"; they are obtained analytically from the post-flyby relative velocity ("an infinite number of post fly-by resonant solutions exist ... They differ in the inclination of the resonant orbit"; a minimal-inclination-change baseline can be assumed) (Sec. IV.A, citing Wagner and Wie [28]).
4. **Dynamic programming (Sec. V).** Tree-graph expansion; Bellman's principle prunes paths arriving at the same node at the same depth: single-objective (SODP) keeps the minimum; multi-objective (MODP) keeps the Pareto-optimal subset ("the Pareto-optimal set containing this speciőc node would include the Pareto-optimal subset of nodes before and after the visited one [63]"). Compared against full evaluation (FE), depth-first, breadth-first and beam search. Search is complete on the transcribed grid ("guaranteeing global optimality" there), not on the continuous space.
5. **Refinement (Sec. VI).** Defects are replaced by mid-course DSMs of the MGA-DSM model. An analytic single Newton-Raphson step with the state transition matrix gives the DSM that removes a v_inf defect at the next flyby while keeping the position constraint (Eqs. 5-11); the "leveraging ratio" is delta-v_inf / |delta-v|. Refinement re-optimises with PSO: departure epoch within +/-30 days of the grid value, visiting epochs and flyby parameters within +/-15 percent; "approximately 40 iterations with 1200 particles on 4 GHz laptop", "few seconds". Appendix proves d|v_rel|/dt_f = 0 at rendezvous.

Search space as printed: Lambert arcs between planets (Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn; plus comet 67P in the last case) over grids of launch date and time of flight, number of heliocentric revolutions N_rev <= 1 or 2; flyby altitude enters only through r_p,min in delta_max (value not stated in the text; "as in [41]"). Dynamical model: heliocentric two-body Lambert arcs with planetary ephemerides (the ephemeris source is not stated in the text; epochs are in MJD2000).

### C. Defect-to-DSM conversion and leveraging (Sec. VII.C)
Large defects in the Venus-Venus leg are replaced by DSMs, "reducing from more than 1 km/s to approximately 400-600 m/s"; the V-V leg has the highest leveraging ratio; in the E-V leg the ratio is "generally less than 1 but exceeds 1 towards the end of the leg, reaching approximately 1.4"; in the J-S leg of Cassini and Cassini-2 the ratio stays below 1. ## Does it contain any repeating, periodic, resonant-return or cycler trajectory, or any moon tour? (question 3)
**Cycler: no. Moon tour in the Jovian, Saturnian or Uranian system: no.** The words "cycler" and "cycle" do not appear in the text (checked by reading in full; the closest items are the resonant legs listed below). All computed trajectories are one-way Earth-to-target transfers (Earth to Saturn; Earth to comet and back) with a non-repeating body sequence and finite launch window.

What it does contain, for completeness, and the quotes:

- **Moon tours are mentioned only as application context for Tisserand graphs, not treated.** "The Tisserand graph has also been employed in building the winning trajectory of the 6th edition of the Global Trajectory Optimization Competition (GTOC) [51]. Several modified versions were developed to adapt it to specific purposes, as in the Saturn moon tours [52], the case of the circular-restricted 3-body problem [53], and low-thrust propulsion trajectories [54]." (Sec. III). "Non-resonant ballistic trajectories can also be used to bridge the gap between different contours, especially if small bodies are involved in the transfer (e.g., Saturn moons) [55]." (Sec. III). "Nevertheless, one can envisage applications such as Jupiter and Saturn moon tours [48, 49, 52] in which longer sequences might be needed, depending on specific mission-related constraints. Therefore, incomplete enumeration processes [60] may be relevant." (Sec. III). The cited works are Heaton et al. (Europa Orbiter tour), Kloster et al. (Europa Orbiter tour with Io gravity assists), Campagnola and Russell (Endgame Part 1). No Jovian, Saturnian or Uranian moon is a node in any computation, table or figure of the paper.
- **Resonant legs at inner planets inside one-way transfers.** EVEMMMJ with "2:1 and 3:1 resonant ratio on each consecutive MM leg" (Sec. III, Fig. 4), generated by Tisserand exploration, described as approximating the "theoretical global optimum resulting from a fully ballistic transfer" (not a computed table entry; Fig. 4 only). The comet case (Table 12) has two Earth flybys exactly two years apart (Oct 2032 and Oct 2034 outbound; Oct 2040 and Oct 2042 return); the paper does not call these resonances in the text beyond the general statement on resonant orbits, and no period or resonance ratio is stated for them.
- **Resonant-return handling in the method:** "Tisserand-informed exploration of planetary fly-bys as from section III also identifies the need for resonant orbits on legs that visit the same planet consecutively. These can occur when the time of flight is such that the spacecraft encounters the same point for two encounters. Such transfers are characterized by a ratio of integers between the planet and spacecraft orbit periods." (Sec. IV.A). This is the paper's only treatment of resonance and it is about connecting two flybys of the same planet inside a one-way sequence, not about a closed cycle.
- The comet mission returns to Earth, but as a sample-return round trip with a 6 to 12 month science stay, not a repeating cycle.

## Relevance to a cycler-search project (facts only)
Method elements present that a patched-conic gravity-assist code could reuse conceptually: Lambert-arc grids over departure date and time of flight; the v_inf-defect cost of Eq. 3 with the delta_max bound of Eq. 4; the triplet-node graph structure; Tisserand-graph breadth-first sequence enumeration used as a filter; Bellman-principle pruning (single- and multi-objective); an STM-based DSM leveraging-ratio estimate (Eqs. 5-11); PSO refinement into an MGA-DSM model. Bodies treated: Sun-centred Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn and one comet (67P). Systems treated: heliocentric only. The paper does not search for closed or repeating orbits, does not impose periodicity or return-to-start-state constraints, and does not use the circular restricted three-body problem or any planetary-moon system. The unusual features: it keeps the whole Pareto front rather than one optimum, and it lists the integer-ratio resonant leg handling for repeated same-planet encounters.

## What it does NOT contain
- Any cycler, quasi-cycler, repeating-encounter or periodic trajectory.
- Any Jovian, Saturnian or Uranian moon tour, any inter-moon transfer, any three-body (CR3BP) computation, any V-infinity leveraging tour computation (the leveraging ratio here is the DSM-to-defect ratio, unrelated to the moon-tour V-infinity leveraging technique of [52]).
- Any table of results for Earth-Mars or Earth-Venus cyclers.
- A statement of the ephemeris model, flyby altitude floors, or Lambert solver used.
- Numbers for the "Jupiter" mission class beyond the Earth-Jupiter Tisserand count (27 sequences) and the J flyby of the Saturn transfers (the abstract mentions "missions to Jupiter" but no Jupiter-arrival result table is printed; the conclusions refer to missions "similar to the JUICE and Cassini missions").

## Suggested topology label
**mga-tour** (non-repeating multi-flyby tours). Justification: every case is a one-way sequence of distinct planetary encounters ("Fly-by sequences, departing dates, transfer times, number of revolutions around the Sun and manoeuvres size and location do not need to be known a priori") with a finite launch window, and the only repeated-body legs (MM in EVEMMMJ, EE in the comet case) are resonant connectors inside a non-cyclic sequence. Strictly it is a heliocentric planetary MGA method, not a moon tour; the label applies to scope of method, not to any moon system.
